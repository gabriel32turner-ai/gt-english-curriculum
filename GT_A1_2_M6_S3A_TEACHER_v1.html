TEACHER NOTE — Vocabulary Focus

"Appointment" — false friend alert: "cita" in Spanish covers both personal appointments (doctor, hairdresser) and business meetings. In English, "appointment" is more formal — a scheduled meeting with a specific person. For informal meetings, say "meeting." "Escort" — in a professional context this means "to accompany." It has no negative connotation in business English. Students sometimes hesitate — normalize it: "I'll escort you to the meeting room" is standard corporate English. "Refreshments" — 4 syllables: re-FRESH-ments. Students often forget the middle syllable. Drill x3.

Colloquial — Real Client Visit English
4 phrases that make you sound like a professional host
5 min
"This way, please."
The most natural phrase when guiding a visitor through your building. More professional than "Follow me" or "Come here." Use it every time you escort someone. Example: "This way, please — our meeting room is just down the hall."
"Nice to meet you!"
The standard A1 greeting when meeting someone for the first time. Warmer than "It's a pleasure" — use it with peers and colleagues. "It's a pleasure" is for senior contacts and formal situations. Know when to use each.
"Can I help you with anything?"
Open offer to a visitor — shows hospitality. Use it when someone is looking around reception or waiting. "Can I help you with anything?" signals you are attentive and professional. Follow with: "Are you here for a meeting?"
"Make yourself comfortable."
Tells a visitor to relax while they wait. More natural than "Please sit down and wait." Example: "Please make yourself comfortable — Ing. Torres will be with you in 5 minutes." Pair it with offering refreshments.
TEACHER NOTE — Colloquial Drill

Drill as a physical role play: Teacher is the visitor arriving. Students must use all 4 phrases in sequence: (1) "Can I help you with anything?" → (2) "Nice to meet you!" → (3) "Make yourself comfortable." → (4) "This way, please." Run this 3 times with different students as the receptionist. The physical sequence builds procedural memory — students will remember the phrases IN CONTEXT, not as isolated vocabulary. Ask: "Which of these 4 phrases can you use at your company TOMORROW?" All 4 hands should go up.

Grammar — "There is / There are" + Prepositions of Place
Problem → Tool · Office tour context · A1.2 consolidation
12–15 min
⚠️ REAL WORK SITUATION

Your international visitor asks: "Where is the restroom?" and "Is there a coffee machine near here?" You want to answer clearly — but in English, you freeze.

❌ "The bathroom is in the left and the coffee... um... it's at there." This is the moment Module 6 grammar helps you: there is / there are for describing what exists, and prepositions of place for describing exactly where things are.

There is / There are — Describing the Office

StructureUseOffice example
There is + singularone thing existsThere is a coffee machine on the second floor.
There are + pluralmore than oneThere are two meeting rooms on this floor.
There isn't a…something doesn't existThere isn't a gym, but there is a break room.
There aren't any…plural doesn't existThere aren't any parking spaces on the street.
Is there a…?asking if something existsIs there a printer near the conference room?
Are there any…?asking about pluralAre there any elevators in the building?

Prepositions of Place — Office Navigation

PrepositionMeaningOffice example
on the right / leftdirection when walkingTurn left — the restroom is on the right.
at the end ofat the far end of a hallwayThe conference room is at the end of the hall.
next tobeside somethingThe printer is next to the water cooler.
on the [floor] floorwhich level of the buildingHR is on the third floor.
opposite / across fromdirectly facing somethingThe elevator is opposite the reception desk.
between… and…in the middle of two thingsThe break room is between the stairs and Room 204.
TEACHER NOTE — Grammar Delivery

Use the PROBLEM card to hook students — everyone has frozen when asked "where's the bathroom?" in English. Then reveal the structure as the solution. Draw a simple floor plan on the board: reception, elevator, 3 rooms, restrooms. Practice: teacher asks "Is there a meeting room?" → students answer using "there is/are" + preposition. This spatial practice is the most effective way to internalize both structures simultaneously. Common error: "There are a meeting room" — singular needs "is." Write on board: is = 1 / are = 2+.

Activity A — Quick Poll: Is it correct?

1. "There are a coffee machine next to the elevator."

Singular noun → "There IS a coffee machine." "There are" is for plural: "There are two coffee machines."

2. "Is there any parking spaces in the building?"

Plural noun → "Are there ANY parking spaces?" Use "Is there A…?" for singular, "Are there ANY…?" for plural.

3. "The restroom is at the end of the hall, on the left."

✅ Perfect — "at the end of the hall" + "on the left." Clear, professional directions.

4. "There isn't any elevators in this building."

Plural noun → "There AREN'T any elevators." "There isn't any" works with uncountable nouns (water, space), not plural countable nouns.
🗣️ MICRO-SPEAKING
Tell your partner 3 true sentences about your real workplace using "There is/are": where the coffee machine is, where the meeting rooms are, and one thing that doesn't exist at your company.

Activity B — Put the Office Tour in Order

Drag the tour guide's sentences into the correct logical sequence.

  • ⠿"There is a great view of the city from here. And there are two conference rooms — Room A and Room B."
  • ⠿"Welcome to ConnectPro! Let me show you around our office."
  • ⠿"This is the meeting room for today. Please make yourself comfortable. Can I offer you a coffee?"
  • ⠿"This way, please. The elevator is opposite the reception desk — we're going to the third floor."
  • ⠿"On the third floor, there is our operations team — about 40 people work here. The restroom is at the end of the hall, on the right."
GT ORIGINAL — The Office Tour as A1 Grammar Integration

The office tour is the most natural context for "there is/are" + prepositions at A1.2. After Activity B, have students recreate the tour using their OWN building as the context. 3 minutes: students prepare 5 sentences about their workplace ("There is a cafeteria on the ground floor. The production area is on the left of the main corridor."). Then: one student gives the "tour" to the class as if they were receiving an international visitor. This is the Module 6 closing arc beginning — real language, real building, real professional identity.

Listening — A Client Arrival at ConnectPro
Mónica Herrera · ConnectPro International · Guadalajara
8–10 min
Client Arrival — ConnectPro International, Guadalajara
A1.2 · M6 · S3A · Voice: Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.80
ElevenLabs Script — ~160 words · ~60 seconds at 0.80

Hello. My name is Andrea Soto. I'm the receptionist at ConnectPro International in Guadalajara. Today I want to describe a client visit — it happened this morning.

At 9 AM, a visitor arrived at reception. His name is Kevin Park and he is from NorTech Solutions in Chicago, in the United States. He had an appointment with our Sales Manager, Mónica Herrera, at 9:30.

I said: "Good morning! Welcome to ConnectPro. Can I have your name, please?" He said his name and I checked the appointment list. Then I asked him to sign in and I gave him a visitor badge.

I offered him refreshments — he asked for a coffee. I called Mónica and told her that Mr. Park was at reception. She arrived in five minutes.

Mónica said: "Mr. Park, it's a pleasure to meet you. Please, this way — there is a conference room on the second floor. There is a great view of the city from there."

Everything was professional and the visit started well. This is what happens every week at ConnectPro.

Comprehension — True or False

1. Kevin Park is from NorTech Solutions in New York.

He's from NorTech Solutions in Chicago, Illinois — not New York.

2. Kevin's appointment with Mónica was at 9:30 AM.

✅ "He had an appointment with our Sales Manager at 9:30."

3. Andrea gave Kevin a visitor badge and offered him refreshments.

✅ Both correct — badge + refreshments (coffee). This is the complete reception protocol.

4. The conference room is on the third floor.

"There is a conference room on the SECOND floor" — not the third.

5. Mónica used "This way, please" and "there is" when receiving Mr. Park.

✅ "Please, this way" + "there is a conference room on the second floor" + "there is a great view." All three structures from today in one real situation.
🗣️ MICRO-SPEAKING
From memory: Describe what Andrea did in 3 sentences. Then: describe what Mónica said in 2 sentences. Use "there is/are" at least once. No looking at the script.
TEACHER NOTE — Listening S3A M6

Play once for gist — students identify the situation. Second listen: students track every "there is/are" they hear (answer: 2 — "there is a conference room" / "there is a great view"). Third question: "Which vocabulary words from §3 appeared?" Expected: appointment / sign in / visitor badge / refreshments / reception / pleasure / this way. Count them together. Key insight for students: Andrea and Mónica used ALL the vocabulary from today's class — in one real 5-minute visit. This is not abstract language — it's what happens every week at international companies in Guadalajara.

TEACHER NOTE — Speaking Practice M6 S3A

Q3 (full reception role play) is the S3A peak — it integrates vocabulary, colloquial phrases, and the grammar in one flowing scenario. Walk the room.
