§1
Warm-Up — What do you remember?
No notes · 5 forms from memory · Diagnostic before drilling
⏱ 8 MIN
📘 Pedagogical Note
Do NOT give grammar hints at this stage. The goal is diagnostic — surface gaps before drilling. Collect student sentences on the board. Note which future forms they avoid (usually: may vs might distinction, and going to vs present continuous). These gaps guide your energy in §2 and §3.
No notes, no book. In this warm-up, you only use what you remember. That's the whole point — to find out which forms are automatic and which still need work. Be honest with yourself.
Round 1 — 5 Forms from Memory
Without looking at your notes, give one example sentence for each form: Present Continuous (confirmed) · May · Might · Going To (intention) · Won't. Use a real work situation.
Partner checks: did you use each form for the right type of future event?
Round 2 — Quick Decision
Partner A describes a situation (e.g., "Your flight is booked for Tuesday"). Partner B gives the correct sentence instantly. Switch after 4 situations. Speed matters — this tests automaticity, not just knowledge.
💡 Situations: booked appointment · uncertain client · personal task · cancelled event · probable outcome
Round 3 — The Hard One
What's the difference between "I'm going to call the client" and "I'm calling the client at 3 PM"? When would you use each? Give a real example of each from your own job. Can you explain the rule to your partner?
💡 If students can explain it — they own it. If they say "they're the same" — that's your teaching target for this session.
💡 Engagement Tip
After Round 3, write the two forms on the board: "I'm calling at 3 PM" vs "I'm going to call." Ask hands: who thinks they're different? Who thinks they're the same? The disagreement creates the learning moment. Don't resolve it yet — let it simmer until §2.

PRESENT CONTINUOUS — Confirmed Arrangements
"I'm meeting the board at 3 PM Thursday." → invite sent, both confirmed
"We're flying to CDMX on Wednesday — tickets booked."
"She's presenting at the conference on Friday." → on the program
"I'm having lunch with the director at noon." → restaurant reserved
⚠️ Key rule: If it's in the calendar (both sides confirmed) → use present continuous, NOT going to.
✗ "I'm going to meet the board at 3" (if it's booked) → ✅ "I'm meeting the board at 3"
GOING TO — Personal Intentions
"I'm going to review the supplier data this afternoon." → solo task
"She's going to reorganize the project files." → her decision
"We're going to implement the new system next quarter." → planned intention
"I'm going to call the supplier when I have time." → no specific time set
⚠️ The upgrade: Once you book something with another person → switch from going to → present continuous.
"I'm going to call him" → [you agree on 3 PM] → "I'm calling him at 3 PM."
MAY — ~50% Possibility
"The client may call tomorrow." → manager said "probably"
"The flight may be delayed." → weather warning issued
"We may need to extend the deadline." → depends on supplier
"She may not be available on Monday." → possible conflict
⚠️ Never: "He mays call" / "She may to call" → modals never conjugate, never take "to"
MIGHT — ~30% Less Certain
"I might travel to Monterrey — it depends on approval." → unlikely but possible
"She might not attend — she's very busy." → probably won't
"The contract might be delayed — legal is reviewing." → uncertain
"He might need to extend his stay." → client wants extra meeting
⚠️ May vs Might: Use may when it's roughly 50/50. Use might when it's less likely — less certain, more tentative.
WON'T — 0% / Cancelled / Decided Against
"She won't attend — the CEO confirmed." → cancelled
"The training won't happen this quarter." → budget cut
"I won't need to travel — the client is coming here." → not necessary
"We won't extend the deadline — final answer." → firm decision

✏️ Activity A — Choose the Correct Form
1. You booked the hotel, got the confirmation email, seat assigned. → "I _____ at the Marriott at noon on Monday."
2. You decided alone to organize your email inbox — no one else involved, no specific time. → "I _____ organize my inbox this week."
3. The supplier hasn't replied. Their manager said "we'll probably get back to you." → "They _____ confirm by Friday."
4. The conference was officially cancelled by the organizers. → "The conference _____ happen this year."
5. The meeting is in both calendars, invite accepted. → "We _____ at 10 AM Tuesday."
✏️ Activity B — True or False?
"I'm going to present to the board at 3 PM" is correct when the presentation is already in the calendar and the board is expecting you.
"She might to come" is a grammatically correct use of might.
May and might can both be used to express uncertainty, but might signals less certainty than may.
"I'm going to call the client at 3 PM" and "I'm calling the client at 3 PM" always mean exactly the same thing.
"Won't" is used when something is certain NOT to happen — cancelled, decided against, or 0% likely.

§5
Colloquial Wall — M4 Phrases You Own Now
All key phrases from S3 + S4 · Click to flip · Can you use all 8 without looking?
⏱ 8 MIN
PHRASE 1 · S3
"We'll see."
Uncertainty · Not decided yet
Use when something is uncertain — neither confirmed nor cancelled. Signals you're aware of the variable but not stressing about it.
"Are you coming to the conference?" — "We'll see — it depends on the budget approval."
PHRASE 2 · S3
"It depends."
Conditional · Context-based answer
The professional answer when an outcome relies on another factor. Often followed by "on [factor]" to be specific.
"Will you stay an extra night?" — "It depends on the client meeting on Thursday."
PHRASE 3 · S3
"I'll keep you posted."
Update promise · Ongoing situation
Commit to updating someone when things change. Used in emails and calls when something is still TBD. Instantly professional.
"Is the flight confirmed?" — "Not yet — I'll keep you posted."
PHRASE 4 · S3
"TBD."
To Be Determined · Calendar shorthand
Standard in English-language calendars, emails, and meeting agendas. Tells everyone "we'll confirm later."
"What time is the dinner?" — "TBD — we're waiting for the client to confirm."
PHRASE 5 · S4
"My calendar is packed."
Busy schedule · No free slots
Immediately understood by any English speaker as "I have no free time." Much more natural than "I am very busy."
"Can we meet Tuesday?" — "My calendar is packed — how about next Thursday?"
PHRASE 6 · S4
"Let me check my availability."
Professional response to meeting requests
Use instead of "I don't know" or "maybe." It buys time, sounds organized, and signals that you have a real schedule to manage.
"Are you free Thursday?" — "Let me check my availability and get back to you."
PHRASE 7 · S4
"I have a conflict."
Scheduling clash · Two things at the same time
The standard professional way to say two events overlap. More precise than "I'm busy" — it tells the other person the specific problem.
"The 2 PM works." — "Actually, I have a conflict — I'm presenting to the board then."
PHRASE 8 · S4
"Put it in the calendar."
Confirmation signal · Seals the arrangement
This phrase = the moment a plan becomes a confirmed arrangement. Grammar shifts: "going to meet" → "meeting." Key insight from S4.
"So Wednesday at 3?" — "Perfect. Put it in the calendar and I'll send the invite."

Error Hunt — Find & Fix the Mistake
10 sentences · Some correct · Some wrong · Click to reveal the verdict
⏱ 10 MIN
💡 How to run this
Students read each sentence silently and decide: correct or wrong? They click to reveal the verdict. Run as a team competition: +2 for spotting an error AND fixing it correctly. +1 for correct sentences identified. Wrong guesses = 0. First team to 14 points wins badge 🔍.
0
Error Hunt Points
1. "I'm going to present to the board at 3 PM on Thursday — the room is booked and all directors confirmed."
❌ ERROR — The presentation is confirmed in the calendar. Use: "I'm presenting to the board at 3 PM on Thursday." Confirmed arrangement = present continuous.
2. "She might to call tomorrow — we're not sure if she has the number."
❌ ERROR — No "to" after modals. Fix: "She might call tomorrow." Rule: might + base verb, always.
3. "The flight may be delayed — there's a weather warning for Tuesday morning."
✅ CORRECT — May for ~50% possibility with a clear contextual reason. Perfect use.
4. "I mays travel to Monterrey next month — it depends on the budget."
❌ ERROR — Modals never conjugate. Fix: "I may travel to Monterrey." May has no -s, -ed, or -ing form.
5. "I'm going to review the Q4 data this afternoon — it's on my to-do list."
✅ CORRECT — Solo task, personal intention, no arrangement with others. Going to is exactly right here.
6. "The team won't attends the conference — the CEO cancelled it."
❌ ERROR — Won't + base verb, no -s. Fix: "The team won't attend the conference."
7. "I'm meeting the supplier at 10 AM — the invite is in both our calendars."
✅ CORRECT — Confirmed arrangement, both parties, calendar invite sent. Present continuous is the right form and signals commitment.
8. "He might will come to the office tomorrow."
❌ ERROR — Never two modals together. Fix: "He might come to the office tomorrow." Choose one modal only.
9. "She might not be available on Monday — she may have a conflict."
✅ CORRECT — Excellent! Two different uncertainty forms used for two different reasons. Might not = low probability of availability. May have = reasonable possibility of a conflict.
10. "My calendar is full — I going to check my availability and send you the slots."
❌ ERROR — Missing "am" before going to. Fix: "I'm going to check my availability." Always: am/is/are + going to.
§7
Integrated Speaking — 3 Real Work Scenarios
Use all M4 forms in context · Pair production · Timed
⏱ 12 MIN
📘 How to run §7
Each speaking scenario uses all 5 forms. Students produce in pairs. Role-play format: one person is the manager, one is the team member. After each scenario (3–4 minutes), switch roles. Teacher circles and notes: (1) which form they avoid most, (2) any modal errors ("might to / mays"), (3) going to where present continuous would be more professional. Correct in the debrief, not mid-speech.
Scenario 1 — Monday Morning Briefing
You are Andrea Soto. Your manager asks: "What does your week look like?" Give a complete weekly briefing — confirmed meetings, personal tasks, uncertain events, and anything that won't happen.
  • At least 2 confirmed arrangements → present continuous
  • At least 2 personal intentions → going to
  • At least 1 uncertain event → may or might
  • At least 1 thing that won't happen → won't
  • Use 2 colloquial phrases naturally
I'm meeting… I'm going to… …may… …might… …won't… My calendar is packed
Scenario 2 — Travel Update Call
Andrea's trip to Guadalajara has been disrupted: Tuesday flight cancelled, hotel confirmed, client meeting uncertain, Wednesday warehouse visit probably not happening. Brief your manager in 4 sentences — one for each certainty level.
  • "The hotel is confirmed" → I'm checking in at noon
  • "The client meeting — 50% chance" → may
  • "The warehouse visit — unlikely" → might not
  • "The Tuesday flight — cancelled" → won't
I'm checking in… The meeting may… I might not… The flight won't… I'll keep you posted
Scenario 3 — New Employee 90-Day Plan
You just started a new position. Your manager asks: "What are your plans and priorities for the first 90 days?" Give a 5-sentence plan that mixes what's already confirmed with what you personally intend to do, and acknowledge one thing that's uncertain.
  • At least 1 confirmed (orientation session in calendar) → present continuous
  • At least 3 personal goals or tasks → going to
  • At least 1 uncertain outcome → may or might
  • Professional tone throughout — no "I will try"
I'm attending… I'm going to… …may… Let me check my availability
