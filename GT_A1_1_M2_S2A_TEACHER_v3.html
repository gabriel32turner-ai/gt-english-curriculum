§ 01
What does your colleague do?
🔄 Activates S1 vocab · Shifts focus from "I" to "he/she" · Creates the need for 3rd person before the rule
⏱ 5–8 MIN
🔄 Quick S1 review: Say 2 things YOU do every day at work. Use "I…" — 30 seconds. (Teacher: note the verbs on the whiteboard to use them in §4.)
👥 Now about your colleague: Think of someone you work with. What time do they arrive? What do they do every day? Try to say it in English — as best you can!
🤔 Feel the gap: Say this sentence about Ana: "Ana ___ work at 8." Does it sound right to say "Ana start work at 8"? Try it. What do you feel is missing?
⚡ Teacher note: Let students try to describe a colleague. They will use the base verb ("She start") → this creates the natural error you correct in §4. Write 3–4 attempts on the whiteboard EXACTLY as they say them — errors included. Do not correct yet.
⚡
Quick Win — 30 segundos
Say these two sentences about Ana: "She starts work at 8. She checks her email first."
Say the sentences out loud. Why do they sound different? In §4 you will understand everything. 🎯
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Write the student's attempts on the whiteboard exactly as they say them — "She start at 8" — without correcting. Esos errores son el material vivo del §4.
  • The goal of the warm-up is for the student to FEEL that something is missing. When they get to §4 and see the rule, they will say: "Ah, that's why!"
  • The 30-second S1 Review activates episodic memory. If the student can already say "I check email every day" fluently → celebrate that progress before moving on.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "In S1 you learned to talk about YOURSELF. Today you learn to talk about OTHERS. That doubles your work English." 📈
Layer 3 · Retención
  • "By the end of this session you will be able to describe any colleague in English — what they do, what they don't do, and ask questions about them."

GT Original — La Trampa del -S para Spanish speakers 🎯
  • ⚠️ El error más común en A1.1: "She start at 8" — olvidar el -s. En inglés, HE/SHE/IT SIEMPRE lleva el -s en afirmativo. Sin excepciones.
  • 🚫 But in negatives and questions — do NOT add -s to the main verb: "She doesn't start" — el -s ya está DENTRO de "does". "Does she start?" — mismo principio.
  • 🧠 Memory tip: If you see DOES or DOESN'T → the main verb is ALWAYS the base form. "She does not start" (NOT starts). El -s viajó al auxiliar.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Student dice la forma base, luego adivina la 3rd person ANTES de flip the card.
  • El spelling grid es referencia — no lo memorizas ahora, lo usas durante los ejercicios.
  • GT Original: el truco del "El -s viajó al auxiliar" es poderoso para A1.1. Repítelo 3 veces con ejemplos distintos.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "There are only 3 spelling rules. How many verbs do you know that end in -y? And in -sh?" 🤔
Layer 3 · Retención
  • "En S2B el Transformer WOW te pone a prueba con verbos aleatorios. El que más rápido produce the correct form gana."
§ 04
He / She / It — does & doesn't
📐 Complete 3rd person singular · Aff/neg/?/short answer table · The -s trap in negatives · Real workplace context
⏱ 12–15 MIN
🎯 Situación Real — Antes de la Regla
Your HR director in the US asks you:
"Can you tell me about your coordinator? What does she do every day? Does she travel for work? Does she manage a team?"

You know the answers — Ana works from 8 to 6, manages the production calendar, doesn't travel. But how do you say it in English? "She work from 8 to 6" sounds wrong. What is the rule?
➡ La herramienta: 3rd person -s para afirmativo + does/doesn't para negativos y preguntas.
↓
✅ The Tool: He / She / It
Cuando el sujeto es he, she o it → the verb cambia: agrega -s (o -es o -ies) en sentences afirmativas.
Para negativos y preguntas → usa does/doesn't + verbo BASE (sin -s en the main verb).
FormaFórmulaEjemploShort Answer
✅ AfirmativoHe / She / It + verbo+s"She manages the team every day."—
❌ NegativoHe / She / It + doesn't + verbo base"He doesn't travel for work."—
❓ PreguntaDoes + he/she/it + verbo base + ?"Does she attend the Monday meeting?"Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.
💬 Short AnswerYes, he/she/it does. / No, he/she/it doesn't."Does he work Sundays?" → "No, he doesn't."Nunca repitas el verbo completo
⚠️ Critical trap — do NOT add -s after doesn't:
✗ "She doesn't works on weekends." — ERROR! The -s is already INSIDE "doesn't".
✓ "She doesn't work on weekends." — Base verb. Always base verb after does/doesn't.
⚡ Common Errors — Spanish Speakers
✗ "She don't attend meetings."→✓ "She doesn't attend meetings."
"Don't" es para I/you/we/they. La 3rd person SIEMPRE usa "doesn't".
✗ "She doesn't manages the team."→✓ "She doesn't manage the team."
The -s moved to the auxiliary (doesn't). The main verb goes in base form.

3. ❓ "___ she manage the production schedule every day?"
4. 📋 "The manager ___ his weekly report every Friday afternoon."
5. 💬 "— Does Ana work on weekends? — No, ___."

🗣️
Micro-Speaking · 90 segundos
Think of a real colleague. Tell your partner: 2 things they DO (affirmative, with -s) + 1 thing they DON'T DO (doesn't) + 1 question "Does he/she…?" 👥
➡ "My manager leads the weekly meeting. He reviews reports on Fridays. He doesn't travel. Does your manager travel?"
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Return to the §1 errors on the whiteboard and correct them now with the table. "You wrote 'She start' — look at the table: what is the correct form?"
  • The -s after doesn't trap is the #1 error at this level. Practice it at least 3 times with the student's own examples.
  • Quick oral drill after the table: "Does Ana travel? → No, she doesn't." 8 rounds, 45 seconds. Alternate he/she.
  • Analogía: "El -s es como una pelota — en afirmativo está en el verbo (manages). En negativo viajó al auxiliar (doesn't). No puede estar en los dos al mismo tiempo."
Layer 2 · Engagement

Layer 3 · Retención
  • "En S3A aprenderás con qué frecuencia hace las cosas — always, usually, never. Hoy dominas el QUÉ. El siguiente paso es con qué frecuencia."

Hi John! Here is a quick overview of my team for the HR report. 📋

3rd Person Transformer
🔄 WOW component · Receive subject + base verb → produce the correct form in a complete sentence · 8 rounds · All patterns
⏱ 10–12 MIN
👤 Question 1 — Your colleague
Describe a real colleague. Tell your partner: 2 things they do (with -s) + 1 thing they don't do (doesn't) + 1 question "Does he/she…?"
💡 "My colleague ___s ___ every day. She ___s ___. She doesn't ___. Does your colleague ___?"
👔 Question 2 — Tu manager
Describe a tu manager directo. Usa al menos 4 sentences: 2 afirmativas, 1 negativa, 1 pregunta para your partner sobre SU manager.
💡 "My manager leads ___. He/She manages ___. He/She doesn't ___. Does your manager ___?"
⭐ Question 3 — Team Report Challenge
In 60 seconds, give a verbal HR-style report on a real colleague — like Carlos on Ana. Include: name, role, 3 things they do, 1 they don't. Without notes.
💡 "[Name] is our [role]. He/She starts at ___. He/She ___s and ___s. He/She doesn't ___. He/She finishes at ___."
Sample Answer — Carlos describes Ana:
"Ana works at a manufacturing company in Celaya. She starts work at eight. Every morning, she checks her email and logs in to the system. She has a team meeting every Monday at nine. She doesn't eat at her desk — she goes to the lunch room at two. She leaves the office at six. She doesn't work on weekends. Does she manage a team? Yes, she does."
