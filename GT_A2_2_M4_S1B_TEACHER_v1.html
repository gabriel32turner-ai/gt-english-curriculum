§1
Hot Take — Certainty or Uncertainty?
Andrea's situation · 60-second spoken response · May / Might production from the first minute · 5 min
⏱ 5 MIN
🔥 Andrea's Hot Take — Thursday morning, 8 AM
Andrea just got a WhatsApp from her manager: "Hi — the director confirmed the meeting. You're presenting Friday at 10 AM in Guadalajara. Flight may be available — check ASAP. Hotel TBD. Let me know."

Is Andrea excited or stressed? What should she do first? What might she be feeling — and what will she definitely need to do?
🎯 Use may / might at least 3 times ✅ Use will for 1 certain thing ⏱ 60 seconds — no notes 🏆 Badge for completing the timer
Model structure (do NOT show until after students speak)
"She might feel nervous because it's her first big presentation. She will definitely need to book the flight tonight. The hotel may be arranged by the company — but it might not be. She should probably…"
⚠️ Notice: "might feel" / "will need" / "may be" — three different certainty levels in 3 sentences.
60
⚡ Teacher Note — S1B Opening
S1B is 100% production from minute one. No new grammar — only activation. Listen for: (1) bare infinitive after may/might — correct "might to" immediately, (2) confusion between will and might — ask "are you 100% sure?" to help students self-correct. Count how many times may/might appear in each student's 60 seconds — the class record becomes motivation.
§2
Pitch Round — Remote Work vs Office
Two sides · Uncertainty language in a real professional debate · May / Might integrated · 10 min
⏱ 10 MIN
💬 Setup: The company is deciding whether to send the team on a 3-day business trip to Mexico City or hold a virtual conference. Your manager asked: "What do you think might happen with productivity? What may be the risks?" Assign sides — A argues for the trip, B argues for virtual.
Side A — "The trip will be worth it"
  • The team may build better relationships in person
  • Productivity might increase after face-to-face alignment
  • The client may respond better to an in-person visit
  • The company might win the contract if we show commitment
  • People will feel more motivated — travel is energizing
Side B — "Virtual might be smarter"
  • The trip may cost more than the company can afford right now
  • Someone might get sick or have a travel delay
  • The meeting may be just as effective online
  • The team might lose two days of production work
  • We may not need to travel — technology is good enough
Grammar-Integrated Starters — Click to hear
I think we may benefit from… There's a chance the client might… We will definitely need to… The risk is that we might not… It may turn out that… I'm not sure, but it might… The company may have to reconsider…
⏱ Debate timer — 3 min per side
3:00
§3
Relay — Certainty Upgrade Chain
Transform each situation from "uncertain" to "confirmed" using the right modal · 5 prompts · 8 min
⏱ 8 MIN
🔄 Certainty Upgrade — Might → May → Will
0 / 5 revealed
💡 Each card starts with an uncertain situation. Student A reads it aloud using "might." Student B transforms it to "may." Student C confirms it with "will." Then reveal the model answers and compare. Rotate after each card.
Situation: The director hasn't responded to the flight booking request. It's possible — maybe 30% chance — that the trip is approved.
🎯 Might (30%): "The trip might be approved — we're still waiting."
📈 May (50%): "The trip may be approved — she usually responds by noon."
✅ Will (100%): "The trip will be approved — I just got her email."
Situation: Andrea searched for hotels but hasn't booked yet. About 50% chance she stays in Guadalajara overnight — depends on the meeting length.
🎯 Might (30%): "She might stay overnight — it's not clear yet."
📈 May (50%): "She may stay overnight — the meeting usually runs late."
✅ Will (100%): "She will stay overnight — the hotel is booked for Friday."
Situation: The client hasn't confirmed the second meeting. Very unlikely — maybe 30% — that they will add it to the agenda on Friday.
🎯 Might (30%): "The client might request a second meeting on Friday."
📈 May (50%): "The client may add a second meeting — they mentioned it last week."
✅ Will (100%): "The client will attend the second meeting — it's in the calendar."
Situation: Andrea's presentation slides are 70% done. There's a ~50% chance she finishes them tonight — depends on how long the team meeting runs.
🎯 Might (30%): "She might finish the slides tonight — if the meeting ends early."
📈 May (50%): "She may finish the slides tonight — she's already halfway done."
✅ Will (100%): "She will finish the slides tonight — she just sent me the final version."
Situation: The regional director heard about Andrea's promotion. Maybe — around 50% — she'll ask Andrea to present to the full leadership team, not just her department.
🎯 Might (30%): "The director might ask Andrea to present to the full leadership team."
📈 May (50%): "The director may invite the full team — she was impressed by the Q4 numbers."
✅ Will (100%): "The director will have the full leadership team present — she confirmed it this morning."

Speed Round — 10 Cards · Modal Flash
Quick-fire may / might / will decisions · Flip and score · 6 min
⏱ 6 MIN
⚡ Speed Round — Flip to reveal · Mark correct or wrong
0 pts
"The flight _____ be delayed — there's a storm." (~30%)
might
~30% = might. Storm is possible but not certain.
"I _____ stay at the Marriott — the reservation is confirmed." (100%)
will
100% = will. Reservation is already confirmed.
"She _____ need a visa — it depends on her passport." (~50%)
may
~50% = may. Unclear — depends on a condition.
"My luggage _____ not arrive on time — the connection is tight." (~30%)
might
might not = negative possibility ~30%. Tight connection = risky, not certain.
"The meeting _____ be cancelled — the director hasn't confirmed yet." (~50%)
may
~50% = may. No confirmation yet, but not unlikely.
"I _____ send the expense report by Friday — it's required." (100%)
will
100% = will. It's required — no uncertainty.
"Andrea _____ present to the full board — they may invite her." (~30%)
might
~30% = might. Possible but uncertain — depends on the invitation.
"The virtual option _____ save money — it depends on the plan." (~50%)
may
~50% = may. "It depends" signals around 50% — use may.
"The client _____ not attend the afternoon session — they mentioned a conflict." (~30%)
might not
might not = ~30% negative. Mentioned a conflict but not confirmed.
"She _____ travel internationally next year — the director confirmed it." (100%)
will
100% = will. Director confirmed = certain. No may/might needed.

⏱ 8 MIN
🎯 Solo Challenge — 2 Minutes · No Notes
You are Andrea Soto. It's Thursday evening. Your manager Daniel has just asked for a full update on tomorrow's Guadalajara trip. You have 2 minutes to cover everything:

— What is confirmed (will) · What is possible (may) · What is uncertain (might) · One thing that might NOT happen · How you're feeling about it
1
Use will at least 2 times for confirmed plans
2
Use may at least 2 times for ~50% possibilities
3
Use might at least 2 times for ~30% uncertain situations
4
Use might not or may not at least once
5
NO "might to" — bare infinitive rule enforced by partner
2:00
⚡ Teacher Note — Solo Challenge Scoring
Award 10 points for each correct modal use (will / may / might / might not). Deduct 5 for "might to." The student who uses the most modals correctly in 2 minutes goes on the leaderboard in §7. Typical A2.2 range: 8–14 modal uses in 2 minutes. Record the class record.

Source Analysis — Real Uncertainty in Context
4 authentic work messages · Identify may / might / will · Metacognitive question each · 8 min
⏱ 8 MIN
💬 How to use: Students read each source, identify the modal, explain the certainty level, and answer the metacognitive question. These are real work communication formats — WhatsApp, email, calendar note, meeting agenda.
📱 WhatsApp — Manager to Andrea
"Hi Andrea — the director may want to reschedule for Monday instead. Nothing confirmed yet. I might have more info by 5 PM. I'll keep you posted."
❓ How many different certainty levels appear here? Which phrase confirms the uncertainty?
📧 Email — Regional Director to the team
"Dear team — our Q2 results will be presented at the all-hands on Thursday at 3 PM. The CFO may join us for the second half. A video recording might be available for those who cannot attend in person."
❓ Which event is 100% certain? Which are only possible? How do you know from the language?
🗓️ Calendar Note — Andrea's Friday agenda
"9:00 AM — Flight to GDL (may be delayed — check app). 11:30 AM — Meet director at Solaris office (confirmed). 2:00 PM — Client meeting (TBD — might be moved to Monday). 6:00 PM — Return flight (might not be available — check alternative)."
❓ If YOU were Andrea, which item would stress you most? Why? What would you do about the uncertain ones?
📋 Meeting Agenda — Q2 Review Planning
"Agenda items: (1) Q2 sales results — CONFIRMED · (2) New supplier — may be discussed if time allows · (3) Team restructure update — TBD, might be postponed to next quarter · (4) International expansion — will be presented by Andrea Soto."
❓ What does this agenda tell you about professional English? Why use different modals for different items?

🎯 This week at work
3 Phrases You Will Use Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"The [meeting/trip/call] may be [delayed/cancelled] — TBD."
Write this in your next team update when something isn't confirmed. "TBD" signals professional uncertainty. "May be" tells colleagues the probability without committing. Zero ambiguity — maximum precision.
PHRASE 2
"I might not make it — I'll confirm by [time]."
Use this when you're ~30% sure you can't attend something. The second half ("I'll confirm by") turns uncertainty into a commitment. Your manager will see this as professional, not evasive.
PHRASE 3
"I will [action] — and I may also [action] if [condition]."
The corporate structure: one certain commitment + one conditional possibility. "I will send the report by 5 PM — and I may also include the Q1 data if I get the numbers in time." This is how senior professionals communicate plans.
