§ 01
Meet someone new — Introduce them!
🤝 Activates S1A + S2A · The student tries to introduce someone before learning the formula · Creates the need for "This is…" in real context
⏱ 5–7 MIN
🤝 Scenario: A new colleague arrives at your company today. Your manager asks you: "Please introduce [colleague] to the team." What do you say? Try it now — with everything you know from M3. Where do you start?
❓ Two key questions: When someone introduces you to a new person, what are the TWO first questions you ask in Spanish? Now try saying them in English: "What do you do?" / "What are you like?" — which one is correct for asking what someone is LIKE?
🌐 Formal vs. informal: How do you introduce your manager in an important meeting VS. how do you introduce your colleague to a friend in the hallway? Does the language change? In English too — today you learn both versions.
⚡ Teacher note: Write on the whiteboard: "This is ___. She's a very ___ ___. ___'s ___ is ___" — leave the blanks. This is the full M3 formula the student will master today. Connect it with S1A (adj) and S2A ('s). "Today we use everything together."
⚡
Quick Win — 45 segundos
Choose ONE real person from your workplace. Introduce them with what you already know: "This is [name]. She/He is a [role]. She/He is very [adj]."
Add a possessive if you can: "[Name]'s [object] is very ___."
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Quick role — you are the "new American colleague" arriving. The student introduces you to an imaginary coworker using all of M3 so far.
  • Explicit connection: "In S1A you learned adj. In S2A you learned 's. Today we combine them: This is [name] — S1A + S2A together in a real introduction."
  • "What's she like?" — if the student does not know this phrase, write it on the whiteboard. It is the question that activates all of M3's descriptions.
  • Formal vs. informal: do not go into detail yet — let the student feel that there is a difference. The answer comes in §2 and §3.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "The first impression in English is not just 'Hi, I'm Ana' — it is 'Let me introduce you to Ana. She's our Production Coordinator and she's a very organized team player.' That opens doors." 🚀
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "By the end of this session you will be able to introduce any person on your team in full English — formal format for a meeting, informal for the hallway and digital for a Zoom call."

Intro Phrases + "What's she like?"

🤝 Introduction Phrases — Formal · Informal · Digital · Corporate
🟢 INFORMAL / NEUTRAL — día a día
"This is [name]."
Esta es / Este es [nombre].
"This is Ana. She's our Production Coordinator. She's very organized and quite friendly." — Perfecto para pasillos, slack, llamadas informales.

"Meet [name]!"

GT Original — Respuesta Perfecta a "Nice to meet you" 🤝
  • "Nice to meet you" → correct response: "Nice to meet you too!" or "Pleased to meet you!" — NOT "Nice to meet you also" (sounds literal).
  • After the introduction → immediate question: "What do you do exactly?" or "What's your role here?" — shows genuine interest and activates the M3 description.
  • On Zoom: always say your name + company at the start: "Hi everyone, I'm [name] from [company]." Americans value digital clarity.
  • 🔴 False Friend: "introduce" en inglés = presentar a alguien. "To introduce myself" = presentarme a mí mismo. "My name is Ana — let me introduce myself" ✅
  • 📧 Email de bienvenida: Subject line = "Welcome [name] to the team!" — frase estándar en empresas americanas. The student puede usarla ya.

  • GT Original "Nice to meet you too": practice the complete exchange 3 times. It is the most universal mini-dialogue at the A1.1 level.
  • "What do you do exactly?" — connects to M1. The student already knows this question. Today they use it in an introduction context, not just for self-introduction.

§ 04
The M3 Description Chain
⛓ Combines intro formula + adj (S1A) + possessive 's (S2A) + role · Full to be in descriptive context · The perfect professional introduction
⏱ 12–15 MIN
🎯 Situación Real — Antes de la Regla
You are the host of a Zoom video call with 8 people from your company and 3 clients from Chicago. Your manager arrives late and says: "Can you introduce everyone? Tell them who's who, what they do, and what they're like."

You have 60 seconds to introduce 3 people from your team in full English — name, role, personality, and something that belongs to them. Where do you start?
➡ La herramienta: La Description Chain — 4 pasos en secuencia. Cada paso es una herramienta de M3 que ya conoces.
↓
⛓ M3 Description Chain — 4 pasos para una presentación perfecta
1
INTRO PHRASE
"This is Ana García." / "I'd like to introduce Ana."
Formula by context: hallway / formal / digital (§2 and §3)
2
ROLE
"She's our Production Coordinator." / "She's a team leader in our department."
to be + role. Vocab from S1A (roles) + "our/the/a" as article
3
ADJECTIVES
"She's very organized and quite serious." / "She's a hardworking and friendly coordinator."
to be + adj / adj + noun (S1A) — very / quite as modifiers
4
POSSESSIVE
"Ana's reports are always excellent." / "Her work is creative and precise."
Possessive 's (S2A) or possessive pronoun his/her/their — adds extra context
✅ La Cadena Completa — Ejemplo en 4 pasos
"This is Ana García. [1 — intro] She's our Production Coordinator. [2 — role] She's very organized and quite serious at work — but she's also very friendly. [3 — adj] Ana's reports are always excellent and her desk is the most organized in the office!" [4 — possessive]
PasoEstructuraEjemplo
1 · IntroThis is / Meet / I'd like to introduce"This is David — my colleague."
2 · RoleShe/He + is + (article) + role"She's our team leader." / "He's an intern."
3a · to be + adjShe/He + is + (very/quite) + adj"She's very organized." / "He's quite shy."
3b · adj + nounShe's + a + adj + noun"She's a hardworking coordinator."
4 · Possessive[Name]'s + object + is/are + adj"Ana's work is always excellent." / "David's desk is creative."
❓ What's she like?What's + pronoun + like?"What's she like? — She's very friendly but quite serious."
Paso 1–2
Intro + Role
Presentación + cargo
  • This is Ana. She's our coordinator.
  • Meet David — he's our intern.
  • I'd like to introduce Carlos. He's the CEO.
  • She reports to Carlos.
  • She's in charge of production.
Paso 3
Adjectives
Personalidad + apariencia
  • She's very organized and quite serious.
  • He's a creative and easygoing colleague.
  • They're very hardworking.
  • She's quite shy but very kind.
  • He's tall and has short hair.
Paso 4
Possessive
Genitivo 's en contexto
  • Ana's reports are excellent.
  • David's ideas are very creative.
  • Her work is always precise.
  • His desk is quite organized.
  • Their department's results are great.
💡 The secret of a good introduction in English: Order matters. First name → then role → then personality → then a possession detail. That is the exact order American professionals use. It is not random — it is a proven formula.
⚡ Errores Comunes — Presentaciones en inglés
✗ "What's she like? — She's tall." (físico, no personalidad)→✓ "She's very organized and friendly." (personalidad)
"What's she like?" pregunta personalidad/carácter. "What does she look like?" pregunta apariencia física. Preguntas distintas, respuestas distintas.
✗ "I introduce you Ana." (sin preposición)→✓ "I'd like to introduce you TO Ana." (to + persona)
"Introduce" siempre requiere "to" antes de la persona. "Let me introduce you TO my colleague."
✗ "What are she like?" (auxiliar incorrecto)→✓ "What's she like?" (What + to be + pronoun)
La pregunta usa "What + is/are + pronombre". Singular: "What's he/she like?" · Plural: "What are they like?"

⏱ 10–12 MIN
👨‍💼 Carlos Rivera
Production Manager · Celaya, Guanajuato 🇲🇽
📧 All Team Email
To: Full Department
Monday, 8:00 AM

Good morning, team! 👋 I'd like to take this opportunity to introduce two new members of our department.

First, I'd like to introduce Sofia Reyes. Sofia joins us today as our new Quality Control Coordinator. Sofia is very organized and quite serious about her work — she's a very professional coordinator. She has long hair and is always very confident in meetings. Sofia's reports are always excellent and very detailed. She reports to me directly. Welcome, Sofia!

Second, I'd like you all to meet Miguel Torres — our new intern. Miguel joins us from the University of Guanajuato. He's quite shy at first, but he's very creative and hardworking. He's a very talented intern. Miguel's ideas in his first project were very impressive. He works with Ana on the production team.

What are they like? Sofia is professional and efficient — and she's also very friendly. Miguel is creative and quiet — but ask him about his ideas and he's very passionate! I'm sure you'll love working with both of them. 💼

Please join me in welcoming Sofia and Miguel to our team. Their department's success starts with great people — and these two are excellent! ✅
