§2   Grammar Consolidation  ·  15 min

Second Conditional — Full Reference

THE COMPLETE PICTURE
If + past simple  →  would / could / might + base verb
UseModalM4 Example
Certain hypothetical result would If companies valued employees, quiet quitting would not exist.
Possible or optional result could If she had a mentor, she could integrate much faster.
Uncertain or speculative result might If prejudice didn’t exist, immigrants might find it easier to belong.
Negative main clause wouldn’t If managers acknowledged stigma, employees wouldn’t hide burnout.
Formal (all subjects) were + would If I were HR director, I would redesign the onboarding program.
⚠ NEVER: “If I would …” — the IF clause always uses past simple, never ‘would.’
ACTIVITY A — MIXED TOPICS — Choose the correct form

§3   Integrated Reading  ·  10 min

Carlos Mendez — End of Module Reflection

Over the past four sessions, I’ve asked executives across the Bajío region a simple question: if you could change one thing about your workplace culture tomorrow, what would it be? The answers, session after session, fall into four categories.

Some say mental health: “If we talked about burnout openly, people would stay longer.” Others say gender equality: “If we promoted more women into leadership, our decision-making could improve dramatically.”

A growing group says engagement: “If we recognised effort — not just results — quiet quitting might disappear entirely.” And increasingly, leaders talk about belonging: “If our teams were truly diverse, we might unlock perspectives we cannot even imagine right now.”

What these four answers share is the same grammar: Second Conditional. Not “this is impossible.” Not “this will never happen.” But: if something changed, something better would follow. That’s not pessimism. That’s precisely the mindset that drives real leadership.

The question for you, at the end of this module, is simple: if you could change one thing about your workplace, what would it be? And what’s stopping you from trying?

Carlos Mendez — Module 4 Closing:

We spent four sessions on topics that most companies call “sensitive.” Mental health. Gender roles. Quiet quitting. Immigration. And you know what I noticed? Nobody avoided the conversation. You engaged. You argued. You changed your position at least once. That is exactly what advanced English does for you: it gives you the vocabulary to think more clearly, and the grammar to express nuance.

Second Conditional is not just a grammar structure. It is a cognitive tool. If I were a more empathetic manager, what would I do differently? If this company genuinely valued diversity, what would change tomorrow? These are not abstract questions. They are the questions that drive better decisions.

Here is what I want you to take away from Module 4. Three things. First: the English language gives you access to global conversations that your industry is already having — about wellbeing, inclusion, and engagement. Second: Second Conditional is your tool for hypothetical leadership thinking. Use it in your next meeting. And third — and this is the most important: if you could make one real change at your workplace, you now have the English to propose it.

That is not a small thing. That is why you are here.

§SP   Speaking Marathon  ·  15 min

One Question Per Topic — Full Circuit

One question from each module topic. Each learner speaks for 60 seconds per question. Aim to use vocabulary from that session + Second Conditional.

● SESSION 1 — MENTAL HEALTH
If your company implemented mandatory mental health days, would you use them? What would you do, and would you feel comfortable telling your team?
Use: burnout, stigma, boundary, wellbeing
● SESSION 2 — GENDER ROLES
If gender norms in your industry disappeared overnight, what would look different at your company in one year? Would it be better, worse, or complicated?
Use: stereotype, bias, equality, evolve
● SESSION 3 — QUIET QUITTING
If your company tripled salaries tomorrow but kept everything else the same, would employees become more engaged? Or is it about something deeper?
Use: engagement, recognition, loyalty, disillusionment
● SESSION 4 — IMMIGRATION
If your company hired 10 people from different countries this year, what would change in your team? Would you see it as an opportunity or a challenge?
Use: diversity, integration, contribution, belong
⭐ FINAL SYNTHESIS QUESTION
Of the four topics in this module — Mental Health, Gender Roles, Quiet Quitting, and Immigration — which one would you say affects your daily work life the most right now? And if you could make ONE change related to that topic at your company, what would it be?
Use vocabulary from ANY session. Aim for 90 seconds. This is your module closing argument.

The Last Word

WRITING + SPEAKING CHALLENGE

Carlos Mendez ends every module by asking his coaching clients to complete a “Leadership If” statement. Write yours below, then say it out loud to the group.

“If I could change one thing about the way my company treats its people, I would…”

After writing: say it out loud. Then listen to one other person’s. Note: did they use Second Conditional correctly? Did they use module vocabulary?

The “Leadership If” is a GT signature format: it forces personal, authentic production at module close. Do not correct grammar during the reading-aloud phase — let learners speak without interruption. Collect errors mentally and address 2-3 patterns after everyone has spoken. This is the moment of ownership: learners are no longer practising English, they are using it.
