T-S
Mónica is sick and can't attend the Monday meeting. She needs to notify her manager, Daniel Ochoa, and apologize. What information must the email include?

"The email needs: (1) the reason she can't attend, (2) the specific date/meeting, (3) a clear apology, (4) what she will do — send notes, reschedule, or ask a colleague to cover. Subject line: Absence Notification — Monday Meeting."

Role play: Partner A sent a report 3 days late. Write and read a 4-sentence apology email to Partner B (your manager). Include: subject, apology, reason, and what you will do next time.
Use: "I'm sorry for…" / "I apologize for…" / "This will not happen again."
"Subject: Apology for Late Report. Dear Daniel, I'm writing to apologize for the delay in sending the weekly report. I'm sorry — I had a technical problem with the system on Thursday. Please find it attached now. This will not happen again. Best regards, Mónica."
Class
In your company, how do people usually report absences or delays? By WhatsApp, email, or phone? Is there a formal protocol in English?
Think: HR policy / manager expectations / international clients
"In manufacturing companies in the Bajío, absences are usually reported by: (1) WhatsApp to the direct manager — informal but immediate. (2) A formal email to HR — required for medical leave or certificates. (3) For international clients: always a formal email in English with a professional apology and a solution."
TEACHER NOTE — Speaking Rounds

Round 2 is where apology culture becomes interesting. Mexican professionals often apologize excessively ("mil disculpas, de verdad lo siento mucho") — in English, professional apologies are shorter and immediately followed by a SOLUTION. Train this: Apology → Reason → Action. The email should never end on the apology alone. After Round 3: ask the class — does your company have a formal HR email policy in English? If yes, students can bring a real template next class as a reference.

Colloquial — Real Apology & Absence English
4 phrases that make professional apologies sound natural
5 min
"My apologies."
Short, professional alternative to "I'm sorry." Sounds more formal and controlled. Example: "My apologies for the late reply — I was traveling yesterday." Use in emails and meetings.
"I appreciate your patience."
Said when someone has waited or been flexible. Much warmer than "thank you for waiting." Example: "I appreciate your patience while we resolved the issue."
"This won't happen again."
The key commitment sentence in any apology email. Shows professionalism and accountability. Example: "I apologize for the error in the report. This won't happen again."
"Please let me know how I can help."
After an apology, this shows you want to fix the situation. More proactive than just apologizing. Example: "I'm sorry for the delay. Please let me know how I can help to make this right."
TEACHER NOTE — Colloquial Drill + Cultural Note

Cultural moment: In Mexico, apologies tend to be long and emotional. In international business English, the formula is short: Apology → Reason → Action/Solution → Appreciation. Drill this structure: "My apologies for X. The reason was Y. I will do Z. I appreciate your understanding." Students practice this with 3 different real situations from their job. The most powerful moment: when a student realizes their real email from last week would be twice as good with just this formula.

Grammar — Wh-Questions Full System + Error Correction
Problem → Tool · M5 grammar consolidation · Spin the Wheel
12–15 min
⚠️ REAL WORK SITUATION

A client emails you: "Please send details." You don't know what details they need. You want to ask: ¿De qué detalles hablan? ¿Para cuándo? ¿A quién se los mando? — but in English.

❌ Students often reply: "What details?" — grammatically ok but sounds abrupt. The tool: Wh-questions with full structure inside a polite email response, consolidated across all M5 contexts.

Wh-Questions — Full A1.2 System (Email + Phone + Meeting)

Wh-wordStructureEmail / phone example
WhatWhat + is/are + noun?What is the deadline for the report?
WhenWhen + is/will + noun?When is the delivery scheduled?
WhereWhere + is/are + noun?Where is the meeting — your office or online?
WhoWho + is/will + verb?Who is the contact person for this order?
How manyHow many + noun + do you need?How many copies of the document do you need?
How longHow long + does + noun + take?How long does the approval process take?
How muchHow much + does + noun + cost?How much does the express delivery cost?

TEACHER NOTE — Wh-Questions Consolidation

This is the final grammar exposure of M5 — all Wh-words in one table. Key error: word order. Students often write "What is the deadline?" (correct) but also "What the deadline is?" (wrong — this is an indirect question structure, not for A1.2). Drill: teacher asks a situation → student forms the Wh-question. Example: "You need to know the price" → "How much does it cost?" / "You need to know the contact person" → "Who is the contact person?" Run 6-8 rapid-fire examples before Activity A.

Activity A — Error Correction: Fix the Email

Each sentence has ONE error. Click the correct version.

1. "I am write to apologize for the delay in sending the report."
✅ Present continuous: I am writing. Always use -ing after "am/is/are" for the email opening.
2. "Sorry for any inconvenient. I appreciate your understanding."
✅ "Inconvenience" is the noun (uncountable). "Inconvenient" is the adjective — can't be used here.
3. "Could you send to me the updated schedule as soon as possible?"
✅ Object pronouns: "send me the schedule" (pronoun before noun) or "send it to me" (pronoun replaces noun).
4. "What the delivery date is? We need to confirm it."
✅ Wh-question word order: What + is + the + noun? Never "What the noun is?" in a direct question.
5. "First, please sign the document. After, send it to HR. Finally, confirm with your manager."
✅ Sequence connectors: First → Then → Finally. "After" alone is not a connector — use "Then" for step 2 in a sequence.

Listening — An Absence Notification + Apology Email
Mónica Herrera · ConnectPro International · Guadalajara
8–10 min
Absence Notification Email — ConnectPro International, Guadalajara
A1.2 · M5 · S4A · Voice: Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.80
ElevenLabs Script — ~155 words · ~58 seconds at 0.80

Hello. My name is Mónica Herrera. Today I want to share two emails — an absence notification and an apology. These are emails I write regularly in my job at ConnectPro International. Listen for the structure: apology, reason, and action.

Email 1 — Absence Notification:
Subject: Absence Notification — Monday, May 19th. Dear Daniel, I'm writing to notify you of my absence on Monday, May 19th. I have a medical appointment in the morning and I'm afraid I can't attend the 9 AM team meeting. My colleague Andrea will cover my responsibilities. Please find my medical certificate attached. I appreciate your understanding. Best regards, Mónica.

Email 2 — Apology for Late Report:
Subject: Apology — Q2 Sales Report. Dear Daniel, I'm writing to apologize for the delay in sending the Q2 sales report. The reason was a technical problem with our system on Thursday. Please find it attached now. This will not happen again. I appreciate your patience. Sincerely, Mónica Herrera, Sales Manager, ConnectPro International.

Comprehension — Multiple Choice

1. Why is Mónica absent on Monday?

✅ "I have a medical appointment in the morning."

2. Who will cover Mónica's responsibilities?

✅ "My colleague Andrea will cover my responsibilities."

3. What was the reason for the late Q2 report?

✅ "The reason was a technical problem with our system on Thursday."

4. What commitment does Mónica make in Email 2?

✅ "This will not happen again." — The key commitment sentence in any apology.

5. Which closing phrase does Mónica use in Email 2?

✅ "Sincerely, Mónica Herrera" — used for more formal apology emails.
🗣️ MICRO-SPEAKING
From memory: Give the 4-part apology formula. Apology → Reason → Action → Appreciation. Use Mónica's email as your example. 30 seconds.
TEACHER NOTE — Listening + Structure Analysis

After the listening, map both emails on the board using the 5-part email structure from S1A: subject / greeting / opening / body / sign-off. Ask: "Which parts are in both emails?" Answer: all 5. Then: "What makes Email 2 an apology — what specific phrases signal it?" Answer: "I'm writing to apologize" / "The reason was" / "This will not happen again" / "I appreciate your patience." These 4 phrases are the apology formula students take with them from today's class.
