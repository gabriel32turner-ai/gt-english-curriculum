Hot Take — Superlatives & Comparison
Remember from S4A · Final A2.2 integration under pressure · 8 min
⏱ 8 MIN
⚡ Remember from S4A
S4B produces the full S4A toolkit: superlatives (the best, the most efficient, the fastest) + all five A2.2 structures in one professional sentence. Key grammar: short adjectives = the [adj]-est; long adjectives = the most [adj]; irregular = the best / the worst. NEVER "the most best" or "the most fast." Four S4A conclusion phrases: "All things considered," "The bottom line is," "When you weigh it all up," and "It's a no-brainer." The Hot Take measures how automatically students combine superlatives with their existing A2.2 structures — the true measure of A2.2 integration fluency.
🔥 Hot Take Round 1 — 60 seconds
"The most valuable professional skill a person can develop in their 30s is NOT technical expertise — it is the ability to compare options clearly and communicate that comparison in English."
Agree or disagree using at least 2 superlatives + one A2.2 structure. Include one of the 4 S4A conclusion phrases. Best argument earns first leaderboard points.
🔥 Hot Take Round 2 — 60 seconds
"In the Bajío region, the companies with the most competitive advantage right now are the ones that used to operate locally but are now expanding internationally."
Agree or disagree using: superlative + used to (M1) + going to or might (M4). Bonus: close with ALL FOUR S4A conclusion phrases in natural sequence. That is the ceiling task.
⚡ Teacher Note — Hot Take
Round 1 is a direct test of S4A superlative production in an opinion context. Listen for: (1) do students use superlatives correctly (no "the most best"), (2) do they combine a superlative with another A2.2 structure spontaneously, (3) does a conclusion phrase appear naturally at the end? Round 2 has the highest ceiling in the whole M6 B series: superlative + used to + going to + all four conclusion phrases in sequence. Any student who achieves this in under 60 seconds is demonstrating B1-approaching integration fluency. Name them and add 10 bonus points to the leaderboard. This sets the energy tone for S4B — the final production session before the A2.2 exit lesson.

Pitch & Debate — The Comparison Pitch
3 rounds · T-S / S-S / Class · Model answers · 20 min
⏱ 20 MIN
T-SRound 1 — The 2-Minute Comparison Pitch
Choose a REAL comparison from your work: two suppliers, two candidates, two processes, two projects. Deliver a 2-minute structured pitch covering: (1) superlative to identify the best option, (2) comparative to explain why, (3) one other A2.2 structure (should/might/going to/used to), (4) one S4A conclusion phrase. Class scores with rubric. Timer below.
Model: comparing two logistics suppliers

"We evaluated five options. All things considered, Lumina Express is the most reliable supplier we have ever tested. Their delivery time is faster than the current provider, and their prices are more competitive than any of the other four.
We are going to switch to Lumina in Q2 — the board approved it. We used to have delays every month. The bottom line is: Lumina is the best decision we could make this year. It's a no-brainer."

Structures: superlative + comparative + going to + used to + all 4 S4A phrases = maximum integration.
S-SRound 2 — The Real Decision (S-S)
Student A: present a REAL work decision your team is currently facing — or a realistic comparison from your industry (two options, two candidates, two approaches). Student B: give a structured recommendation using: superlative + should + comparative + because + one conclusion phrase. Then switch. Partner A evaluates: "Your recommendation was [X] because you used [which structures]."
S4B Full Integration Formula:
1. Superlative: "Option B is the most viable / the best choice of the three"
2. Comparative justification: "...because it is more flexible than Option A"
3. A2.2 structure: "...and you should / we might / we are going to / we used to"
4. Conclusion phrase: "All things considered / The bottom line is / When you weigh it all up / It's a no-brainer."

Partner evaluation protocol: After the recommendation, Partner A counts: "You used [superlative + comparative + A2.2 structure + conclusion phrase] = [X] / 4 elements."
ClassRound 3 — The Great A2.2 Debate
Class debate: "Which is the most important A2.2 structure you learned in M6: used to (M1), should/could (M3), going to/might (M4), or comparatives/superlatives (M5)?" Each student defends their choice in 2 sentences using AT LEAST TWO of the four structures in their argument. Class votes: most integrated argument wins 5 bonus points.
Scoring for leaderboard:
1pt: one superlative used correctly
2pts: superlative + comparative
3pts: superlative + comparative + one other A2.2 structure
4pts: all above + one S4A conclusion phrase
5pts BONUS: class votes most integrated AND most convincing argument

Best 4-point argument (defending comparatives): "Comparatives and superlatives are the most universally useful structures because without them, all your other grammar sounds incomplete. You should always compare — it's a no-brainer." (superlative + comparative + should = 4pts)

Exit task preview: This debate reveals which structures students feel most confident with. Those are the structures they will lead with in the S5 Exit Task. Note the distribution.
2-MINUTE COMPARISON PITCH TIMER
⚡ Teacher Note — Pitch & Debate
Round 1 is the S4B summative production task: the 2-minute comparison pitch with 4 elements is essentially the S5 exit task in preview. Students who score 12+/15 on Round 1 are ready for S5. Round 2 generates the richest language: real work comparisons + real superlative + real A2.2 structures. The partner evaluation protocol (counting elements aloud) creates the highest quality peer assessment in the M6 B series. Round 3 (the Great A2.2 Debate) is S4B's most memorable activity: students defend a grammar structure they believe in. It generates genuine conviction-driven language, the most fluent kind. The debate also reveals confidence mapping: students who defend comparatives are usually the strongest; those who defend "used to" may still be consolidating M1. Use this as your final differentiation data before S5.

Speed Round — Superlative Integration Under Pressure
6 prompts · 15 seconds each · Full A2.2 integration · 10 min
⏱ 10 MIN

15 seconds per prompt. No notes. 1pt: superlative correct. 2pts: superlative + comparative. 3pts: superlative + any other A2.2 structure. 4pts: all three + one S4A conclusion phrase. Maximum 4pts per prompt.

Prompt 01
Identify the BEST and the WORST aspect of your current role using superlatives. One sentence each.
Target: "The best part of my role is... The worst challenge is..." Short adj = -est, long adj = the most.
Prompt 02
Your company used to have a problem. Now it is solved. Describe it using: used to + superlative + but now + superlative.
Target: "We used to have the slowest delivery time in the region, but now we have the most efficient system."
Prompt 03
You are going to make a major change at your company. Describe it using: going to + superlative outcome + because + comparative.
Target: "We are going to achieve our best results this year because our new system is more efficient than the old one."
Prompt 04
Give advice using the full S3A formula + a superlative to justify it. Should + comparative + because + superlative conclusion.
Target: "You should choose this supplier because it is more reliable, and all things considered, it is the most viable option."
Prompt 05
Use ALL FOUR S4A conclusion phrases in one connected professional statement about your company. 15 seconds.
Target: "All things considered, this is our best quarter. When you weigh it all up, the bottom line is we made the right decisions. It's a no-brainer."
Prompt 06
CEILING TASK: one sentence combining 5 A2.2 structures + a superlative + a conclusion phrase. Real content from your work.
Example: "We used to have the worst response time, but now we should focus on becoming the most efficient team, and going to achieve it might be our biggest win. When you weigh it all up, it's a no-brainer."
15-SECOND RESPONSE TIMER
⚡ Teacher Note — Speed Round
Prompt 05 (all four conclusion phrases in sequence) is the most revealing item: students who can chain "All things considered → When you weigh it all up → The bottom line is → It's a no-brainer" in one connected statement in under 10 seconds have fully automatized the S4A collocational set. Prompt 06 is the A2.2 ceiling in the entire program: 5 structures + superlative + conclusion phrase. Any student who produces this in 15 seconds is demonstrating that their English has a complexity ceiling ABOVE A2.2 — they are producing B1 patterns. Say: "What you just did in 12 seconds? That is the baseline for B1.1. You are not preparing for B1 — you are already producing it. One module formalizes what you are already doing."
