🗣️ Speaking Topic — Stress at Work

8 min

What are your main sources of stress? Does your company do enough about wellbeing?

Today's speaking topic. Work through all 3 rounds. Use the vocabulary and ideas that come up naturally — you will need them again in §5.

Stress at Work · 3 Discussion Rounds
T→S Round 1
Tell me: what is the biggest source of stress in your current role? Is it the workload, the deadlines, or the expectations?
S↔S Round 2
Ask your partner: "Has your stress level at work changed in the last year? What caused the change?" Share what your partner says with the class.
Class Round 3
Class debate: "Companies should be required to measure and limit employee stress levels." Agree or disagree? Give two reasons.

Stress at B1.1 level — what a natural answer sounds like:

"Honestly, my main source of stress is the workload. I often have too much on my plate at the same time — my manager told me we would get extra support, but he said it hadn't been approved yet. I set limits as much as I can, but it's hard to disconnect after work. I think companies should do more, but honestly, individuals need to communicate better too."

Note the highlighted patterns: vocab from today's session · Reported Speech · colloquial phrases

Teacher note — Speaking: Say: "Listen to your partner carefully. What exact words do they use? You will need to report them later. Example: 'My partner said her main stress was the deadlines.'" This primes Reported Speech naturally before §5 introduces it formally.

Error anticipation: Students will say "My partner said me..." → Correct immediately and gently: "She told me OR she said (that). Not 'said me.'"

💬 Colloquial English — Stress & Pressure

5 min
4 natural phrases your colleagues use — learn them, own them
"I have too much on my plate."
When to use
When you feel overwhelmed by the number of tasks. "I can't take on that project — I already have too much on my plate this week."
"I needed a break."
When to use
When explaining why you stopped working or stepped away. "After that 3-hour meeting, I really needed a break before I could focus again."
"I try to disconnect after work."
When to use
When talking about leaving work mentally at the end of the day. "I try to disconnect after work — no emails, no WhatsApp messages about projects."
"It's a lot of pressure."
When to use
When describing a high-stress situation simply. "Being responsible for the whole audit — it's a lot of pressure, but I can handle it."
Teacher note — Colloquial drill: Run a quick substitution drill for the first phrase. Say "I have too much on my plate. Change 'plate' to 'schedule' — does it work? No. Now say the phrase with your own workload in mind. Ready? Go." This forces active production rather than passive reading.

Cultural bridge: "Too much on my plate" is a food metaphor — the idea is your plate is so full, nothing else fits. Mexican students who know the phrase "tengo mucho trabajo" can translate the idea directly. Point this out explicitly.
Retention layer: Ask students: "Which phrase could you use in a meeting with your supervisor THIS WEEK?" Take 2 answers. This transforms the colloquial section from passive vocabulary into a personal commitment. Return to it in the §8 '3 Phrases' block.

⚡ Grammar — Reported Speech (Introduction)

12–15 min
Grammar — Reported Speech · The tool for reporting what people say at work
🎯 Real Work Situation

You are in a team meeting on Monday. Your manager told you on Friday: "The deadline has moved. The client needs it by Wednesday."

Now your colleague arrives late and asks: "What did the manager say?" You want to report his exact message — but in English, you cannot just repeat it word-for-word. You need to shift the tenses back.

🔧 The Tool — Reported Speech

Reported Speech lets you tell others what someone said — using said or told + person. The key rule: tenses move back one step in time.

“The deadline has moved.” → He said the deadline had moved.
“I need it by Wednesday.” → He said he needed it by Wednesday.
“We can finish this.” → She told me we could finish it.
Direct SpeechReported SpeechTense shift
"I am tired."She said she was tired.am/is → was
"We have finished."He said they had finished.have/has → had
"I will send the report."She said she would send it.will → would
"We can meet at 3."He said they could meet at 3.can → could
"The deadline moved."She said the deadline had moved.past simple → past perfect
Common errors to correct:
✗ "She said me she was tired." → She told me she was tired. / She said she was tired.
✗ "He said that he is tired." (if said = past) → He said that he was tired.
✗ "They said they will finish." (after said) → They said they would finish.
🗣️ Micro-Speaking: Think of something your manager or colleague told you recently. Tell your partner using Reported Speech: "She told me that..." or "He said that..." One sentence is enough — make it real. 45 seconds.
Teacher note — Grammar intro: At B1.1, Reported Speech is referential. Do NOT drill the tense-shift table. Use it as a reference card only. The two activities below are where the learning happens. Say: "You already know all these tenses separately. Reported Speech just connects them. It's like translation — from 'now' to 'then.'"

🎧 Listening — A Conversation About Burnout

10–12 min
Listening · Rodrigo Vega reports back to his team · Vertex Logistics, Celaya

Listen to Rodrigo Vega, plant manager at Vertex Logistics in Celaya, as he describes a conversation he had with HR about workplace stress and burnout. Answer the questions below.

Rodrigo Reports Back — Vertex Logistics, Celaya
B1.1 · M5 · S1A · Voice: Adam (American English) · Speed: 0.85 · ElevenLabs
Hi everyone. I had a meeting with HR this week and I want to share what they said, because it affects all of us. First, they told me that the results from last month's employee survey were not good. Specifically, they said that more than sixty percent of the team had reported feeling high levels of stress. HR said the main causes were the workload, the short deadlines, and the feeling that expectations were not clear. The HR manager told me she was concerned. She said that two people in the department had already spoken to her about burnout. She said they had been feeling exhausted for weeks and that they had not communicated the problem earlier because they were afraid it would affect their performance reviews. She told me the company was going to make some changes. She said they would introduce a monthly check-in between each team leader and their direct reports. She also said they could not reduce the overall workload immediately, but that they would try to prioritize better and be more realistic about deadlines. I want to be honest with you. I told her that our team also had too much on their plates right now. She said she understood and that she would talk to the operations director this week. I think the most important thing she said was this: if you feel you cannot handle the pressure, tell me. I am here. That is what the company wants. Open communication, no judgment.
Listening note: This is a monologue rich with Reported Speech examples. Before the second listen, ask students to count how many times they hear "said" or "told." After listening: "What tenses did you notice after 'said'? Were they present or past?" This connects the listening directly to §5.
📊 Comprehension — Multiple Choice
1. What did HR tell Rodrigo about the survey results?
2. Why hadn't the people with burnout communicated the problem earlier?
3. What change did HR say they would introduce?
✅ True / False
HR told Rodrigo that the workload and unclear expectations were main causes of stress.
Rodrigo told HR that his team had plenty of capacity right now.
HR said the company would immediately reduce the overall workload.

Class Question 5 — Challenge

⚡ Solo Challenge — 90 seconds

Report a real or invented conversation about stress at your workplace. Use at least 4 sentences with Reported Speech. Include: something your manager said, something you said back, and how it made you feel.

Teacher note — Production: Q2 is your richest activity. After students report their partner, write the best Reported Speech sentences on the board and leave them there. They become the class's own examples — far more meaningful than textbook sentences. Use any errors you hear as the board correction example (anonymously).
Speaking layers: Layer 1: Observe — who is using Reported Speech naturally vs. defaulting to direct speech. Layer 2: Correct — tense shifts only (don't correct pronunciation in this section, focus is grammar). Layer 3: Expand — ask the student who gives the best Reported Speech sentence: "Can you add 'and she also told me...'?" This models extension fluency.
🎯 This week, you can use these 3 phrases at work
3 Phrases for This Week
PHRASE 1
"My manager said the deadline had moved to Friday."
Use this any time you need to report what your boss said to a colleague. It is natural Reported Speech at B1.1 level — professional, clear, and shows you can handle complex information in English. Replace "deadline" with any topic: budget, meeting, policy.
PHRASE 2
"I have too much on my plate right now."
Use this when a colleague asks you to take on extra work and you genuinely cannot. It is polite, professional, and instantly understood by any native speaker. You can follow with: "Can we revisit this next week?"
PHRASE 3
"I try to disconnect after work."
Use this in any casual conversation about work-life balance — with a new colleague, in a team lunch, or in an English interview. It shows self-awareness and emotional intelligence. Natural, honest, and instantly relatable.
