§1
Hot Take — Defend the Bus
Activate S3A language under time pressure · intensifiers + adverbs · 5–8 min
⏱ 5–8 MIN

Remember from S3A: much / far / slightly / significantly · arrives more punctually / works more comfortably · not worth the hassle · most cost-effective · depends on your priorities. Now use it all — fast.

🔥 Hot Take — 60 Seconds — Defend the Bus
"The bus is always the most cost-effective option for short regional business travel in Mexico. Flying is not worth the hassle for routes under 400 km."
Use at least 2 intensifiers (much, far, significantly) + 1 comparative adverb (more punctually, more comfortably) + 1 colloquial phrase from S3A. Make the class agree with you in 60 seconds.
HOT TAKE
🔥 Hot Take — Counter-Argument — 45 Seconds — Defend Flying
"Time is money. For any professional meeting, the plane is the only logical choice — it is significantly faster and saves your most valuable resource."
Challenge: use "although" or "it saves a lot of time" in your counter-argument. Concede the cost point, but push back on the time argument. 45 seconds.
COUNTER
⚡ Teacher Note — Hot Take
Model first: pick the bus side and use the language explicitly — "The bus is FAR more affordable. It arrives MORE PUNCTUALLY. It is SIGNIFICANTLY cheaper." Then assign the flight side to the class. After both rounds: "Did anyone use an intensifier naturally without thinking about it?" Celebrate those students by name. This is fluency — language that runs on autopilot. Seed for S4: "Next session we compare two entire cities for a business decision. Same language, bigger picture."
§2
Pitch / Debate — The Travel Policy Showdown
3 rounds · Andrea presents to the board · model answers · 10–12 min
⏱ 10–12 MIN

S3B expands from the email (S3A reading) to a live presentation. Andrea now defends her bus proposal in front of the executive board. Same language — higher stakes — spoken not written.

T-SRound 1 — Teacher Models Andrea's Pitch
Andrea presents her 3 main arguments to the board in 90 seconds. Teacher models: use every intensifier and comparative adverb from S3A. Show the language working in a real presentation context.
"Good morning. I want to propose an update to our travel policy. After comparing both options, I believe the bus is the most cost-effective solution for short regional routes.

First: the fare. The bus is far more affordable than the flight — $420 vs $2,800. That is significantly lower and saves the company money on every trip.

Second: reliability. The bus arrives more punctually than regional flights on this route. Our data shows an average flight delay of 45 minutes — the bus is almost always on time.

Third: productivity. I can work more comfortably on a first-class bus than on a small aircraft. The Wi-Fi is more reliable than most regional airports.

The time difference is only slightly faster by plane — about 30 minutes. It is not worth the hassle. My recommendation: bus for all routes under 400 km."
S-SRound 2 — Pair Role-Play: Andrea vs Board Member
Student A = Andrea (defends the bus). Student B = skeptical board member who always flies (defends the plane). 90 seconds each, then switch. Board member must raise at least 2 objections using comparatives. Andrea must counter each one with an intensifier.
Board member objections:
"The flight is much faster than the bus — time is more valuable than money for executives."
"The bus is less professional than flying for client-facing roles."
"The image of our company is more important than the cost savings."

Andrea counters:
"The time difference is only slightly faster — 30 minutes. It is not worth the hassle."
"The first-class bus is more comfortable than a regional aircraft and arrives more punctually than most flights."
"The savings are significantly higher than any image benefit. Our clients care about results, not transport."
ClassRound 3 — Class Vote + Policy Design
Class votes: approve Andrea's bus policy or reject it? Each student must justify their vote in one sentence using a superlative. Then: groups of 3 design their own company travel policy with 3 rules, each using a comparative or superlative. 3 minutes, then present.
How to run Round 3: After voting, group students by their vote (approve / reject). Each group has 3 minutes to design a 3-rule travel policy using all of today's language. Rules must include: 1 superlative, 2 comparative adverbs, 1 intensifier, 1 colloquial phrase. Then each group presents for 60 seconds. Scoring: teacher awards 1–3 stars for language use. Feed scores into leaderboard. This is the most complete production task of the session — every structure from S3A appears simultaneously under natural communicative pressure.
⚡ Teacher Note — Pitch/Debate
Round 2 is the core of S3B: the adversarial role-play forces students to produce language reactively, not just in rehearsed turns. This is real professional English — defending a position under pushback. Say before Round 2: "In your next job interview or client call, someone will push back on your idea. Today you practice saying: I hear you, but the data shows it is significantly better." Round 3 policy design should feel like a mini-board meeting — give groups 3 minutes max and keep energy high.

§4
Speed Round — Intensifier Drills
8 quick-fire prompts · teacher says situation · student produces sentence · 10 sec each · 8 min
⏱ 8 MIN

Teacher reads the situation. Student has 10 seconds to produce one correct sentence using an intensifier or comparative adverb. No notes. Click each card after the student responds to reveal the model answer.

⚡ Prompt 1
The bus costs $420. The flight costs $2,800. Compare using FAR.
Target: "The bus is far more affordable than the flight."
⚡ Prompt 2
The bus takes 3.5 hrs. The flight takes 3 hrs. Compare using SLIGHTLY.
Target: "The flight is slightly faster than the bus."
⚡ Prompt 3
The bus rarely has delays. The flight is often late. Use: MORE PUNCTUALLY.
Target: "The bus arrives more punctually than the flight."
⚡ Prompt 4
The first-class bus has more space. Use: MORE COMFORTABLY.
Target: "I can work more comfortably on the bus than on the plane."
⚡ Prompt 5
The reimbursement for the bus takes 2 days. The flight takes 7. Use: SIGNIFICANTLY.
Target: "The bus reimbursement is significantly faster than the flight."
⚡ Prompt 6
The flight is 30 minutes faster. Is that a big or small difference? Use: ONLY SLIGHTLY.
Target: "The flight is only slightly faster than the bus for this route."
⚡ Prompt 7
Your colleague asks: "Is flying worth it for Celaya-GDL?" Use a colloquial phrase.
Target: "It is not worth the hassle — the bus is far more cost-effective."
⚡ Prompt 8
Your manager asks which option is best overall. Use a superlative + reason.
Target: "The bus is the most cost-effective option because it is significantly cheaper and arrives more punctually."
⚡ Teacher Note — Speed Round
Run as a team relay: alternate between teams, one prompt per turn. 10 seconds only — keep it fast. If the student hesitates past 10 seconds, say "pass" and move on. No penalties for wrong answers, but only correct grammar earns a point. After all 8 prompts: "Which prompt was hardest? Why?" Usually Prompt 4 (more comfortably) because students revert to "more comfortable" after an action verb. Review the IS/VERB distinction one more time if needed.

GT Originals — S3 Session Notes
  • 📌 S3A→S3B arc: written (email) → spoken (pitch). This is authentic professional English progression — the email is the draft, the pitch is the deliverable. Tell students: "What you wrote in S3A, you now say in S3B. That is the skill."
  • ⚠️ Most common error in Solo Challenge: students use "more comfortable" after action verbs (I work more comfortable on the bus). Use the final 2 minutes to drill: "I WORK more comfortABLY. It IS more comfortABLE." Write both on the board during the challenge.
  • 🔹 Upsell moment: "At B1.1 you write formal recommendation reports using exactly these structures. You already have the grammar. The B1.1 program adds writing and formal register to what you already do in speech."
  • 🌟 Extension for strong students: add a third option to the pitch (e.g., driving). Requires 3-way superlative: "of all three options, flying is the fastest but the least cost-effective; driving is the most flexible but the most stressful; the bus is the most cost-effective overall."
🎯 This week, use these 3 phrases at work:
From S3 — travel decision language ready for real professional conversations.
"The [option] is far more [adj] than the [option]."
Use "far" to signal a large, clear difference in any comparison — cost, speed, quality, comfort. More impactful than "much more" in written or formal spoken contexts.
"It arrives more punctually than [option]."
Replace "punctually" with any adverb: more efficiently, more comfortably, more reliably. Use after action verbs to compare HOW something happens — marks you as a precise communicator.
"The savings are significantly higher than the time difference."
This specific construction is Andrea's strongest argument. Use it whenever you need to flip a trade-off: "the [benefit A] is significantly [adj] than the [cost B]." Works in any decision context.

⏱ 5–8 MIN
☐ Intensifiers in practice
much / far (big difference) · slightly / a little (small) · significantly (clear, measurable). Use these to make your comparisons precise — not just "more" but HOW MUCH more.
☐ Adverb after action verb
IS more comfortable. BUT: arrives more comfortably, works more comfortably, runs more punctually. The verb type determines the form. Adjective after IS, adverb after action.
☐ Travel language at work
fare · layover · itinerary · accommodation · reimbursement · punctual · route · budget · "not worth the hassle" · "most cost-effective." All ready to use in real work emails and presentations.
