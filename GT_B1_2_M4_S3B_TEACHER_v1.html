⚡ Hot Take  ·  5 min

Quiet Quitting — Where Do You Stand?

“If quiet quitting is a problem, the company created it. Employees are just responding rationally.”
Voted. Justify in 30 seconds using Second Conditional + S3A vocabulary.
This Hot Take is intentionally provocative — it assigns full blame to companies. Even learners who initially agreed in S3A may feel differently here because the phrasing is more extreme. Expect more “complex” votes than in previous sessions. This is progress: nuanced thinking = more language. Force Second Conditional: “If companies had…, employees would not have…”
🗣 Pitch Round  ·  10 min

The Engagement Diagnosis

Your plant in Celaya has a quiet quitting problem. Each pair diagnoses the root cause and proposes a solution. 2 min prep — 2 min pitch — group votes on best solution.

DIAGNOSIS A — RECOGNITION FAILURE
You believe the root cause is simple: employees work hard but are never acknowledged. If managers recognised effort publicly and consistently, disengagement would drop significantly. Propose a recognition system that could work at your company.
Must use: recognition, engagement, loyalty, Second Conditional
DIAGNOSIS B — EXPECTATION MISMATCH
You believe the problem is unspoken expectations. The company expects extra effort that is never written anywhere. If those expectations were made explicit and compensated, quiet quitting would stop. Propose a solution around transparency.
Must use: expectation, performance, overwork, Second Conditional
DIAGNOSIS C — CULTURE OF FEAR
You believe employees disengage because they fear speaking up. If the company built psychological safety — where people could raise concerns without risk — engagement would return. Propose a culture change program.
Must use: disengage, incentive, disillusionment, Second Conditional
DIAGNOSIS D — WRONG HIRES
You believe the hiring process selects for compliance, not motivation. If companies hired people who genuinely fit the mission, not just the job description, quiet quitting would be less common. Propose a better hiring framework.
Must use: performance, loyalty, engagement, Second Conditional
PREP TIME
PITCH TIME
Ground the pitches locally: “Think about your actual company. Which diagnosis fits most?” This makes the language immediately transferable. After all pitches, vote on the most convincing AND the most realistic for the Bajío context. Two separate votes produce more discussion.
🏃 Relay  ·  8 min

Escalation Ladder Relay

Each step represents one stage in a quiet quitting journey. Each learner takes ONE step and speaks for 30–45 seconds: what is the employee thinking, feeling, and doing at this point? Use Second Conditional for alternatives: “If the company had… at this stage, the employee might have…”

1
😀 High Engagement — Month 1
Employee starts excited. Goes above and beyond. Stays late. Volunteers for projects.
2
😐 First Disappointment — Month 4
Extra effort not acknowledged. Promotion goes to someone else. First doubts appear.
3
😕 Gradual Withdrawal — Month 8
Stops volunteering. Does exactly what the contract says. Updates LinkedIn profile.
4
😠 Full Disengagement — Month 12
Mentally checked out. Meets deadlines but adds no value. Team notices.
5
💬 The Conversation — Month 14
Manager finally asks what happened. What does the employee say? What should the manager have said months earlier?
Group challenge: After all 5 steps, discuss: “At which step could the company have intervened? If they had acted at Step 2, what would the outcome have been?” Use Second Conditional for the alternative timeline.
The Escalation Ladder is S3B’s unique relay format — unlike S1B’s narrative or S2B’s spectrum. It maps a real organisational journey, making the language directly relevant to learners’ professional context. Step 5 is the richest — assign it to the strongest speaker. The group challenge at the end forces Second Conditional in a hypothetical timeline discussion. This is management-level English.
⚡ Speed Round  ·  6 min

30 Seconds — Quiet Quitting Edition

Pick a card. Timer. Click when done.

Explain quiet quitting to someone who has never heard of it — in exactly 3 sentences.
No jargon. Clear, accurate, brief.
If you quietly quit tomorrow, what would be the first thing you would stop doing? Be honest.
Personal + Second Conditional
Name 3 things a manager could do TODAY to re-engage a disengaged employee.
Practical, specific, realistic for your industry
“At the end of the day, quiet quitting is just professionalism.” Agree or disagree in 30 seconds.
Use the M4 colloquial phrase + vocabulary
What is the difference between quiet quitting and quiet firing? Define both.
“Quiet firing” = when a company pushes someone out without formally firing them
If you were a manager and discovered a team member was quietly quitting, what would you say in your first 1-on-1?
Professional language. No accusations. Second Conditional.
30s SPEED TIMER
Card 5 (quiet firing vs quiet quitting) introduces a new concept at B1.2 level — extend it if the group finds it interesting. Card 6 (manager 1-on-1) is the highest-value card for professional English: it forces learners to use management vocabulary, empathetic language, and Second Conditional simultaneously. Real B1.2 production benchmark.
🌟 Solo Challenge  ·  8 min

The Employee Survey Response

Your Scenario
Your company has sent an anonymous engagement survey. One open question asks: “If you could change one thing about how this company manages its people, what would it be and why?”

You have 90 seconds to answer out loud — as if speaking directly to the leadership team. Be honest, constructive, and professional. Use what you know from this module.
✅ Use Second Conditional as the core structure
✅ Include at least one concrete suggestion (not just criticism)
✅ Use at least 3 words from S3A vocabulary
✅ Keep a professional tone — honest but constructive
❌ No clichés. No generic answers. Make it specific to your real context.
90s SOLO TIMER
This is the kind of English you could use in a real skip-level meeting or leadership conversation. The language produced here has direct professional value.
💰 After the Solo Challenge: “What you just said in English — could you have said that a year ago?” Let them reflect. Then: “By B2.1, you’ll be writing this kind of feedback in formal English emails and presenting it to international leadership.” The gap between now and next level is visible and motivating.
