§2 Speaking — How AI Is Changing Your World 3 rounds · Passive voice emerging naturally · Model B1.1 answer ⏱ 8 MIN
Session Topic
AI in Your Daily Life — Exciting or Terrifying?
Round 1 — 60s · T→S
Describe one way AI is used in your work or life — even without you realising it.
"AI is used in almost everything I do — even if I don't notice it. My emails are filtered by an algorithm. My Netflix recommendations are generated by AI. At work, reports are sometimes summarized by AI tools now."

Ask your partner: Would you trust an AI to make an important decision for you?
"I think it's exciting and terrifying at the same time. Jobs are being changed — not necessarily eliminated, but transformed. The part that worries me most is bias: decisions about loans, jobs, and medical care are sometimes made by systems that weren't designed to be fair."

Should AI be regulated by governments? Who should make the rules?
"I believe AI should be regulated — but I'm not sure governments understand it well enough yet. The question is: who decides what's fair? Right now, the rules are being written by the companies building the tools."

§4 Colloquial English — Talking About AI 4 natural phrases · AI & opinion context · Used daily by native speakers ⏱ 5 MIN
"I think both sides have a point."
Balanced opinion in debate. Acknowledges merit on both sides without committing too strongly. Very natural in professional discussions about technology, politics, and ethics.
"Should AI be regulated? I think both sides have a point — freedom and safety matter."
"It's complicated."
Acknowledges complexity without oversimplifying. A powerful hedge in English — it signals you understand the nuance of an issue. Confident speakers use this more than simple yes/no answers.
"Is AI good or bad? It's complicated — the same tool that saves lives can also destroy jobs."
"That depends on how it's used."
Nuanced response to technology questions. Avoids black-and-white thinking. Very common in B2+ English — it shifts focus from what something is to how it functions in context.
"Is AI dangerous? That depends on how it's used — a hammer can build or break."
"I'm not sure governments understand it well enough."
Expresses concern about regulation without being rude. "I'm not sure" softens the criticism. "Well enough" implies a higher standard is needed. Very natural in debates about tech policy.
"People say AI should be regulated, but I'm not sure governments understand it well enough to write good laws."
💡 Quick Drill A student says "AI will replace all teachers." What do you respond? Push for passive: "Teachers might be replaced by AI in some areas, but…" + opinion language: "That depends on how it's used — AI can't replace the relationship between a teacher and a student." Drill: student makes a strong claim → partner responds with one of today's four phrases.
§5 Grammar — Passive Voice: AI & Technology Context AI news · Referential approach · Modal passives · 2 activities + Poll ⏱ 10 MIN
📘 Referential Teaching Note Today's key insight: AI news is FULL of passive voice because companies don't want to claim responsibility. "Mistakes were made." "Data was accessed." "Users were affected." Help students recognise this as a rhetorical choice — passive hides the agent. In business English, this is common and intentional. When students read tech news, they should ask: who is the hidden agent?
StructureFormAI Example
Present Passiveis/are + past participle"Your data is processed by AI every second."
Past Passivewas/were + past participle"This algorithm was trained on millions of photos."
Passive + byis/are + past part + by"Decisions are made by AI systems, not humans."
Passive + modalcan/must/should + be + past part"AI should be regulated by international bodies."
Error — missing beverb alone✗ "This data processed automatically."
✅ "This data is processed automatically."
💡 The rule that matters most: Passive always needs "be." "Data is processed." / "This algorithm was trained." Without "be," it's an error — the most common mistake at B1.1. For modal passives: should/can/must + be + past participle.
⚠️ Correct immediately:
✗ "This data processed automatically." → ✅ "This data is processed automatically."
✗ "The algorithm trained on millions of images." → ✅ "The algorithm was trained on millions of images."
✗ "AI should regulated by governments." → ✅ "AI should be regulated by governments."
⚡ Quick Poll — 30 pts · Earn 🎯 badge at 30
1. "This AI tool ______ by a team of 500 engineers over 3 years."
✅ Correct! "was built" — Past Passive. "Was" = past of "be" + "built" = irregular past participle of "build". Note: "builded" is not an English word.
❌ "Builded" is not a word in English. The past participle of "build" is "built". Passive: was + built.
❌ "Is building" is Present Continuous active. Passive needs: was + past participle → "was built".
2. "Millions of images ______ to train the facial recognition system."
✅ Correct! "were used" — Past Passive. "Millions of images" is plural → "were". "Used" = regular past participle.
❌ "Was used" uses singular "was" — "millions of images" is plural: use "were used".
❌ "Used" alone is missing the "be" verb. Passive = were + past participle → "were used".
3. "Personal data ______ to third parties without users knowing."
✅ Correct! "is sold" — Present Passive. "Data" is singular → "is". This is the kind of passive that hides who is doing the selling — deliberately.
❌ "Sells" is Present Simple active — who sells? The passive "is sold" deliberately hides the agent (the company).
❌ "Was selling" is Past Continuous active. Passive Present = is + past participle → "is sold".

§6 Listening — Rodrigo and AI at Work Sarah (AmE) · Speed 0.82 · Passive voice throughout · MC + T/F ⏱ 8 MIN
Rodrigo and AI at Work
B1.1 · M4 · S3A · Voice: Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.82
Six months ago, AI tools were introduced at my company. At first, I was worried. My job involves writing reports, analyzing data, and summarizing long meetings — exactly the kind of tasks that are supposed to be replaced by AI. So I thought: am I being made redundant?

But here's what actually happened. The AI tools were used to handle the repetitive parts — formatting, basic summaries, initial drafts. And what was left for me was the interesting part: judgment, context, relationships. The work that can't be automated easily.

What surprised me most was this: my output was doubled in the same time. Reports that used to take two hours were finished in forty-five minutes. That time was then used for strategy and client communication. My value didn't decrease — it shifted.

That said, I'm aware that not everyone's experience is like mine. In manufacturing and logistics, thousands of jobs have been eliminated. The impact is real and unequal. I think AI should be regulated — not to stop it, but to make sure the benefits are distributed fairly. Right now, the productivity gains are captured by companies, not workers. That needs to change.

I don't think AI is good or bad. I think it depends on who controls it — and that's a political question, not a technical one.

§7 Production — Your AI Opinion Opinion meter · 4 speaking prompts · 90-sec challenge · Hot Seat Dice ⏱ 12 MIN
📊 Opinion Meter — Where do you stand?
"AI is more dangerous than beneficial."
💬 How to run this: Students answer using passive voice. Partner marks: (1) any missing "be" verb in passive, (2) any modal passive error ("should be regulated" not "should regulated"). Correct after each response. Q1 = structured, Q2 = peer, Q3 = hypothetical, Q4 = critical thinking.
Question 1 — T→S · Passive Warm-Up
Describe how AI is used in your industry. Use passive: "AI is used to…", "Decisions are made by…", "Data is processed to…"
💡 Push for specific industry examples — finance, healthcare, retail, education. The passive sounds analytical and shows B1+ grammar naturally.
Question 2 — S→S · Partner Question
Ask your partner: What job do you think will be replaced by AI first? And last?
💡 Listen for passive: "Jobs will be replaced by…" / "The last job to be replaced will be…" Note any missing "be" verbs and correct after.
Question 3 — T→S · Hypothetical
If AI replaced half of all white-collar jobs in Mexico in 5 years, what should be done by governments? Companies? Workers?
💡 Note how the question uses passive: "was replaced." Push for modal passives: "People should be retrained by…" / "Benefits should be distributed…" / "Taxes must be increased to…"
Question 4 — Class · Critical Thinking
Should AI be regulated internationally — or should each country decide its own rules?
💡 Push for: "AI should be regulated by…" / "Standards must be agreed on…" — modal passive as analytical tool. Who is the hidden agent if AI regulates itself?

3 Phrases Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"This was designed to maximize profit, not protect users."
Passive voice used critically — about AI tools, apps, and platforms. Use it next time someone defends a tech company's data practices. Shows B1+ grammar and analytical thinking in one sentence.
PHRASE 2
"That depends on how it's used."
Nuanced tech opinion. Use it whenever anyone asks a black-and-white question about AI — "Is it good?" / "Is it safe?" It signals you understand complexity, which is exactly what B1+ sounds like.
PHRASE 3
"I believe AI should be regulated — but carefully."
Opinion + passive modal in one sentence. Use it in any conversation about technology, government, or the future of work. It's balanced, sophisticated, and shows you can use modal passives naturally.
