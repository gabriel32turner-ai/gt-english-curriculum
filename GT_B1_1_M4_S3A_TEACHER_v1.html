§1

🔥 Warm-Up — AI in Daily Life

5–8 min
Module 4 · S3A — How much of your daily life is already shaped by AI?
Q1 — Daily Use
Do you use AI tools in your daily work or life? Which ones? ChatGPT, Copilot, image generators, voice assistants? How often are they used?
Q2 — Excited or Worried?
When you think about AI, do you feel more excited or more worried? What's the biggest opportunity you see — and the biggest risk?
Q3 — Your Job
Could AI replace part of your job? Has automation already changed how your team works? What tasks are now done by machines that were done by people five years ago?
Q4 — Trust
Would you trust an AI to make a medical diagnosis? A legal decision? A hiring decision about you? Where do you draw the line on what AI should be allowed to decide?
⚡ Quick Win — 20 seconds
Say this out loud: "I use AI every day, but I'm not sure I trust it completely — especially when decisions are made by it without human review."
Two passives hidden in that sentence. Can you spot them?
Quick Win debrief: The two passives are "decisions are made by it" (present passive — "by it" is kept because it identifies the agent causing concern) and "made… without human review" (the action focus). After the timer, ask: "¿Por qué 'by it' se queda aquí?" The agent (AI) is the whole point of the sentence — it adds essential meaning.

🗣️ Speaking Topic — AI in Daily Life

8 min
Module 4 · S3 — AI in Daily Life · The question every professional is asking

Rodrigo Vega — The AI Question at the Plant

Rodrigo is an engineer at a manufacturing plant in Celaya. His company is introducing an AI system to monitor production quality. Defects are now detected automatically by the system — no human inspector needed. His team is split: some are excited, others worried about their jobs. Rodrigo has to present his opinion to the plant manager next week.

Round 1 · 60 seg T→S
"Defects are detected by the AI faster than any human. Is Rodrigo's team right to be worried — or should they welcome the change?"
Round 2 · 90 seg S→S
"Ask your partner: Has AI been used in your company? What tasks have been automated? What do you think will be replaced next?"
Round 3 · 90 seg Class
"How do you use AI? Are you excited or worried about it? Should AI decisions be regulated — or should companies be free to use it as they want?"
Model Answer

"I use AI tools every day — mainly to write emails faster and generate reports. I think it's genuinely useful. But I'm also worried. In my factory, some inspections are now performed by machines that used to be done by people. Those people were reassigned — but what happens in five years? I think AI should be regulated. Not stopped — but controlled. I'm not sure I trust it completely when it comes to decisions that affect people's jobs. It's getting more powerful — and that's exactly why rules are needed."

💬 Colloquial — Talking About AI in English

5 min
Everyday English — how real people talk about AI at work and at home
"I use it every day."
When: describing your AI usage habits naturally
"ChatGPT? I use it every day. It's been integrated into my workflow. Some tasks are now done in minutes that used to take hours."
"I'm not sure I trust it completely."
When: expressing healthy skepticism about AI
"The AI-generated report looks good — but I'm not sure I trust it completely. I always verify before I send it to the client."
"It's getting more powerful."
When: talking about how fast AI is developing
"Every month there's a new tool. It's getting more powerful faster than anyone expected — and I think it should be regulated before it causes problems."
"I've started using AI to help me write faster."
When: sharing a practical AI habit at work
"I've started using AI to help me write faster. My English emails are checked by the tool before I send them. It saves me 20 minutes a day."
Discussion drill: "¿Cuál de estas frases describes mejor tu relación con la IA?" Ask 3–4 students. After each answer, ask the class: "¿Escucharon alguna estructura pasiva?" The colloquial phrases already embed passive naturally — "are now done", "are checked", "should be regulated."

🎯 Grammar — Passive Voice: Future & Opinion Language

12–15 min
Passive in the future — and combining it with I think / I believe / In my opinion
🚨 Real Situation
Rodrigo needs to present his opinion about the AI system to his plant manager. He wants to say:
"Creo que la IA debería ser regulada. Creo que más empleos serán reemplazados. Pienso que las decisiones importantes no deberían ser tomadas por una máquina."
❓ Three opinion statements — all in passive voice. How do you combine "I think / I believe / In my opinion" with passive structures in English?
✅ The Tool: Opinion + Passive
"I think AI should be regulated before it causes harm."
"I believe more jobs will be replaced in the next five years."
"In my opinion, important decisions should not be made by machines alone."

Pattern: Opinion phrase + subject + passive verb (modal + be + past participle)
Key modals: should be / will be / must be / can be / might be + past participle
REFERENCIAL. S3 grammar builds on S1 (passive basics) and S2 (by-phrase decisions). Now the focus is: using passive with opinion language — which is exactly what the exit task requires. Students need "I think AI should be regulated" — not just "AI should be regulated" in isolation. The opinion frame makes it personal AND professional.
Opinion Phrase+ Subject+ Passive Structure
I think / I believeAIshould be regulated by governments.
In my opinionmore jobswill be replaced by automation in 10 years.
I thinkbiasmust be detected and corrected before AI is deployed.
I don't thinkimportant decisionsshould be made by AI without human review.
I'm not sureall datacan be trusted when it's generated by AI.
⚠️ Common Errors
✗ "I think AI should regulated" → ✅ "should be regulated" (need "be" before past participle)
✗ "I believe it will replaced" → ✅ "it will be replaced" (will + be + p.p.)
⚠️ The #1 error at B1 with modal passives — correct every time:
✗ "AI should regulated" → ✅ AI should be regulated — never skip "be"
✗ "Jobs will replaced" → ✅ Jobs will be replaced — will + be + past participle
✗ "It must detected" → ✅ It must be detected — modal + be + p.p. is the fixed pattern

✏️ Activity B — True or False?
"AI should regulated" is a complete and correct passive sentence in English.
"I believe jobs will be replaced by automation" uses opinion language + future passive correctly.
"In my opinion, bias must be detected" — the word "be" is optional before the past participle.
"I don't think important decisions should be made by AI alone" is grammatically correct and natural.

GT Original — AI & Passive in Mexican Corporate English
  • 🇲🇽 Real context Bajío: In Celaya, Silao and Querétaro manufacturing plants, AI quality systems are being introduced by German and Japanese companies. Engineers are asked to present their opinions in English to international managers. "I think this process should be automated" / "I believe bias must be detected" — these exact sentences happen in real meetings.
  • ⚠️ The "be" trap: Modal + passive always = modal + BE + past participle. Students delete "be" constantly. Write it on the board: SHOULD BE / WILL BE / MUST BE / CAN BE. Never: "should regulated" — always: "should BE regulated."
  • 🌍 S3 AI debate (from program): "AI should be regulated" is perfect passive AND opinion language combined — this is the exit task's core sentence type. Every student should leave able to say this.
  • 🔊 Pronunciation: "regulation" /ˌrɛɡjʊˈleɪʃən/ — 5 syllables. Stress on the 4th: reg-yu-LAY-shun. Students often say "regu-LA-ción" (Spanish influence) — the vowels are different.

🎧 Listening — AI at the Factory: A Real Conversation

8 min
An engineer and a plant manager discuss introducing AI at a Mexican manufacturing facility
AI at the Factory — A Real Conversation
B1.1 · M4 · S3A · Voice: Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.85 · Pitch: 1.0
"So Rodrigo, you wanted to share your thoughts before we implement the new system?"

"Yes. I think the AI quality system should be introduced carefully. I'm not against it — I believe defects will be detected faster and costs will be reduced. That's good for the plant. But I'm worried about my team."

"What specifically worries you?"

"Three of my inspectors will probably be reassigned. They've been doing this work for years. I think they should be retrained — not just moved. Their experience is valuable. It shouldn't be wasted."

"I understand. What else?"

"I believe the AI system must be tested properly before it's trusted with final quality decisions. I've read that bias can be introduced into these systems by the training data. If a defect isn't included in the original dataset, it won't be detected. I think human oversight should be kept — at least for the first year."

"That's a very reasonable position. What's your overall recommendation?"

"In my opinion, AI should be used as a tool — not a replacement. Jobs should be redesigned around it, not eliminated by it. If that's done well, I think both the team and the company will benefit."
📎 ElevenLabs: Voice — Sarah (American English) · Speed 0.85 · Pitch 1.0 · B1.1 M4 S3A
Grammar treasure hunt: Ask students to count passive structures WHILE listening (not after). There are at least 12. Then ask: which ones kept "by"? (introduced by the training data, detected by the system, eliminated by it) — and were those decisions correct? Great discussion for S2's grammar point revisited.

🎤 Production — Speaking Practice

12 min
Opinion + passive — the full B1.1 combination
💬 How to run: Pairs. Partner B listens for: (1) opinion phrase used, (2) correct modal + be + past participle structure, (3) at least one "by" decision. Partner A answers then checks partner B's count. Switch after Q3. Q6 is solo timed.
Question 1 — AI in Your Job
Describe 2 tasks in your job that could be automated by AI. Use passive voice. Then say: should they be automated — or should humans keep doing them? Why?
💡 Use: "I think [task] could be automated by… / I believe it should be kept by humans because… / In my opinion, [task] should not be replaced."
Question 2 — Regulation
Do you think AI should be regulated by governments? Should limits be set on what decisions can be made by AI? What should be prohibited — and what should be allowed?
💡 Use: "I think AI should be regulated because… / I believe decisions about [X] should not be made by… / In my opinion, [rule] must be introduced."
Question 3 — Trust & Bias
Can AI be trusted to make fair decisions? How can bias be detected and corrected in AI systems? Have you ever received an AI-generated output that you didn't trust — and why?
💡 Use: "I don't think AI can be fully trusted when… / Bias must be detected before… / I believe data should be checked by…"
Question 4 — Jobs & Automation
In your industry, what jobs will be replaced by automation in 10 years? What new roles will be created? Should workers be retrained by companies — or is that their own responsibility?
💡 Use: "I believe [jobs] will be replaced by… / Workers should be retrained by… / In my opinion, [skill] will be needed in the future."
Question 5 — Privacy & Data
When you use AI tools at work, is your data being collected and used to train the system? Should employees be informed when their data is used this way? Who should be held responsible?
💡 Use: "I think employees should be informed by… / Data must be protected… / I believe companies should be held responsible for…"
Question 6 — ⭐ 60-Second Challenge
You are Rodrigo. Present your complete AI opinion to the plant manager in 60 seconds. Include: what should be introduced, what must be protected, what will be changed, and what you believe is the right approach. Use passive voice at least 4 times.
💡 Model: "In my opinion, AI should be introduced carefully. Jobs will be changed but should not be eliminated. Bias must be detected early. I believe this technology can be used well — if it is managed properly."

🎯 This week at work
3 Phrases You Can Use Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"I've started using AI to help me write faster."
Use this in any team meeting, standup, or casual conversation when someone asks how you manage your workload. It's natural, practical, and immediately relatable. Shows you're adapting to technology — a professional signal in any company.
PHRASE 2
"I'm not sure I trust it completely — I always verify."
Use this when discussing AI tools with your team or manager. It shows critical thinking, not resistance. Perfect balance: you use AI AND you're responsible. Your manager will respect this more than blind acceptance or blind rejection.
PHRASE 3
"I think AI should be used as a tool, not a replacement."
This is the sentence that will make you sound like Rodrigo in that meeting. Opinion + modal passive in one natural sentence. Use it whenever AI comes up — in HR, operations, or any team discussion. It takes a clear position without being extreme.
