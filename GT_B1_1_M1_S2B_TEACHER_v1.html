§ 01
Hot Take — React, Don't Plan
5 min
⚡ Provocative Statement · React in 60 seconds
Companies say they have values, but their actions show their real priorities.
1:00
Agree or disagree — react now, in English, no notes.
Frames to deploy: "I've seen companies that have claimed… but they've never actually…" / "I've worked at places where leaders have said one thing and done another." / "I've always believed that real values show up in the hard moments."
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • This is a diagnostic. No correction during the 60 seconds. Listen for: (a) how many spontaneous Present Perfect uses, (b) does the student deploy "I've seen / I've worked / I've never"? (c) does the student fall into simple past mid-stream?
  • If the response is under 30 seconds: probe — "Can you give me one specific company or moment? Use 'I've seen / I've experienced.'"
  • B1.1 mastery signal here: 3+ PP uses in 60 seconds without any prompting. Note as baseline for the rest of the session.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "This statement is uncomfortable on purpose. Hot takes wake the brain up. There is no wrong answer — there is only a real opinion in real English."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "Every leadership conversation in English starts with a position. Today you take one — and you defend it in Present Perfect. That is executive-level discourse."
§ 02
Values Debate — Two Positions, One Grammar
10 min

Two positions on personal values. The student picks Side A or Side B, defends it for 90 seconds, then defends the OPPOSITE side for 60 seconds. Every argument must use Present Perfect.

Side A
Personal values are fixed — they've been with me since childhood.
Suggested arguments (use Present Perfect)
"I've valued honesty since I was a kid — that has never changed."
"My parents have shaped my values, and I've kept them my whole life."
"I've worked in three industries — and my core values have stayed the same in every one."
"I've never compromised on fairness, not once, in fifteen years."
Side B
Values evolve — every experience has reshaped what matters to me.
Suggested arguments (use Present Perfect)
"I've changed roles three times, and each role has taught me a new value."
"What I've valued at 25 is not what I've valued at 40 — and that's healthy."
"I've lived through difficult years, and those years have rewritten my priorities."
"I've always believed something — but what that something IS has shifted."
Flow: 90 sec defending chosen side · 60 sec defending opposite · 60 sec teacher rebuttal in PP · 30 sec student final word. Total: 4 minutes of pure Present Perfect production.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • Forcing the student to defend BOTH sides is the magic move. Side they didn't pick = more cognitive load = more grammar focus. The second round is where the real production happens.
  • Teacher rebuttal must be in Present Perfect at B1.1-appropriate density (6-8 uses in 60 sec). Model what fluent disagreement sounds like: "I hear you, but I've seen the opposite — I've watched leaders abandon values the moment pressure hits."
  • Count PP uses per minute. Target at this stage: 5+ per minute of student talk. If under, slow them down and ask them to reformulate one argument.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Pick the side you DISAGREE with first if you want a real challenge. The grammar is the same — the thinking is harder. That's the B1.1 push."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "Boardroom English is not agreeing politely. It is defending a position with evidence and tense control. You just did both."
§ 03
Relay Values — Build the Chain
8 min

How it works: Teacher starts the chain with a Present Perfect values sentence. Student adds the next link — same value or a connected one. Then teacher again. Five links minimum, no repetition of structure. Every link includes a duration marker (for / since) OR a frequency marker (ever / never / always).

Example Chain — Use as Model
01
Teacher: "I've valued honesty for over twenty years — it's the only currency I trust at work."
02
Student: "I've valued honesty too, since I lost a client over a small lie I told to protect a colleague."
03
Teacher: "I've never been comfortable working in environments where loyalty is demanded but not returned."
04
Student: "I've experienced that — for three years I stayed too long out of loyalty, and it cost me growth."
05
Teacher: "Since that role, have you redefined what loyalty means to you?"
06
Student: "Yes — I've learned loyalty has to flow in both directions, and I haven't accepted one-way loyalty since."
Relay Rules: (1) Every link in Present Perfect. (2) Every link includes for / since / ever / never / always. (3) No two links can use the same opener. (4) Connect to the previous link — don't pivot to a new topic. (5) If the student stalls 4+ seconds, teacher feeds the next opener.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • The relay's purpose is sustained PP production with mandatory duration markers. Each student link MUST include for / since / ever / never / always. If missing, ask: "Try that again with 'since' or 'for.'"
  • Connection is critical — the student is NOT producing isolated sentences. They are continuing a conversation. This trains discourse-level PP, not just sentence-level.
  • Target: 5 student links, 4+ correct PP forms, 4+ duration/frequency markers. That = solid B1.1 production.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "This is jazz — you're not reading a script, you're building one with me. If you stumble, I'll feed you the opener. Just keep the chain alive."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "Native English conversation IS this — a chain of related Present Perfect statements about lived experience. You just did it. That's not a drill — that's a conversation."

Speed Round — 3-Second Cue Cards
7 min

Click each card. You have 3 seconds to produce a Present Perfect values sentence using the cue. No pauses. No "uhhh." If you stall, the card stays unfired. Aim for 8/8.

01
honesty
"I've valued… since…"
02
disrespect
"I've never tolerated…"
03
ambition
"I've always had…"
04
recognition
"I've worked for… for…"
05
unfairness
"Have you ever experienced…?"
06
family time
"I've prioritized… since…"
07
growth
"I've never stopped…"
08
a comfort zone
"I've never been satisfied with…"
Pace: 3 seconds per card · target 8/8 fired · teacher counts PP accuracy. Tap a card when the student fires a clean sentence.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • Speed is the point. 3 seconds = no time to translate from Spanish. The student must produce from English-internalized PP, not from translation. That is the B1.1 fluency threshold.
  • Don't correct mid-round. Mark fires with a tap. After all 8, do a 60-second debrief on the 2-3 weakest forms.
  • If the student gets fewer than 5/8, that's a flag — they need more drilling on duration markers. Schedule a 5-minute recap at the start of S3A.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Three seconds. No thinking. Just fire. Whatever sentence falls out of your mouth — that's your real English."
  • "Eight out of eight. That's the target. Anyone who hits eight in a row gets the Mastery Reflex badge — automatic."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "Speed of response is the single best predictor of professional fluency. Anyone can write a perfect sentence with five minutes. Saying one in three seconds — that is B1+."
§ 05
Solo Challenge — My Top 3 Values & Why
12 min
You have 60 seconds. Deliver a clean, structured monologue: "My Top 3 Values & Why."
Open: "I've always known that three things matter most to me…"
Pick three values. For each one, say: (1) what it is, (2) how long you've held it (for/since), (3) one moment that has tested or shaped it.
Close: "What I've learned is that my values have built the professional I have become."
Live Rubric — Teacher Marks During the 60 Seconds (tap to score)
≥ 6
Present Perfect sentences delivered correctly
≥ 2
Duration markers used (for / since)
≥ 1
Frequency marker (ever / never / always)
0
Spanish filler words (no "este," "o sea," "bueno")
60s
Continuous speech — no stops longer than 3 seconds
3
Distinct values clearly named and justified
🏆
Badge b3 · Values Architect
Earned when all 6 rubric items are checked. This is the highest-tier mastery badge for M1.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • Zero interruptions during the 60 seconds. Tap rubric items in real time. After: name one excellent moment + one specific correction + ask the student to redeliver ONE sentence corrected. That's it. Maximum 3 minutes of feedback.
  • Most common error at this stage: the student says "I have valued honesty since 5 years" instead of "for 5 years" or "since I started working." Drill: "since + start point / for + duration." If this slips, redo §4 cue 1 and 4.
  • Optional second take: 60 seconds again with corrections incorporated. The B1.1 ceiling is unlocked when the second take feels effortless. That's the b3 badge moment.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "This monologue is what people pay for. 60 seconds of clear, confident English about who you are and what you stand for. Job interview, networking dinner, board reset — same speech."
  • If they hit all 6 rubric items: "That's the b3 badge — Values Architect. Three students this month have hit it. You're one of them."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "Record this 60 seconds on your phone. Listen tomorrow. The difference between the version in your head and the version you delivered — that gap is what we close in S3A."
§ 06
Source Analysis — Reading Real Values
8 min

Read the "About Me" page below. It's a real-style professional bio. Two tasks: (1) identify every Present Perfect form, (2) evaluate — do the values sound authentic, or like corporate branding? Defend your evaluation in Present Perfect.

Marina Castillo
VP of People · Global Logistics Group

For most of my career, I have believed that people are not "resources" — they are the company. I have spent the last fifteen years building teams in three continents, and one principle has stayed with me since the very beginning: trust scales, control doesn't.

I have always valued transparency over comfort. I have never been the leader who softens hard news — I deliver it directly, and I have learned that teams respect that more than reassurance.

Since 2018, I have led talent strategy for over 4,000 employees. We have built a culture where feedback flows upward and decisions are explained, not announced. We haven't gotten everything right — but we have apologized when we've fallen short, and I have personally rewritten two policies after employee pushback.

What matters to me, and what has always mattered: integrity in the hard moments, growth for everyone, and the courage to say what others won't.

Analysis Questions — Answer in Present Perfect
1. How many Present Perfect forms can you identify? Name three uses of for / since, and three uses of always / never.
2. Which sentence has the strongest authenticity signal — and why? Use: "What's made this sentence real for me is…"
3. Is "We haven't gotten everything right — but we have apologized" credible or rehearsed? Defend your answer in 30 seconds, Present Perfect throughout.
4. Compare to your OWN values statement from §5. What has Marina done with Present Perfect that you haven't yet?
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • Count: the text contains 12+ PP forms. The student should find at least 10. The underlined forms are scaffolded — that's why the count is high but findable.
  • Key teaching moment: "I have always valued" vs "What has always mattered." The second is a more advanced inversion structure. Highlight it explicitly.
  • The authenticity question is where B1.1 students get to use PP for evaluation, not just description. Push: "What sentence has felt rehearsed to you? Which has felt real?"
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "This is a real-world reading task. Every executive bio you'll read on LinkedIn is written exactly like this. You can now decode AND write at this level."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "Homework optional: rewrite your LinkedIn 'About' section using 5 Present Perfect sentences. Bring it to S3A. We'll workshop it together."
