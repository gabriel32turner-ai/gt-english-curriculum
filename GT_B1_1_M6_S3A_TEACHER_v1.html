§1

🍕 Warm-Up — Food, Culture & Identity

5–8 min
Module 6 · Session 3A — Food, Culture & Identity · What does food say about who you are?
Q1 — Your food identity
What is ONE food that is inseparable from your culture or your family? Why does it matter? Could you live without it?
Q2 — Memory and food
Think of a meal that takes you back to a specific time or place. Who cooked it? What does it remind you of? Why do you think food is so connected to memory?
Q3 — Other cultures
Have you ever tried food from a culture very different from yours? Did it change the way you saw that culture? What happened?
Q4 — The First Conditional angle
"If we stop preserving traditional food recipes, we will lose part of our cultural identity." Agree or disagree? What is the consequence of losing food traditions?
⚡ Quick Win — 20 seconds
Say out loud: "Food has always been a big part of my culture and identity. If we don't share and preserve our recipes, future generations will lose that connection."
Present Perfect + First Conditional. The grammar of cultural memory.
Teacher note — PROGRAMA GT Original: This session is documented in the PROGRAMA as one of the best of the entire B1.1 level for Mexican students. Food is deeply personal, emotionally connected, and immediately available as a speaking topic. Every student has a story. Do not rush the warm-up. Let it breathe. The grammar target (First Conditional + PP) will arise naturally if you ask: "What will happen to this dish if no one teaches it to the next generation?" That question generates authentic language.
§2

🗣️ Speaking Topic — How Important Is Food to Your Cultural Identity?

8 min

How important is food to your cultural identity? Is there a food you couldn't live without?

Personal, cultural, emotional. The richest speaking topic of Module 6. The grammar serves the story — opinion language + First Conditional — but the story is what matters here.

Speaking · 3 rounds · Your food, your culture, your First Conditional argument
T→S Round 1
Describe a food that is part of your cultural identity. What is it, who makes it, and what does it mean to you? Use vocabulary: identity, tradition, memory, heritage. Close with a First Conditional: "If I couldn't eat it anymore, I would feel..."
S↔S Round 2
Share a food memory with your partner. When did you eat it? Who was there? What do you associate it with? Use: "That dish reminds me of..." and Present Perfect for how the food has become part of your identity: "This food has always been..."
Class Round 3
The cultural question: "If Mexican food traditions disappear in the next 50 years, what will we lose beyond just recipes?" Use First Conditional + opinion language to make the argument.

In my opinion, food is one of the strongest carriers of cultural identity. For me, tamales aren't just food — they're a whole ritual. My grandmother has made them the same way for fifty years. Every Christmas, every family reunion — the recipe has never changed. "That dish reminds me of home" in a way that nothing else does. I strongly believe that if younger generations don't learn these recipes, we will lose something that no museum can preserve. The reality is — food is memory made physical. If we stop cooking together, we will stop transmitting who we are.

Opinion · First Conditional ×2 · Present Perfect · Colloquial

Teacher note: The model answer uses First Conditional twice with different consequences: one about knowledge, one about identity transmission. This is the most advanced level of FC argumentation at B1.1 — stacking conditionals to build a complete argument. After sharing the model, ask: "Can you build a two-FC argument about any food from your region?"

💬 Colloquial & Opinion Language

5 min
"That dish reminds me of home."
When to use
The most personal colloquial phrase in Module 6. Use when you want to connect food to identity or memory in conversation. "That dish reminds me of home — and if I don't make it at least once a month, I feel disconnected from where I come from."
"I love trying new food."
When to use
Use when expressing cultural openness. "I love trying new food — and every time I do, I understand that culture a little better. If more people tried the food of others, there would be less division." Open + First Conditional.
"Some things you just can't give up."
When to use
Use for cultural anchors — the non-negotiables. "I have adapted a lot since I moved here. But some things you just can't give up. If I had to stop eating [X], I would lose a piece of who I am." Honest, direct, human.
🎯 Opinion Language — Applied to Food & Culture
I strongly believe that… — "I strongly believe that food is the fastest way to understand another culture."
In my opinion… — "In my opinion, losing a traditional recipe is as serious as losing a language."
I see both sides, but… — "I see both sides — globalization brings new foods AND threatens old ones. But I think the gain outweighs the loss."
That's a good point, however… — "That's a good point, however not everyone has access to traditional ingredients — and that affects cultural continuity too."
The reality is… — "The reality is that food traditions are already disappearing in many regions — and if we don't document them, they will be gone."
§5

⚡ Grammar — First Conditional as Cultural Argument

12 min
Grammar · First Conditional for cultural consequences · Stacking conditionals for deeper arguments
🔧 Stacking First Conditionals for Stronger Arguments

One First Conditional states a consequence. Two First Conditionals build a chain of reasoning. Three create a complete advocacy argument. Each builds on the last.

If younger generations don't learn to cook traditional dishes, the recipes will disappear.
If the recipes disappear, we will lose a form of cultural memory that no book can replace.
And if we lose that memory, future generations will have no connection to where they came from.
→ Three conditionals = one complete cultural argument.
Common errors in S3A context:
✗ "If we will lose these traditions, things will change." → No will in IF clause. "If we lose these traditions..."
✗ "Food is always connected to memory." → Use Present Perfect for a state that has developed over time: "Food has always been connected to memory."
✗ "I associate tamales with Christmas." (correct) vs. "I associate tamales to Christmas." (wrong preposition in English)

🎧 Listening — Rodrigo and the Japanese Business Dinner

10 min
Listening · Rodrigo on the meal that changed how he saw another culture

Rodrigo speaks informally about a business trip to Japan and the meal that changed his perspective on cultural identity. Listen for opinion phrases, First Conditional, and Present Perfect.

The Japanese Dinner — Rodrigo Vega
B1.1 · M6 · S3A · Voice: Adam · Speed: 0.85 · ElevenLabs
A few years ago I traveled to Osaka for a supplier meeting — first time in Japan. And I want to tell you about the dinner, because it changed the way I think about food and culture. Our host took us to a small restaurant — maybe eight tables. And the meal lasted three hours. Three hours. Every dish was presented as if it were a piece of art. And between dishes, the chef came out and explained what we were eating — where the ingredients came from, what season they were connected to, what memory the dish was supposed to evoke. I've eaten in many countries. But I had never experienced food as a complete philosophy before that night. In my opinion, that dinner taught me more about Japanese culture than any book I've read. The way they preserve their culinary traditions — the precision, the connection to seasons and memory — it was extraordinary. And here's what I thought on the flight home: if we had that relationship with food in Mexico — the same intentionality, the same reverence — our culinary heritage would be presented to the world completely differently. We have UNESCO recognition. We have incredible variety. But if we don't invest in preserving and presenting it with the same pride, we will risk losing it to fast food and globalization. That dish reminded me of something my grandmother used to say: "If you cook with memory, the people you feed will remember." I think about that a lot now.
📊 Comprehension
1. What surprised Rodrigo most about the Japanese dining experience?
2. What concern does Rodrigo express about Mexican culinary heritage?

🎯 This week, use these 3 phrases at work
3 Phrases for This Week
PHRASE 1
"That dish reminds me of home."
Use when someone asks about your food preferences, your region, or your family. It immediately makes the conversation personal and authentic. Follow it with more: "That dish reminds me of home — my grandmother has made it the same way my whole life."
PHRASE 2
"I love trying new food."
Use this when talking about travel, other cultures, or simply your own curiosity. Add a First Conditional: "I love trying new food — and if you give me a recommendation, I'll try it this weekend." Enthusiasm + action = memorable phrase.
PHRASE 3
"Some things you just can't give up."
One of the most naturally English colloquial phrases in Module 6. Use it about food, family, habits — anything that connects deeply to identity. "I've adapted a lot in my life, but some things you just can't give up."
