§1
Hot Take — Who Gets the Job?
Activate S2A language under time pressure · comparatives + colloquial · 5–8 min
⏱ 5–8 MIN

Remember from S2A: IS more qualified / HAS more experience · the most qualified / the strongest fit · ticks all the boxes · tough call · stands out. Now use it all — fast.

🔥 Hot Take — 60 Seconds
"I think Valeria was the right hire for Andrea — and I can give you three reasons using today's language."
Use at least 2 comparatives, 1 superlative, and 1 colloquial phrase from S2A. No notes. Convince the class in 60 seconds.
HOT TAKE
🔥 Hot Take — Counter-Argument — 45 Seconds
"Actually, Roberto was the better choice. Valeria is too expensive and not immediately available."
Defend Roberto using comparatives. Challenge: use "although" or "even though" to concede one point while making your argument stronger. 45 seconds.
HOT TAKE 2
⚡ Teacher Note — Hot Take
Say: "No notes — you know this from last session. This is the moment to use it." Round 1: T-S model first — use Valeria confidently. Round 2: assign Roberto's side to the class — create disagreement. The challenge word "although" previews B1.1 concession language; plant it as a bonus: "If you can use although or even though today, that is already intermediate English." This seeds aspiration for the next level without making A2.2 students feel behind.
§2
Pitch / Debate — The Hiring Panel
Role-play · 3 rounds · New context: comparing cities for a transfer · Model answers · 10–12 min
⏱ 10–12 MIN

S2B expands the comparison context: same structures, new scenario. Andrea's manager now asks her to compare two cities for a new team transfer. Comparatives and superlatives work for people, products, AND places.

T-SRound 1 — Teacher Models
Andrea must recommend a city for a new regional office: Celaya or Querétaro. Use what you know about both cities. Which is more convenient for logistics? Which has the best infrastructure? Which is more affordable for office space?
"Celaya is more affordable than Querétaro for office space and housing. However, Querétaro is better connected than Celaya for international clients — it has a larger airport and more direct flights than any city in the Bajío. In terms of talent, Querétaro has the most qualified engineering workforce in the region. It's a tough call, but for a logistics operation with international accounts, Querétaro is the stronger choice."
S-SRound 2 — Pair Debate
Student A defends Celaya. Student B defends Querétaro. Each person must use at least 3 comparatives and 1 superlative in their argument. 90 seconds each — then switch sides. Who is more convincing?
Celaya defender: "Celaya is more centrally located than Querétaro — it connects Guadalajara, Mexico City and Monterrey more easily. The cost of living is significantly lower than Querétaro, which means salaries can be more competitive for the same budget. And our existing team is already here — that makes us the most efficient option."

Querétaro defender: "Querétaro has more multinationals than Celaya — 300+ compared to fewer than 100. The workforce is the most bilingual in the region, which is critical for our international accounts. And the airport is much better than Celaya's for client visits. The pros definitely outweigh the cons."
ClassRound 3 — Class Vote + Justification
Class vote: which city is better for a logistics regional office? Each student must justify their vote in one sentence using a superlative. "I vote for [city] because it is the most [adj] for our industry."
How to run: After voting, create two groups (Celaya vs Querétaro). Each group has 2 minutes to build the strongest argument using ALL of today's language: comparatives, superlatives, and at least 2 colloquial phrases from the module. Then each group presents for 60 seconds. Class votes again after hearing arguments — did anyone change their mind? Award 3 leaderboard points to the group that caused the most mind-changes. This is advanced persuasion skill — celebrate it.
⚡ Teacher Note — City Comparison Extension
Expanding from candidates to cities is intentional: it shows students that their grammar toolkit is versatile. Say: "Same structure — different noun. You already know how to do this." If students have knowledge of their own city, encourage personal examples. The Celaya vs Querétaro debate is especially relevant for students in the Bajío corridor — they have real opinions. Use that energy. For stronger groups: add a third option (León) and ask "of all three, which is the most [adj]?" — this forces superlative use naturally.

Relay — Open the Box
6 boxes · 1 speaking prompt each · comparatives + HR vocab · 10 min
⏱ 10 MIN
🔒 Open the Box — Comparison Prompts
Click a box · get your prompt · speak for 45 seconds
1 🔒
Compare two colleagues you know — who is more experienced? Who is more proactive? Who would you choose for a leadership role and why?
2 🔒
You are the hiring manager. You have two candidates: one is more qualified but less available. The other is available immediately but has less experience. Who do you hire? Give 2 reasons using comparatives.
3 🔒
Compare your current job with your previous job (or with a friend's job). Which is more demanding? Which has better benefits? Which is more flexible? Use at least 3 comparatives.
4 🔒
You work in HR. Describe the most qualified candidate you have ever interviewed or worked with. What made them stand out? Use superlatives and at least one colloquial phrase from this module.
5 🔒
Compare your city with another city in Mexico for attracting international business. Which is more competitive? Which has the most qualified workforce? Which is the cheapest for operations? Make a recommendation.
6 🔒
You are comparing yourself as a candidate to a competitor for a promotion. What makes you more qualified than them? What is your strongest quality? Use: "I am more [adj] than..." and "I am the most [adj] person in my team because..."
Click any box to reveal a speaking prompt for your team.
⚡ Teacher Note — Open the Box
How to run: teams take turns clicking boxes. Each team has 45 seconds to discuss the prompt after it opens. Scoring: 1 point per comparative used, 2 points per superlative, 3 bonus points for a colloquial phrase. Teacher tracks on board — feeds into leaderboard in §5. Box 6 (self-comparison) is the most powerful for learner motivation: it forces students to use the grammar about themselves. Reserve it for the strongest student or most engaged pair. After Box 6: ask the class "Who can top that? Can you describe yourself as the most [adj] person for your role?" — creates positive competition.
§4
Speed Round — HR Error Correction
Fix the mistake · Adj vs noun · IS/HAS · then produce your own · 10–12 min
⏱ 10–12 MIN

Each sentence has ONE error. Find it and click the correct version. These are the most common mistakes Mexican learners make when comparing candidates in English.

Candidate A is more experience than Candidate B in project management.
Of all the candidates, Valeria is the more qualified for this position.
Roberto has more experienced in logistics — he worked there for eight years.
She is as more bilingual as any candidate we have interviewed this year.
His salary expectation is more lower than hers — that is an advantage.
GT Originals — Error Pattern Analysis
  • ⚠️ Error 1 (IS more experience): mixing noun with "is" — the most common A2 error in HR writing. Fix: IS + adjective / HAS + noun. Write the two columns on the board every session until it sticks.
  • ⚠️ Error 3 (HAS more experienced): the mirror error — mixing adjective with "has." Students who produce both errors in one session are confusing the IS/HAS rule completely — dedicate 5 extra minutes with the two-column drill.
  • ⚠️ Error 4 (as more bilingual as): students add "more" inside an as…as construction — the result of interference with comparatives. Rule: as…as uses the BASE adjective — never "more" or "-er" inside.
  • 🔹 Upsell: error correction is a B1.1 skill students are already doing — tell them that. "Noticing and fixing your own errors is what intermediate learners do. You are already there."

🎯 Solo Challenge — The Hiring Pitch
Scenario: You are a team leader or HR coordinator. Your company needs to hire one person for a role you know well. You have reviewed two candidates (real or invented). You have 3 minutes to present your comparison and make a final recommendation to the hiring committee.

Required language:
▶ At least 3 comparatives with adjectives (more qualified than, more experienced than)
▶ At least 2 comparatives with nouns (has more experience than, has more references than)
▶ At least 2 superlatives (the most qualified, the strongest fit)
▶ At least 1 colloquial phrase from S2A or S2B
▶ A clear final recommendation with justification

🎯 This week, use these 3 phrases at work:
Session 2 language — ready for your next HR conversation, performance review or team meeting.
"[Name] is more qualified than [Name] for this role."
The core HR comparison sentence. Use in any hiring, promotion or project assignment conversation. Add "because" and a reason to sound fully professional.
"She stands out from the others."
Use when recommending someone who is clearly the best choice. More professional than "she is the best." Works in performance reviews, client recommendations, and team feedback.
"It's a tough call, but [name] is the stronger fit."
Use when two options are close and you still need to decide. Shows analytical thinking — you considered both sides before choosing. Essential in any real hiring or business decision conversation.

Wrap-Up — M5 Session 2 Complete
Key takeaways · Checklist · Session certificate · My Win Today · 5–8 min
⏱ 5–8 MIN
☐ IS / HAS distinction
IS more qualified (adj). HAS more experience (noun). These are the two different ways to compare people — both use "more … than." Never mix them.
☐ Superlatives for people + places
The most qualified candidate. The strongest fit. The most bilingual city in the region. The same structure works for candidates, cities, products — any noun.
☐ HR colloquial toolkit
"Ticks all the boxes" · "Tough call" · "Stands out from the others" · "Strong fit for the role." These phrases mark you as a professional communicator in English.

I Can Now...

☐Compare candidates using both adjective (IS more qualified) and noun (HAS more experience) forms
☐Use superlatives confidently: "the most qualified," "the strongest fit," always with THE
☐Apply comparison language to cities and places — same structure, different context
☐Use HR colloquial phrases naturally in a recommendation or performance review
☐Give a 3-minute hiring pitch with clear structure: comparison → superlative → recommendation
