§ 01
Picture Prompt — Who is it?
🖼️ Choose a photo · Describe a las personas usando he/she/they · No hay respuesta incorrecta · Habla desde el primer segundo
⏱ 5–7 MIN
🚫 S2B — SIN gramática nueva · SIN lecturas · 100% producción oral · Todo el contenido de S2A reciclado productivamente
⚡
Quick Win — 30 seconds · You can do it!
Sin mirar notas: di 2 cosas sobre un compañero usando 3rd person.
"She starts at eight. He doesn't work on weekends." — from memory. You already know it! 🎯
👀 Choose a photo. Look at the people. Say 4–5 sentences: What does this person do? What don't they do? Where do they work? Use "he/she" + verb with -s. No wrong answer — any sentence counts!
Photo 1
👩‍💼 La Coordinadora
ELEGIDA ✓
Photo 2
👨‍💼 El Supervisor
ELEGIDA ✓
Photo 3
👥 El Equipo
ELEGIDA ✓
🗣️ Say 4–5 sentences sobre your chosen photo.
Usa: "He/She manages…" · "He/She doesn't…" · "Does he/she…?" · "He/She starts at…" · "He/She travels to…"
Scaffold visible al student:
"He/She works at ___."  ·  "He/She manages ___."  ·  "He/She doesn't travel."  ·  "He/She starts at ___."  ·  "Does he/she run meetings?"
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Student debe producir al menos 1 afirmativo (con -s), 1 negativo (doesn't) y 1 pregunta (does…?) about the photo.
  • S2B starts warmer than S2A. Student already knows the grammar — now they use it freely. If they hesitate: "You know this. Last class you did it. Any sentence is a win."
  • Do not correct in the moment. Listen, take mental note of -s errors. Correct them in §3 Audio Model.
  • Photo 3 (team) allows using "They work / They don't / Do they…?" — use it if the student already handles he/she and you want the extra challenge.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Last time you needed the grammar table. Now you are doing it from memory. That IS progress." 📈
Layer 3 · Retención
  • "In 50 minutes you will describe a real colleague in an HR role play — without notes, without aids. This picture prompt is the warm-up." 🎯
§ 02
Recap Speedrun — S2A Key Points
⚡ Memory activation pura · Sin contenido nuevo · 5 flip cards · Say the answer BEFORE clicking
⏱ 4–5 MIN

⚡ Click each card — say the answer BEFORE seeing the back. Without thinking too much. Speed!

1
She / He —
Afirmativo
tap to see
Verb + S (or -ES/-IES)
"She manages. He travels. It finishes."
2
She / He —
Negativo
tap to see
doesn't + BASE verb (no -s)
"He doesn't travel. She doesn't manage."
3
Does she…?
Pregunta
tap to see
Does + he/she + base verb + ?
"Does she run meetings?" "Yes, she does."
4
go → ?
study → ?
tap to see
go → goes · study → studies
-o → +ES · consonante+y → -IES
5
The trap —
doesn't + ?
tap to see
❌ "She doesn't manages" → ✓ "She doesn't manage"
El -s está en "doesn't". No se duplica.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Student says the answer out loud BEFORE clicking. Speed — without thinking too much.
  • Card 5 (the trap) is the most important. If the student hesitates → review with 3 quick oral examples before moving to §3.
  • If the student answers all 5 correctly in under 60 seconds → celebrate: "That is automaticity. Your brain already has it." 🧠
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "5 cards, 5 rules, 60 seconds. If you can do it fast without thinking — that is yours." ⚡
Layer 3 · Retención
  • "Estas mismas 5 reglas aparecen en S3A con adverbios de frecuencia. Student que llegue sabiendo la 3rd person llega con ventaja."
§ 03
Audio Model — Listen and Repeat
🎧 Carlos describes Ana · ElevenLabs model · Shadowing phrase by phrase · Then you describe your colleague like Carlos
⏱ 8–10 MIN
🎙️ Audio Modelo — Carlos describes Ana (completo)
ElevenLabs · speed 0.78 · Listen completely first · Then shadowing phrase by phrase ↓

🔊 Listen to each phrase → repeat out loud → listen again. Pay attention to the sound of the -s. Minimum 3 rounds per phrase.

1
"She starts work at eight every morning."
2
"She attends the team meeting at nine."
3
"She doesn't run the meeting — I run it."
4
"She handles all supplier questions in the afternoon."
5
"She doesn't travel for work."
💡 Phrases 3 and 5 are key: both have "doesn't" + base verb. Can you hear that there is NO -s on "run" or "travel"?
🎙️ Now You — Mismo Formato que Carlos
Describe a real colleague of yours in 4–5 sentences — just like Carlos described Ana. Include: role, 2 things they do, 1 thing they don't, time they finish.
➡ "[Name] is our [role]. He/She starts at ___. He/She ___s and also ___s. He/She doesn't ___. He/She finishes at ___."
Sample Answer — Describing a colleague:
"My colleague is Carlos. He works in production. He starts work at eight thirty every day. He checks his email first — he gets about thirty messages a day. He has a team meeting every Monday. He doesn't eat at his desk — he goes out for lunch. He leaves at six. He doesn't work on weekends. Does he manage a team? Yes, he does — he manages five people."
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Shadowing — student says the phrase BEFORE the audio, then verifies with 🔊. Not the other way around.
  • Frase 3 ("She doesn't run — I run it") es perfecta para trabajar el contraste audible entre "doesn't run" (sin -s) e "I run" (también sin -s). Son iguales — eso refuerza que el base verb es siempre base verb.
  • In the "Now You" — immediately correct any "doesn't manages" or "she don't". This is the moment for maximum precision before the role play.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Acabas de hablar exactamente igual que Carlos describió a su equipo para su director en EE.UU. Eso ya es inglés de trabajo real." 💼
Layer 3 · Retención
  • "En el role play del §4 vas a usar este mismo formato — pero sobre tu propio equipo, en vivo, sin modelo. El audio fue el ensayo general." 🎭
§ 04
Role Play — HR Update Call
🎭 Main S2B activity · Guided → semi-free → free · Describe your real colleague as if for your HR director in the US
⏱ 15–18 MIN
🎭 Round 1 — Guiada (con scaffold visible)
📞 "Tell me about your colleague"
Your HR director in Chicago calls you. They want to know about a colleague of yours for a report. You have 60 seconds to describe them. The scaffold is available.
👔 Teacher (HR Director)
David Morrison · Chicago
Empieza: "Hi! Can you tell me about your coordinator? What does she do every day? Does she manage a team? Does she travel?"
🗣️ Student (you)
Your name · Celaya
Respond with the name, role and 3–4 things about your colleague. Use he/she + verbs with -s. Include 1 negative with doesn't.
Scaffold Round 1 (visible):
"Sure! My colleague is [name]. He/She is our [role]."
"He/She starts at ___. He/She ___s and ___s every day."
"He/She doesn't ___. He/She finishes at ___."
"Does he/she manage a team? Yes, he/she does. / No, he/she doesn't."
🎭 Round 2 — Semi-free (no scaffold, just prompts)
📞 Second call — different colleague
Same situation, but now describe your manager. No scaffold. Just the teacher's prompts as a guide.
👔 Teacher — available prompts
Preguntas para guiar
"What does your manager do every morning?" · "Does she/he travel?" · "Does your manager run meetings?" · "How many people does she/he manage?"
🗣️ Student — free
No scaffold
Minimum 4–5 sentences. 1 affirmative, 1 negative, answer at least 2 of the teacher's questions with short answers.
⭐ Round 3 — Free (Challenge: 60 seconds non-stop)
🎤 Team Report — Solo performance
No teacher as interlocutor. Student speaks alone for 60 seconds describing any colleague. Teacher times and counts correctly used 3rd person verbs.
Teacher counts: How many correct 3rd person verbs did the student use in 60 seconds?
5+ correct verbs = ⭐ Excellent · 3–4 = ✅ Good · 1–2 = 📋 Review at home
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: R1 — student puede ver el scaffold. R2 — solo prompts de preguntas. R3 — cronómetro, sin ayuda, sin interrupción.
  • In R1 and R2: immediate recast of -s errors. "She manage → She manages. Repeat." In R3: don't interrupt — just count correct verbs.
  • If the student gets stuck en R2 → devuélvele una pregunta: "Does she travel? Just answer that." Una pregunta es suficiente para reactivar el flujo.
  • R3 is the highest emotional impact moment of S2B. If they reach 5+ verbs → celebrate specifically: "You used 7 correct 3rd person verbs in 60 seconds. That is A1.2 territory."
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Acabas de hacer lo que haría un profesional mexicano en una llamada real con su HR director en Chicago." 📞
Layer 3 · Retención
  • "En S3A agregarás adverbios de frecuencia — 'She usually manages / She always attends.' Hoy tienes la base. El siguiente paso la enriquece." 📈
§ 05
Guessing Game — "Who Am I Talking About?"
🎮 Wordwall-style game · Describe a colleague without saying their name · Partner guesses · 3rd person in real context · Actividad WOW de S2B
⏱ 10–12 MIN
🎮 Guessing Game — "Who Am I Talking About?"
Describe a real colleague without saying their name. Teacher guesses. 3 rounds.
Step 1 — Choose a real colleague
Piensa en alguien de tu trabajo. No digas su nombre todavía. Solo piensa en lo que hace cada día.
💡 It can be your manager, a colleague from your area, someone from another department — anyone you know well.
Step 2 — Describe with 4–5 sentences
Usa solo he/she — sin nombres. Incluye: qué hace, qué no hace, a qué hora llega, si viaja, si maneja un equipo.
➡ "He/She works in ___. He/She manages ___. He/She doesn't travel. He/She starts at ___. He/She runs the ___ meeting."
Step 3 — Teacher guesses
Teacher asks yes/no questions to guess: "Does he manage more than 5 people? Does she travel a lot? Does he arrive before 8?"
Student responds with short answers: "Yes, he does." / "No, she doesn't."
🏆 Goal: the teacher needs minimum 5 questions to guess. If they guess in 3 or fewer → the student was too obvious. Round 2 with more detail.
Variation for 2 more rounds
Round 2: the teacher describe a "Ana García" usando los datos de S2A — the student adivina de qué persona habla (Ana o Luis).
Round 3: libre — the student elige una persona pública que trabaja en algo conocido.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Student must use minimum 4 correct 3rd person verbs before you can start guessing.
  • Cuando the teacher pregunta "Does he travel?" → the student practica short answer in real context de comunicación, no como ejercicio mecánico.
  • Round 2 (tú describes a Ana) es poderosa porque invierte the roles y the student practica el listening de 3rd person para identificar la persona correcta.
  • Correction during the game: if the student says "He don't travel" → ask "Sorry — does he or doesn't he?" → the student self-corrects in context.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "See? You just had a real conversation about a real person — using real grammar — and you didn't even notice it was grammar." 🎯
Layer 3 · Retención
  • "En S3A el mismo juego añade adverbios — 'He usually travels. She always attends. He never runs meetings.' Hoy tienes la estructura. El siguiente paso la enriquece."
§ 06
Debate — Práctica Oral Libre
🗣️ 3 thought-provoking questions about work · Answers with he/she/they · Free opinion in English · Sin estructura obligatoria
⏱ 8–10 MIN
💬 Question 1 — The best colleague
Think of the best colleague you have ever worked with. What does he/she do that makes them great?
➡ Usa: "He/She always ___s. He/She never ___s. He/She manages ___ really well."
💬 Question 2 — El manager ideal
What does the ideal manager do every day? What does the ideal manager NOT do?
➡ Usa: "The ideal manager ___s ___. He/She doesn't ___. He/She always ___s when ___."
⭐ Question 3 — Challenge
Your new American colleague asks: "What does a typical Mexican professional do every day? How is it different from the US?" Answer in English — at least 5 sentences.
➡ Free — any structure with he/she/they + 3rd person verbs.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Este es el bloque de mayor fluidez. Mínima corrección — solo recast suave de errores graves de -s.
  • Question 3 es la más rica — permite hablar de cultura laboral mexicana vs. americana. Student tiene mucho que decir sobre esto. Deja que fluya.
  • If the student says "In Mexico, people work hard" → pregunta: "Your colleague — what does HE work on specifically?" → regresa al foco de 3rd person.
  • These questions work as a warm-up for S3A where they will add frequency adverbs to exactly this type of sentences.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Estás teniendo una opinión en inglés sobre el trabajo. No estás traduciendo — estás pensando en inglés. That is diferente." 🧠
Layer 3 · Retención
  • "In S3A you add 'usually, always, sometimes, never' to exactly these sentences. Today's debate was the general rehearsal for that." 📈
