§1

✈️ Warm-Up — Travel & Perspective

5–8 min
M6 · S4A — Travel & Perspective · Has travel changed the way you see the world?
Q1 — Has travel changed you?
Think of a trip that changed how you see the world. What happened? What do you understand now that you didn't before? Use PP: "Travel has taught me..."
Q2 — Is it a privilege?
"Not everyone has the chance to travel." Is travel a privilege? What does it mean that some people never leave their city and others travel internationally every year?
Q3 — Your assumption was wrong
Did you ever travel somewhere and discover your assumption about the place or people was completely wrong? What did you assume? What did you find?
Q4 — First Conditional frame
"If more people traveled outside their comfort zone, there would be less prejudice in the world." Agree or disagree? Use First Conditional + opinion language.
⚡ Quick Win — 20 seconds
Say out loud: "Travel has changed the way I think. I strongly believe that if you travel with real curiosity, your assumptions will dissolve — and you will become a better professional and a better person."
PP + Opinion + FC. Your Exit Project in one sentence.
Teacher note: Q4 introduces the Exit Project logic. At the end of S4A you will formally reveal the Exit Project brief. Today is the preparation. Students who can answer Q4 fluently with PP + FC + opinion language are already performing at Exit Project level.
§2

🗣️ Speaking Topic — Has Travel Changed the Way You See the World?

8 min

Has travel changed the way you see the world? Is it a privilege?

The travel question connects assumption, privilege, openness, and perspective. All require PP + FC + opinion language. The Exit Project theme appears here.

Speaking · 3 rounds · PP + FC + Opinion Language · Exit Project preview
T→S Round 1
Has travel changed your perspective? Use PP: "Travel has taught me... / I have discovered..." Then add one FC: "If I hadn't gone, I would never have..." Use one opinion phrase to open.
S↔S Round 2
Privilege debate: "Is travel a right or a luxury?" One argues access, one argues privilege. Required: PP + FC each. Counter-phrase: "That's a good point, however..."
Class Round 3
Exit Project preview: "What is one thing you feel strongly about?" Class shares topic ideas. One volunteer tries 60 seconds of their Exit Project argument. Class gives feedback.

Travel has changed the way I see the world. Before a work trip to Monterrey — just 3 hours away — I had assumptions about how people there worked. I was wrong about most of them. I strongly believe that if you never leave your own city, you will always see the world through the same lens. In my opinion, travel doesn't mean flying abroad. If you explore the region around you with real curiosity, you will discover things that challenge your assumptions. "I saw things differently after that trip."

PP · FC ×2 · Opinion · Colloquial

Teacher note: The model uses FC twice with different subjects: "if you never leave" (general) and "if you explore" (specific action). This is the most sophisticated FC use in M6 — varying the subject of the condition. After sharing, ask students to identify both and then produce their own with different subjects.

💬 Colloquial & Opinion Language

5 min
"I saw things differently after that trip."
When to use
When describing how an experience changed your view. Works with any experience — not just travel. "I saw things differently after that project / that meeting." Adaptable, human, B1.1 level.
"Not everyone has the chance to travel."
When to use
Acknowledges privilege with empathy and realism. "Not everyone has the chance to travel — and if more people did, I believe the world would have fewer misunderstandings." Privilege + FC = nuanced argument.
"Even short trips open your mind."
When to use
Democratizes the conversation. Works for travel AND for learning: "Even short courses open your mind." "Even a 30-minute conversation with someone different opens your mind." Adaptable, optimistic.
🎯 Opinion Language — All 5 Phrases: Final Active Repaso
I strongly believe that… — "I strongly believe that travel builds empathy more than any classroom."
In my opinion… — "In my opinion, it's not the distance that matters — it's the openness you bring."
I see both sides, but… — "I see both sides — travel is a privilege AND a mindset. But if the mindset is right, even a short trip will change you."
That's a good point, however… — "That's a good point, however not all travel changes people — some visit 40 countries and never challenge a single assumption."
The reality is… — "The reality is that most people never leave their comfort zone — geographically or intellectually."
Teacher note — Exit Project bridge: At the end of §4, pause and say: "You have been using these 5 phrases for four sessions. In S5, you will use them in a 3-minute talk on any topic you choose. Start thinking now." First formal preview of the Exit Project — full reveal comes at §8.

⚡ Grammar — PP + FC + Opinion: The Complete B1.1 Opinion Toolkit

12 min
Grammar · The full toolkit · Exit Project level argument
🔧 The Complete B1.1 Opinion Framework

A complete B1.1 opinion has three components working together. By S4A you should produce all three naturally, without prompting.

EVIDENCE (PP)
"Travel has changed the way I see the world. I have discovered that..."
CONSEQUENCE (FC)
"If more people traveled with curiosity, assumptions would dissolve."
POSITION (OPINION)
"I strongly believe that openness is the most important skill a traveler can have."
S4A error focus:
✗ "Travel has change my perspective." → has changed (PP = has + past participle)
✗ "If I will travel more, I will understand more." → IF clause = present simple: If I travel more, I will understand more.
✗ Mixing: "If I had traveled, I will understand." → Choose FC (real) or 2C (hypothetical). Never mix.

§6

🎧 Listening — Rodrigo on the Trip That Humbled Him

10 min
Listening · Rodrigo on the supply chain visit to Oaxaca that changed his professional assumptions

Rodrigo tells his team about a work visit to a rural supplier in Oaxaca that completely changed how he saw his assumptions. Listen for PP, FC, and opinion phrases.

The Trip That Humbled Me — Rodrigo Vega
B1.1 · M6 · S4A · Voice: Adam · Speed: 0.85 · ElevenLabs
I want to tell you about a trip I made two years ago — not for vacation, for work. We had a new supplier in a rural community in Oaxaca. Small operation, family-run. And I drove there thinking I would evaluate their process, give some recommendations, and leave. That was my assumption. What actually happened was completely different. I spent three days there. By the end of the first day, I understood that their production system — which looked inefficient on paper — was actually designed around the seasons, the community, and a set of values that I had never considered in my logistics models. Travel has humbled me many times. But that trip humbled me professionally. In my opinion, if we had not made that visit in person, we would have made a decision that lost us one of our best suppliers. We would have measured them against a standard that had nothing to do with their reality. I strongly believe that if you only see your suppliers on a spreadsheet, you will never understand what drives their quality. I see both sides — efficiency matters, numbers matter. But if you don't connect with the people behind the numbers, something essential is missing. The lesson I brought back: every time I have traveled with assumptions, I have come back without most of them. And every time that has happened, I have become a better professional.
📊 Comprehension
1. What was Rodrigo's original assumption when he went to Oaxaca?
2. What did Rodrigo discover about the supplier's production system?
3. What lesson does Rodrigo say he brings back from every trip where he had assumptions?

🎯 This week, use these 3 phrases at work
3 Phrases for This Week
PHRASE 1
"I saw things differently after that trip."
Works with any experience — not just travel. "I saw things differently after that project / conversation / training." Personal, honest, B1.1. Follow with PP: "...because travel has shown me that my assumptions were wrong."
PHRASE 2
"Not everyone has the chance to travel."
Shows empathy and awareness of privilege. Adaptable to any opportunity. "Not everyone has the chance to [learn English / study abroad / work internationally] — and if they did, things would be different." Awareness + FC = sophisticated.
PHRASE 3
"Even short trips open your mind."
Democratize the argument. Applies to travel AND learning. "Even short conversations with people from different departments open your mind — if you listen with real curiosity." This week: use it in any context where small actions lead to big perspective shifts.
