§1
Hot Take — Defend Your Career Choice
Activate S4A language under pressure · all 6 comparison structures · 5–8 min
⏱ 5–8 MIN

Remember from S4A: significantly higher · far more collaborative · not as secure as · the most exciting · step up · pros outweigh the cons · not just about the money. Now use it all — live.

🔥 Hot Take — 60 Seconds — Money First
"Salary is the most important factor in any job decision. Everything else — culture, flexibility, growth — is less important than a significantly higher salary."
Use at least: 1 superlative, 2 comparatives with intensifiers, and "the pros outweigh the cons." 60 seconds — convince the class.
HOT TAKE
🔥 Counter-Argument — 45 Seconds — Culture First
"Workplace culture is far more important than salary. It is not just about the money — a toxic culture is significantly more damaging to your career than a lower salary."
Use "not just about the money" + at least 2 different comparison structures. Must include one "as...as" or "not as...as." 45 seconds.
COUNTER
⚡ Teacher Note — Hot Take
Model Round 1 first — use the language explicitly and dramatically. Then assign Round 2 to the class and listen for: (1) did anyone use "not as...as" naturally? (2) did anyone use "significantly" + a long adjective? Celebrate both by name. These are the two hardest structures from S4A. If Round 1 produces "more better" or "the most best," note it silently and address it in the Speed Round (§4) using the specific student's sentence as an anonymous example. End with: "In your next job interview, someone may ask: why do you want to leave? You just practiced the answer."
§2
Pitch / Debate — The Job Interview Simulation
3 rounds · real interview questions · model answers · 10–12 min
⏱ 10–12 MIN

The job interview is the highest-stakes professional conversation in English. S4B puts today's comparison structures into the 3 most common interview questions. Students practice answering them with the language they already have.

T-SRound 1 — Teacher Models
Interview Question: "Why do you want to leave your current company?" Teacher models a 60-second answer using at least 4 comparison structures and 2 colloquial phrases from M5.
"Thank you for asking. My current role has been a great learning experience, but I have been in the same position for three years, and the growth opportunities are significantly more limited than what I need at this stage of my career. It is not just about the money — the salary at my current company is not as competitive as the market rate for my experience level. I am looking for a role where the culture is more collaborative and more international than what I have now. This position is clearly the most exciting opportunity I have seen this year, and the pros outweigh the cons. It is a step up, and that is exactly what I need."
S-SRound 2 — Pair Role-Play
Student A = interviewer. Student B = candidate. 3 questions in rotation: (1) Why leave your current job? (2) What makes this role better than your current one? (3) What is your biggest strength compared to other candidates? Switch roles after all 3 questions. 90 seconds per question.
Q1 — Why leave?
"I am looking for a role with significantly better career growth. My current role is not as challenging as I need it to be. It is a step up from where I am now."

Q2 — Why this role?
"This role is much more aligned with my goals than my current position. The culture here is far more collaborative than where I work now. Of all the opportunities I have seen, this is the most attractive."

Q3 — Biggest strength?
"I am more experienced in cross-functional projects than most candidates at my level. I am also significantly more comfortable working in bilingual environments. I believe I am the strongest fit for this role."
ClassRound 3 — Best Answer Competition
Class votes on the best answer for Q3 "biggest strength." Each student presents their answer in 30 seconds. Class scores using: 1 point per comparative, 2 per superlative, 3 per colloquial phrase. Teacher tallies on board — feeds leaderboard.
How to run Round 3: After the pair practice, select 3–4 students to present their Q3 answer to the full class. Give them 30 seconds each. Class votes by raising hands for the most persuasive. Teacher awards: +3 for colloquial phrase used naturally, +2 for superlative, +1 per comparative. Record on board. The best answer is usually the one that feels most personal — encourage students to use real details about themselves, not generic phrases. This activity is intentionally competitive: it creates the social pressure of a real interview panel.
⚡ Teacher Note — Interview Simulation
The interview simulation is the most real-world activity in M5. Before Round 2: "This is a real interview conversation. Your partner is a hiring manager. Be professional, be clear, and use today's language." After Round 2: ask students "Which question was hardest to answer? Which comparison structure did you avoid?" This reveals where the gaps are — address them in the Speed Round. Round 3 (best answer) creates healthy competition — it is more engaging when students use their own real professional experience, not invented scenarios. Encourage authenticity. Upsell: "At B1.1 you answer the same questions using Present Perfect and reported speech. You are already building the vocabulary that makes those structures powerful."

⚡ Teacher Note — Flash Cards
8 cards: front = English term or scenario prompt, back = definition + example using comparison structures. Run as team relay: Team A gets a card, has 30 seconds to say a sentence using the term in a career comparison context. Then Team B gets the next card. The catch: every sentence must contain at least one comparison structure. Teacher awards 1 point per correct structure. Cards 5 and 8 are the hardest — "not as secure as" (as...as) and "the most attractive of all the options" (superlative with "of all"). These are the two structures most often forgotten under pressure. Reserve them for the strongest students or use them as class-wide bonus rounds.
§4
Speed Round — Career Structure Drills
8 prompts · 10 seconds each · all 6 comparison structures · 8 min
⏱ 8 MIN

Teacher reads the situation. Student has 10 seconds to produce one correct sentence. No notes. Targets every structure from S4A. Click each card to reveal the model answer after the student responds.

⚡ Prompt 1 — Short adjective
Multinational salary = $31K. Current salary = $22K. Compare using FAR.
Target: "The new salary is far higher than my current one."
⚡ Prompt 2 — Long adjective
New company = collaborative culture. Current = traditional. Compare using MORE + SIGNIFICANTLY.
Target: "The new workplace culture is significantly more collaborative than my current company."
⚡ Prompt 3 — Irregular
New job opportunities = excellent. Current job opportunities = limited. Use BETTER.
Target: "The career growth at the new company is much better than at my current one."
⚡ Prompt 4 — As...as
New job security = less than current. Use NOT AS...AS.
Target: "The new position is not as secure as my current role."
⚡ Prompt 5 — Superlative
This job = best opportunity of the year. Use THE MOST.
Target: "Of all the offers I have received, this is the most attractive opportunity."
⚡ Prompt 6 — Colloquial
New job: longer commute + better salary and culture. Use PROS OUTWEIGH.
Target: "The commute is longer, but the pros outweigh the cons — the salary and culture are far better."
⚡ Prompt 7 — Interview answer
Interviewer asks: "Why do you want this job?" Use: STEP UP + SIGNIFICANTLY.
Target: "This role is a step up from my current position, and the career growth is significantly better."
⚡ Prompt 8 — Full integration
Compare your current job with an ideal job using 3 different structures in one sentence.
Target: "My ideal job is much more flexible than my current one, the salary is significantly higher, and it is not as demanding in terms of overtime."
⚡ Teacher Note — Speed Round
Prompt 4 (as...as) is the most skipped structure — students default to "less secure than" instead of "not as secure as." Both are correct, but "not as secure as" is more natural in professional English. Prompt 8 is the integration challenge — award 3 points if the student uses 3 different structures correctly in one sentence. This is the hardest task in the session. If a student produces Prompt 8 perfectly, call it out: "That sentence just contained everything we have practiced in Module 5 in one breath. That is A2.2 grammar at its best."

🎯 Solo Challenge — The Career Pitch
Scenario: You are in a real job interview. The interviewer has just asked: "Tell me about your career so far, why you want to leave your current company, and what attracts you to this role." You have 3 minutes to answer. Use your real career or invent a professional scenario.

Required language:
▶ At least 2 short comparative structures (-er than or irregular: better/worse/higher/closer)
▶ At least 2 long comparative structures (more + adjective + than, with optional intensifier)
▶ At least 1 as...as or not as...as
▶ At least 1 superlative (the most/the best/the worst)
▶ At least 2 colloquial phrases from M5 S4
▶ A clear conclusion — what you want and why, spoken without notes

🎯 This week, use these 3 phrases at work:
From S4 — career comparison language for interviews, performance reviews and professional conversations.
"I am looking for a role that is significantly more [adj] than my current position."
The professional sentence for any job interview or networking conversation. Add your own adjective: challenging, flexible, international, collaborative. Shows clarity, ambition, and vocabulary in one phrase.
"It is a step up, and the pros outweigh the cons."
Combine both S4 colloquial phrases into one powerful justification sentence. Use in salary negotiations, job offer discussions, or performance reviews when recommending a decision to a manager.
"Of all the options, this is the most [adj] for my career goals."
Use the superlative with "of all" to signal a final, considered decision. More professional than "this is the best." Shows you compared multiple options before choosing.
