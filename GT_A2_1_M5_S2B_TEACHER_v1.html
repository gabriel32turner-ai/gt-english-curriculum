Hot Take — "Predictions Are Just Guesses."
Instant opinion · Use will + adverb under pressure · 60 seconds · b1 badge
⏱ 5 MIN
Context: In the quarterly business meeting, the sales director says: "Revenue will probably increase 12% next quarter." A colleague rolls their eyes and says: "Predictions in business meetings are just educated guesses — never trust them."
"Predictions in business meetings are just educated guesses — they will never be accurate. Agree or disagree — you have 60 seconds."

💡 Layer 1 — Accuracy focus: This is the B-session — no new grammar. Listen for will + adverb combos: "It will probably...", "They will definitely...", "I will certainly...". Common errors: placing adverb before will ("probably will increase" — acceptable in English but less natural in professional speech; flag gently). Watch for missing will: "It probably increase next quarter."
🔥 Layer 3 — Authenticity: Ask: "Has someone at your company ever made a prediction that turned out completely wrong? Or exactly right?" This connects the grammar to lived experience and forces a spontaneous will prediction sentence as the student recalls the story.
§2
Promise Debate — Over-Promise or Under-Promise?
2 students · Each defends their promise strategy · Must use will + adverbs in arguments
⏱ 10 MIN
Scenario: Your company is pitching a new client. The team is divided: Side A believes in over-promising to win the contract, then delivering what you can. Side B believes in under-promising — set conservative expectations and always exceed them. You are assigned one side. Defend your approach using will + adverbs in every argument.
Side A — Over-Promise to Win
"I'll definitely deliver the project on time. We will certainly exceed your expectations. Our team will probably find solutions you haven't thought of yet."
  • "The client will definitely choose us if we promise the best outcome."
  • "I'll personally guarantee the deadline. It will certainly be worth the risk."
  • "Won't a bold promise show more confidence than a cautious one?"
  • "The client will probably ask for more anyway — we should start high."
Side B — Under-Promise, Over-Deliver
"I'll probably deliver in six weeks — but I'll aim for five. That way the client will certainly be impressed when we're early."
  • "If I over-promise and fail, the client will definitely leave us for a competitor."
  • "I won't make a promise I can't keep. Trust will certainly be lost if I do."
  • "A realistic promise will probably build a stronger long-term relationship."
  • "The client will likely prefer honesty over a bold claim that falls short."
🎭 Layer 2 — Engagement: Assign sides even when students personally agree with the opposite view. This forces creative use of will + adverb in arguments they must construct, not just recall. Listen for: does each adverb choice reflect the correct confidence level? "definitely" should signal certainty; "probably" should signal likelihood. If a student uses "definitely" for an uncertain outcome, flag it.
💡 Pedagogical focus: The debate context is ideal for adverb precision. "We will possibly win" vs "We will definitely win" — these carry radically different commitments. After the 3-minute debate, spend 2 minutes asking: "Which adverb was riskiest to use? Which was most honest?" This builds metalinguistic awareness.

Relay Predictions — Q4 Forecast Chain
Team relay · Each student adds a prediction using will + adverb about next quarter · No repeats
⏱ 8 MIN
Situation: Your management team is building the Q4 forecast presentation. Each person must add one prediction using will + adverb. No student can repeat an adverb already used in the chain. Build the forecast together.
RELAY CARD 1 — REVENUE
Student 1: predict what will happen to revenue next quarter. Use "probably".
Model: "Revenue will probably increase by 8% if the new product launch goes well."
RELAY CARD 2 — COSTS
Student 2: predict operational costs. Use "definitely".
Model: "Costs will definitely go down once the new supplier contract is signed next month."
RELAY CARD 3 — NEW CLIENT
Student 3: predict how the new product will attract clients. Use "certainly" or "likely".
Model: "The new product will certainly attract clients in the tech sector — they've been asking for this feature."
RELAY CARD 4 — TEAM GROWTH
Student 4: predict team or headcount changes. Use "possibly".
Model: "We will possibly hire two more account managers if demand increases as predicted."
RELAY CARD 5 — RISK / CHALLENGE
Student 5: predict one potential problem. Make a promise using will to handle it.
Model: "Supply delays will probably slow delivery times. I'll personally monitor the supplier weekly to prevent any impact on clients."
💡 Layer 1 — Adverb tracking: Write the 5 adverbs on the board as students use them (probably / definitely / certainly / likely / possibly). The rule that no adverb can repeat forces students to think about confidence levels and select precisely. If the group is small, students take two cards each — they must use a different adverb for each card.
🎭 Layer 2 — Challenge mode: After the chain, have the class vote: "Which prediction is most realistic? Which adverb was used incorrectly for its sentence?" This creates peer feedback on adverb-strength matching — a key A2.1 → B1 skill.

Model: "I'll correct it right now and send it to you in 10 minutes. I promise."

Model: "It won't happen again. I'll set up a weekly check-in so you always know our status."
💳 The client just found an unexpected charge on their invoice and they're threatening to escalate.
Model: "I'll review the invoice now and I'll call you back within 20 minutes with a full explanation."
🔒 The client says a member of your team was rude to them on a call. They want action.
Model: "I'll speak to the team member today. I'll personally follow up with you by end of day."
📦 The wrong product was delivered to the client's warehouse. They have no time to return it themselves.
Model: "I'll arrange a pickup today and I'll send the correct order first thing tomorrow morning."
📋 A key client hasn't received their signed contract — the original was sent to the wrong address.
Model: "I'll send a digital copy right now and I'll resend the original by courier today."
📱 The client's account manager changed without notice and the client is upset about the lack of communication.
Model: "I'll introduce you to your new contact this afternoon. I'll make sure the handover is smooth."
🏆 The client says they're considering moving to a competitor. They want one reason to stay.
Model: "I'll schedule a call with our director this week. We will definitely find a solution that works for you."
🎭 Layer 2 — Speed pressure: Students click a card to reveal the full situation, then have 3 seconds to start speaking before they must produce a complete promise sentence. Teacher tracks: (1) did they use "will" or "I'll"? (2) was it a promise or a prediction? (3) did they include any adverb for emphasis? Debrief after all 8 cards.
💡 Layer 1 — Form check: In high-pressure production, students often drop the "I'll" and say "I correct it now." Mark every instance. The contracted form "I'll" signals authentic native-level will production — it is the target for A2.1 mastery.

Solo Challenge — Q4 Forecast Delivery
60-sec timed production · ≥4 prediction will · ≥2 promise will · ≥2 adverbs · b3 badge
⏱ 12 MIN
Brief: You are presenting the Q4 forecast to your company's leadership team. You have 60 seconds. Deliver your forecast using will for predictions and promises. Your adverbs show confidence level — use them strategically.
≥ 4 prediction will sentences
"Revenue will probably increase..."
≥ 2 promise will sentences
"I'll personally monitor..."
≥ 2 adverbs of probability
probably / definitely / certainly / likely / possibly
0 Spanish filler words
No "este...", "pues...", "o sea..."
60
Model Forecast (teacher reference only)
"Thank you. For Q4, revenue will probably increase by around 10% driven by the new product line. Customer satisfaction scores will certainly improve — we've already received positive pilot feedback. Costs will likely remain stable, though supply chain risks could push them up slightly. The new enterprise contract will possibly close by December, which would be a major win for the team. On my side: I'll personally track the pipeline every week and I'll send you a progress update every Friday. This quarter will be strong — I'm confident."
Tally: prediction will ×5, promise will ×2, adverbs: probably ×1, certainly ×1, likely ×1, possibly ×1. Above rubric on all counts.
🔥 Layer 3 — Real stakes: Ask the student to think about their actual company's situation. What ARE their real Q4 predictions? Using real data from their job makes the will sentences emotionally anchored — students are far more likely to remember and reuse language when it connects to lived context.
💡 Rubric: ≥4 prediction + ≥2 promise + ≥2 adverbs = b3 badge (full marks). If student misses 1 element, let them retry for 30 seconds targeting the missing category. Common gap: using "going to" instead of "will" for predictions ("we're going to increase" — implies a plan already made, not a forward-looking prediction from evidence).
§6
Source Analysis — Forecast Email in the Wild
Business forecast email using will + adverbs · Identify promise vs prediction · Rate adverb confidence level
⏱ 8 MIN
📧 Email — Q4 Business Forecast to Leadership
Subject: Q4 Forecast Summary — October Update

Hi Team,

Following our planning session, here is a brief summary of what we expect for Q4.

Revenue will probably grow by 8–12% if the enterprise deals close on schedule. The new product will definitely generate interest at the November conference — three clients have already requested demos. Operating costs will likely remain flat, though logistics will possibly become more expensive in December due to seasonal demand.

On the commitment side: I'll send weekly pipeline updates every Monday. We won't let any client go more than 5 days without a response. I'll personally follow up with the two at-risk accounts this week.

If you need more detail on any area, I'll prepare a full breakdown within 24 hours.

Best,
Santiago Reyes · Commercial Director
prediction will promise will adverb confidence
💡 For each highlighted will: (1) Is it a promise or prediction? (2) Which adverb signals the highest confidence? Which the lowest? (3) Why does the email switch between adverbs rather than using "definitely" for everything?
Analysis Key
"will probably grow" — prediction, moderate confidence (condition attached: "if deals close")
"will definitely generate" — prediction, high confidence (evidence: 3 demo requests already)
"will likely remain flat" — prediction, moderate-high confidence
"will possibly become more expensive" — prediction, low confidence (uncertain seasonal factor)
"I'll send weekly updates" — promise (binding commitment to a recurring action)
"won't let any client" — promise (negative commitment — reassurance)
"I'll personally follow up" — promise (personal accountability)
"I'll prepare a breakdown" — promise (conditional offer)
Discussion Questions
1. If Santiago used "definitely" for every prediction — would the email sound more or less professional? Why? (Less: using "definitely" without evidence sounds overconfident and loses credibility.)
2. The promises use no adverb. Why? (Promises are 100% commitments by definition — adding "probably" would weaken them: "I'll probably send updates" is not a promise.)
3. What one extra will + adverb sentence could you add to strengthen this forecast?
💡 Layer 1 — Key insight: The adverb confidence scale (possibly < probably < likely < certainly < definitely) is the S2B core takeaway. Students who can match adverb to evidence strength are operating at near-B1 level. This is the metalinguistic skill that separates fluent A2 from ceiling-breaking A2. Spend 3 minutes on Discussion Question 1 — it encodes the scale permanently.
🔥 Layer 3 — Output extension: Challenge: "Rewrite this email for your own company's Q4. Keep the same structure — 4 predictions with adverbs, then 2 promises without." Students who complete this outside class have produced a real professional document using target language — the highest-value retention activity available.

🎯 This week at work
3 Will + Adverb Phrases You Can Use Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"This will definitely improve our results."
The confident prediction. Use when you have clear evidence or data behind you — "definitely" signals you are not guessing. Replaces vague statements with precise, professional will prediction.
PHRASE 2
"Revenue will probably increase if we [action]."
The conditional forecast. Use in meetings or reports to show analytical thinking: "probably" + condition = professional hedging. Sounds more credible than "definitely" when the outcome depends on a variable.
PHRASE 3
"I'll make sure this won't happen again."
The client recovery promise. Combines a forward commitment (I'll make sure) with a negative guarantee (won't happen again). Two will forms in one sentence — powerful, professional, and reassuring.
