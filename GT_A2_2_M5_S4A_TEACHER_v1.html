§1
Warm-Up — Would you change your job?
Activate work vocabulary · personal stakes · connect to real decisions · 5–8 min
⏱ 5–8 MIN

The full comparatives toolkit: more + adj + than · much/far/slightly/significantly · the most + adj · adverbs after action verbs. Today: highest-stakes domain — your career.

💼 Would you change your job right now? What would need to be better: salary, location, growth, culture, flexibility? Which factor is the most important for you?
💡 This is a personal disclosure question — let students answer freely. Collect real vocabulary on the board: sueldo, crecimiento, horario, ambiente. Translate and use in §2.
📋 Two offers, one decision: Job A: $22,000 MXN, 5 min from home, no growth plan. Job B: $18,000 MXN, 45 min commute, excellent career development. Which would you choose and why?
💡 No grammar correction yet. Let students argue with their real values. Collect their reasoning phrases — they will reappear in the reading and listening as professional language.
⚖️ Remote vs Office: Is working from home better than working in an office? Which is more productive? Which is more social? Which is best for your career long-term?
💡 Strong debate topic — students have strong opinions. Use the Opinion Meter in §SP to revisit this. Preview: "We will come back to this question at the end of class with the grammar you learn today."
👩‍💼 Andrea's situation: Andrea has two job offers — her current company (stable, familiar) vs a new multinational (higher salary, more responsibility, less job security). What should she do?
💡 Plant the session scenario. Andrea is a career changer — this decision is the emotional anchor for S4. Revisit it in every section. Her choice at the end of S4B becomes the S5 exit task setup.
💼
Quick Win — 20 seconds
Say this out loud: "The new job is more challenging than my current one, but it offers significantly better career development and a much higher salary."
3 comparatives + 2 intensifiers in one sentence. Today you build the vocabulary to say this about any career decision — your own included.
⚡ Teacher Note — Warm-Up
S4 is the highest-stakes session in M5: students are comparing their own career reality. Make it personal from the first minute. Say: "This is not a grammar exercise. This is a conversation that every professional in Mexico has at some point. Today you learn to have it in English." Card 1 and Card 2 often produce the most authentic language in the whole module — note student phrases and quote them back in §4 as examples. The Andrea scenario (Card 4) anchors reading, listening and grammar — return to it constantly. Layer 3 (strongest students): "Andrea has a third option — starting her own business. How does that change the comparison?"

⚡ Teacher Note — Vocabulary
False friend: "salary" is not "salario exacto" in English usage — it always means monthly/annual fixed pay. "Benefits" is plural in English — always say "benefits" not "benefit" when referring to the package. Drill: "My company offers good _____." (benefits). "Work-life balance" is a compound noun — teach it as a chunk. Pronunciation trap: "flexibility" — 5 syllables, stress on 3rd: flex-i-BIL-i-ty. Personal connection drill: teacher models — "For me, the most important factor is flexibility. What about you?" Each student answers in one sentence using vocabulary from the grid.

§3
Colloquial — Career Decision Language
4 phrases for real job conversations · 5 min
⏱ 5 MIN
"It is a step up."
Use when a new job or role is clearly better or more senior than the current one. Implies career advancement — not just a change.
"The multinational offer is definitely a step up — more responsibility and a significantly higher salary."
"The pros outweigh the cons."
Use after listing advantages and disadvantages. Signals your final decision: the advantages are more important than the disadvantages.
"The commute is longer, but the pros outweigh the cons — the salary and growth are far better."
"It is a better fit for me."
Use when a role matches your values, skills, or goals better than the other option — more personal than "better job." Shows self-awareness.
"The startup culture is a much better fit for me than a large corporation with strict hierarchy."
"It is not just about the money."
Use to signal that you value non-salary factors: growth, culture, balance, purpose. Shows professional maturity in any job interview or career conversation.
"The lower salary is acceptable because it is not just about the money — the career development is significantly more valuable."
⚡ Teacher Note — Colloquial
These 4 phrases complete the module colloquial arc: S1 = product decisions ("no-brainer", "pros outweigh the cons"), S2 = people decisions ("tough call", "ticks all the boxes"), S3 = travel decisions ("not worth the hassle"), S4 = career decisions. "It is not just about the money" is the most powerful of the session — it shows interview-level self-awareness. Drill: teacher gives a scenario ("You have two offers: one pays $5,000 more but has worse culture"), student must respond with one colloquial phrase + one comparative. 2 minutes, everyone participates once.

🚨 Real Work Situation
Andrea needs to explain her decision to a friend in English. She wants to say: "El trabajo nuevo paga mucho más y el crecimiento es significativamente mejor, pero la distancia es bastante más larga y no es tan seguro como mi trabajo actual." She knows the basic structures but needs to combine ALL of them: short comparatives, long comparatives, intensifiers, as...as, and superlatives in one connected explanation.
You think: "I know each structure separately, but how do I combine them naturally? And which one do I use when — the -er form, more, the most, as...as, or an intensifier?"
🔧 The Full Toolkit — Choosing the Right Structure
Short adj (≤2 syllables) → -er than: higher, better, shorter, closer, longer
Long adj (≥3 syllables) → more...than: more flexible, more collaborative, more demanding
Irregular → memorize: good → better → the best · bad → worse → the worst · far → further
Size of difference → intensifier: much / far (big) · slightly / a little (small) · significantly (clear)
Equal comparison → as + adj + as: "The new job is not as secure as my current one."
Best of all options → superlative: "Of all the offers, this is the most attractive."
StructureWhen to useCareer Example
higher / closer / betterShort/irregular adjThe salary is higher than my current one. The commute is worse than before.
more flexible / more collaborativeLong adj (≥3 syllables)The culture is more collaborative than my current company.
much / far / significantly + compBig or measurable differenceThe salary is significantly higher than my current one. Growth is far better.
slightly / a little + compSmall differenceThe hours are slightly longer than my current schedule.
not as + adj + asEqual/less than comparisonThe new job is not as secure as my current position.
the most / the best / the worstBest or worst of 3+Of all the offers I have received, this is the most attractive.
⚠️ Common Errors — Job Comparison:
✗ "The salary is more higher than my current one." → ✓ higher than (never "more" + "-er")
✗ "This job is more better for my career." → ✓ better (irregular — never "more better")
✗ "It is the most best offer I have received." → ✓ the best (never "most" + superlative)
✗ "The culture is more good than my current company." → ✓ better than (use irregular form)
⚡ ACTIVITY A — Choose the Correct Comparative Structure

Use the Andrea job table above. Choose the correct form — think about the adjective type and size of difference before answering.

1. The multinational salary ($31K) vs current ($22K) — "The new salary is _____ my current one."
2. The multinational culture (collaborative, modern) vs current (traditional, formal) — "The new workplace culture is _____ my current company."
3. New commute = 55 min. Current commute = 5 min. "My commute to the new job is _____ my current commute."
4. "The new job is _____ my current position in terms of job security." (security = not as good)
5. "Of all the job offers Andrea has received this year, the multinational is _____."

Reading — Andrea's Career Decision
Color-coded grammar · 5 True/False · 8–10 min

Andrea Soto
Career Changer · A2.2 Recurring Character
LinkedIn message to a mentor
M5 · S4A
🔑 Comparatives + intensifiers (orange) · Superlatives (cyan) · as...as (green)

Hi Carlos, I need your advice. I have two job offers and I have to decide by Friday.

My current company is offering me a small raise — but the role is not as challenging as I need at this stage of my career. The salary would be slightly higher than now, but the growth plan is still much weaker than what I was promised two years ago.

The multinational offer is significantly more attractive in almost every way. The salary is far higher than my current one — almost 40% more. The culture is more collaborative and more international than anything I have experienced before. And the career track is faster than the traditional model.

The only disadvantage is the commute — it is much longer than my current one, and the position is not as secure as a permanent contract. But honestly, I think the pros outweigh the cons. It is a step up, and it is not just about the money. Of all the opportunities I have had in the last five years, this is the most exciting and the most aligned with my goals.

What do you think? Should I take it?

Andrea

Comprehension — True or False?

1. Andrea's current company is offering her a significant salary increase.
2. The multinational salary is approximately 40% higher than her current salary.
3. Andrea thinks the commute is the most important disadvantage of the new offer.
4. Andrea uses the phrases "a step up" and "not just about the money" in her message.
5. Andrea has already decided to accept the multinational offer.
🗣️
Micro-Speaking
From memory: summarize Andrea's comparison in 2 sentences using one intensifier and one superlative. Example: "Andrea thinks the multinational is far more attractive than her current offer because the salary is significantly higher and the culture is the most collaborative she has experienced." 30 seconds — go!
⚡ Teacher Note — Reading
This is the richest reading text in M5 — it integrates all 6 structures in one authentic professional message. Say: "Count the comparatives. How many can you find?" (Answer: not as challenging as, slightly higher, much weaker, significantly more attractive, far higher, more collaborative, more international, faster, much longer, not as secure as, the most exciting, the most aligned). 12 comparison structures in one paragraph. For strong students: ask them to write a 3-sentence reply from Carlos using at least 3 different comparison structures.

§SP
Speaking Practice — Your Career, Your Choice
6 guided questions · Pair & group · personal stakes · 10–12 min
⏱ 10–12 MIN
T-S Question 1 — Recall
What were Andrea's 3 main reasons for choosing the multinational? Use a different comparison structure for each reason.
Grammar hint: "The salary is significantly higher... the culture is far more collaborative... and it is the most exciting opportunity she has had."
S-S Question 2 — Apply
Compare your current job with a previous job or an ideal job. Use at least 4 different comparison structures. Which is better overall and why?
Grammar hint: Use: -er than, more...than, not as...as, significantly/far, the most. Try to use all 5 in your answer.
S-S Question 3 — Debate
Student A says salary is the most important factor in a job. Student B says workplace culture is more important than money. Debate for 90 seconds using comparatives. Who makes the strongest argument?
Grammar hint: "Salary is far more important than culture because... / Culture is significantly more valuable than a higher salary because..."
Class Question 4 — Ranking
Class ranks today's 10 vocabulary words from most to least important for a job decision. Use superlatives to justify the top 3 and comparatives to justify the rankings.
Grammar hint: "Career growth is the most important factor because... Salary is more important than benefits because..."
T-S Question 5 — Interview
Teacher plays the interviewer. Student explains why they want to leave their current company. Must use at least 2 comparatives, 1 superlative, and 1 colloquial phrase from today. This is real job interview language.
Grammar hint: "I am looking for a role with significantly better career growth and a more collaborative workplace culture. It is a step up from my current position."
Class Question 6 — Challenge
Each student designs their ideal job using 5 superlatives. Then compare two students' ideal jobs with the class using comparatives. "Luis's ideal job is more flexible than Ana's, but Ana's has a higher salary."
Grammar hint: "My ideal job has the best work-life balance, the most flexible hours, the highest salary..." Then compare two descriptions.
⚡ Teacher Note — Speaking
Question 5 (interview simulation) is the most transferable activity in M5 — reserve 5 minutes for it. Tell students: "You will use this exact language in a real interview. The question is: why do you want to leave your current job? Today you practice the answer." For strong students: add a follow-up question from the interviewer: "What are your biggest strengths compared to other candidates?" — forces superlatives under pressure. Question 6 (ideal job comparison) works especially well with 2-3 students who have shared it first — use their real answers as the content for the class comparison. This makes the grammar deeply personal.
🎯 This week, use these 3 phrases at work:
Career comparison language from S4A — ready for job interviews, performance reviews and professional conversations.
"I am looking for a role with significantly better career growth."
The professional way to say "I want a better job." Use in interviews, networking conversations, and LinkedIn messages. Add your own adjective: more flexible, more collaborative, more international.
"The pros outweigh the cons."
Use after listing advantages and disadvantages of any decision. Works for job offers, project proposals, supplier comparisons, and any professional recommendation. Shows structured analytical thinking.

☐ Choosing the right structure
Short adj → -er than. Long adj → more...than. Irregular: good→better, bad→worse. Intensifier: much/far/significantly/slightly. Equal/less: not as...as. Best of all: the most.
☐ Double error rule
Never "more better," never "more higher," never "the most best." One form per comparison. If you use -er, no "more." If you use "the most," no "-est."
☐ Career vocabulary in context
salary · benefits · commute · flexibility · career growth · work-life balance · job security · workplace culture · responsibility · promotion — all usable in real job interviews.
