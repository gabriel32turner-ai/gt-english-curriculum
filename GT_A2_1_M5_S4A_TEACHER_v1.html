§1
Warm-Up: Client Crisis Mode
React fast with the right Will function
8 MIN
Layer 1 — Activate: Drop three client-crisis scenarios cold. Students must react in 5 seconds using Will. Listen for spontaneous decisions (I'll), promises (won't), and reflexive offers.
Layer 2 — Watch for: Students who default to "I'm going to" for spontaneous moments. Correct gently — "Right now, in this moment" = Will.
Layer 3 — Production target: Every student produces at least one Will sentence per scenario, no Spanish filler.
Crisis A. A key client just emailed: "The product I received is wrong." You see the email right now. React in one sentence using Will.
Crisis B. Your team accidentally sent a wrong invoice. The client calls to complain. Make two commitments using Will (one offer + one promise).
Crisis C. A new client asks: "Will this project really finish on time?" Make a prediction using Will + adverb (probably / definitely).
0:90
Quick Win
Each student must respond aloud within 5 seconds of the prompt. Slow responses = re-do. We're training reflex Will.

Grammar Integration: Will's Full System
All 5 Will functions + Going-To contrast — one master reference
15 MIN
Layer 1 — Anchor: Walk through the master table once. Highlight that Will = 5 functions but Going-To = 2 (pre-planned + evidence prediction).
Layer 2 — Diagnose: The 3 errors below are the typical A2.1 mistakes Mexican students make. Each error corresponds to a different function confusion. Fix on the board.
Layer 3 — Output: Students sort 8 sentences by function via drag-and-drop. Aim for 6/8 minimum before moving on.
FunctionFormExample
1. Spontaneous decisionWill / I'll"I'll send you the report right now."
2. OfferWill / I'll"I'll handle that for you."
3. Promise (positive / negative)Will / won't"We'll deliver on time." / "It won't happen again."
4. Prediction (opinion)Will + adverb"It'll probably succeed."
5. Polite requestWill you...?"Will you confirm by Friday?"
6. Pre-planned actionGoing to"We're going to launch the campaign in March (scheduled)."
7. Prediction (evidence)Going to"Look at these numbers — it's going to be a record quarter."
Which form is correct? "Your package is late. Don't worry — _____ check the system right now."
Correct. "Right now" = spontaneous decision = Will.
❌ Common error 1: "I'm going to call you back in five minutes." (just decided)
❌ Common error 2: "Will we launch the new portal next Monday?" (already on the calendar)
❌ Common error 3: "I think it's going to be fine." (pure opinion, no evidence)
✓ Correct 1: "I'll call you back in five minutes." (spontaneous)
✓ Correct 2: "Are we going to launch the new portal next Monday?" (pre-planned)
✓ Correct 3: "I think it'll be fine." (opinion-based prediction)

Client Call Framework: A · O · P · P
Four steps for every difficult client conversation
10 MIN
Layer 1 — The model: Acknowledge → Offer → Promise → Predict. Drill this order. It's how senior managers handle 90% of escalations.
Layer 2 — Watch for: Students who skip Acknowledge and jump straight to Offer. The client feels unheard. Force the pause.
Layer 3 — Production: Each student plans a 60-sec response using the framework before they speak. No improvising in this step.
1
Acknowledge
"I understand you're frustrated."
2
Offer (Will)
"I'll personally review your account today."
3
Promise (Will + won't)
"I promise we won't let it happen again."
4
Predict (Will + adverb)
"It'll definitely be resolved within 48 hours."

Planning grid (60 seconds — write before speaking)

Scenario
An angry client just emailed: their order arrived damaged AND late. You're the account manager.
Plan ONE sentence per framework step. Then speak the full response in 60 seconds.

§5
Role-Play: Live Client Calls
3 rounds, escalating difficulty
18 MIN
Layer 1 — Scaffold: Round 1 is friendly (information request). Round 2 is mildly stressed (complaint). Round 3 is angry (escalation). Match difficulty to student readiness.
Layer 2 — Force-functions: In Round 2, student must use at least 1 of each — offer, promise, prediction. Track on the rubric.
Layer 3 — Recovery: If a student blanks, freeze the call, give 5 seconds to plan, restart from the previous turn. No Spanish.
Round 1 · Friendly inquiry
The client asks: "When will the new dashboard be ready?" Respond with a Will offer + a Will prediction.
Target sentence: "I'll check the timeline now — it'll probably be ready by mid-March."
Round 2 · Complaint
The client says: "I haven't received my invoice — it's been a week." Use ALL 5 Will functions in your response.
Targets: I'll [offer] / I'll [spontaneous] / I promise we'll [promise] / It won't happen [negative promise] / it'll definitely [prediction].
Round 3 · Escalation (3-min solo)
The client is angry: "This is the third time. I'm considering switching providers." De-escalate using the A·O·P·P framework.
Don't argue. Acknowledge first. Then commit using Will. End with a prediction grounded in a planned (Going-To) action.

Isabel Torres · Customer Success Manager · Difficult Client
~ 215 words · 95 sec · Sarah (en-US) @ 0.82×
"Mr. Fuentes, thank you for calling. I understand you're frustrated, and I want to address your concerns right now. First — I'll personally review your account today. I promise I won't leave the office until I have a full picture of what happened. Second, our technical team is going to send you a detailed report by Thursday — that was already scheduled. But beyond that, I'll make sure you receive a direct call from our Director by tomorrow morning. Now, about the timeline issue. It'll definitely be resolved within 48 hours. I can assure you we won't repeat this mistake. Will there be any service credit? Yes, there will. I'll arrange that immediately. What about next month? I think the process will run much more smoothly — we're going to implement new tracking software next week, and it will probably eliminate these delays completely. I know this has been frustrating, Mr. Fuentes. But I'm confident this partnership will improve significantly. I'll send you a summary email in the next hour. You have my word."
