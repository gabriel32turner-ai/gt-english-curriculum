Hot Take — "Change of Plans, Always."
React fast · Defend your position with going to · 60 seconds, no preparation
⏱ 5 MIN
Context: The Monday morning meeting just got rescheduled — again. Some people think this is normal. Others think it signals a deeper problem.
"Rescheduling a meeting is always a sign of bad planning. If you were going to meet, you should meet. Agree or disagree — 60 seconds."

💡 Pedagogical: This is a B-session — no new grammar. Listen for going to in both affirmative and negative forms. Common errors: "I going to" (missing am), "we reschedule it" (missing going to). Mark every instance silently; debrief after the 60 seconds.
🔥 Engagement boost: If the student agrees, have them defend rescheduling with one real example from their own job. If they disagree, ask: "Was there ever a time when rescheduling was the right call?" Forces authentic going to production.
§2
Scheduling Debate — Stick to the Plan vs Change It
Two positions · Use going to + rescheduling vocabulary · Argue your side
⏱ 10 MIN
Scenario: Valeria's team had a critical supplier review scheduled for this Thursday at 2 PM. Two things happened: (1) The main supplier can't attend. (2) The project is already behind schedule. The team must decide: keep Thursday's meeting anyway, or reschedule it for next week?
Side A — Keep Thursday
"We're going to keep the meeting on Thursday. We're going to discuss what we can without the supplier. We're not going to fall further behind schedule."
  • "We're going to lose more time if we reschedule again."
  • "The team is going to review the parts we can control."
  • "If we postpone now, we're going to push the launch back a week."
Side B — Reschedule to Next Week
"We're going to reschedule to Tuesday. We're not going to make decisions without the supplier. We're going to have a more productive meeting with everyone present."
  • "We're going to get better results if we wait."
  • "The supplier is going to bring the numbers we need."
  • "We're going to push it back just 4 days — that's not a disaster."
🎭 Engagement: Assign sides. 3 minutes per side, then 2 minutes of rebuttals. Coach students to respond directly: "You said you're going to keep Thursday, but without the supplier we're not going to get the numbers we need." This creates natural back-and-forth production.

§3
Relay Changes — Pass It On
Chain announcements · "Originally going to… now going to…" · Each student adds a new change
⏱ 8 MIN
How it works: Student 1 announces a schedule change. Student 2 repeats a short summary and adds a new change. Each student must use the pattern: "Originally we were going to [plan], but now we're going to [new plan] because [reason]."
"Originally we were going to meet on Tuesday, but now we're going to meet on Thursday because the director is travelling."
Starter Scenario
Card 0 — Teacher reads this aloud
Valeria's team had a full week of plans. Now everything has changed. Student 1, you start with the first change below.
Relay Round 1 — Student A
The Monday supplier review was going to be in-person. Now it's online because the supplier is in Guadalajara.
Model: "Originally we were going to have the supplier review in person on Monday, but now we're going to do it online because the supplier is in Guadalajara."
Relay Round 2 — Student B
The Wednesday budget presentation was going to be at 10 AM. Now it's at 2 PM because the finance director has a conflict.
Model: "...and we were going to present the budget at 10 AM on Wednesday, but now we're going to present at 2 PM because the finance director is busy."
Relay Round 3 — Student A
The Thursday training was going to be 3 hours. Now it's going to be 1 hour because the trainer cancelled half the agenda.
Model: "...and the training was going to be 3 hours on Thursday, but now we're going to have 1 hour because the trainer cancelled part of it."
Relay Round 4 — Student B
The Friday lunch with the client was going to be at La Cantina. Now it's going to be at the office because the restaurant is closed.
Model: "...and we were going to have lunch at La Cantina on Friday, but now we're going to eat at the office because the restaurant is closed this week."
Relay Round 5 — Free Round
Each student invents ONE real change from their own work this week. Must use "Originally we were going to… but now we're going to… because…"
Teacher: Encourage them to use a real situation. This is where authentic production happens. Coach rescheduling vocabulary: reschedule, postpone, push back, move, cancel.
💡 Layer 1 support: If the student struggles with the "originally" structure, give the formula on a sticky note: Originally + subject + was/were going to + verb / but now + subject + am/is/are going to + verb + because + reason.
🔥 Layer 3 push: Challenge advanced students to add a Simple Present fixed schedule item to contrast: "Originally we were going to meet at 10, but the flight lands at 9:30 — so now we're going to start at 11."

§6
Source Analysis — Going To vs Simple Present in the Wild
Real email excerpt · Identify going to vs Simple Present · Explain the contrast
⏱ 8 MIN
📧 Email — Schedule Change Announcement
Hi Daniel, heads up — a few changes this week.

We're going to reschedule the supplier review to Thursday. The supplier can't make it on Tuesday. I'm going to send the updated agenda by tomorrow morning.

Also, the safety briefing starts at 8 AM sharp on Wednesday — that part doesn't change. But we're going to move the afternoon session to the new conference room because our usual room is being repainted.

The client call isn't going to happen on Friday — they asked to postpone. We're going to reschedule for next Monday instead.

I'll keep you posted. — Valeria
🤔 Analysis questions: (1) Find every "going to" in the email. How many? (2) Find the Simple Present item — why does Valeria use Simple Present for that one? (3) Find "isn't going to" — what does this tell you about Valeria's relationship to the client call? (4) Which rescheduling words appear? List them.
💬 WhatsApp — Team Chat
Valeria: Change of plans — we're going to push back Thursday's session to Friday.
Carlos: Got it. What time?
Valeria: 10 AM. The room opens at 9:30 (fixed). We're going to start at 10 sharp.
Carlos: Is the supplier going to confirm today?
Valeria: Yes. I'm going to call them in 10 minutes.
Carlos: 👍
🤔 (1) "The room opens at 9:30" — why Simple Present? (2) "I'm going to call them" — why going to? (3) "Is the supplier going to confirm?" — form this correctly in your head: what is the structure? (4) What does "going to start at 10 sharp" + the Simple Present "opens at 9:30" tell us about the difference in planning?
🔎 Contrast Summary
Going to — Personal Decision
Valeria decided this. She can change it. "We're going to reschedule." "I'm going to call." "We're going to push back."
Simple Present — Fixed Schedule
Not Valeria's decision. Set by a system or institution. "The briefing starts at 8." "The room opens at 9:30." She can't change these.
💡 Pedagogical: The metacognitive insight here is crucial. Students need to feel the difference: going to = my agency, Simple Present = someone else's system. Ask: "Which things in your workday are going to (you decide) vs Simple Present (already fixed)?" This is the real-world hook.

🎯 This Week at Work
3 Phrases You Can Use Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"Change of plans — we're going to [new plan]."
The fastest way to announce a change in English. Two seconds, professional, clear. Use it in WhatsApp, standup, or Slack instead of writing a paragraph.
PHRASE 2
"We're going to push it back to [day/time]."
"Push back" is the most natural business English phrase for delays. More natural than "postpone" in conversation. Use when a deadline or meeting moves later.
PHRASE 3
"Originally we were going to [plan], but now we're going to [new plan]."
The complete schedule-change sentence. Shows you can explain the change and the new direction in one breath. This is what project managers say every single week.
