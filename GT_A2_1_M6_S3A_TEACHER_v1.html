§2 Speaking — Read, Analyze, Respond 8–10 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — EMAIL ANALYSIS

Show the annotated email below. Ask students to identify which sentences are past, which are going to, and which are will. This analysis before production is a key A2.1 metacognitive skill — they're not just reading, they're decoding grammar in context.

FROMvaleria.torres@solarismfg.com
TOdaniel.ochoa@solarismfg.com
SUBJApex Project — Weekly Update
Simple Past
Going To
Will

Hi Daniel,

I hope you are doing well. I wanted to give you a quick update on the Apex project.

Last week, we completed the quality check and the supplier delivered all the components on time. We also had a short meeting with the Apex coordinator on Wednesday — it went very well.

Looking ahead, we are going to start the new production cycle on the 15th. We are also going to present the Q1 results to the Guadalajara team next month.

In terms of next steps: I will send you the full report by Thursday. I will also call the Apex coordinator to confirm the delivery schedule.

Please let me know if you have any questions. I will keep you posted on any changes.

Best regards,
Valeria Torres · Operations Coordinator · Solaris Manufacturing

T-S
Look at the email. Count: How many Simple Past sentences? How many going to? How many will? Why do you think the email follows that order (past → plan → will)?

4 Simple Past / 2 Going To / 3 Will. The order mirrors time: what already happened → what is decided → what I commit to right now. This structure makes the reader trust you — they see you tracked the past, you have a plan, and you take action.

S-S
Partner A reads one sentence from the email — but changes the tense incorrectly. Partner B must catch the error and correct it. Example: "We will complete the quality check last week." → Partner B: "No — past: We completed the quality check last week."

Key error patterns to drill: Will → Past when there's a time marker (last week/yesterday). Going to → Will for spontaneous decisions. Past → Going to for future plans. The student correcting earns the point, not the one making errors.

Class
Class co-write: The teacher writes the subject line on the board. Students contribute one sentence each — rotating through tenses. Build a 6-sentence email together. Teacher highlights the tense of each contribution in color.

Subject → Opening (past context, 2 sentences) → Plans (going to, 1–2 sentences) → Commitments (will, 1–2 sentences) → Closing. Total: 6–8 sentences. This is the canonical M6 email structure students will use in S3B and the Exit Project.

§4 Colloquial — Email Sign-Off Phrases 5 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — REGISTER AWARENESS

These phrases close emails naturally. The biggest error at A2.1 level is translating Spanish closings literally: "Until next time" or "I send you greetings". Today we replace those with real English closings. After drilling, ask students to write these on a sticky note for their desk.

"Please find [X] attached."
The standard way to reference a file in a professional email. Never "I attach" or "I send you a file."
"Please find the updated contract attached to this email."
"Do not hesitate to contact me."
The professional way to say "you can message me anytime." Much more natural than "If you need something, write me."
"If you have any questions, do not hesitate to contact me."
"Best regards," / "Kind regards,"
Standard professional sign-offs. "Best regards" = formal. "Kind regards" = slightly warmer. Never "Regards and hugs" (direct translation from Spanish).
"Best regards, / Valeria Torres · Operations Coordinator"
"I look forward to hearing from you."
Closes an email while inviting a response. Polite and professional. Note: "look forward TO" + gerund — never "look forward for."
"I look forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience."
💰 RETENTION + UPSELL NOTE

At this point in M6, students are building a portable business English toolkit. These 4 closing phrases = immediate professional upgrade. Say: "Use one of these today — in a real email — before our next class." Follow up in S3B warm-up: who used it?

§5 Grammar — The Three-Tense Email Structure 12–15 min
Real situation: You need to email your international client about a project. You have three things to communicate:

(1) What happened last week — Simple Past
(2) What your team is planning — Going To
(3) What you personally commit to doing today — Will

The tool: a three-paragraph email structure. One tense per paragraph. Clear. Professional. Trustworthy.
Email ParagraphTensePurposeTypical opener
Para 1 — ContextSimple PastReport what happened"Last week / Last Tuesday / Yesterday we..."
Para 2 — PlansGoing ToDescribe decided future steps"Next month / On Friday / This quarter we're going to..."
Para 3 — ActionsWillMake personal commitments"I will / I'll send / I'll call / I'll copy you..."
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — CONNECT TO EXIT PROJECT

This table is the skeleton of the A2.1 Exit Project email. Students who master this three-paragraph structure in S3A are 80% ready for their final assessment. Tell them: "This exact structure is what you'll use for the Exit Project. We're building it now."

ACTIVITY A — Complete the Email: Choose the Correct Form

Complete Valeria's email to a client in Monterrey.

1. "Last Monday, we ___ (receive) the new components from your warehouse."
2. "We ___ (not / finish) the installation on time because of a power issue."
3. "Next week, our engineers ___ (visit) your facility to complete the installation." (already scheduled)
4. "I ___ (send) you the technician schedule by end of today." (spontaneous commitment)
5. "I ___ (copy) the project manager on this email so he is informed." (right now decision)

🗣️ Quick: Say the three email opening lines out loud — one for each tense. No looking at the table.

ACTIVITY B — DRAG TO REORDER: Rebuild Valeria's Email

These email paragraphs are out of order. Drag them into the correct sequence: Past context → Going To plans → Will commitments → Closing.

  • ⠿I will send you the final report by Thursday morning. I will also copy Daniel Ochoa on this email.
  • ⠿Last week, we completed the quality audit and identified two areas for process improvement.
  • ⠿Thank you for your time. I look forward to hearing from you. Best regards, Valeria Torres.
  • ⠿Next month, we are going to implement the new procedures and train the production team.

§6 Listening — A Real Email Read Aloud 8–10 min

For an extra challenge: play the audio once straight through. Then play it sentence by sentence and have students type or write each sentence. Valeria's Email to Client — Lumina Finance, León
A2.1 · M6 · S3A · Voice: Valeria (Mexican accent) · Speed: 0.82
Dear Mr. Reyes, I hope this email finds you well. I am writing to give you an update on the Solaris integration project. Last month was a productive period for our team. We completed the first phase of the system integration and delivered the technical documentation on schedule. We also had a review meeting with your IT team on the 12th — the feedback was very positive. However, we identified one issue with the data migration process, and we resolved it by the end of the week. Looking ahead, next quarter we are going to begin the second phase of the integration. We are also going to schedule a training session for your staff in León — we want to make sure everyone is ready before we go live. The training is going to happen in March, and we will send you the agenda two weeks in advance. In terms of immediate actions: I will send you the Phase 1 completion report by this Friday. I will also forward the training materials to your team coordinator so she can review them in advance. If anything needs to be adjusted, please do not hesitate to contact me. I look forward to hearing from you. Best regards, Valeria Torres · Senior Operations Coordinator · Solaris Manufacturing

TRUE / FALSE

1. The team delivered the technical documentation late.
2. The data migration issue was resolved before the end of the week.
3. The training for the León staff is going to happen in April.
4. Valeria will send the Phase 1 report by Friday.
5. Valeria will also forward the training materials to the team coordinator.

§SP Speaking Practice — Your Email Out Loud 10–12 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — READ ALOUD DRILL

A powerful exercise: have students read their email drafts from the grammar timer activity OUT LOUD to their partner. The partner listens and identifies which tense each sentence uses. This reinforces both writing and listening at the same time. High-value, low-prep, 100% student-driven.

QUESTION 1 · T-S
Imagine you're reading your own work email to me right now. Start: "Last week I..." — describe 2 completed tasks using Simple Past.
No notes. From memory. Use: I completed / I reviewed / I attended / I submitted / I called...
QUESTION 2 · T-S
Continue the email: "Next [week/month/quarter] we are going to..." — describe a real team plan.
Must be a REAL plan. Going to = already decided. Add a time expression.
QUESTION 3 · S-S
Close the email with 2 will commitments to your partner. They must decide: is each one realistic? Would they trust this email?
Partner gives thumbs up (believable) or thumbs down (too vague). Discuss why.
QUESTION 4 · S-S
Now use the closing phrases from §4: end your email with "Please find X attached" + "I look forward to hearing from you." Say it naturally, not robotically.
Practice the TONE — professional emails sound calm, clear, and confident. Not nervous.
QUESTION 5 · Class
Hot Seat: one student reads their 3-sentence email draft to the class. Class votes: which paragraph is strongest — past, plan, or commitment? Why?
Teacher moderates. No grammar police — only encouragement and one targeted suggestion per student.
🎯 3 Phrases for This Week In the next email you send at work
"Please find [X] attached."
Replace "I attach" or "I send you." Use in the first email you write in English this week. Instant professional upgrade.
"I look forward to hearing from you."
End every professional email with this. It's standard, it's warm, and it sounds completely natural to native speakers.
"I'm writing to follow up on..."
Open any follow-up email with this. More professional than "I write because" or "With this email I want to..."
