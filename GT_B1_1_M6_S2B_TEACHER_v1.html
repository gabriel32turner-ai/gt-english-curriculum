Hot Take — Education & Employment

10 min
Hot Take · Pick a side · PP + FC required · 90 seconds on the clock
Today's Hot Take
"Universities produce graduates who are unemployable — and the companies that hire them know it before the interview is over."
✅ Agree
Degrees have become credentials, not competencies. Employers spend months training new graduates in skills school should have taught. The gap between what universities teach and what industries need has widened every decade.
✗ Disagree
Universities build critical thinking, adaptability, and intellectual foundations. A degree is not a job manual — it is a mind-building exercise. The best graduates apply what they learned; the gap is in application, not education.
T→S Round 1 — Your Hot Take (90 sec)

State your position using an opinion phrase opener + PP evidence (what has happened) + FC consequence (what will happen if nothing changes). Vocabulary required: curriculum, gap, skill, application, prepare.

S↔S Round 2 — The Counter (60 sec)

Counter with "That's a good point, however..." then use PP + FC together: "The gap has grown, yes — but if companies invested in graduate training, the transition would be smoother." Alternate. Both must produce at least one PP + one FC in their counter.

Teacher note: The "unemployable graduates" statement is deliberately extreme — most students will know it is partially true and partially unfair. That productive tension is the point. Students who have hired people (team leaders, coordinators) will have particularly strong opinions based on real experience. Let those voices lead Round 1. The most authentic production of the session often comes in the first 5 minutes when the topic is personal.

Pitch / Debate — The Education Reform Proposal

15 min
Pitch · Reform the system · PP evidence + double FC argument · Professional English
T→S Pitch Round 1 — The Reform Proposal (3 min)
You are advising the Secretary of Education. Propose ONE concrete change to the national curriculum that would close the skills gap. Structure: (1) What the system has failed to do — PP evidence — (2) What will happen if nothing changes — FC warning — (3) Your specific proposal — (4) What will happen if it is implemented — FC opportunity. Minimum: 2 FC + 1 PP + 2 opinion phrases + 3 vocabulary words.
Vocabulary: curriculum, critical thinking, gap, application, develop, preparation, skill, fail.
S↔S Debate Round 2 — The Traditionalist (90 sec)
Partner plays a conservative education official: "You said the curriculum has failed — but it has also produced engineers, doctors, and scientists for decades. If we change it too quickly, we will lose the academic foundation that makes our universities respected." Pitcher responds: "That's a good point, however the gap has widened precisely because..." Use PP + FC to defend the reform.
Key structure: "I see both sides, but the reality is that..." then PP evidence then FC consequence.
Class Debate Round 3 — Best Reform Vote (2 min)
Class votes on the most realistic and impactful reform proposal. The winner summarizes: "The class has agreed that... / Several colleagues have pointed out that if this change is implemented, graduates will..." Close with: "The system needs to change — and this is how."
Colloquial close: "Practical skills matter just as much."
Teacher note — Reform Proposal: Students in manufacturing and logistics have direct experience of the skills gap — they work with graduates and with self-taught technicians. The best proposals will draw on this: "If we introduced factory simulation projects in engineering programs, students would arrive ready to work." Ground the grammar in real observations. The most convincing pitches are specific, not abstract.
Upsell note: After the best proposal, say: "If you could present this argument in English to your company's HR director or the director of an engineering school — would they listen? After today, you have the language to do exactly that."

Relay — The Education Reform Chain

10 min
Relay · Education Reform Chain · 4 voices · Student → Teacher → Company → Government
🏃 Education Reform Chain — 4 Voices, One Reform
Voice 1
🎓 The Student
Identifies a skill they didn't learn + FC
→
Voice 2
📚 The Teacher
Scales to curriculum level: "If we taught X, students would..."
→
Voice 3
🏢 The Company
States the employment impact: "If graduates arrived with X, we would..."
→
Voice 4
🏙️ The Government
States national consequence with PP + double FC
How it works:
Voice 1: "School didn't prepare me for professional communication. If it had, my first year at work would have been completely different."
Voice 2: "If we introduced real project work in the curriculum, students would develop critical thinking and communication skills together."
Voice 3: "If graduates arrived with those skills, we would reduce our onboarding time significantly — and invest that time in innovation instead."
Voice 4: "Companies have been absorbing the cost of inadequate preparation for decades. If the curriculum changes, productivity will improve. And if productivity improves, GDP will grow — which is the argument that makes governments listen."

Target language: Each voice must include at least one FC. Voice 4 must use PP + double FC. The chain must be logically consistent from individual experience to national policy.
Starter skills for Voice 1 (teacher chooses)
"School never taught me how to present my ideas clearly to a group."
"I learned financial basics on the job, not in any classroom."
"No one taught me how to negotiate, manage conflict, or give feedback."
Teacher note — Relay debrief: Ask Voice 1 after Voice 4 speaks: "Is that the same reform you proposed? Did anything change?" The best relay chains maintain logical continuity. The most common failure: Voice 3 (the Company) breaks the chain by introducing a new topic. Use this as a teachable moment: "In professional English, clarity of argument across multiple speakers requires each person to build on what the previous person said — not restart."

Speed Round — 30 Seconds Each

10 min
Speed Round · 30 seconds · PP + FC + opinion language · No pausing

30 seconds, one student. Each response must include PP + FC + one opinion phrase. Click Done when complete.

Prompt 1
Use "School didn't prepare me for everything" as your opener. Give 2 specific things school failed to teach you and one FC consequence of that failure.
Prompt 2
Describe the skills gap at your company using PP: "We have had to train graduates in... / The gap has cost us..." Then FC: "If universities don't change..."
Prompt 3
Use "I strongly believe that..." to defend one of these: exams, degrees, vocational training, or online learning. PP + FC required.
Prompt 4
Report what Rodrigo said about education in S2A listening. "He said the curriculum had never taught him... / He believed that if the system didn't change..."
Prompt 5
Argue: "Practical skills matter just as much as academic knowledge." Use PP evidence from your own experience + double FC.
Prompt 6
Defend the university system: "I see both sides, but..." Use PP to acknowledge what universities have achieved, then FC for what they could improve.

Solo Challenge — The Education Pitch

15 min
Solo Challenge · 2 minutes · Reform pitch to HR director · PP + double FC + opinion language
🎯 The Challenge Brief
Your company's HR director asks you: "We keep hiring graduates who need 6 months of extra training before they are productive. What do you think the real problem is — and what should change?"

Answer as yourself in 2 minutes. Include:
• What has happened with graduate readiness in your experience — PP evidence
• What will happen if universities don't change — First FC (warning)
• What will happen if they do change — Second FC (opportunity)
• At least 3 opinion phrases used naturally
• At least 4 vocabulary words from S2A
• Close with: "The system needs to change — and here is where we start."

No notes. HR director register: professional, honest, solution-focused.
Teacher Rubric — Click when met
PP Evidence
"Graduates have arrived without... / The gap has been growing..." Correct PP form + honest specifics.
FC Warning
"If universities don't change X, companies will keep spending Y." IF clause = present simple.
FC Opportunity
"If schools introduce Z, graduates will arrive ready to contribute." Logical and specific.
Opinion Phrases
3+ phrases: "I strongly believe / In my opinion / I see both sides / That's a good point / The reality is..."
Vocabulary
4+ S2A words: curriculum, critical thinking, gap, application, develop, preparation, skill, fail.
Fluency & Register
Professional, solution-focused, 90+ seconds, no Spanish filler.
Teacher note — HR Director framing: This scenario is real for every student who has ever interviewed, hired, or been responsible for team development. The best answers will include specific examples from real experience: "We hired an engineer who had a degree but had never used Excel for data analysis. We spent three months training him on a skill that should have been in the curriculum." Specificity at this level is B1.2-adjacent performance. Name it explicitly when you hear it.
