1 The Scenario

Read this.
Then we talk.

Every Speaking Lab starts with a real situation. Read the scenario below and imagine you are there. This is your context for the entire session.

📍 Situation

It's Tuesday, 9:15 AM. You work at a manufacturing plant in Guanajuato. Your manager walks in and says: "Corporate from Houston is here. They want to see the production line and talk to the team. They didn't tell us they were coming."

🎯 Your role

You are a quality supervisor. You need to explain what your team does, show the production floor, answer their questions about processes, and handle any issues they find — all in English.

🧠 Think about this

What's the first thing you say when you meet the auditor? How do you feel? Nervous? Confident? What words do you need?

2 Key Vocabulary

5 words you'll
need today

These aren't textbook words. These are the words you hear on the production floor when things get real. Read them, say them out loud, and use them in the next sections.

01
Compliance
/kəmˈplaɪ.əns/
Following the rules, standards, and regulations required by the company or by law.
"We are in full compliance with the safety standards from corporate."
02
Downtime
/ˈdaʊn.taɪm/
Time when a machine or production line is not working — planned or unplanned.
"We reduced unplanned downtime by 30% this quarter."
03
Throughput
/ˈθruː.pʊt/
The amount of product that moves through the production process in a given time.
"Our throughput increased after we reorganized the assembly stations."
04
Non-conformance
/ˌnɒn.kənˈfɔː.məns/
When a product or process doesn't meet the required specifications. A quality issue.
"We found a non-conformance in the welding area and corrected it the same day."
05
Corrective action
/kəˈrek.tɪv ˈæk.ʃən/
A step or process you implement to fix a problem and prevent it from happening again.
"The corrective action we implemented eliminated the root cause."
3 Action Verbs

5 verbs to
take control

When you're in a high-pressure situation, you need verbs that show confidence and action. Not "I think" — but "I implemented", "I detected", "I resolved".

To implement
implemented · implementing
To put a plan, decision, or system into action.
"We implemented a new inspection protocol last month."
To detect
detected · detecting
To discover or identify something — especially a problem.
"Our team detected the issue before it reached the customer."
To resolve
resolved · resolving
To find a solution and fix a problem completely.
"We resolved the non-conformance within 24 hours."
To monitor
monitored · monitoring
To watch and check something regularly over time.
"We are monitoring the scrap rate on a daily basis."
To walk (someone) through
walked through · walking through
To explain something step by step. Very common in audits and presentations.
"Let me walk you through our quality control process."
💡 Pro tip

Notice how these verbs sound active and in control. In an audit, you don't say "we have a problem." You say "we detected an issue, implemented a corrective action, and we're monitoring the results." Same situation. Completely different impression.

4 Speaking Practice

Answer out loud.
No shortcuts.

Read each question, think for 5 seconds, then answer out loud — in English. Tap any question to see a hint. Use the vocabulary and verbs from the previous sections.

1
Warm-up
The auditor just arrived. Introduce yourself and your role. What do you say?
▼
Hint
Start simple: "Good morning, my name is ___. I'm the quality supervisor here at ___. Welcome to our plant." Don't overthink it — confidence first.
2
Warm-up
The auditor asks: "Can you tell me a little about this production line?" What do you say?
▼
Hint
Use "walk you through": "Sure, let me walk you through the process. This line produces ___. We run ___ shifts and our daily throughput is approximately ___."
3
Core
The auditor finds a non-conformance on the floor. They ask: "How do you handle quality issues here?" Explain your process.
▼
Hint
Use the detect → resolve → monitor flow: "When we detect a non-conformance, we stop the line, investigate the root cause, implement a corrective action, and then monitor the results for at least two weeks."
4
Core
The auditor looks at your scrap report and says: "This number is higher than last quarter. Why?" How do you respond?
▼
Hint
Don't panic. Acknowledge, explain, and show action: "You're right, the number went up in March. We detected that the issue was related to ___. We implemented a corrective action in April and since then the trend is going down. We're monitoring it weekly."
5
Core
The auditor asks: "What's the biggest challenge your team faces right now?" Be honest — what would you say?
▼
Hint
Being honest shows professionalism: "Right now our biggest challenge is ___. We're working on it by ___." Auditors respect honesty more than perfection.
6
Challenge
At the end of the visit, the auditor says: "Is there anything you'd like corporate to know?" This is your chance. What do you say?
▼
Hint
This is the hardest question because it's open. Highlight your team: "I'd like corporate to know that this team is committed to continuous improvement. We've reduced downtime by ___% and we're implementing new processes every month. We're proud of what we're building here."

Respond in real time.

The auditor speaks. You respond — out loud. Don't read. Don't write. Just talk. If you need to pause and think, that's fine. But say something. Silence is not an option in an audit.

🎯 After the roleplay

How did it feel? Did you use the vocabulary? Did the verbs come naturally? If you froze at any point, that's exactly where you need to practice. Go back and try again. In a real audit, you don't get a second chance.
