§ 01
Picture Prompt — What's Happening?
5–7 min
Pick one photo. Start speaking from the first second — no prep. What's happening? What instructions are being given? How often does this happen? Minimum 3–4 sentences.
⚡ Quick Win — 20 seconds
Open meeting small talk: "What do you usually do in your free time?" "I [verb] and I also [verb]. How about you?"

👉 Describe your photo in 3–4 sentences. Use: imperatives ("Open / Please send / Don't share") · frequency ("once a week / every Monday") · dates ("on March 20th / in April") — Mix all three S1A structures.
Scaffold visible to the student:
"They are having a meeting…"  ·  "The manager says: 'Please…'"  ·  "This happens every…"  ·  "The deadline is on…"  ·  "Don't…"
Teacher Push — Push for more output
"What instruction would the manager give to the team? Say it in English."
"How often does this type of meeting happen at your company?"
"Is there a deadline visible? What date would you say?"
"Now describe Photo 2 or 3 in one sentence only — challenge yourself."
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Show the photos BEFORE giving the instructions. The student starts speaking the moment they pick a photo — without reading the scaffold first.
  • The scaffold is visible, but the student should NOT read it before speaking. Let them flow spontaneously for 30–45 seconds, then point to the scaffold as support if they get stuck.
  • Minimum acceptable output: 3 sentences with at least 1 imperative + 1 frequency expression. If they do it without the scaffold = S1A mastery.
  • Strict timing: 5–7 min. The core of the session is §4. Don't linger here longer than needed.
  • If the student says "Please you send the report" → recast without stopping: "Yes! Please SEND — exactly. Good idea." Keep the flow going.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "In S1A you read these instructions in a Teams chat. Now you're creating them yourself. That's the jump from comprehension to production — and you just made it."
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "In 50 minutes you'll have a complete conversation: small talk, instructions, dates — without notes. That's already real corporate English."

Role Play — 3 Rounds · The Monday Call
12–18 min
Round 1 — Guided
With scaffold · 4–5 min
Scenario: You are the Operations Manager at your company. You open a video call with David Park in Phoenix. First make small talk (hobby + frequency), then give 3 instructions to the team about the Q2 project and mention the deadline.
"Good morning, David! How are you? Busy weekend? I usually ___. I ___ [frequency]."
"Okay, let's start. Please ___ the ___. Don't ___ it ___."
"The deadline for the Q2 report is on ___. We have check-ins [frequency]."
"Please confirm your attendance ___ [day/date]."
Teacher — Respond as David Park (Phoenix)
"Good morning! Great to hear you! What's your hobby? That's amazing — I work out too!"
"Got it. Should I share the agenda with the full team or just the leads?"
"And the deadline — is that April or May? I want to block my calendar."
Round 2 — Semi-free
Prompts only · 5–6 min
Scenario: Now you are Miguel Torres — the new team member. David Park calls to ask you to do several tasks before Friday's meeting. Respond, ask for clarifications about dates, and confirm the instructions with an imperative.
hobby + frequency as opener receive 2 instructions from David ask: "When is the deadline?" confirm: "Please send me the…" close with the date of the next call
Teacher — Respond as David Park (demanding)
"Miguel, please update the database before Thursday. And don't send the old version."
"The deadline is on the 18th — that's next Monday. Can you confirm by email?"
"Also — how often do you check your messages? I need fast responses."
Round 3 — Free
No support · Timer on · 4–6 min
Scenario: Now you are YOURSELF — with your name, your company, your real job. David Park calls you for a surprise call. You must: make hobby small talk, give real instructions from your job, mention your next real deadline, and ask "How often do we have these calls?" — All without notes. All real.
Timer
3:00
Teacher — Be a real American interlocutor
Ask unexpected questions to break the student's script: "Wait — what exactly IS your hobby? Do you do it alone or with family?"
Simulate a date misunderstanding: "Sorry — did you say March or May? The connection is bad."
Ask for more instructions: "Okay, and what about the rest of the team? What should THEY do?"
Correction protocol: ZERO corrections during the round. Only at the end: 1 specific celebration + 1 improvement point.
✅ Self-Evaluation — After Round 3
  • I made small talk about my hobby and frequency without notes.
  • I gave at least 2 imperatives in a real work context.
  • I mentioned a date with "on" or "in" correctly.
  • I answered David's unexpected questions without long pauses.
  • I finished Round 3 without reading any notes.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: §4 is the CORE of S1B. Protect a minimum of 12 minutes of real speaking here. Don't cut it for any reason.
  • Round 1: if the student reads the scaffold — that's fine. The goal is feeling the full flow (small talk → instructions → date). Minimal correction.
  • Round 2: remove the scaffold. The student uses only the prompts. If they get stuck on a date → give them time (3 seconds) before offering a hint.
  • Round 3 — A1.2 mastery standard: 60 seconds of fluent conversation with at least 1 hobby + 2 imperatives + 1 correct date = full success for M1 S1B.
  • 3-minute timer: enough for Round 3. If the student finishes before 3 min → excellent. If they go silent before 90 seconds → help with "And what about the deadline?"
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "What you just did — that conversation in English, no notes, with real information from your job — that's exactly what your company needs from you."
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "In S2B you do role plays where you RECEIVE a client in English. Same 3-round format — greater complexity. Progress is visible session by session."
  • If the student is hooked: "In M3 you describe your full company — products, employees, numbers — in a 2-minute oral presentation. That's the A1.2 level."

§ 06
Debate — Free Opinion · No Script
7–10 min
GT debate rules: Minimal correction — flow is what matters. Allow minor grammar mistakes as long as communication is clear. Don't interrupt. Only at the end: 1 improvement point + 1 celebration. Goal: the student speaks without fear.
Question 1
"Is small talk really important in business? Or is it a waste of time?"
Student gives their opinion — minimum 3 sentences. Push with: "Why? Can you give me an example from your work?" — Have them mention frequency if possible.
Question 2
"Should companies give instructions in English to their Mexican teams? What are the advantages and disadvantages?"
Push with: "Do you get instructions in English at your company? Give me a real example — what does your manager say?" — Wait for a real imperative.
Question 3
"What is YOUR hobby? How often do you do it? Does it help you with your job in any way?"
This personal question closes the module loop: the student uses their real hobby in English — exactly what they learned in S1A. If they do it fluently = mastery confirmed.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Present one question at a time. Listen without interrupting. Note the single most important error — just one — to comment on later.
  • Question 3 is deliberately personal — that emotional anchor is what gets retained most. If the student talks about their real hobby with confidence → celebrate it explicitly.
  • If the student only says 1–2 sentences: push with "And? What else? Give me more." The goal is 3+ sentences per question.
  • If the student uses an imperative spontaneously in the debate (not as an exercise) → flag it as evidence of internalization. Tell them: "Did you notice you used an imperative naturally? That's already automaticity."
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Debate in English. Your own opinion. No translating. That's not A1.1 anymore — that's A1.2. That's what you did today."
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "In M2 the debate is about products and companies — 'Should your company sell internationally?' Same structure, more vocabulary. That's the next level."
