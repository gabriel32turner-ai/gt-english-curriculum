§2   Grammar Consolidation  ·  12 min

Linking Words — Complete System

ALL 5 FAMILIES — WITH CAPSTONE EXAMPLES
FunctionWordsCapstone example
Contrastalthough / even though / whereas / despite + -ingAlthough I’m still figuring out my direction, I have a clear vision of the kind of work I want to do.
Resultas a result / therefore / consequentlyAs a result of everything I’ve learned from my failures, I’m a better risk-taker now.
Additionfurthermore / in addition / besidesFurthermore, I want to build a team that shares my values, not just my goals.
Concessionhowever / nevertheless / even soThe path isn’t certain. However, the direction is clear — and I’m committed to it.
Contrast (parallel)whereasWhereas before I measured progress by title, I now measure it by impact.
⚠ Final reminder: “Despite she is ambitious” → WRONG · “Despite being ambitious” → CORRECT · Despite + noun/-ing only.
ACTIVITY A — MIXED TOPICS — Full linking word system
1. (University) _____ she spent four years studying economics, her career in design was built entirely through self-teaching and portfolio work.
A. Although
B. As a result
C. Furthermore
2. (Success) He redefined success from wealth to legacy. _____, he now measures his work by the people he has developed, not the revenue he has generated.
A. As a result
B. Although
C. Despite
3. (Failure) _____ the project failing publicly, she was promoted six months later because of how professionally she handled the aftermath.
A. Despite
B. Whereas
C. However
4. (Remote work) _____ managers value physical presence, remote employees increasingly measure success by output and impact.
A. Whereas
B. Furthermore
C. Despite
ACTIVITY B — UNJUMBLE — Two sentences, two different linker families

Sentence 1 (contrast + result):

  • she now leads
  • despite failing
  • with more confidence than before
  • publicly last year

Sentence 2 (parallel contrast):

  • whereas before
  • I now pursue
  • I pursued credentials
  • clarity and impact
💬 Quick: Say one sentence about your vision for the next 5 years using all three: although + as a result + furthermore. One connected thought. Say it out loud now.
Activity B Sentence 2 is the most elegant of M6 — the parallel structure “whereas before I pursued X, I now pursue Y” is a classic B1.2 construction for describing personal transformation. It will appear naturally in the Capstone talks. The micro speaking challenge (all three linkers in one connected sentence about their vision) is the bridge from grammar exercise to real production. Do it as a group: everyone simultaneously, to their partner. 30 seconds of productive noise.
§3   Integrated Reading  ·  8 min

Carlos Mendez — Module 6 Closing Reflection

Over four sessions, you have been asked to speak in English about the things that actually matter: whether your education was worth the cost, what success means beyond salary, how you handle failure when it’s real, and what kind of work arrangement allows you to thrive as a human being — not just as an employee.

Although these might seem like personal topics, they are also the most professionally consequential conversations you will ever have in English. The people who advance fastest in international organisations are rarely those who speak English most correctly. They are those who speak it most clearly — about what they want, what they’ve learned, and where they’re going.

Whereas a language exam tests whether you know the rules, a real professional conversation tests whether you can use the language to say something true. Despite being difficult, honesty in a second language is the highest form of fluency. When you can be vulnerable, persuasive, reflective, and ambitious in English — you are genuinely fluent.

Furthermore, fluency is not a destination. It is, as Carlos would say, a relationship between what you want to express and your ability to express it. As a result of everything you have done in this level, that relationship has changed. You have come a long way.

Today’s question is simple — and the most important of the level: Where do you see yourself in five years? You have three minutes. No notes. Your story.

Carlos Mendez — Module 6 Closing:

I want to say something that most coaches won’t say at the end of a programme.

The question “where do you see yourself in five years?” is not really about the future. It is about who you are right now — what you value, what you’re afraid of, and what you’re willing to commit to. Although the question asks about the future, the answer reveals the present.

Whereas a prepared answer sounds rehearsed, a true answer sounds like a person who knows themselves. The most impressive answer I’ve ever heard to this question in an interview was: “I don’t know exactly where I’ll be. Despite that uncertainty, I know the direction I’m moving in, the values I won’t compromise, and the impact I want to have. That’s enough for now.”

Furthermore, I believe the most honest version of this answer is the most powerful. As a result of everything you have done in this level, you have the language to say something true. Use it. Three minutes. No notes. Go.

Celebrate any version that is honest and structurally coherent. The quality of language at this point is genuinely different from where learners started. §5   Capstone  ·  25 min

Where Do You See Yourself in 5 Years?

🎯 Your 3-Minute Answer — No Notes
This is not a presentation. It is not a role play. It is not an exercise.

You are being asked to speak English about your real life, your real future, and your real self. That is not a language exercise. That is the thing itself.

Three minutes. No notes. Unscripted. True.
🎯 WHAT TO INCLUDE (no particular order — make it yours):
  • Where you are now: Use the vocabulary of M6. Connect your current role, values, and work preference honestly.
  • What you’ve learned: Although / despite / as a result — use at least one reference to a setback or shift that changed your direction.
  • Where you’re going: Whereas before / I now / furthermore — use linking words to show contrast between your past and future self.
  • What you’re committed to: Not a title or a salary — a value, a kind of work, an impact. Use S5 vocabulary: vision, clarity, potential, commitment.
  • One M6 phrase: “I’ve come a long way” / “no looking back” / “that’s the goal” — used naturally, not forced.
FLUENCY
Speaks for 3 full minutes with natural pace. Uses silence productively — not to hide, but to think.
LINKING WORDS
Uses at least 3 different linking word families accurately. Each linker connects real ideas, not decoration.
VOCABULARY
Uses S5 vision vocabulary accurately: vision, clarity, commitment, impact, potential, direction.
HONESTY
The talk sounds true. Not polished or rehearsed — real. This is the hardest criterion and the most important.
COHERENCE
The talk has a direction — it moves from now to future, from experience to vision, from past self to present commitment.
CONFIDENCE
The speaker occupies the room. They know this is their story and they own it — in English.
3-MINUTE CAPSTONE
PEER LISTENER CHECKLIST
The Capstone is the final proof of fluency for B1.2. Do NOT correct during the talk. Do NOT interrupt. Do NOT give a score immediately. After each learner finishes: 3 seconds of silence. Then: “What one thing will you remember from that talk?” (to the room). Then: teacher closes with 1-2 observations from what they actually heard. Record if possible — learners are always surprised at how much more they said in English than they thought they could. That surprise is the moment of belief in their own fluency.
💰 After the last Capstone: “You have completed B1.2 — the highest level of this programme. In the past six modules, you have discussed media and influence, your future and your values, your failures and your successes — all in English, without a script, in front of other people. That is not a small thing. B2.1 is available when you’re ready. But first: take a moment to recognise what you’ve done. You’ve come a long way. No looking back.”
