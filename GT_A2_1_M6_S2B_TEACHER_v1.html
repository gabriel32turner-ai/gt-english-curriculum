§1 Hot Take — Will vs. Going To: Does It Really Matter? 8 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — B SESSION OPENER

Callback to S2A: "Last session we studied will vs. going to. Today you don't think about it — you produce it. This Hot Take is designed to provoke: some students will say the distinction doesn't matter. Let them argue. The debate IS the production activity." Keep it energetic — this is the warm-up, not the lesson.

"In real Mexican work life, nobody cares if you say 'will' or 'going to' — what matters is that your message is clear."
Disagree50/50Agree
50%
✓ AGREE — Use in debate
International partners understand context. Native speakers often mix them. The message is more important than the exact structure. A Mexican engineer who says "I going to send the report" still gets the job done.
✗ DISAGREE — Use in debate
Using the wrong tense signals a planned action vs. a spontaneous one — and in business, that distinction changes expectations. "I'll handle it" = now. "I'm going to handle it" = later. International managers notice.
💡 ENGAGEMENT NOTE

After the debate, ask: "Has anyone ever had a misunderstanding at work because of tense — in Spanish or English?" This surfaces real stakes. Even one good story from the group makes the grammar real. The answer to the Hot Take: both sides are partly right — which is why S2B focuses on production, not perfection.

90 seconds: each side presents their best argument using project management vocabulary.

§2 Pitch Round — The PM Decision Wheel 12 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — PM DECISION WHEEL

This is the unique visual for S2B. Each scenario presents a real work situation. Students must decide: is this a will moment (spontaneous) or a going to moment (pre-planned)? They click their choice, then speak a full sentence using the correct tense. Run all 4 scenarios — class votes after each one. Award 2 points per correct tense + 1 point for a complete, natural sentence.

⚙️ PM DECISION WHEEL — Will or Going To?
SCENARIO 1 — The Meeting Surprise
You're in a meeting. Your manager just said: "We need someone to call the Monterrey supplier today." Nobody volunteers. You decide — right now — to do it.
WILL
"I'll call them this afternoon."
GOING TO
"I'm going to call them this afternoon."
✓ WILL — you decided RIGHT NOW, in this meeting. Going to would suggest you had a plan before this moment.
SCENARIO 2 — The Pre-Planned Review
Your team has had the Q2 review on the calendar for three weeks. Someone asks: "What's the plan for Thursday?"
WILL
"We'll do the Q2 review."
GOING TO
"We're going to do the Q2 review."
✓ GOING TO — the plan existed before this conversation. It was already decided three weeks ago.
SCENARIO 3 — The Stakeholder Promise
A stakeholder emails you: "We need the budget report by Friday." You reply immediately with a commitment.
WILL
"I'll send you the budget report by Friday."
GOING TO
"I'm going to send you the budget report by Friday."
✓ WILL — spontaneous promise made right now in response to their request. This is exactly what will is for.
SCENARIO 4 — Both Work
Your colleague asks: "Are you going to handle the handover next week?" You decided last Monday to do it AND you're also confirming right now.
WILL
"Yes, I'll handle the handover."
GOING TO
"Yes, I'm going to handle the handover."
✓ BOTH WORK — when the plan was pre-decided AND you're confirming now, both tenses are correct. Context and emphasis change the nuance slightly, but both are natural.

§5 Solo Challenge — The 90-Second Project Update 12 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — SOLO FORMAT

Each student gives a 90-second project update as if presenting to a senior manager. They must use at least 4 PM vocabulary words from S2A naturally — not forced, not announced. After each update, the class identifies which vocabulary words they heard. Award 1 bonus point per identified word (max 4 bonus). This makes the listening as productive as the speaking.

🚀 SOLO CHALLENGE — 90-Second Project Update
Your mission: You are presenting to your senior manager. Give a complete project update in 90 seconds.

You must include:
✓ Simple Past — what happened (at least 3 sentences)
✓ Going To — what's planned (at least 2 sentences)
✓ Will — what you commit to right now (at least 2 sentences)
✓ PM vocabulary — use at least 4 words from S2A naturally
✓ 1 colloquial phrase from S2A ("We're back on track" / "Keep me in the loop" / etc.)

No notes. No pausing. Speak like a professional.


💰 RETENTION + UPSELL NOTE

The 90-second update is the exact format used in international weekly check-ins at manufacturing companies across the Bajío. Engineers who can do this in English get assigned to international projects — which often comes with compensation increases. Tell students this directly: "What you just did — you can do this next Monday with your actual manager, in English. That's the point."

§6 Speaking Cards — PM Vocabulary in Sentences 10 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — SPEAKING CARDS FORMAT

Each card gives a PM vocabulary word, a required tense, and a context cue. Students draw a card, produce a real sentence using their actual work, and the class votes: thumbs up (natural + correct tense) or thumbs down (forced or wrong tense). No grammar explanations — only feedback on whether it sounds real. This is fluency training, not accuracy drilling.

🃏 SPEAKING CARDS — Draw & Speak
SIMPLE PAST · Use: DELAY
Tell your partner about a real delay you experienced at work. What caused it? What did you do?
Use "delay" naturally — not as the first word. Embed it in your story.
GOING TO · Use: MILESTONE
What milestone is your team going to reach in the next two months? When exactly?
Must include: "going to" + "milestone" + specific time expression.
WILL · Use: STAKEHOLDER
A stakeholder just asked for an update. Make 2 spontaneous commitments using will + stakeholder.
"I'll send the stakeholders..." / "I'll update the stakeholder on..." — make it real.
SIMPLE PAST · Use: APPROVAL
Did you need approval for something at work recently? What happened? Did you get it?
Use: "We got approval" / "We didn't get approval" / "They approved..." — all valid.
GOING TO · Use: SCOPE + RISK
Describe one change your team is going to make to a current project. Mention the scope and one risk.
Challenge: use BOTH scope and risk in the same 2-sentence answer.
ALL 3 + COLLOQUIAL
Give a complete project update: past + going to + will. End with: "Are we on the same page?" or "Keep me in the loop." Make it feel natural — not scripted.
Class votes: did it sound like a real check-in call? Thumbs up / thumbs down.

🎯 3 Phrases for This Week — Use Them At Work

"We're back on track."
Use it this week after solving any problem — delay, missed deadline, supplier issue. Three words. Maximum professional impact.
"I'll [commitment] — keep me in the loop."
Chain a will commitment with a request for updates. Use in one real message or meeting this week.
"We're going to [plan] on [specific day]."
Replace "we will do it" with this whenever the plan existed before the conversation. The specificity of the day makes it more professional.
