1. "What did you do last week with the Apex supplier?"

S-S
Pairs role-play a real check-in call. Partner A: the manager — ask at least 4 questions across all three tenses. Partner B: the team member — answer naturally, then add information the manager didn't ask for (proactive communication). Switch after 90 seconds.

A: "What happened with the budget?" B: "We stayed within budget — actually we saved 8%. And I'm going to reallocate the savings to the training next month." A: "Good. What will you do today?" B: "I'll update the cost report and send it before noon." — Note: Valeria answered AND added information. That's professional English.

Past: "What did you submit?" / "When did you call?" / "Who did you meet?"
Going to: "What are you going to present?" / "When is the meeting going to happen?"

§4 Colloquial — Spoken Business Fillers & Transitions 5 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — WHY FILLERS MATTER

Mexican professionals often go silent when thinking in English — which reads as hesitation or lack of confidence to international colleagues. These 4 phrases buy thinking time AND sound professional. Say: "In English, silence is uncomfortable. These phrases keep you in control while you think." Drill each one with a 3-second pause to simulate real thinking time.

"That's a good point."
Buys 2–3 seconds while sounding engaged. Use before adding your own perspective. Never just agree and stop.
"That's a good point. I think we also need to consider the supplier timeline."
"Let me think about that for a second."
Professional pause. Much better than silence or "Ummm." Signals you're taking the question seriously.
"Let me think about that for a second… I think we went over budget on the installation."
"To be honest..."
Signals a direct, sincere answer is coming. Builds trust in professional conversations. Use for opinions or concerns.
"To be honest, I'm not 100% confident we'll meet the deadline."
"Moving on..."
Transition phrase to change topics in a meeting or presentation. Smooth, professional, universally understood.
"Great. Moving on — let's talk about the Q2 targets."
💰 RETENTION + UPSELL NOTE

"To be honest" and "Let me think about that" are the two highest-value phrases in this session. They directly address the #1 fear of A2.1 students in English conversations: being put on the spot. Assign them as their "comfort phrases" for the week. These two phrases alone can transform a student's perception of their own fluency.

§5 Grammar — Wh- Questions in All Three Tenses 12–15 min
Real situation: You're in a meeting and your manager asks:

"What did you do?" / "What are you going to do?" / "What will you do?"

Three questions. Three tenses. Same Wh- word. The structure changes each time — and if you get it wrong, the tense collapses.

The tool: Wh- question formation across Simple Past, Going To, and Will.
TenseWh- Question StructureExample
Simple Past Wh- + did + subject + base verb? "What did you do last week?"
Going To Wh- + am/is/are + subject + going to + base? "What are you going to present on Friday?"
Will Wh- + will + subject + base verb? "Who will handle the client call?"
Common Wh- words: What / When / Where / Who / Why / How / How long / How many
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — THE HIDDEN ERROR

The most common error: "What you did last week?" (missing auxiliary). Students transfer Spanish word order directly. Say: "In English, the auxiliary does the work — 'did', 'are going to', 'will' — you can't drop it." Run a quick substitution drill: same question, all three tenses. "What did / are going to / will you [verb]?" — students produce all three versions for the same situation.

ACTIVITY A — Choose the Correct Question Form
1. You want to ask your colleague about their past actions: "___ you do last Friday?"
2. You want to ask about a planned future action: "___ the team going to present on Tuesday?"
3. In the meeting right now, you offer to do something. Your manager asks: "___ you do?" (spontaneous)
4. "___ the supplier deliver the components?" (past — asking about a specific event)
5. "___ handle the Guadalajara account next quarter?" (asking about future responsibility)

🗣️ Quick: Ask your partner the same question in all three tenses: "What did/are you going to/will you [verb]?"

ACTIVITY B — REORDER: The Check-In Dialogue

These lines from a check-in conversation are scrambled. Put them in the correct logical order.

  • ⠿I'll send you the updated risk report this afternoon.
  • ⠿Daniel: What did you work on last week?

Daniel & Valeria — Monday Check-In, Solaris Manufacturing
A2.1 · M6 · S4A · Voices: Daniel & Valeria · Speed: 0.82
Daniel: Good morning, Valeria. Do you have a few minutes? Valeria: Of course, Daniel. Go ahead. Daniel: Great. Let me start with last week. What did you work on for the Apex project? Valeria: Last week I focused on the supplier coordination. I called them on Monday and Thursday, and we reviewed the new delivery schedule. We also identified a small quality issue with component seven, but we resolved it by Wednesday. Daniel: Good. And what did the Apex coordinator say about the delay? Valeria: He understood the situation. He said the delay was acceptable and he appreciated the communication. We're back on track. Daniel: Excellent. Now, moving on — what are you going to do about the Q2 production review? Valeria: We're going to schedule it for the third week of next month. I'll also include the risk analysis — I think the board will want to see it. Daniel: Great. That's exactly what I needed. To be honest, Valeria, this project is going very well under your management. Valeria: Thank you, Daniel. I'll keep you posted on anything that changes. Daniel: Perfect. Let's pick this up again next Monday. Have a good week.
