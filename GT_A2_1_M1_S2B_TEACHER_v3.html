Compound Past Builder — Professional Model
Basic: "I attended a meeting." → Professional: "I attended the team standup, organized the files for the audit, and presented the results to management."
Negative: "I didn't complete the catalogue update because the supplier call lasted two hours longer than planned."
Drill: The teacher says 3 verbs. The student builds a compound sentence about their real work. Minimum 3 rounds. Increase the speed each round.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • Flashcard Wall: the student says the past form AND the sentence BEFORE flipping. If they only give the form ("notified") without a sentence: "Now use it in a real sentence about your week."
  • Compound Builder: the ⭐ cards (notify, reply) are the ones most often omitted in free production. Specifically force their use in the drill.
  • Threshold for role play: 7/8 cards correct + 1 spontaneous compound sentence. If not: 5 more minutes of compound drill.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You're producing compound past sentences about your real work. That's exactly what you'd write in an update email to your director."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "In S3 you add irregular verbs (went, gave, met). You'll combine regular AND irregular verbs in the same report. That's real professional English."

▶ Show ElevenLabs Script

Marcos (Adam) · single-speaker monologue · Rate: 0.82 · ~85 sec


Good morning team. Here is my quick update from last week. On Monday, I attended the supplier meeting and organized the files for the Q2 audit. On Tuesday, I prepared the agenda for today and replied to all client emails before five PM. On Wednesday, I notified the sales team about the new pricing structure and presented the Q1 summary to finance. Management approved the budget on Thursday — no delays this time. I did not complete the pricing report, because the supplier delayed the final numbers. This week, my plan is simple: I will complete the pricing report, notify the supplier by Wednesday, and prepare the Q2 presentation for the leadership review. Overall, last week was productive. The team responded quickly, the meetings finished on time, and we closed three of four pending items. That is everything from my side. Thank you.

ElevenLabs: Adam voice (Marcos). Single-speaker monologue. Rate 0.82, stability 0.65. This audio recycles ALL the verbs from S2A in a self-recorded weekly recap. Record once, no mixing required. Upload to Google Drive with iframe /preview.
Listening Task — Oral Response
Listen once. No writing. The teacher asks — the student answers with past verbs.
"What did Marcos complete last week? What didn't he complete — and why?"
"What did Marcos do on Wednesday? Find 3 verbs he used."
"Was the budget review delayed? What happened exactly?"
"Now YOU give the same kind of update. Your manager asks: 'What did YOU do last week?' — answer with 3 verbs and 1 negative."
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • Task 4 is the most valuable: the student responds as if they were giving the same monologue — free production with real data. Demand compound past (minimum 2 verbs) + 1 negative with "because".
  • Attention: Marcos uses "notified" and "replied" — both -ied verbs. If the student misses them: replay those specific sentences.
  • Focus task on 1st listen: "Count how many past verbs you hear." (Answer: 12+ — attended, organized, prepared, replied, notified, presented, approved, did not complete, delayed, responded, finished, closed).
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Marcos described a whole week in one short monologue. Organized + professional. That's the standard — not a list of 10 things, but a few well-built sentences."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "In S3 of this module you'll add 'went to', 'gave', 'met with' — irregular verbs. The recap will sound even more natural: 'I went to the client meeting, gave my presentation, and met with the supplier.'"

Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "Compound past + 'because' = professional English reporting. International executives expect that, not lists of verbs."

🎭 Role Play 1 — Monday Morning Standup (Full)
You are Marcos. Your manager (the teacher) leads the Monday standup. Report your past week — what you completed, what you didn't complete and why, and what's pending this week.
Role A — Marcos (student)
Minimum 5 sentences: (1) 2–3 completed using compound, (2) 1 negative with "because", (3) something "in progress". Use your S2 verbs: attended, organized, prepared, notified, approved, completed, delayed, replied. No notes.
Role B — Teacher (Sarah, manager)
Questions that force past verbs: "Did you notify the team?" / "Why didn't you complete it?" / "Did the supplier delay anything?" / "Who approved it?"
[Sarah]: "Good morning. Quick update — Marcos, what did you complete last week?"
Goal: the student produces compound past sentences naturally, uses -ied at least once, gives a reason for the negative, and answers Did/Didn't questions spontaneously.
🎭 Role Play 2 — Email Follow-Up Call (as Laura)
Roberto (the teacher) calls Laura to follow up on Wednesday's daily report. Laura (you) explains verbally what she wrote — and adds details that weren't in the email.
Role A — Laura (student)
Use the email from §5 of S2A as your base. Explain verbally: (1) what you attended and organized; (2) what you prepared and presented; (3) why you didn't complete the catalogue; (4) when you will deliver it. Add 1 detail that was NOT in the email.
Role B — Teacher (Roberto)
"Did the supplier approve the full timeline?" / "How many tasks did you complete?" / "Who did you notify about the pricing?" / "Did you attend the finance meeting personally?"
[Roberto]: "Hi Laura, I read your report. Can you give me a bit more detail about this morning?"
Goal: the student recycles the content of S2A's reading in free oral production — without reading the email. When answering Did questions: short answer + full answer with compound structure.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • Role Play 1 uses REAL DATA from the student. If they don't know how to say a specific work task: "Tell me in Spanish and we'll build it together in English."
  • Role Play 2 recycles the email from S2A in verbal production — the student already knows the content. The additional invented detail ("something that wasn't in the email") activates spontaneous production.
  • Most likely error: the student gives only short answers ("Yes, I did.") without elaborating. Push: "And? What exactly did you complete? Compound sentence."
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You just gave a complete standup in English. Marcos did it in the audio — you did it with YOUR real work. That's harder and more valuable."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • Upsell: "In S3 you add 'went', 'gave', 'met', 'made' — the most common verbs in English. Your standup will go from 'I attended' to 'I went to the meeting, gave my presentation, and met the client.' Much more fluent."
§ 06
Debate — Work Reporting Culture
8–10 min
🗣 Free Speaking — Debate
"Laura always explains WHY she didn't complete something. Is that necessary in professional work culture — or is it just making excuses? Defend your position using past tense examples."
📌 Always explain the reason
"I didn't complete it because the supplier delayed the numbers."
"The team didn't approve it — they needed more information."
"We didn't attend because the client cancelled last minute."
"A reason shows professionalism, not weakness."
🔄 Results, not excuses
"I didn't complete it — I'll have it tomorrow."
"The report wasn't ready — end of story."
"Too many explanations = no accountability."
"Managers want results, not reasons."
I didn't complete X because… The team didn't approve… In my company, we always… Last week, I organized…
🎤 Optional Extension — Full Week Summary · 2 minutes
"Describe your full work week from last week. What did you complete? What didn't you finish? What's pending this week? 2 minutes. No notes."
Minimum 6 sentences. At least 5 different S2 verbs. At least 1 compound sentence. At least 1 negative with "because". At least 1 verb with -ied.
Teacher Follow-Up — After Debate
"Did YOU complete everything last week? If not — give me the professional reason."
"Name 3 things you organized OR prepared this month. Compound sentence."
"Did anyone at your company delay something important? What happened?"
"In one sentence: what's the most important thing you completed last week?"
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • The debate activates past verbs in argumentative discourse — the highest level of production. Rule: the student MUST use at least 4 past verbs during the debate. If they don't use them naturally: "Now say the same thing with a past-tense example."
  • The teacher takes the opposite side. The follow-up questions are the final diagnostic: do they answer Did/Didn't instantly? Compound sentences naturally?
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You just debated workplace culture in English using past verbs. That's A2 executive English."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • Testimonial: "If your director asked you in English 'What did your team accomplish last month?' — could you answer with compound past sentences? Yes. That's what you learned today."
§ SP
Speaking Practice
10–12 min
🗣 Guided Speaking — Check-In Call Simulation
Your Weekly Report — Free Production
No role cards. No script. Respond as yourself — your real job, your real week. Your teacher is your manager. Answer with full sentences: "Yes, I did. I…" or "No, I didn't. I…" Use as many S2 verbs as you can: attended, notified, completed, resolved, delivered, organized, submitted, confirmed.
Question 1
"Good morning — quick check-in. What did you complete last week? Give me two things."
Hint: I completed… / I submitted… / I delivered… / Yes, I did. I finished…
Question 2
"Did you attend the team update? Did you notify the client about the status?"
Hint: Yes, I did. I attended… / No, I didn't. I notified them by email instead… / I confirmed…
Question 3
"Did anything NOT get done? What didn't you finish — and why?"
Hint: No, I didn't finish… because… / I didn't resolve… / I didn't submit… / The deadline moved…
Question 4
"What's your priority this week? What do you need to deliver by Friday?"
Hint: I need to complete… / I'm going to submit… / I have to deliver… / My priority is…
Question 5
"Your turn — ask ME something about the project or the team using a past question."
Hint: Did you receive…? / Did the team confirm…? / Did you resolve…? / What did you decide about…?
⏱ 10–12 min total 📋 5 questions 🎯 Min. 6 past verbs 👥 Teacher as manager
⚡ Speaking Challenge — Closing
"Give me your real Friday update — 60 seconds, no notes. What did you complete, what didn't you finish, and what's your plan for next week. Use at least 4 of today's verbs."
Teacher evaluates: full Yes/No answer structure (not just short answers) · at least 4 S2 verbs used · at least 1 negative (didn't + BASE) · natural transition between what was done vs not done. Compare to S2A §SP: are they producing full sentences faster? That speed delta is the evidence of mastery.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • S2B students have already done Role Play (§5) with structured scripts and Debate (§6). This §SP removes all scaffolding — they respond as themselves, in real time, with no preparation.
  • Stay in the "manager" frame throughout Q1–Q4. Don't break character to explain grammar — recast errors as follow-up questions. If they say "I attend the meeting," respond: "You attended — good. And what was the outcome?"
  • Q3 is the most valuable: many students avoid negatives in free speech. Force it — if they don't say "didn't," ask: "Was everything completed, or was there something you didn't finish?" Make them produce it.
  • Q4 pivots to future (going to / have to) — that's intentional. It's a preview of M2 grammar and tests whether students can switch time frames naturally.
  • Target: 6+ past verb uses across all questions. If a student only uses attended and submitted, push for the rarer ones: "Did you organize anything? Did you resolve any issue?"
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "That was a real manager check-in — not a classroom exercise. You answered questions about your actual work, in English, on the spot. That's the skill."
  • After Speaking Challenge: "In S2A you needed hints to remember those verbs. Just now you used them automatically. That's what one session does."
  • If student was hesitant: "The hesitation you felt — that's normal in real check-ins. Every time you do this, the hesitation gets shorter. Today it was 3 seconds. Next week it'll be 1."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "In S3A you'll add irregular verbs — went, had, made, gave, met. Your standup becomes much more natural with those. They're the verbs native speakers use most."
  • Upsell: "By S4 you'll handle duration — 'I worked on this for 3 hours' / 'from 9 to 5' — which makes your reports sound truly professional, not just grammatically correct."
  • Testimonial trigger: "If your manager asked you this same question in English tomorrow morning — how would you feel compared to two weeks ago?"
