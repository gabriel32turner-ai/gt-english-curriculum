§2 Pitch Round — Spin the Wheel: Tense Challenge 12 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — SPIN THE WHEEL

Spin the wheel to get a tense + topic combination. The selected student has 45 seconds to speak about their real work using only that tense. Other students listen and raise their hand if they catch a wrong tense. Award 2 points for speaking, 1 point for a correct correction. Run 4–5 rounds minimum.

🎡 SPIN THE WHEEL — Tense + Topic
TEACHER NOTE — HOW TO RUN

Project on screen. One student spins — the wheel lands on a tense. The teacher then assigns the topic from the list below. Student speaks for 45 seconds. Class listens for errors. Works great as a competition: keep score on the board.

🏭 Something in production

PAST
Describe 3 things you COMPLETED last week. No going to. No will. Only Simple Past verbs.
Use: I reviewed / attended / submitted / called / resolved / negotiated...

"Last week I reviewed the Q1 budget, I called our supplier in Monterrey twice, and I submitted the risk report to Daniel. We didn't finish the quality audit — we completed it Thursday morning."

GOING TO + WILL
Describe what your team is PLANNING next month AND make 2 spontaneous commitments to your partner right now.
Plan: "We're going to..." · Commit: "I'll... right now / today / this week"

"Next month we're going to renegotiate two supplier contracts and launch the new inventory system. Going to is for plans — I decided this last week. And right now — I'll send you the project timeline. I'll also copy you on the supplier email today."

§3 Relay — The Three-Tense Story Chain 10 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — RELAY FORMAT

This is the visual relay unique to S1B. The chain shows how a story flows through all three tenses. Student 1 sets the past context (2 sentences, Simple Past). Student 2 adds the plan (2 sentences, going to). Student 3 closes with commitments (2 sentences, will). Then reverse: Student 4 asks a Wh- question in each tense to the person before them. Run 2 full chains minimum.

🔗 THE THREE-TENSE CHAIN — Build the Story Together

WILL
Student 3: What's your commitment? 2 sentences.

WH- QUESTIONS
Student 4: Ask one question per tense to each student.
S4 — Questioner
💡 ENGAGEMENT NOTE

Run the chain twice with different topics. Round 1: Apex project (fictional Valeria scenario). Round 2: a real project from the class. The second round always produces better English because the stakes are real. After both chains, ask: "Which round was easier?" — usually Round 2, because they own the content.

§5 Solo Challenge — The 2-Minute Project Pitch 12 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — SOLO CHALLENGE FORMAT

Each student gets 2 minutes to present a real work project using all three tenses. No notes. The class listens and counts: how many past sentences? How many going to? How many will? They report their count after each presentation. Teacher awards 1 point per correct tense use (max 10 per student). Keep score on the board.

⏱️ SOLO CHALLENGE — 2-Minute Project Pitch
Your mission: Present a real project from your work in exactly 2 minutes.

You must include:
✓ At least 3 Simple Past sentences (what you did / completed / had)
✓ At least 2 going to sentences (what's planned / decided)
✓ At least 2 will sentences (what you commit to right now)
✓ At least 1 colloquial phrase from S1A

No notes. No stopping. No translating out loud. Just English.


💰 RETENTION + UPSELL NOTE

The Solo Challenge is the closest simulation of a real corporate presentation. After each student finishes, ask the class one specific question in each tense about their presentation: "What did [name] say about the supplier?" / "What is [name] going to do?" / "What will [name] send today?" — this tests listening AND tense recognition simultaneously. It also tells you who is ready for A2.2.

§6 Open the Box — Spontaneous Production 10 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — OPEN THE BOX FORMAT

Students take turns clicking a box to reveal a speaking prompt. They have 30 seconds to respond using the correct tense indicated. The rest of the class votes: thumbs up (correct tense + natural) or thumbs down (wrong tense or too robotic). Award 2 points for a thumbs-up response. This is the highest-energy activity of S1B — keep the pace fast.

📦 OPEN THE BOX — Click to reveal your speaking prompt
1 PAST: What happened the last time you had a problem with a supplier?
2 WILL: Your manager just asked for a report. Commit to 3 things you'll do right now.
3 GOING TO: What's your team going to achieve in the next quarter?
4 ALL 3: Give a 30-second update using one sentence in each tense. Same project.
5 PAST + WILL: Something went wrong last week. What happened? What will you do to fix it?

🎯 3 Phrases for This Week — Use Them At Work

"Last week I [past verb]..."
Open every Monday update with this. It signals you track your work, you report clearly, and you're accountable.
"We're going to [plan] on [day]."
Replace "we will do it" with this when describing pre-decided plans. The distinction matters to English speakers.
"I'll [action] — I'll keep you posted."
The perfect one-two punch: spontaneous commitment + follow-up signal. Use it to close every conversation this week.
🗓️
Simple Past = Done
Report completed actions. Irregular verbs and did/didn't structure mastered.
📅
Going To = Planned
Decided before this conversation. Use for confirmed meetings, scheduled launches, team plans.
⚡
Will = Right Now
Decided in this moment. Offers, promises, spontaneous commitments. Never planned before.
