§1
Warm-Up — How do you travel for work?
Activate travel experience · connect comparatives to real trips · 5–8 min
⏱ 5–8 MIN

Remember from S1 and S2: more + adj + than · the most + adj · as + adj + as. Today: same toolkit, new context — business travel.

✈️ Do you travel for work? Where do you go most often? How do you travel — by car, bus, or plane? If no: does your company send colleagues on trips?
💡 Collect travel vocabulary in Spanish — note on board to translate in §2. Let students discuss freely before any grammar correction.
📋 Quick comparison: If you had to travel from Celaya to Mexico City for a client meeting — would you go by car, by bus, or by plane? Which is faster? Which is cheaper? Which is more comfortable?
💡 Let students debate without correction. Collect errors (more faster, more cheap) on the board. Use them in §4 Grammar as examples to fix.
🏢 Hotel vs Airbnb: For a 3-night business trip, which is better — a hotel or an Airbnb? Which is more professional? Which gives you more privacy? Which is the most convenient?
💡 No right answer — use to activate opinions from personal experience. Students who use "no-brainer" or "tough call" from previous sessions should be celebrated.
👩‍💼 Andrea's trip: Andrea needs to travel from Celaya to Guadalajara for a 2-day supplier meeting. Option A: direct flight ($2,800 MXN, 1 hour). Option B: first-class bus ($420 MXN, 3.5 hours). Which would you choose?
💡 This scenario anchors the reading, listening and grammar. Ask: "What would your company's travel policy say?" — makes it real.
✈️
Quick Win — 20 seconds
Say this out loud: "The direct flight is more expensive than the bus, but it is much faster and more convenient for a business trip."
3 comparatives in one breath. Today you build the full travel vocabulary to make any version of this sentence your own.
⚡ Teacher Note — Warm-Up
S3A introduces the third comparison domain: business travel. S1 = products/suppliers, S2 = people/candidates, S3 = travel options. Say explicitly: "Same grammar — different world. You can compare anything in English once you know the structure." The Andrea scenario (Card 4) is the narrative anchor — return to it in every section. Layer 3 (fastest): "What if Andrea also had the option of driving? How would that change your comparison?"

§3
Colloquial — Travel & Decision Language
4 phrases used in real business travel conversations · 5 min
⏱ 5 MIN
"It saves a lot of time."
Use to justify choosing a faster — usually more expensive — option. The time saving justifies the higher cost.
"The direct flight is more expensive, but it saves a lot of time — I arrive 3 hours earlier."
"It is not worth the hassle."
Use when a cheaper option has too many complications. The inconvenience outweighs the savings.
"The connecting flight is cheaper, but with a 4-hour layover, it is not worth the hassle."
"It is the most cost-effective option."
Use when recommending an option that gives the best result for the money. More professional than "it is the cheapest."
"The first-class bus is the most cost-effective option — lower fare and still comfortable enough."
"It depends on your priorities."
Use when both options have real advantages. The best choice depends on what matters most: time, cost, or comfort.
"It depends on your priorities — if the budget is tight, take the bus. If time is critical, fly."
⚡ Teacher Note — Colloquial
Connect to module arc: "It saves a lot of time" pairs with S1 "the pros outweigh the cons" — both justify paying more for something better. "It depends on your priorities" shows critical thinking without being confrontational. Drill: teacher gives a scenario ("Flight $2,800. Bus $420. Client meeting at 9am."), students respond with the correct colloquial phrase. Rotation: each student uses a different phrase. 3 min.

🚨 Real Work Situation
Andrea wants to write to her manager: "El vuelo llega mucho más rápido pero cuesta bastante más." She knows the basic comparatives, but how does she say "mucho más" (much more) and "un poco más rápido" (a little faster)? And how does she compare HOW something happens — not just what it is?
You think: "I know faster than and more expensive than — but how do I say MUCH faster? A LITTLE more expensive? And how do I compare HOW something runs — not just what it is?"
🔧 New Tools — Intensifiers + Comparative Adverbs
Intensifiers — how big is the difference?
  much / far = big difference: "The flight is much faster than the bus."
  a little / slightly = small difference: "The bus is slightly slower than the flight."
  significantly = clear, measurable: "The fare is significantly lower by bus."

Comparative Adverbs — compare HOW something is done (after action verbs):
  "The bus arrives more punctually than the flight on this route."
  "You can work more comfortably on the bus than on a plane."
  After IS → use adjective: "It is more comfortable." After RUNS / ARRIVES → use adverb: "It runs more punctually."
StructureUseTravel Example
much / far + comparativeBig differenceThe flight is much faster. The fare is far more expensive.
slightly / a little + comparativeSmall differenceThe bus is slightly slower — about 30 minutes difference.
significantly + comparativeClear, measurableThe bus fare is significantly lower than the airfare.
more + adverb (long)Compare HOW — after action verbsThe bus runs more punctually. You work more comfortably.
adverb + -er (short)Compare HOW — short adverbsThe flight arrives faster. The bus connects closer to the hotel.
⚠️ Common Errors:
✗ "The flight is much more faster." → ✓ much faster (never "more" + "-er")
✗ "It is more comfortably to fly." → ✓ It is more comfortable (adjective after "is")
✗ "The bus runs more punctual." → ✓ more punctually (adverb after action verb "runs")
✗ "The fare is significanter lower." → ✓ significantly lower (adverb, not adjective)

5. "The reimbursement for the bus is _____ simpler than for the flight."

GT Originals — Teacher Notes
  • 💼 Real corporate use: "significantly lower" and "far more expensive" appear in actual travel policy reports and budget justifications. Students can use these in work emails today.
  • ⚠️ IS + adjective / ACTION VERB + adverb: draw two columns on the board. "IS more comfortable" vs "RUNS more punctually." Drill with 5 quick sentences — students point to the correct column.
  • 🌿 Intensifier scale to draw: a little / slightly → significantly → much / far. Students place any comparison on this scale. Teaches precision, not just direction.
  • 🌟 Upsell: "In your B1.1 program you use these structures in formal emails and reports. You are already starting that today."

Reading — Andrea's Travel Request Email
Color-coded grammar · 5 True/False comprehension · 8–10 min
⏱ 8–10 MIN
Andrea Soto
Regional Sales Coordinator · Celaya, Guanajuato
Travel Request Email
M5 · S3A
🔑 Comparatives + intensifiers (orange) · Comparative adverbs (cyan) · Superlatives (green)

Subject: Travel request — Guadalajara supplier meeting, 14–15 November

Hi Daniel,

I need to travel to Guadalajara for the supplier review on November 14th. I have compared two options and I want to share my recommendation before submitting the request.

Option A — Direct Flight ($2,800 MXN): The flight is much faster than the bus — total travel time is about 3 hours versus 3.5. However, the fare is significantly higher than Option B, and the reimbursement process is more complex than for ground transport.

Option B — First-Class Bus ($420 MXN): The bus is far more affordable than the flight and arrives almost as quickly as Option A. It also runs more punctually than the flight — there were no delays on the last three trips our team made. I can also work more comfortably on the bus than on a short flight.

In my opinion, Option B is the most cost-effective choice for this trip. It is not worth the hassle of flying for such a short route. The savings are significantly higher than the time difference.

Please let me know if you approve. I can book today.

Thank you,
Andrea

Comprehension — True or False?

1. Andrea recommends the direct flight for the Guadalajara trip.
2. The bus fare is significantly lower than the flight fare.
3. Andrea says she can work more comfortably on the plane than on the bus.
4. The bus has been more punctual than the flight on recent team trips.
5. Andrea uses the phrase "it is not worth the hassle" in her email.
🗣️
Micro-Speaking
From memory: summarize Andrea's recommendation in 2 sentences using an intensifier and a comparative adverb. Example: "Andrea recommends the bus because it is far more affordable and it runs more punctually than the flight." 30 seconds — go!
⚡ Teacher Note — Reading
First email-format reading in M5 — shows students how comparison structures appear in professional writing. Say: "This is an email you could write to your manager right now. After today, you have all the language you need." Real-life connection: "Does your company have a travel policy? Do you need approval before booking?" For stronger groups: write a 3-sentence reply from Daniel approving or rejecting the request — using comparatives in the response.

Listening — The Travel Policy Proposal
ElevenLabs audio · Andrea's monologue · Voice: Sarah · Speed 0.82 · 4 comprehension questions · 8–10 min
⏱ 8–10 MIN
The Travel Policy Proposal — Vertex Logistics, Celaya
A2.2 · M5 · S3A · Voice: Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.82 · ~60 sec
Hi, I am Andrea Soto, Regional Sales Coordinator at Vertex Logistics. Today I want to talk about our company travel policy and why I think we should update it. Right now, our policy says that all employees must fly for any business trip longer than two hours. But I think the bus is actually a much better option for short regional routes, like Celaya to Guadalajara or Celaya to Leon. Here is why. First, the bus fare is significantly lower than the airfare. Sometimes six or seven times cheaper. Second, the bus arrives more punctually than the flight on these short routes. We checked the data: the average delay for regional flights is 45 minutes, while the bus is almost always on time. Third, I can work more comfortably on a first-class bus. There is more space than on a small regional aircraft, and the Wi-Fi is more reliable. I understand that flying is faster, but only slightly faster for these short distances. About 30 minutes difference when you count the airport time. The savings are far more significant than the time difference. My recommendation: for trips under 400 kilometers, the bus should be the preferred option. The company saves money, employees arrive less stressed, and the reimbursement process is much simpler. It is the most cost-effective travel policy for our region.
⚡ Teacher Note — Listening
ElevenLabs: Voice = Sarah, Speed = 0.82, Stability = 0.65, Similarity = 0.80. Before playing: "Listen for intensifiers — how many can you count?" (Answer: much better, significantly lower, more punctually, more comfortably, more reliable, only slightly faster, far more significant, much simpler — 8 instances). After listening: "Did Andrea use any colloquial phrases from §3?" Key grammar moment: "only slightly faster" — the intensifier makes the comparison precise, not just directional.

2. How much faster is the flight than the bus, according to Andrea?

Speaking Practice — Your Travel Decisions
6 guided questions · Pair & group · Opinion Meter · 10–12 min
⏱ 10–12 MIN
T-S Question 1 — Recall
What were Andrea's 3 main reasons for choosing the bus over the flight? Use an intensifier in each reason.
Grammar hint: "The bus is far more affordable... it runs more punctually... and the fare is significantly lower."
S-S Question 2 — Apply
Think of a real work trip someone at your company makes regularly. Compare two ways to make that trip. Use at least 2 intensifiers and 1 comparative adverb.
Grammar hint: "The [option] is much [adj] than the [option], and it runs more [adverb] for our team."
S-S Question 3 — Debate
Your partner prefers flying for all business trips. You prefer ground transport. Debate for 90 seconds. Use "it saves a lot of time" on one side and "it is not worth the hassle" on the other.
Grammar hint: Concede first, then push back — "Yes, flying is faster, but only slightly for short routes. The savings are far more significant."
Class Question 4 — Discussion
Should companies always choose the cheapest travel option? Or does employee comfort matter too? Use comparatives to build your argument.
Grammar hint: "Comfort is more important than cost because employees arrive much more productively when they are well-rested."
T-S Question 5 — Personal
When you travel for work or leisure, what is the most important factor — speed, price, or comfort? Explain with 2 comparative sentences and 1 colloquial phrase.
Grammar hint: "For me, comfort is far more important than speed. It depends on your priorities, but arriving well-rested is the most valuable outcome."
Class Question 6 — Challenge
You are a travel coordinator. Design the ideal travel policy for routes under 400 km. Present your 3-rule policy to the class using superlatives and intensifiers.
Grammar hint: "Rule 1: The bus is the most cost-effective option for routes under [X] km because it is far more affordable."
"For business travel under 5 hours, the bus is always a better option than the plane."

⚡ Teacher Note — Speaking
Question 3 is most generative: plant disagreement explicitly. "I say flying is always better — who disagrees?" The debate forces students to use intensifiers naturally to express degree ("only slightly faster" vs "far more expensive"). Question 6 (policy design) is the highest cognitive demand — use as leaderboard-earning activity. Score 1 point per comparative, 2 per superlative, 3 per colloquial phrase. Opinion Meter: "55% agree. Find someone who voted differently and convince them to change their mind using today's language."
🎯 This week, use these 3 phrases at work:
Travel and decision language from S3A — ready for emails, meetings and trip planning.
"It is far more [adj] than the other option."
Use "far" to express a big, clear difference. Works for any comparison — cost, speed, comfort, quality. More professional than "it is much better" alone.
"It is not worth the hassle."
Use when the complications of a cheaper option outweigh the savings. Essential in business travel discussions, project decisions and supplier evaluations.
"It depends on your priorities."
Use when two options each have real advantages. Shows analytical thinking — you understand both sides before recommending one.
