Warm-Up — Module 2 Quick-Fire
⏱ 8 min · 90 sec per student · Full M2 activation · Badge ⚡
Layer 1 · Logistics

90-second Quick-Fire activates ALL of Module 2: irregular pasts (S1A — had / went / made / gave / met), regular -ed pasts (S2A — worked / arrived / decided), sequence connectors (S2B — First / Then / After that / Finally), Wh- past questions (S3A — What did you / When did / Why did). Run as a rapid round-robin — never correct mid-answer, mark errors and address after.

Layer 2 · Pedagogical Focus
  • Q1: Irregular past — S1A content (had / went / made / met / gave)
  • Q2: Regular -ed + sequence connectors — S2A + S2B content
  • Q3: Wh- past questions — S3A content (Did + Wh-word patterns)
  • Q4: Business storytelling structure — S4A synthesis
Layer 3 · S5 Purpose

This is not a new lesson — it is a performance session. Students should feel the accumulated weight of 4 sessions. If the warm-up reveals specific gaps (e.g., "didn't went" reappears), note them for targeted correction during the Exit Task feedback. Never re-teach in S5 — redirect: "What did we say about negatives in S4B?"

⚡ QUICK-FIRE · 90 SECONDS PER STUDENT

🔵 Irregular Past — S1A Review
Tell me 4 things that happened in last week's most important meeting. Use 4 different irregular verbs: had / went / gave / met / made / spoke / sent. Each sentence with a past time marker.
Push: "Did the team make any decisions? Who spoke first?"
⭐ Regular Past + Connectors — S2A/S2B Review
Tell me yesterday's morning at work using First, Then, After that, Finally. Use 4 regular -ed verbs: worked / arrived / called / finished / decided / planned.
Push: "What did you finish first? Then what happened?"
🟢 Wh- Past Questions — S3A Review
Interview your partner about a real project from last month. Ask 4 Wh- questions in the past: What did / When did / Why did / How long did. Partner answers in full sentences.
Push: "Who did you work with? How did the project end?"
🟣 Business Storytelling — S4A Synthesis
Tell a 60-second story about a project from last month: setting → first event → main challenge → outcome. Use ≥3 past forms, ≥2 connectors, ≥1 negative with didn't. No notes.
Push: "How did it end? Did everything go as planned?"

Layer 1 · Logistics

Layer 2 · Priority Words for Exit Task

Grammar Review — Full M2 Integration
⏱ 14 min · 3 polls · Activity A (MC) · Activity B (drag & drop) · Hot Seat Dice · Badge 🎯
Layer 1 · Logistics

3 class polls (instant error correction, no shame), Activity A (5 MC mixed M2 grammar), Activity B (2 drag-and-drop scrambled sentences), Hot Seat Dice (6 integration prompts). Badge 🎯 at 4/5 in Activity A. Polls: class votes, reveal, brief correction — 30 seconds each. Never dwell. Keep energy high.

Layer 2 · Error Hierarchy
  • 🔴 P1: "didn't went" / "didn't made" — base verb after didn't, every time
  • 🔴 P2: "When you did go?" — Wh-word + DID + subject + BASE verb
  • 🟡 P3: regular -ed spelling (planed vs planned, tryed vs tried) — flag, correct
  • 🟡 P4: missing connector between events — prompt: "First… then what?"
  • 🟢 P5: missing past time marker — prompt only, don't correct
Layer 3 · S5 Grammar Philosophy

No new grammar in S5. The goal is fluency and confidence with existing forms. A student who says "First we met the client, then we discussed the budget, but in the end we didn't reach an agreement" is demonstrating Module 2 mastery. Celebrate that sentence.

Practice — Sequence Builder
⏱ 8 min · Click the events in order · Build the story
Layer 1 · Logistics

Two short business mini-stories. Students click the scrambled events in chronological order. After ordering, they read the full story aloud using First / Then / After that / Finally. 5-minute total — no slow re-explanation.

Layer 2 · Pedagogical Focus

The ordering is mechanical. The speaking is the goal. Once a student reaches a correct order, immediately prompt: "Now tell me the whole story with First, Then, After that, Finally." That is what activates the Module 2 grammar.

Layer 3 · Hidden Difficulty

Story B includes a Wh- past question embedded in the events — students must produce the question while sequencing. This is the bridge from input (ordering) to output (Wh- production).

STORY A — THE LATE DELIVERY

Drag into chronological order, then retell the story aloud using sequence connectors.

  1. ⠿The client called us at 4 PM, very angry.
  2. ⠿The truck arrived two hours late on Monday morning.
  3. ⠿In the end, we kept the client — but it was a hard day.
  4. ⠿We sent a written apology and a 10% discount.

STORY B — THE NEW HIRE

Order the events. Pay attention — one is a Wh- question.

  1. ⠿HR asked: "How did you find this candidate?"
  2. ⠿We interviewed three candidates last Wednesday.
  3. ⠿Finally, the new hire started on the first of May.
  4. ⠿After that, we made an offer to the best one.

🗣 Speaking Bridge

After both stories are ordered, retell each one aloud with full connectors: "First… Then… After that… Finally…" — using past forms throughout. 60 seconds per story.

Role-Play — Tell Me Your Story
⏱ 10 min · 3 rounds · Real work stories · Full M2 integration
Layer 1 · Logistics

3 rounds of storytelling. Each round increases in complexity. Students use their real job data — no invented scenarios. Push for varied tenses: irregular pasts, regular -ed pasts, negative with didn't, sequence connectors, and at least one Wh- question per role-play exchange. Partner asks 2 Wh- follow-ups per story.

Layer 2 · Pedagogical Focus
  • Round 1: simple narrative — "what happened" with First / Then
  • Round 2: storytelling with Wh- interrogation — partner drives the story
  • Round 3: full business story with outcome ("It went well" / "It didn't go as planned") — bridges directly to Exit Task
Layer 3 · Retention Moment

Ask: "Before Module 2, could you say 'First we met the client, then we discussed the budget, but in the end we didn't reach an agreement'?" Most will say no. Ask them to say it now. That single sentence is the evidence of Module 2.

🗣
Your Module 2 World — Stories, Projects, Past Events
Round 1 — A Simple Past Event (60 sec)
Tell your partner one thing that happened at work yesterday or last week. Use First and Then at minimum. Partner listens and marks: did you use ≥2 past forms?
💡 Starter: "First, I arrived at the office around 8. Then, I had a quick meeting with…"
Round 2 — Story + Wh- Interrogation (90 sec)
A tells a 30-second project story. B asks 3 Wh- past questions: What did / When did / How long did / Why did / Who did. A answers in full sentences. Then swap roles.
💡 Partner B must use the structure: Wh-word + DID + subject + BASE verb.
Round 3 — Full Business Story (2 min)
Tell the story of a real project from last quarter. Use the full structure: Setting → First event → Then → After that → Finally / Outcome. Minimum: 5 past forms, 3 connectors, 1 negative, 1 outcome expression. No notes.
💡 This is a direct rehearsal for the Exit Task. Think of it as your dress rehearsal.

"So, what happened was — last March we had a difficult project with a new client. First, we met the team in Querétaro and we discussed the timeline. Then, the client sent us new requirements every week — that was the main problem. After that, my manager spoke to the director and we renegotiated the scope. Finally, we delivered the project two weeks late, but we didn't lose the client. In the end, it went better than expected — they signed a renewal in May."

Contains: 7 past forms (5 irregular + 2 regular) · 5 connectors · 1 negative · 1 outcome ✅

Good morning, team. I want to tell you the story of the Alpha project — the one we finished last month. It was a difficult one, but I learned a lot.

First, in January, we had a kickoff meeting with the client in Querétaro. The scope was clear and the timeline was short — eight weeks. Then, in week three, our lead engineer left the company. How did we get there? Honestly, we didn't plan for that risk.

After that, the supplier sent the wrong parts twice. We worked all weekend and we called three other vendors. I spoke to the director on a Sunday — that was not a normal day. We made a tough decision: we paused the project for one week.

Why did we pause? Because rushing didn't help. Finally, in week seven, we found a new supplier in León. They delivered on time, the team tested everything, and we went live two days before the deadline.

In the end, the client was happy — they signed a renewal in April. What did I learn? Plan for the people, not just the parts. Long story short — the team really came through.

MULTIPLE CHOICE

1. Where was the kickoff meeting in January?

2. What was the first major problem on the project?

3. Why did Mateo pause the project for one week?

4. What was the final outcome of the Alpha project?

TRUE / FALSE

1. The original Alpha project timeline was eight weeks.

2. The team called only one other vendor when the supplier sent the wrong parts.

3. Mateo spoke to the director on a Sunday.

4. The new supplier in León delivered late.

🗣 Speaking Response — Bridge to Exit Task

Imagine YOU are Mateo. Tell your partner the same story — but use your real project, a real challenge, and a real outcome. 5 sentences minimum. Use: 3 past action verbs, 2 connectors, 1 Wh- self-question, 1 outcome expression.

Starter: "Last month, we had a difficult project. First, we met… Then, the supplier sent… But we didn't… In the end, it went…"

§7 — MODULE EXIT TASK
The 90-Second Business Story — Your Performance
⏱ 18 min · No notes · 90 sec each · Real story · Module evidence
Layer 1 · Logistics

This is the Module 2 Exit Task. Students choose ONE scenario (A, B, or C) and perform a 90-second spoken business story using real data from their job. No notes. Partner marks the rubric in real time. Teacher does NOT interrupt during performance. After: 2-minute feedback using the rubric cards. Badge 🌟 for completing.

Layer 2 · Exit Task Rubric
  • ✅ ≥8 past forms used correctly (mix of irregular + regular -ed)
  • ✅ ≥3 sequence connectors (First / Then / After that / Finally / In the end)
  • ✅ ≥2 Wh- self-questions ("Why did we…?" "What did we learn?")
  • ✅ ≥1 negative with didn't + base verb
  • ✅ ≥1 outcome expression ("It went well" / "It didn't go as planned")
  • ✅ A clear setting → events → outcome arc
Layer 3 · Corporate Evidence Moment

This performance IS the product. The moment a student says "First we met, then we discussed, but in the end we didn't sign — what did we learn? Plan for the people, not just the parts" in fluent English, that is evidence of ROI. Record it (with permission). That 60-second clip is: HR report · Manager update · Program renewal conversation. Never skip this section.

SCENARIO A — A Project That Went Well
Tell the story of a real project from the last 3 months that ended successfully. Open with the setting (when, who, what) → describe 3 events in sequence → close with the positive outcome. Minimum: 8 past forms, 3 connectors, 2 Wh- self-questions, 1 outcome expression.
💡 Use real names, dates, and metrics. The more specific, the more credible.
SCENARIO B — A Difficult Project With a Lesson
Tell the story of a real project that had problems but taught you something. Setting → first challenge → what you tried → what you learned. Use ≥2 negatives with didn't and ≥2 Wh- self-questions ("Why did we…?" / "What did we learn?").
💡 This is professional honesty — the kind of story managers respect in English.
SCENARIO C — An Unexpected Event at Work
Tell the story of an incident, surprise, or change that happened at your company last quarter. Setting → moment of surprise (Then suddenly…) → what people did → outcome. Use vivid past forms and at least one outcome expression.
💡 Opener idea: "So, what happened was — last month, in the middle of an audit…"
