§ 01
Picture Prompt — What Questions Would You Ask?
5–7 min
Pick a photo. You are David Park — you've just seen this situation for the first time. What Wh- questions would you ask? Generate at least 4 different questions using What, When, How long, How often, How many, How far. Start speaking from second one.

The Packed Calendar

👉 Generate 4 Wh- questions about the photo. One of each type: What time / How often / How long / How many or How far — and answer them yourself as if it were your company.
Visible scaffold:
"What time do/does ___?"  ·  "How often do ___?"  ·  "How long does it take to ___?"  ·  "How many ___ do you have?"  ·  "How far is ___?"
Teacher Push — Push for more specific questions
"That question is fine — now do it in 3rd person: 'What time does she start?' instead of 'What time do you start?'"
"Ask a question with 'How long' about the call: 'How long does the meeting usually last?'"
"How many participants are there? 'How many people are on the call?' — answer it."
"Now answer as Ana: use a complete sentence with the answer."
⚡ Quick Win — 20 seconds
Ask 3 quick Wh- questions out loud: "What time does it start? How long does it last? How many people are coming?" Fire them off without pausing.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Show the photo — student starts generating questions immediately, without reading the scaffold first.
  • S4B Picture Prompt has a key difference from S3B and S1B: here the output is questions, not descriptions. That activates a different cognitive circuit — the student has to decide which Wh- to use before building the question.
  • If the student only generates "What time" repeatedly → push: "Do you have 'How often'? And 'How long'?" — without giving the full question.
  • Timing: 5–6 min. §4 is the core.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "In S4A you learned the Wh- questions. Now you're firing them off about a photo without thinking. That's the goal — that the questions come out automatically."
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "In M3 you use 'How long does it take' to welcome clients and give directions. The same Wh- you just used."

🗣️ 3 Phrases for This Week
Three model Wh- questions for real calls. Use them this week. 💬
1
"What time does your team usually start?"
¿A qué hora suele empezar tu equipo?
✅ What time + does + subject + verb. Standard opener for any onboarding call.
2
"How long does the meeting usually last?"
¿Cuánto dura normalmente la reunión?
💼 How long + does + subject + last. Watch out — "last" not "take" for duration of an event.
3
"How many people are on your team?"
¿Cuántas personas hay en tu equipo?
🏆 How many + plural noun + are. Note: "are on" not "have in" — common L1 transfer error.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Listen 1 global → listen 2 shadowing the questions (only David's side) → "Your Turn" in alternating roles.
  • "Your Turn" has 2 micro-roles: David (asks questions) and Ana (answers). Alternate with the student. This is the exact model for §4 Role Play.
  • Shadowing technical note: David's questions have a falling rhythm in English — intonation drops at the end of Wh- questions (different from Spanish). Point it out.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You just shadowed David Park's questions — a US director on a real call. Did you hear the rhythm? That's authentic corporate English."
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "In M5 — Business Communication — you're the one opening calls with these questions. You already have the model. In M5 you also formalize it in emails."

Role Play — 3 Rounds · The Onboarding Call
12–18 min
Round 1 — Guided
With scaffold · 4–5 min
Scenario: You are David Park. You have an onboarding call with Miguel Torres, new coordinator of the Celaya plant. You need to ask him 5 Wh- questions to understand how his team works: schedule, meeting frequency, workday duration, number of people, and distance to the airport.
"Hi Miguel! I have a few questions. First — what time does your team usually start?"
"And how often does the full team meet together?"
"How long does the morning meeting usually last?"
"How many people are on the production team?"
"One more — how far is the plant from the Celaya airport?"
Teacher — Answer as Miguel Torres (new employee)
"We usually start at 7:30 AM — early! The plant opens at 7."
"We have a full team meeting every Monday and a quick check-in on Wednesdays."
"The Monday meeting lasts about an hour. The Wednesday one is only 20 minutes."
"Production has 15 people. Operations has 8. Total about 23 in my area."
"It's about 12 km from the airport — takes 20 minutes by taxi."
Round 2 — Semi-guided
Prompts only · 5–6 min
Scenario: Now the roles flip. It's YOU — with your real company, team, and schedule. David Park asks you Wh- questions about your job. Answer them with your real data and complete sentences. Then, YOU ask David 2 Wh- questions about Phoenix.
David asks about your schedule David asks about your meetings David asks how many people on your team You ask about David's team You ask about the distance to his office
Teacher — David Park asks questions (Round 2)
"What time do you usually wrap up? Is it always at the same time?"
"How often do you have calls with international clients?"
"How many people report directly to you?"
When it's the student's turn: answer as David. "Our office is in downtown Phoenix — about 25 km from the airport." / "We have about 8 people on my direct team."
Round 3 — Free
No help · Timer · 3 min
Scenario: You are the Operations Director of your company. You get a surprise call from Sofía Reyes, new VP of Operations at corporate. She wants to fully understand how your area works — she asks and you answer. Halfway through the call, she asks YOU to ask her about her area in Chicago. 3 minutes nonstop.
Timer
3:00
Teacher — Sofía Reyes asks unexpected questions
"What time do your most important meetings start? Always the same time?"
"How long does your typical workday last? From when to when?"
"How many international clients do you work with regularly?"
Halfway: "Now YOU ask ME about Chicago." — Wait for at least 3 Wh- questions from the student.
Correction protocol: zero during the call. 1 celebration + 1 point at the end. Prioritize if they mix "How long" / "How often."
✅ Self-Evaluation — After Round 3
  • I used at least 4 different Wh- in the role play (What time, How long, How often, How many / How far).
  • I answered with complete sentences — not single words.
  • I generated Wh- questions for my partner without hesitating.
  • I kept the conversation flowing with no long pauses.
  • I completed Round 3 without reading any notes.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: §4 is the core. Protect at least 12 minutes. Round 3 has an extra difficulty — the student has to ASK questions, not just answer them.
  • Round 3 is the most demanding in M1 so far: the student asks and answers Wh- questions in real time, with their own data, no notes. A1.2 S4B mastery = 3 spontaneously generated Wh- questions + complete answers in 3 minutes.
  • Common question error: "How long does it take…?" vs "How often do you…?" — if the student mixes them → recast during Rounds 1–2, not Round 3.
  • Round 3: when the moment comes for the student to ask questions → if they go silent for more than 5 seconds → give a hint: "Ask about the team size, maybe?"
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You just asked and answered business questions in English for 3 minutes. That's a real corporate call. With questions. With answers. With real data."
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "S5A and S5B — the last pair of M1 — integrate EVERYTHING: imperatives, dates, routine, Wh- questions. The full M1 Exit Task is 3 minutes using all of that together."
§ 05
WOW — Corporate Guessing Game
10–12 min
⚡ WOW Component — Guessing Game
Corporate Guessing Game
A corporate answer appears. The student must generate the correct Wh- question that would produce that answer. No peeking at the question! Student guesses first — then reveal. 8 rounds. The key: think in reverse — from answer to question.
Press "Generate" to start
—
What Wh- question would produce this answer?
Round 0 of 8
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Teacher presses Generate — student sees the answer and generates the question out loud before the Reveal.
  • This WOW is radically different from S4A's Question Builder (which builds questions from guided structure): here the student infers the question from the answer — reverse production. Higher cognitive load, more transfer.
  • More than one valid question may produce the same answer. Celebrate correct alternatives: "That one works too! And the model's version is ___."
  • 8 rounds is enough. Keep energy high — fast pace is part of the WOW.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Thinking from answer to question — that's what your brain does in a real conversation when you want to understand something. That's active English."
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "In the module's S5B you complete the Exit Task — 3 minutes with all of M1. This ability to ask questions is what connects S1's small talk with M2's company presentation."
§ 06
Debate — Asking & Knowing · Free Opinion
7–10 min
GT rules: Prioritize flow. If the student spontaneously generates Wh- questions in the debate → flag it as a win. If they go blank → push with a 2-word hint max. 1 celebration + 1 improvement at the end.
Question 1
"In your opinion — what is the most important question to ask a new international partner in the first call? Why?"
Push: "Use any Wh- you learned. 'How many employees'? 'How often do you travel'? 'What time zone are you in'?" — Let the student build their own criterion of importance.
Question 2
"How many meetings is too many? What is the ideal number of meetings per week for a productive team?"
Push: "Use 'How many' with real numbers. What's it like at your company? What does your team say?" — Natural production of How many + real corporate opinion.
Question 3
"If you could ask David Park only ONE question about his team in Phoenix — what would it be? And why that question?"
Personal, creative question. The student generates a Wh- question of their choice and justifies it. The perfect close of the S4A–S4B cycle: from learning the questions to picking the most important one for their real context.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: One question at a time. Listen without interrupting. A student who spontaneously generates Wh- questions in their answers → strongest signal of automaticity.
  • Question 3 closes the module's Wh- cycle: the student who started S4A learning the structure now autonomously chooses what to ask and why. That's linguistic metacognition — worth pointing out.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You generated Wh- questions in an opinion debate in English, with your real company as context. That's no longer basic A1 — that's functional communication."
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "In M2 you use these same questions to talk about your company — 'How many employees do you have? How often do you launch new products?' The vocabulary changes, the question is the same."
