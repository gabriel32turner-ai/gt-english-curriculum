§1
Warm-Up — Spontaneous or Planned?
Activate going to knowledge · Surface the gap that will fills · Connect to real moments at work
⏱ 5–8 MIN
💡 Pedagogical: Students have used "going to" in M4. The goal here is to surface a situation "going to" can't handle — a sudden decision made at the moment of speaking. Don't teach will yet. Just create the gap. Write student attempts on the board and revisit them in §3.
📅 Going to review: Tell your partner ONE thing you were going to do today at work that was already planned before you arrived. Use: "I was going to…" or "I'm going to…"
⚡ Surprise situation: Your phone rings — it's your biggest client. They have a problem. Right now, in this second, what do you say? "Don't worry, I _____ check it immediately." — Which word goes there? Try!
🤝 Offer moment: Your colleague is struggling with their laptop. You decide — right now — to help. How do you offer in English? Try: "_____ help you with that." What word do you use?
⚡ Teacher note: Expect "I going to" or "I'm going to check" — both are understandable but highlight that "going to" means a pre-planned decision. Today students learn a new tool: will. It fills a gap "going to" can't fill — spontaneous decisions and offers.
⚡ Quick Win — 90 seconds
Think of ONE surprise or unexpected moment from work this week. Tell your partner: what happened and what you said or decided at that moment.

Grammar — Will: Full System
Affirmative · Negative · Questions · Short answers · Will vs Going to contrast
⏱ 15–18 MIN
🚨 Real Situation — The Unexpected Call
It's Monday morning. Your phone rings. A client says: "We have a problem with the delivery."

You had no plan for this call. But right now — in this second — you decide to help.
In Spanish: "No te preocupes, lo reviso ahora mismo."
❓ What English word do you use when you decide something at the exact moment of speaking?
✅ The Tool: Will
"I'll check it right now."
"I'll call you back."

Key insight: Use will when you decide at the moment of speaking — the idea was not in your head before this second. Also for offers, promises, and predictions.
FormSubject + WillCorporate ExampleSpanish hint
Affirmative I'll / You'll / He'll / She'll / We'll / They'll I'll check the status right now. Lo reviso ahora.
Negative I won't / You won't / She won't / We won't We won't let you down. No te vamos a fallar.
Question Will + subject + base verb? Will you send the report? ¿Enviarás el reporte?
Short answer Yes, I will. / No, I won't. "Will we solve it?" — "Yes, we will." Sí. / No.
Wh- Question Who/What/When + will + subject + verb? Who will present the timeline? ¿Quién va a presentar?
🔁 CONTRAST: Will vs Going to
Will (spontaneous) Decided NOW — no plan before The phone rings. "I'll check it." → you just decided. Lo decido ahora mismo.
Going to (pre-planned) Decided BEFORE — plan already in head We were going to present a new timeline — already scheduled. Ya tenía el plan antes.
⚠️ Top 4 errors — correct immediately:
✗ "I will to help you" → ✅ I'll help you — no "to" after will!
✗ "I will helping" → ✅ I'll help — will + base verb (no -ing)
✗ "She wills send it" → ✅ She will send it — will never changes for he/she/it
✗ "I'll going to call you" → ✅ EITHER I'll call OR I'm going to call — never both

GT Original — Will in Real Business English
  • 🔊 Contraction note: "I will" always contracts to "I'll" /aɪl/ in natural speech. "Will not" → "won't" /woʊnt/. Teach the contractions as the default forms — not the full forms.
  • ⚠️ Tone of will: "I'll help you" sounds warmer and more spontaneous than "I'm going to help you." Native speakers use will for offers because it sounds natural and immediate.
  • 📊 Will vs Going to summary: Both are future. Will = decided now / offer / promise / prediction. Going to = pre-planned / intention already in mind / visible evidence of future.
  • 🌍 Business use: In client communication, will is the standard for offers and commitments: "I'll send you the proposal." "We won't miss the deadline." "I'll let you know by Friday."

Listening — The Unexpected Client Call
Marco Rivera narrates a spontaneous client situation · Listen for will vs going to · 4 MCQ + 4 TF
⏱ 10–12 MIN
🎯 Pre-listening: Tell students: "You'll hear Marco, a Sales Manager, narrate a client situation. As you listen, count how many times you hear 'will' or 'I'll'. Write the number down." (Answer: many — see script.) After first listen, discuss what the context was before answering questions.
The Unexpected Client Call — Marco Rivera
A2.1 · M5 · S1A · Voice: Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.82 · Single-speaker monologue
Yesterday afternoon I had an unexpected call from one of our biggest clients — Grupo Ávila. They were worried about their delivery timeline. The moment I answered, I said: "Don't worry — I'll check the status right now and I'll call you back in ten minutes." I didn't have a plan — I just decided at that moment. I pulled up the system, saw the delay, and called Ana in logistics. "I'll handle the Ávila account personally," I told her. "Can you send me the full report?" She said yes — she'll send it by noon. Now, I knew this was going to be a difficult situation — we were already going to miss the original deadline. But will we solve it? Yes, we will. I told the client: "We won't let you down. I'll personally oversee the delivery." They calmed down. Next week, we're going to present a new timeline — that was already planned. But today's calls? All spontaneous. All will. That's the difference.
📎 ElevenLabs: Voice — Sarah (American English) · Speed 0.82 · Pitch 1.0 · A2.1 M5 S1A · ~175 words
✏️ Listening — Multiple Choice (4 questions)
Q1. Why did Marco receive the unexpected call?
Q2. What did Marco say he would do immediately?
Q3. What is Ana going to send?
Q4. What is planned for next week?
✏️ Listening — True or False (4 statements)
💡 Layer 1/2/3: Layer 1 — students answer individually. Layer 2 — compare with partner. Layer 3 — justify in English: "I said False because Marco said he decided at that moment / had no plan."
TF1. Marco had a plan before he called the client back.
TF2. Ana is going to send the full report by noon.
TF3. Marco told the client they would not let them down.
TF4. The new timeline presentation was a spontaneous decision.
🎭 Post-listening discussion: Ask: "Which sentences in the script used will? Which used going to? Why?" Have students find the two going to examples (was going to be / going to present) and explain the difference vs the will examples.
§6
Role-Play — Will in Real Situations
3 rounds · Surprise offers · Client role-play · Full spontaneous scenario
⏱ 12–15 MIN
💡 Delivery tip: Round 1 is teacher-led — you describe a situation, students respond with a will offer in 5 seconds. Round 2 is pair work. Round 3 is performance — one student speaks for 60 seconds straight using will throughout.
Round 1 — 5 Surprise Situations (Teacher-Led)
Teacher reads each situation. Students must respond with a will offer or spontaneous decision in 5 seconds. No preparation — just like real life!
💡 Situations: (1) "Your colleague spills coffee on their keyboard." (2) "The client asks: Can someone check the order number?" (3) "Your manager can't find the report file." (4) "The projector stops working during a presentation." (5) "A client says they haven't received the invoice."
🎭 Expected responses: (1) I'll get some paper towels / I'll help you. (2) I'll check it right now. (3) I'll find it for you. (4) I'll restart the computer. (5) I'll resend it immediately. Accept any correct spontaneous will. Gently correct any "I'm going to" — ask: "Did you plan this before?"
Round 2 — Pair Role-Play: Client Problem
Partner A is the client. Partner B is the sales manager (like Marco). The client has a problem. Partner B must respond with at least 3 will offers and 1 promise with won't.
💡 Client problems: "Your delivery is 5 days late." / "I never received the revised proposal." / "The technical support team isn't answering." — Then switch roles.
"I'm really sorry to hear that. I'll check the delivery status right now. I'll also call the logistics team immediately. And I'll personally send you a written update within the hour. We won't let this happen again — I promise."
⭐ Round 3 — Full Scenario: The Unexpected Call
You are a sales manager. Your biggest client calls unexpectedly with an urgent problem: their order was shipped to the wrong address. Respond naturally using will throughout — make offers, promises, and decisions as they come to you. Speak for 60 seconds minimum. No notes!
💡 Challenge: Include will, won't, a will question, and mention at least one thing that WAS going to happen (pre-planned) that now needs to change.

🎯 This week at work
3 Will Phrases You Can Use Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"I'll take care of it."
The most powerful three words in business English. Use whenever you're stepping up to solve a problem — in any email, call, or meeting.
PHRASE 2
"I'll let you know."
Professional, warm, and natural. Use to promise information. Better than "I send you" (wrong) or "I'm going to tell you" (too formal).
PHRASE 3
"We won't let you down."
A high-impact client promise. Pair it with a specific action: "We won't let you down — I'll personally oversee this delivery."
