🔥 Q1 — Recall: Without looking at notes — what are the two auxiliary verbs for asking about likes? When do you use each one? Say the rule in one sentence.
👤 Q2 — 3rd Person: Tell your partner 2 things your manager or colleague likes. Use complete sentences with the correct form. Partner checks: -s at the end?
❌ Q3 — Negative: Say 2 things YOU don't like at work (food, meetings, tasks…) AND 2 things your manager doesn't like. All 4 sentences in English. Go.
✅ Q4 — Short Answer Drill: Teacher asks rapid-fire: "Does your manager like [food]?" Students answer ONLY with short answers: "Yes, she does." / "No, she doesn't." 10 questions in 60 seconds.
⚡ Teacher Note — S2B Opening

S2B opens with zero new content — it's all production from memory. The goal of Q4 is speed + accuracy. If you hear "Yes, she does like" or "No, she doesn't likes" — stop the clock, correct immediately, restart. The error to kill: "No, she doesn't likes." Say: "S2A was the map. S2B is the road trip. No looking at the map today."

§2 — Pitch / Debate Round
The Lunch Debate — Who Wins?
3 rounds · Structured argument · Do/Does in real opinion context · No notes allowed
⏱ 8–10 MIN
S-SRound 1 — Debate: Mexican vs International
Partner A: "My team only likes Mexican food — it's the best for a work lunch." Partner B: You disagree. Argue for international food using Do/Does questions to prove your point. Example: "Does everyone like spicy food? No, they don't. So we need options." 2 minutes each.
💡 Use: "Does [person] like…? No, they don't." / "I don't like… and she doesn't either." / "Do you really think everyone likes…?"

"I disagree." My US colleague doesn't like spicy food at all. Does Carlos like rice every day? Maybe he does. But do all visitors like the same thing? No, they don't. A good lunch works for everyone — that means options. I prefer a mix.

T-SRound 2 — Teacher Challenge
Teacher picks one student and challenges them: "Convince me that [salad / coffee / tea / juice] is the best option for the whole team." Student must use at least 3 Do/Does sentences in their 60-second pitch. Class judges: did they use the grammar correctly?
💡 Student must include: 1 affirmative, 1 negative, 1 question. Class counts them out loud. Teacher confirms.

"Salad is perfect for everyone." Does our manager like heavy food at lunch? No, she doesn't. Do we like feeling tired after eating? I don't think we do. I like salad every day — and my colleague likes it too. It's fast, healthy, and everyone is happy. Does that work for the team? Yes, it does.

ClassRound 3 — Class Consensus
Class votes: What does EVERYONE in this room like AND not like for a team lunch? Each student says 1 sentence using "We all like…" or "Nobody likes…" or "Some people like…". Teacher writes results on board — all sentences must use correct Do/Does form.
💡 Challenge: Can anyone produce a sentence using ALL 4 forms in one breath? "I like salad, he likes chicken, she doesn't like tea, and they don't like spicy food."

Class board example: "We all like coffee." / "Nobody likes cold food." / "Some people like salad, but others don't." / "Does our manager like the meetings? No, she doesn't — she likes results." (This last one gets a laugh — use it to break tension and reinforce the grammar.)

⚡ Teacher Note — Debate Energy

Keep Round 1 moving — don't let it become a long speech. Stop at 90 seconds per side. In Round 2, the class counting Do/Does sentences out loud is the activity — not just listening. Round 3 is the emotional high point: seeing the board fill up with THEIR sentences, all grammatically correct, is the proof of learning.

§3 — Relay Round
The Do/Does Relay
Team relay game · Each card = one speaking prompt · Earn a point per correct sentence · Badge b1
⏱ 10–12 MIN
🔥 Do/Does Relay — Click for next prompt
Card 1 of 10 · Points: 0
DO / DOES
Ask your partner if they like salad. Wait for a short answer. Then report: "[Name] likes / doesn't like salad."
Tap the card or click NEXT for the next prompt
Teacher Note — How to run the relay

Divide class into 2 teams. Each card is projected on screen (or teacher reads it). First student to answer correctly gets the point — click "+1 Point" to track. Teacher awards the point only if the Do/Does form is 100% correct. Fast-paced: 10 cards in 10 minutes. Winning team gets a symbolic prize (bragging rights, or first choice for next week's class time).

★ GT Original — Why the Relay Works at A1.1

At A1.1 the biggest obstacle to fluency is not knowledge — it's HESITATION. The relay creates mild pressure that forces automaticity. A student who hesitates 4 seconds before "Does she like?" in normal conversation will get it right in 2 seconds under relay pressure. That compression IS the learning. Run it fast. Celebrate every correct answer loudly.

§5 — Solo Challenge
The 90-Second Lunch Coordinator
Individual performance · No notes · Full Do/Does production · Real workplace scenario · Badge b3
⏱ 8–10 MIN
⭐ Challenge — No Notes · 90 Seconds
You are organizing a team lunch for a visit from your US office. Your manager asks you to present: "What does your team like? What don't they like? What do YOU recommend for lunch?" Speak for 90 seconds using Do/Does, likes, doesn't like, and at least 4 foods from today's vocabulary.
💡 Structure: "My team likes… My manager likes… but she doesn't like… I don't like… I recommend [food] because… Do we all like it? Yes, we do!" · Minimum: 6 sentences · Use 3rd person at least 3 times.
90 seconds · Complete the challenge → earn badge ⚡
Teacher Note — Solo Rubric (mental checklist)

While student speaks, count silently: (1) Did they use does/doesn't at least 3×? (2) Did they use do/don't at least 2×? (3) Did they avoid "Does she likes"? (4) Did they use 4+ foods? If yes to all → badge. If 3/4 → "Almost — one more try on Q5 next class." Give feedback in English: "You said 'does she likes' once — change it to 'does she like'. Everything else was excellent."

§6 — Listening
The Team Survey — Ana Reports
Ana García reports the results of her team lunch survey · Full Do/Does in context · Monologue
⏱ 8–10 MIN
Ana's Lunch Survey Report — Vertex Logistics, Celaya
A1.1 · M5 · S2B · Voice: Lauren (American English) · Speed: 0.78 · Monologue
0:00 / 0:00
Good morning. My name is Ana García and I am the project coordinator at Vertex Logistics here in Celaya. Last week I did a lunch survey for our team. I want to share the results with you today.

First, my colleague Carlos. Does Carlos like salad? No, he doesn't. He doesn't like salad at all. But he likes chicken and rice — every day, same thing. Does he like juice? Yes, he does. He likes orange juice in the morning.

My colleague María is different. Does María like salad? Yes, she does — she loves it. She doesn't like chicken, and she doesn't like spicy food. What does she like to drink? She likes tea. She doesn't like coffee.

And my manager, Daniel. Does Daniel like the team lunches? Yes, he does. He likes everything — chicken, salad, rice, bread. Does he like tea or coffee? He likes both. He needs coffee in the morning and tea in the afternoon.

What do I like? I like salad and fruit. I don't like rice every day — it's too much. Do I like the survey results? Yes, I do. Now I know exactly what to order for our next team lunch. Does everyone agree? I think they do. See you next week!
📎 ElevenLabs: Voice — Lauren (American English) · Speed 0.78 · Pitch 1.0 · ~175 words · ~1 min monologue · A1.1 M5 S2B

Can / Can't — Ability & Requests
What you can and can't do at work · Asking for things politely · Skills inventory · "Can I get a…?" in context
🎯 This week at work
3 Phrases You Can Use Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"Does [name] like [food]? Yes, she does."
Use this exact exchange when coordinating lunch for a visitor, planning a team order, or just making small talk with an international colleague. It sounds completely natural and professional — and you already know it cold.
PHRASE 2
"She doesn't like [food] — she prefers [food]."
When you coordinate for someone else — a US visitor, a new teammate, a client — this sentence makes you sound organized and considerate. Two grammar structures in one: doesn't like + prefers. Use it at the next team event.
PHRASE 3
"I need a [marker/folder/stapler] — do you have one?"
Say this in English every time you need an office object this week. Add the question at the end — "do you have one?" — and you've got a complete, natural mini-conversation in English. 11 words. Zero grammar stress.
