Vocabulary: Question & Negotiation Phrases
8 flip cards · Business questions with going to · 3-question vocab quiz
⏱ 8–10 MIN
🃏 Instruction: Click each card to flip it and see the meaning + business example. Students work through the cards before the quiz. Focus on the function of each question — what it does in a negotiation.
Y/N Question
Are you going to attend?
Tap to flip
Asking if someone plans to be present at an event.
"Are you going to attend the budget review on Thursday?" — "Yes, I am."
/ ¿Vas a asistir?
Y/N Question
Is she going to present?
Tap to flip
Asking about another person's planned action. Uses IS for he/she/it.
"Is she going to present the Q3 data?" — "No, she isn't. Daniel is."
/ ¿Va a presentar ella?
Y/N Question
Are they going to reschedule?
Tap to flip
Asking about a group's future plan. Uses ARE for they/we/you.
"Are they going to reschedule the kick-off?" — "Yes, they are."
/ ¿Van a reprogramar?
Wh- Question
What are you going to do?
Tap to flip
Asking about the action or task someone plans to perform.
"What are you going to do about the delay?" — "I'm going to contact the supplier today."
/ ¿Qué vas a hacer?
Wh- Question
Who is going to lead it?
Tap to flip
Asking about which person will be responsible for something.
"Who is going to lead the client onboarding?" — "Carlos is going to lead it."
/ ¿Quién lo va a dirigir?
Wh- Question
When is she going to call?
Tap to flip
Asking about the time of a planned action.
"When is Valeria going to call the client?" — "She's going to call this afternoon."
/ ¿Cuándo va a llamar?
Wh- Question
Where are we going to meet?
Tap to flip
Negotiating the location of a planned meeting or event.
"Where are we going to meet for the review?" — "We're going to meet in Room 3B."
/ ¿Dónde nos vamos a reunir?
Wh- Question
Why are they going to change it?
Tap to flip
Asking for the reason behind a planned decision or change.
"Why are they going to change the process?" — "Because the current system is too slow."
/ ¿Por qué lo van a cambiar?
Wh- Question
How long is this going to take?
Tap to flip
Asking about the duration of a planned project or task.
"How long is the migration going to take?" — "It's going to take about four weeks."
/ ¿Cuánto tiempo va a tardar?

§3
Grammar: Forming Questions with Going To
Y/N form · Wh- form · Short answers · Error correction · MC poll · Question builder drag-drop
⏱ 18–22 MIN
💡 Layer 2 — Grammar Input: Present the tables one at a time. Cover Y/N first (just inversion), then Wh- (add wh-word before the inverted auxiliary). Short answers MUST be drilled orally — students often want to say "Yes, I going to" or "Yes, I do." Stress the pronoun + be form.
Grammar Rule 1 — Y/N Questions

Affirmative: Subject + am/is/are + going to + infinitive
Y/N Question: Am/Is/Are + Subject + going to + infinitive?

AuxiliarySubjectGoing to + VerbExampleShort Answer
Areyougoing to attend?Are you going to attend the meeting?Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.
Ishe / she / itgoing to present?Is she going to present the results?Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.
Arewe / they / you (pl)going to reschedule?Are they going to reschedule?Yes, they are. / No, they aren't.
IsCarlosgoing to lead it?Is Carlos going to lead the team?Yes, he is. / No, he isn't.
Grammar Rule 2 — Wh- Questions

Formula: Wh-word + am/is/are + Subject + going to + infinitive?
The Wh-word comes FIRST, then the inverted auxiliary.

Wh-wordAuxiliarySubjectGoing to + VerbFull Example
Whatareyougoing to do?What are you going to do about the delay?
Whois—going to lead it?Who is going to lead the development?
Whenisshegoing to call?When is Valeria going to call the client?
Wherearewegoing to meet?Where are we going to meet for the kick-off?
Whyaretheygoing to change it?Why are they going to change the system?
How longisthisgoing to take?How long is this project going to take?
📌 Going to vs. Simple Present for fixed schedules: "The meeting starts at 9" (fixed, timetabled — Simple Present) vs. "We are going to discuss the budget" (our plan/intention — Going To). Both can refer to the future, but the function differs.
Simple Present (fixed schedule)Going To (plan / intention)
The flight departs at 6 am.We're going to take the early flight.
The conference starts next Monday.I'm going to present on day two.
The shop opens at 9.Are you going to visit the shop?
Layer 2 — Error Correction
ERRORS TO SPOT: Find the mistake in each sentence. The correct form follows in brackets.

1. "What you are going to do?" → Correct: What ARE you going to do? (auxiliary before subject)
2. "Is they going to reschedule?" → Correct: Are they going to reschedule? (they → ARE, not IS)
3. "Yes, I going to." → Correct: Yes, I am. (short answers use pronoun + be only)
4. "Who are going to lead?" → Correct: Who IS going to lead? (WHO = singular → IS)
5. "When she is going to call?" → Correct: When IS she going to call? (wh-word first, THEN inversion)

Listening: Daniel's Project Kickoff Monologue
Single speaker · All 6 Wh- types + 2 Y/N · ≥3 short answers embedded · 4 MCQ + 4 TF
⏱ 15–18 MIN
🎧 Listening setup: Single speaker: Daniel Flores, Team Lead, speaking to himself (thinking aloud). Voice: Sarah AmE @ 0.82×. Play once without stopping for gist, then play again pausing at each rhetorical question for students to notice the form. Highlight: every wh-question leads directly into its going-to answer.
Daniel Flores — Project Kickoff Reflection
Team Lead, Solaris Tech · AmE @ 0.82× · ~175 words · ElevenLabs: Sarah
"Okay — let me think through this project out loud. First: what are we going to do, exactly? We're going to redesign the client onboarding flow. The current process is too slow — clients are waiting five days for their first login. Now — when are we going to start? We're going to kick off next Monday. I've already confirmed it with the team. Where are we going to work? We're going to work remotely, but we're going to meet in the office every Friday for reviews. Who is going to lead the development? Carlos is going to lead it — he handled a similar project last year. Why are we going to change the current system? Because we lost three clients last quarter due to onboarding delays. And finally — how long is this going to take? It's going to take approximately eight weeks. Are we going to involve the clients in testing? Yes, we are — we're going to run two rounds of user testing in weeks five and six. I'm confident this is going to work. Let's get started."

§6
Role-Play: Negotiate with Questions
3 rounds · Weekend plans · Meeting negotiation · Full 2-min negotiation scenario
⏱ 15–18 MIN
💬 Layer 3 — Production: These rounds move from controlled (Round 1 — personal) to semi-controlled (Round 2 — business negotiation) to free production (Round 3 — full scenario). Monitor for correct question word order and short answer forms. Jot errors for feedback after Round 3.
Round 1 — Weekend Plans (3 min)
Ask your partner 5 going-to questions about their weekend. Use at least 3 different question words: Are you going to…? / What are you going to…? / Where are you going to…? / Who are you going to…? / When are you going to…?
Partner answers with full going-to sentences OR short answers: "Yes, I am — I'm going to…" / "No, I'm not — I'm going to… instead."
A: "Are you going to do anything special this weekend?" B: "Yes, I am — I'm going to visit my sister in Monterrey." A: "What are you going to do there?" B: "We're going to go hiking." A: "When are you going to leave?" B: "I'm going to leave on Saturday morning."
Round 2 — Meeting Negotiation (4 min)
Student A wants to schedule a project review. Student B has questions. B asks: Who is going to attend? / When are you going to schedule it? / Where are we going to meet? / How long is it going to take? / Are you going to invite the client?
Student A answers each question using going to. Switch roles after 2 minutes.
B: "Who is going to attend the review?" A: "The lead team is going to attend — about five people." B: "How long is it going to take?" A: "It's going to take approximately one hour." B: "Are you going to invite the client?" A: "No, we aren't — this is an internal review."
Round 3 — Full Negotiation (2 min per pair)
Scenario: Your team has just received a new project — a product launch. Use ALL question types to negotiate the plan with your partner. Cover: WHAT (deliverable), WHO (responsibilities), WHEN (timeline), WHERE (location), WHY (reason), HOW LONG (duration).
Use short answers when responding: "Yes, we are." / "No, she isn't." / "Yes, I am — and I'm going to..."
Scenario card: Product = "SmartTrack Pro" (a project tracking app). Launch date: in 6 weeks. Budget approved. Team: 4 people.

Sample questions: "What are we going to deliver first?" / "Who is going to manage the client presentations?" / "When are we going to start the beta test?" / "Why are we going to launch in six weeks instead of eight?" / "How long is the user testing going to take?" / "Are we going to present the demo at the conference?"
🔍 Error focus for feedback: (1) Wrong auxiliary (IS/ARE confusion with subject) · (2) Missing inversion: "What you are going to do?" · (3) Short answers with full verbs: "Yes, I am going to." Write 3 errors on the board anonymously — students self-correct before you confirm.
