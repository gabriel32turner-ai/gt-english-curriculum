Hot Take — "I'll Take Care of It."
Instant opinion · Use will under pressure · 60 seconds · b1 badge
⏱ 5 MIN
Context: Your colleague is overwhelmed with three urgent tasks at once. Without thinking, you say: "Don't worry — I'll take care of it."
"Saying 'I'll take care of it' is always a promise you can't keep. Agree or disagree — you have 60 seconds."

💡 Layer 1 — Accuracy focus: Mark every "will" and "won't" use. Common B-session errors: "I will to take" (extra 'to'), "I'll taking" (wrong form), using "going to" instead of "will" for the spontaneous commitment. If they use "going to", gently redirect: "That's a plan you already had — but this is a reaction in the moment. Use 'will'."
💰 Layer 3 — Retention: Ask students to think of a real moment this week when they said something like "no problem, I'll handle it" in Spanish. That's the perfect will context. Challenge: say it in English next time it happens at work.
§2
Offer Debate — Who Handles the Client?
2 students · Each defends their offer approach · Must use will + won't in arguments
⏱ 10 MIN
Scenario: A major client just emailed at 6 PM to say the delivery report has errors. The team has two competing approaches to fix the situation. You are assigned one side — defend your approach using will and won't.
Side A — Respond Now
"I'll call the client right now. I'll explain the error myself. I won't wait until tomorrow — that will make it worse."
  • "I'll take responsibility. The client will appreciate the fast response."
  • "I won't send just an email — I'll call. A call will feel more personal."
  • "Will you back me up if I need help with the numbers?"
  • "This won't happen again. I'll set up a new review process."
Side B — Respond Tomorrow
"I won't call at 6 PM — that will annoy the client. I'll prepare a full corrected report tonight. I'll send it first thing tomorrow with a clear explanation."
  • "A rushed call tonight won't solve it. I'll send a proper response at 8 AM."
  • "I'll prepare all the data so the client will see exactly what happened."
  • "Won't a calm, documented response be better than a panicked call?"
  • "I'll draft a template so this won't happen to any client again."
🎭 Layer 2 — Engagement: Assign sides randomly — even if a student personally agrees with the other side. 3 minutes per team. Encourage cross-team responses: "You said you'll call, but won't that panic the client at 6 PM?" Listen for will/won't precision — both forms must appear in arguments.
💡 Pedagogical focus: Watch for students who make predictions with will (correct!) vs plans with going to (also fine, but flag it: "Is this a plan you already had, or a spontaneous decision right now?"). Both can coexist in this scenario.

§3
Relay Offers — The Client Complaint Chain
Team relay · Each student adds a will offer to handle the situation · No repeats
⏱ 8 MIN
Situation: The client called and said: "The invoice has three errors, the delivery was two days late, and nobody answered my emails last week." Your whole team is in the room. Each person adds one will offer — no repeats allowed. Build the chain.
RELAY CARD 1 — STARTER
Student 1 starts: address the invoice error.
Model: "I'll correct the invoice and send a new version within the hour."
RELAY CARD 2 — APOLOGY
Student 2: apologize for the late delivery and offer a concrete action.
Model: "I'll personally apologize to the client and I'll send a discount on their next order."
RELAY CARD 3 — EMAIL FOLLOW-UP
Student 3: explain what you'll do so emails aren't missed again.
Model: "I'll create a dedicated inbox for this client and I'll check it every morning."
RELAY CARD 4 — PREVENTION
Student 4: promise something that will prevent this situation in future.
Model: "I'll set up a weekly review process. This won't happen again."
RELAY CARD 5 — CLOSING OFFER
Student 5: close with a final offer + a will question to the client.
Model: "I'll schedule a call on Friday to confirm everything is resolved. Will that work for you?"
💡 Layer 1: Each student should produce at least one will affirmative, and the chain should include at least one "won't" and one will question by the end. If the group is small, students take 2 cards each. Track: is their "I'll" spontaneous/responsive (correct!) or pre-planned (should be "I'm going to")?
🎭 Layer 2 — Variation: For a challenge, have students do the relay without looking at the model answers. After the full chain, play it back: teacher reads each offer aloud and class votes thumbs up/down on whether each one sounds natural and professional.

🎭 Layer 2 — Variation: After a student answers, ask: "Is this a will offer, a spontaneous decision, or a promise?" Students should be able to name the function. This builds metalinguistic awareness without slowing fluency.
§5
Solo Challenge — Handle It
60 seconds · Surprise client complaint · will/won't only · b3 badge
⏱ 8 MIN
You are the account manager. You just got this call from a client you've never spoken to before:

"Good afternoon. I'm calling because we ordered 500 units three weeks ago, nobody answered our emails, and the invoice we received doesn't match what we agreed. I'm very unhappy and I need someone to fix this today." You have 60 seconds to respond using only will/won't. No going to, no simple present plans. React in the moment.
✅ 5+ will sentences — at least 5 uses of will or I'll in your response
❌ 1+ won't — at least one promise about what won't happen
❓ 1 will question — ask the client at least one question using will
🚫 No Spanish filler — no "este...", "o sea", or translation pauses
60
Model Response (teacher reference — reveal after student speaks)
"I'm so sorry — I'll take full responsibility for this. I'll look at your order right now while we're on the call. I won't let this go unresolved today, I promise. I'll check the invoice against your original agreement and I'll send you the corrected version within 30 minutes. Will you give me your email address so I can send it directly to you? This won't happen again — I'll set up a direct point of contact for your account starting today."
Tally: will ×7, won't ×2, will question ×1 — above rubric. Flag if student mixes "going to" for plans (acceptable) vs "will" for spontaneous responses (required).
💰 Layer 3 — Retention & upsell: Frame it: "This is the call you will get at work in the next 3 months. You just handled it in English." Ask students to record a voice memo of their response after class to share at next session. This creates accountability and authentic practice evidence.
§6
Source Analysis — Will in the Wild
Business email using will · Identify function: spontaneous / offer / promise · Evaluate appropriateness
⏱ 8 MIN
📧 Email — Client Escalation Response
Subject: RE: Invoice Discrepancy — Urgent

Hi Carlos,

Thank you for reaching out. I will personally review the invoice today and I'll send you the corrected version by 5 PM. I won't let this affect your relationship with us — your account is our priority.

If there's anything else that needs urgent attention, I'll handle it directly. Will you be available for a brief call at 4 PM to confirm the details?

Best regards,
Valeria Torres · Account Manager
spontaneous decision offer promise
🤔 Identify the function of each highlighted "will": is it a spontaneous decision, an offer, or a promise? How many of each? Why does the email sound professional — and what would change if Valeria had used "going to" instead?
Analysis Key
"I will personally review" — spontaneous decision (made in response to the email, not pre-planned)
"I'll send you the corrected version" — offer + promise (commits to a deliverable)
"I won't let this affect" — promise (reassurance to the client)
"I'll handle it" — offer (volunteering to take responsibility)
"Will you be available" — will question (requesting availability — formal, professional tone)
Discussion Questions
1. If Valeria wrote "I'm going to review the invoice" — does the meaning change? (Yes: it implies she had already planned to do it, which is odd if she just received the email.)
2. "Won't" in formal email — appropriate or too casual? (Appropriate — "won't" + reassurance = professional commitment.)
3. What extra will sentence could Valeria add to make the email even stronger?
💡 Layer 1 — Metacognition: The goal is to move beyond production to awareness: students should be able to LABEL will uses, not just produce them. This skill (metalinguistic awareness) is what separates B1 speakers from A2 speakers. Spend 3 minutes on question 1 — the will vs going to contrast here is the core Module 5 insight.

🎯 This week at work
3 Will Phrases You Can Use Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"I'll take care of it."
The most powerful offer in corporate English. Use when a colleague or manager is overloaded. Spontaneous, clear, professional. Replaces "yo me encargo de eso" with native-level will fluency.
PHRASE 2
"I won't let this affect [the client / the project / you]."
The professional promise. Use when something goes wrong and you need to reassure someone. Combines won't with a clear object — sounds decisive, not defensive.
PHRASE 3
"Will you send me the details?"
The professional will question. More formal and direct than "Can you?" in a business context. Use in emails, calls, and meetings. Works as a polite instruction.
