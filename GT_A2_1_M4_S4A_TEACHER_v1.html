Pitching a Project Plan
Integrate the full going to system — affirmative, negative, Wh- questions, Yes/No questions, short answers — with future time expressions and going to vs Simple Present contrast. Deliver a structured 90-second project pitch in English.
§1 — Warm-Up
Review & Real-World Activation
Quick going-to review · Connect to your real work week · Activate future thinking
5–7 MIN
📊 Q1 — Review (T→S): Look at this sentence: “She going to present the plan tomorrow.” What is wrong? Fix it and say it out loud. Hint: going to always needs the correct form of be.
💬 Q2 — Negative (S→S): Ask your partner: “What is one thing you are not going to do at work next week?” Answer with a full negative going-to sentence. Example: “I’m not going to attend the Friday standup.”
🧠 Q3 — Wh- Question (T→S): Teacher asks: “What are you going to do next week at work?” Student gives a real answer — minimum 2 going-to sentences with a future time expression each.
⚡ Teacher note: S4A is the capstone feeder — all M4 grammar integrates here. This warm-up surfaces gaps fast. Listen for: missing auxiliary (I going to), wrong contraction (I amn’t going to), going to + -ing (I’m going to launching). Note 2–3 errors. Use them as live examples in §3.
⚡ Quick Win — 30 seconds
Say this out loud right now: “Next [day/week/month], I’m going to [real action from your job].” Real situation. Say it now!
§2 — Vocabulary & Phrases
Business Pitch Phrases
10 going-to phrases for professional project pitches · Click to flip · Earn badge on quiz
10 MIN

Click each card to flip and see the explanation. Use these in your pitch today and in your real work next week.

“We’re going to launch…”
Opening a pitch with the main action
Tap to flip
Use to introduce the central project action. Subject + be going to + base verb.
Example: “We’re going to launch the new supplier platform in Q3.”
Vamos a lanzar…
“The goal is going to be…”
Stating the objective in the pitch
Tap to flip
Use to present the project objective. Going to + be + noun phrase.
Example: “The goal is going to be a 20% reduction in processing time.”
El objetivo va a ser…
“We’re not going to exceed the budget.”
Addressing concern about cost
Tap to flip
Negative going to to reassure stakeholders. Be + not going to + base verb.
Example: “We’re not going to exceed the budget. We have a contingency plan.”
No vamos a sobrepasar el presupuesto.
“Who is going to lead this?”
Asking about project ownership
Tap to flip
Wh- going-to question. Wh-word + be + subject + going to + base verb.
Example: “Who is going to lead the implementation team?”
¿Quién va a liderar esto?
“Are you going to present to the board?”
Yes/No question about a plan
Tap to flip
Yes/No question: Are/Is + subject + going to + base verb.
Short answers: “Yes, we are.” / “No, we’re not.”
¿Van a presentar a la directiva?
“We’re going to implement the system by the end of July.”
Timeline commitment with future time expression
Tap to flip
Going to + deadline phrase. “By the end of [month]” = deadline. Very useful in project pitches.
Other options: “by next Friday” / “by Q4”
Vamos a implementar el sistema antes de finales de julio.
“The kickoff meeting is on Monday — we’re going to use it to assign roles.”
Going to vs Simple Present contrast
Tap to flip
Simple Present = fixed schedule (the meeting is on Monday — it’s in the calendar). Going to = intention/plan (we’re going to use it for something specific).
La reunión de inicio es el lunes — vamos a usarla para asignar roles.
“What are we going to need?”
Rhetorical self-question in a pitch
Tap to flip
Rhetorical Wh- question used by speakers to guide the audience through the pitch. Creates engagement.
Follow up: “We’re going to need three things: a budget, a team, and a deadline.”
¿Qué vamos a necesitar?
“We’re going to review progress every two weeks.”
Describing recurring check-ins in the plan
Tap to flip
Going to describes a planned recurring action. Different from Simple Present (“We review progress every two weeks” = current routine, already established).
Vamos a revisar el avance cada dos semanas.
“I’m going to confirm the final schedule next month.”
Closing commitment with a future time expression
Tap to flip
Strong closing line for a pitch. Going to + future time expression = clear, confident commitment.
Also: “in Q3” / “by next Monday” / “in two weeks”
Voy a confirmar el calendario definitivo el mes próximo.

1. To say a meeting is in the calendar (fixed), use…
2. “We’re not going to exceed the budget.” This is…
3. Complete: “_____ are you going to lead the implementation?”

§3 — Grammar Integration
The Full Going-To System
All going-to forms at a glance · Going to vs Simple Present contrast · Poll + drag-and-drop
12 MIN
Form Structure Example Notes
Affirmative Subject + am/is/are going to + base verb We’re going to launch the platform next month. Contraction preferred in speech: I’m / we’re / she’s
Negative Subject + am/is/are + not going to + base verb We’re not going to exceed the budget. Contraction: isn’t / aren’t. No “don’t going to”
Yes/No Question Am/Is/Are + subject + going to + base verb? Are you going to present to the board? Short answers: Yes, we are. / No, we’re not.
Wh- Question Wh-word + am/is/are + subject + going to + base verb? What are we going to need? / Who is going to lead this? Wh-words: What / When / Where / Who / Why / How
Short Answers Yes/No + subject + am/is/are (+ not) Are they going to confirm? — Yes, they are. / No, they aren’t. Never repeat “going to” in the short answer
vs Simple Present Simple Present = fixed, scheduled (calendar fact) The kickoff is on Monday. (calendar)   We’re going to use it to assign roles. (plan) SP = timetable/schedule. Going to = intention/plan.
🎯 Poll: “The conference _____ on Thursday. We _____ use it to announce the plan.” Which combination is correct?
❌ Common Going-To Errors — Fix Them
Errors you will hear today:
“She going to present.” → She’s going to present. (missing is)
“We are going to launched the product.” → We’re going to launch the product. (base verb, no -ed)
“What we going to do?” → What are we going to do? (inversion required)
“I’m not going to to confirm.” → I’m not going to confirm. (no double “to”)
“Don’t going to” → isn’t / aren’t going to (no “don’t” with going to)
✏️ Activity — Drag to Build a Correct Going-To Sentence

Drag the pieces into the correct professional order to make a pitch sentence.

  1. ⨟implement the system
  2. ⨟by the end of July.
  3. ⨟We’re going to
  4. ⨟and confirm all roles
  1. ⨟the schedule
  2. ⨟going to change
  3. ⨟We’re not
  4. ⨟without board approval.
💡 Layer 1 — Pedagogical: At S4, students should produce going-to fluently. Focus correction on the contrast row (Simple Present vs going to) — this is the new concept. The other forms were drilled in S1–S3. If a student defaults to “will” for plans, gently redirect: “That’s a prediction. For a plan you already decided, we use going to.”
💡 Layer 2 — Error watch: “The meeting is going to be on Monday” is not wrong — but “The meeting is on Monday” (Simple Present) is more natural for fixed schedule items. Both are acceptable; train students to recognise the register difference.
§4 — Pitch Structure
The 90-Second Project Pitch
4-step planning framework · What? / When? / Who? / Why? · Plan before you speak
10 MIN
The 90-Second Pitch Formula
A professional project pitch answers 4 questions in order. Each answer uses going to. Spend 20–25 seconds on each step.
01
Step 1 — What?
What are we going to do?
“We’re going to implement a new supplier review process.”
02
Step 2 — When?
When are we going to do it?
“We’re going to start next month and finish by the end of Q3.”
03
Step 3 — Who?
Who is going to lead it?
“My team is going to lead it. I’m going to coordinate with operations.”
04
Step 4 — Why?
Why are we going to do it?
“We’re going to do this because it’s going to reduce costs by 15%.”
⚡ Planning Time — 3 Minutes
Before speaking, students write bullet-point notes for their own real (or realistic) project plan. They must include: one going-to negative (“We’re not going to…”), one Wh- question (“What are we going to need?”), and at least two future time expressions.
📚 Layer 1: This planning phase is critical. Students who skip it produce weaker pitches. Set a timer for 3 minutes. Walk the room and check that each student has: (1) a real or realistic project from their work, (2) at least one going-to negative planned, (3) a future time expression per step. If a student can’t think of a project, offer the fallback: “Imagine you’re going to implement a digital filing system in your department.”
💡 Layer 3 — Extension: Advanced students can add a 5th step: “What are we not going to do?” (scope/constraints). This practices the negative form in a highly professional context.
§5 — Role-Play
Pitch, Challenge, Deliver
3 rounds · Pitch + going-to challenge questions + negotiate + full delivery · S5 exit task warm-up
18 MIN
📚 How to run this: Round 1 = Teacher challenges student with going-to questions after the pitch. Round 2 = Pair activity, students negotiate roles using “Who is going to…?” Round 3 = Full solo pitch (60–90 sec) + Q&A. Time each round. Do not correct mid-utterance — note errors and give feedback after each round.
Round 1 · 5 min
Student Pitches — Teacher Challenges
Student delivers their 4-step pitch (from §4 notes). Teacher asks going-to challenge questions after each step to test fluency and short-answer production.
💡 Teacher question bank: “Are you going to involve the finance team?” / “Who is going to approve the budget?” / “When exactly are you going to start?” / “Why are you not going to use the old process?” — Student must answer with going-to short answers or full sentences.
Model — Round 1 Exchange

S: “We’re going to implement a digital filing system next month.”
T: “Are you going to train the whole team?”
S: “Yes, we are. We’re going to run two training sessions in Q3.”
T: “What are you going to do if the IT team isn’t available?”
S: “We’re not going to wait. We’re going to contact external support.”
Round 2 · 7 min
Pairs Negotiate — Who Is Going to Do What?
Students A and B share the same fictional project. They negotiate task allocation: who is going to do what, when, and why. Each student must produce at least 3 going-to sentences and ask at least 1 Wh- going-to question.
💡 Starter: “OK, so who is going to organize the kickoff meeting?” Partner: “I’m going to organize it. It’s on Tuesday. Are you going to prepare the agenda?” Continue for 7 minutes — use the 4 steps from §4.
Negotiation Starters

“Who is going to [lead / organize / present / confirm / schedule]?”
“I’m going to [task]. Are you going to [task]?”
“We’re not going to need [X] — we’re going to use [Y] instead.”
“When are you going to send the report?” — “I’m going to send it by next Friday.”
Round 3 · 6 min
Full 90-Second Pitch Delivery + Q&A
Student delivers the full 4-step pitch without notes (or minimal notes). Partner acts as a sceptical stakeholder and asks 2 going-to questions during the Q&A. Teacher evaluates using the rubric in §7.
💡 Encourage students to stand up. Real pitch stance = better language. After delivery, teacher gives feedback in this order: (1) What was excellent, (2) One grammar point to fix, (3) One phrase to add next time.
💡 Layer 2 — Extension: If time allows, ask the student to give the pitch again immediately after feedback. A second delivery in the same session is 30–40% more fluent than the first. This is the fastest fluency-building technique at A2.1.
§6 — Listening
Elena’s Q3 Project Pitch
~200-word A2.1 business pitch monologue · Going to throughout · Voice: Sarah AmE 0.82× · 4 MCQ + 4 T/F
12 MIN
📌 Listen for: going-to sentences (affirmative + negative) · future time expressions · negative form · going to vs Simple Present contrast
Q3 Initiative Pitch — Elena Vargas, Operations Manager
A2.1 · M4 · S4A · Voice: Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.82×
Good morning, everyone. My name is Elena Vargas, and I’m the Operations Manager at Nexo Logistics. Today, I’m going to present our main initiative for Q3.

What are we going to do? We’re going to launch a new supplier evaluation process. We’re going to review all active supplier contracts and we’re going to implement a digital scoring system. What are we going to need? We’re going to need three things: a project team, a software licence, and approval from the board.

Next month, I’m going to organize the kickoff sessions. The kickoff meeting is on the 15th — we’re going to use it to assign roles and confirm the timeline. We’re going to complete the implementation by the end of July. In Q3, we’re going to present the first results to senior management.

Who is going to lead this project? My team is going to coordinate everything, and the IT department is going to provide technical support. We’re not going to hire external consultants — we’re not going to exceed the approved budget.

Why are we going to do this? The current process is slow. This initiative is going to reduce evaluation time by 40%. It’s going to improve supplier quality and it’s going to save the company time and money in Q3 and beyond.

I’m going to confirm the final schedule next month after the board approves the budget. Thank you — are there any questions?
📎 ElevenLabs: Voice — Sarah (American English) · Speed 0.82 · Pitch 1.0 · A2.1 M4 S4A · Single speaker
✏️ Listening — Multiple Choice (4 items)
1. What is the main project Elena is going to launch in Q3?
2. What three resources is Elena’s team going to need?
3. When is the kickoff meeting scheduled? (Simple Present for fixed schedule)
4. What percentage improvement in evaluation time is the initiative going to achieve?
✏️ Listening — True or False (4 items)
Elena’s team is going to launch a new supplier evaluation process in Q3.
Elena’s team is going to hire external consultants to support the project.
The implementation is going to be complete by the end of July.
The IT department is going to lead the entire project coordination.
💬 Micro-Speaking — From Memory
Close the script. Tell your partner: “What is Elena going to do? When is the kickoff? What is the initiative going to achieve?” Aim for 3 going-to sentences from memory.
📚 Contrast note: Point out to students: “The kickoff meeting IS on the 15th” (Simple Present = it’s in the calendar) vs “We’re going to USE it to assign roles” (going to = our plan for that fixed event). This is the most important contrast in M4 and it appears naturally in the audio. Play that segment twice.

Pitch Builder
Complete all 4 slots to earn the “Project Pitcher” badge. Each answer must: use “going to”, have at least 4 words, and end with . or !

§8 — Module Preview & My Win
Session Checklist & What’s Next
Confirm going-to mastery · Preview S4B & S5 · My Win badge
5–8 MIN
01
Going to is a plan in your head. It covers all forms: affirmative, negative, Wh- questions, Yes/No questions, short answers. One structure — six functions.
02
Simple Present for fixed schedules. “The meeting is on Monday” = calendar fact. “We’re going to use it to assign roles” = our plan for that event. Both in the same sentence = professional English.
03
The 4-step pitch works. What? When? Who? Why? — with going to throughout. Use it in your next real meeting. Your manager will notice the difference from a vague plan update.
🎯 3 Phrases to Use at Work This Week
PHRASE 1
“We’re going to [action] by [time expression].”
The core project commitment sentence. Use it in every status update, standup, or email. Clear, confident, professional.
PHRASE 2
“We’re not going to [risk/cost] — here’s why.”
Pre-empt stakeholder concerns with a confident negative + explanation. Shows you’ve thought about risks. Essential in pitches and proposals.
PHRASE 3
“What are we going to need? Three things:…”
Rhetorical question + numbered list. Classic pitch technique. Makes your presentation sound structured and prepared. Use it in any stakeholder meeting.
