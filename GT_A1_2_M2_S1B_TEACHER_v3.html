Teacher follow-up questions

Layer 1 · Pedagogical

  • Silent recast: if they say "There is a lot of equipments" → you respond "Yes! A lot of equipment here." And keep going. Never interrupt the flow.
  • Minimum §1 standard: 3+ sentences with S1A vocab unaided. A hard 5 minutes.

Monologue: "Our Department's Situation — Q3 Update"
Speaker: Sandra Morales, Purchasing Manager · Duration: ~90 sec · A1.2 · Normal-fast pace

Generate with ElevenLabs voice Rachel or Bella · Normal-fast pace · Clear diction · No music

▶ Show ElevenLabs Script
MONOLOGUE — FASTER PACE THAN S1A

"OK, let me give you the Q3 update for our purchasing department. We have some good news and some challenges.

Good news first: we have a lot of folders and paper in the warehouse — enough for the rest of the quarter. We also have some new equipment in the office: a new printer and three new computers.

Now the challenges. We don't have any ink cartridges left. Zero. I need to order 20 units this week. How much does that cost? About $2,400 MXN. Our budget for Q3 is $18,000 MXN and we've already used $14,000. So there isn't much money left — about $4,000.

How many suppliers do we work with? Currently four. We're evaluating a fifth from Guadalajara — they offer a 15% discount on orders over $5,000. That would be great for Q4. Any questions? Good."

2

Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • 1st listen: gist comprehension. 2nd listen: answer questions. 3rd optional: student counts how many times they hear some/any/much/many.
  • Monologue Imitation: 60 seconds of prep with the scaffold → student delivers it out loud. This imitation exercise is worth more than comprehension alone. "Copying" the pattern is how fluency is built.
  • The S1B audio is intentionally faster than S1A. Normalize it: "How much did you catch? More than in S1A?" — always frame positively.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You just gave a department update in English. Without a full script. That's what you're expected to do in a meeting with an American supplier."
§ 05
Role Play — Placing a Supplier Order
12–15 min
Role Play 1 — Guided · 5 min
📞 The Purchase Request Call
Student = Purchasing Assistant at TechSol México. Calls Pacific Supply Co. to place an order. Teacher = supplier. The student uses the scaffold in their column.
🎧 Teacher — Supplier (Pacific Supply Co.)
"Good morning, Pacific Supply Co., how can I help you?"
"Of course! How many units do you need?"
"We have some in stock. Price per unit: $120 MXN."
"Total: $2,400 MXN. Is there anything else?"
"When do you need the delivery?"
"Great. I'll send the invoice today. Thank you!"
🗣 Student — Purchasing Assistant (TechSol)
"Good morning! I'd like to order some ink cartridges, please."
"I need 20 units of the HP 304XL model."
"Do you have any in stock right now?"
"How much is the total? Is there any discount?"
"We need it before Friday, please."
"Thank you. Please confirm by email."
Role Play 2 — Free · 7 min · No scaffold
📦 Your Own Order — No Script
Same scenario — now the student uses THEIR real company. Teacher responds naturally. The student talks about their own products, quantities and budget. Minimum 6 turns each.
🎯 Free Role Play Rules

Student must use: some at least once · any at least once · how much or how many once · ONE real number from their company · Open with "I'd like to order..." · Close with "Please confirm..."

"Let me introduce my company. We're a logistics company headquartered in Guadalajara. We provide transportation and warehousing services for the manufacturing sector. Our main clients are automotive companies. We have three branches: in Mexico City, Monterrey, and Tijuana. We employ around 180 people across all locations."

🗣️ 3 Frases para Esta Semana
Three model phrases for talking about your company in English. Use them in international meetings. 💬
1
"My company produces industrial equipment."
Mi empresa produce equipo industrial.
✅ Basic formula: My company + produces/sells/provides + product. Instant pitch.
2
"We have about 200 employees in three branches."
Tenemos unos 200 empleados en tres sucursales.
💼 Pattern with approximate numbers: about + number. Very natural.
3
"Our headquarters is in Mexico City."
Nuestra sede central está en la Ciudad de México.
🏆 "Our headquarters is" (singular treated as such). HQ is essential in any corporate pitch.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • Role Play 2: give 2 minutes of silent preparation. Then "OK, I'm the supplier. Ring ring!" — surprise activates instinctive English.
  • Zero interruptions during the role play. Post-play correction: 1 specific celebration + 1 specific correction. Only 1 of each.
  • A1.2 standard: some/any/how much/many correct 4 out of 6 times = mastery.
  • Protect 12 minutes for this block. It's the highest-value moment of S1B.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You just made a purchasing call in English. Without reading — speaking. That's what a bilingual professional does. That's what YOU just did."
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "This English — orders, budgets, suppliers — is used by procurement, manufacturing, and logistics professionals every day. And now you have it."
  • Bajío industrial sector upsell: "This module is the most requested by our corporate manufacturing clients. Your colleagues who speak this have an advantage. Now you do too."
§ 06
Debate — Budget Decision
8–10 min
💬 Opinion Debate · A1.2 Level
Should we spend the remaining budget or save it?
"Our department has $4,000 MXN left in the Q3 budget. We have some supplies but not many. Should we spend it now or save it for Q4? What do you think?"
✅ Spend it now — arguments
"We don't have any ink cartridges — we need to buy some."
"There isn't much time before end of Q3."
"We have a lot of work — we need the supplies now."
"The new supplier has a 15% discount for orders over $5,000."
⚠️ Save for Q4 — arguments
"We still have some paper and some folders."
"There isn't enough budget to buy many items."
"We don't have much information about Q4 needs."
"The discount requires $5,000 — we only have $4,000."
💡 Teacher: take the OPPOSITE position from the student. Give real arguments. Minimum 4 real debate turns. Goal: student uses some/any/much/many in argumentative context.
Additional debate prompts for groups or if time remains
"Many small suppliers or one big supplier? Use: 'If we have many suppliers, there is some risk but also some flexibility...'"
"Your company needs new furniture AND new equipment, but not enough money for both. Which one? Why?"
"How much English does a purchasing manager really need? More or less than an engineer? Use: 'I think... needs a lot of / some English because...'"
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • The debate is where students use grammar in real argumentative context — not automatic responses. It's the internalization test.
  • Be a good opponent — don't give up easily. The student has to work. For groups: 2 teams, 4 minutes, then vote.
  • Post-debate correction: 1 correction + 1 celebration. Only 1 of each.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You just debated about budgets in English. That's not A1-level content — but it is A1-level grammar. That's the GT method."
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • Testimonial trigger — MANDATORY: "Which felt harder — S1A or S1B? Which felt more real? Which felt more like yours?" — Let them answer completely. This reflection anchors retention and generates the insight students share with colleagues.
  • "S2A has the complete structured order email — the same situation as today's role play, in formal written English. That email you can actually send to a supplier next week."
  • Upsell: "The vocabulary of this module — some/any/orders/budget — is exactly what HR departments look for when they say 'we need someone with intermediate business English.' You already have it."
