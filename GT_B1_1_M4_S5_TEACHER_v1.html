RAPID-FIRE REVIEW — T→S

Q1 · Social Media (S1) · T→S "Give me one sentence about social media using passive voice: 'My feed is…'"
Model Answer "My feed is filled with content that was selected by an algorithm — not by me. My identity is shaped by what I'm shown, not what I choose."
Q2 · Screen Time (S2) · T→S "What was Rodrigo's screen time per day? Describe the solution he used — in passive."
Model Answer "Rodrigo was spending over 5 hours a day on his phone. Limits were set using his phone's screen time feature. Notifications were turned off for non-essential apps."
Q3 · AI at Work (S3) · T→S "What surprised Rodrigo most about AI at work? Use passive: 'His output was…'"
Model Answer "His output was doubled when he started using AI tools for reports. He was surprised that so much time had been wasted on tasks that could be automated."
Q4 · Online Communication (S4) · T→S "What rule did Rodrigo create about email feedback? State it with a modal passive."
Model Answer "Critical feedback should be given by phone or video — not by email. Tone can be misread very easily in text. Difficult messages should not be sent without context."

Vocabulary & Key Concepts — Tech Addiction
⏱ 10 min · 8 words · Passive spotlight · Quick check
📋 Logistics

8 vocabulary cards in a 2×4 grid. Students read each card aloud, then cover the definition and say it from memory. Quick vocab check: 8 MC questions. Earn b1 (already extended) at 6+/8. The passive spotlight box reinforces the grammar connection — passive hides the agent (the company).

🎯 Priority Words

Pronunciation focus: "dopamine" /ˈdəʊpəmiːn/ (3 syllables — do-pa-meen), "monetize" /ˈmɒnɪtaɪz/ (not mono-tize), "addiction" /əˈdɪkʃən/ (stress on second syllable). These words should feel natural before the debate in §3.

💡 Passive Connection

After the vocab cards, ask: "Who is doing the action in each sentence?" — "Users are hooked" → by the algorithm. "Dopamine is released" → by the app. "Your attention is monetized" → by the company. The passive is deliberately used in tech industry language to obscure accountability.

🔵 Passive Voice Spotlight — Tech Addiction Language

Notice how tech addiction is described using passive: "Users are hooked", "Attention is monetized", "Dopamine is released" — the company is deliberately hidden. When you use passive in this context, you are either describing a system OR you are hiding who built it.

Debate — Who Is Responsible?
⏱ 12 min · Two-sides debate · Opinion meter · 5 structured questions
📋 Logistics

Two-sides debate card first — let students read both sides silently (90 seconds). Then opinion meter to set the class position. Then 5 structured debate questions — mix of T→S, S→S, and class. Badge 🎯 when any debate-btn is clicked AND student speaks for 60 seconds (timer). Run the 60s timer for each student turn.

🎯 Pedagogical Focus

This is the most linguistically demanding section of M4 S5. Students must use vocabulary from §2 (addiction, dopamine, design, monetize) inside an opinion structure. Target output: "I believe companies should be regulated because dopamine loops are deliberately exploited." — That's 3 M4 words in one passive + opinion sentence.

💡 Regulation as Passive

Q4 (class debate about regulation warnings) naturally pushes passive: "Warnings should be required", "Algorithms should be disclosed", "Children should be protected." Let the debate generate passive naturally — then name it afterwards: "Did you notice you just used 3 modal passives?"

Side A — The User Is Responsible

Adults make their own choices. If apps are used too much, that is a personal decision. Self-control should be exercised. No one is forced to check their phone. Personal responsibility cannot be outsourced to a company.

Side B — The Company Is Responsible

These apps were designed to be addictive. Dopamine loops are exploited deliberately. Young users are especially targeted. The responsibility should be carried by those who profit from the addiction. Design choices are not neutral.

OPINION METER

💬 "Tech companies, not users, are responsible for phone addiction."

DISAGREE — User's faultAGREE — Company's fault

STRUCTURED DEBATE QUESTIONS

T → S
"Is phone addiction real — or is it just a lack of willpower? Why?" Use vocabulary from §2 in your answer.
S → S
"Ask your partner: Have you ever felt addicted to an app? Which one? What happened? What did you do about it?"
T → S
"If you designed a social media app, would you add features designed to maximize engagement — even if you knew they were harmful? Defend your answer."
Class Debate

T → S
"What should be done by governments to protect children from addictive app design? Use modal passive: 'Algorithms should be…', 'Warnings must be…', 'Age limits should be…'"

Grammar Review — Passive Voice Consolidation
⏱ 10 min · Comparison table · Final quiz · Badge 🏆 at 5+/6
📋 Logistics

Final grammar pass for M4. No new input — pure consolidation. The side-by-side comparison table shows active (company visible) vs passive (company hidden). 6-question quiz: earn b3 (capstone badge can also earn b3) at 5+/6. Use checkAct() for all quiz items.

🎯 Key Insight — M4 Passive

Students have seen passive in 4 contexts: social media (S1), screen time (S2), AI (S3), communication (S4). The tech addiction context is the most powerful because passive is USED BY REAL COMPANIES to avoid accountability. "Children were targeted" not "We targeted children." This is not an accident.

💡 The Hidden Agent

Ask before the quiz: "Look at the passive column — can you always identify who did the action?" They can't. That's the point. In tech industry language, passive removes moral responsibility. This grammar point is not just linguistic — it's critical literacy.

ACTIVE VS PASSIVE — COMPANY LANGUAGE

Active — Company VisiblePassive — Company Hidden
"We designed this to maximize your time on screen.""This was designed to maximize your time on screen."
"We sell your data to advertisers.""Your data is sold to advertisers."
"We exploit dopamine to keep you scrolling.""Dopamine is exploited to keep you scrolling."
"We targeted children with addictive design.""Children were targeted with addictive design."
🔵 M4 Passive — All Four Forms Consolidated

Present passive: "Your attention is sold." | Past passive: "These features were designed."
Modal passive: "Algorithms should be disclosed." | Diplomatic passive: "That was misread — let me clarify."

Capstone — Tech Addiction Policy Statement
⏱ 15 min · M4 Exit Task · 3-min speech · Peer feedback
📋 Logistics

M4 Exit Task (program spec): "Describe how technology affects your daily life. Use passive voice at least twice. Give your opinion on one of: social media identity, AI, or tech addiction. Use I think / I believe / In my opinion. Speaking · 3 min." Students build their talk in 4 steps (30+60+60+30 seconds), then deliver for 3 minutes. Timer earns b3.

🎯 Assessment Focus

Target output includes: passive voice × 2 minimum, opinion marker, M4 vocabulary × 3, personal situation (not generic). After delivery, peer feedback checklist. Quality indicator for b3: student used passive naturally, not "translation passive" (forced structure).

💡 Building Block Strategy

The 4-step building block (context → impact → opinion → conclusion) mirrors professional speaking structure. This transfers beyond class: presentations, meetings, panel discussions. Name this: "This is how professionals speak on difficult topics. Four blocks. That's it."

M4 Exit Task (program spec): Describe how technology affects your daily life. Use passive voice at least twice. Give your opinion on one of: social media identity, AI, or tech addiction. Use I think / I believe / In my opinion. Speaking · 3 minutes.

BUILDING BLOCKS — CONSTRUCT YOUR 3-MINUTE TALK

Step 1 · Context
30 seconds
How is technology used in my daily life?
"What was I doing 10 years ago vs now? What technology is used every day? How has my routine been changed by devices?"
Step 2 · Impact
60 seconds
How have I been affected?
"What was changed by technology in my work, relationships, sleep? Use at least 2 passives here: 'My attention is constantly pulled away…', 'My productivity was improved by…'"
Step 3 · Opinion
60 seconds
Who is responsible?
"I think / I believe / In my opinion… Who is responsible — user or company? What should be regulated? What could be changed? Use modal passive: 'Algorithms should be…'"
Step 4 · Conclusion
30 seconds
What have I decided to do?
"What rule have I set for myself? What has been decided? 'I have decided to…' / 'Limits have been set.' One clear action sentence."

CAPSTONE TIMER — 3 MINUTES

Deliver your 3-minute capstone. Use the four building blocks — but speak naturally, not as a list. Add transitions: "On top of that…" / "When it comes to responsibility…" / "To conclude…"

PEER FEEDBACK CHECKLIST

Partner: check each item as your classmate speaks. Discuss after.

  • Used passive voice at least twice
  • Gave a clear opinion with "I think / I believe / In my opinion"
  • Spoke for close to 3 minutes
  • Used M4 vocabulary (at least 3 tech words)
  • Talked about a personal situation — not only generic examples

⏱ 8 min · What I Can Now Do · Module win · Leaderboard
📋 Logistics

Checklist covers all 5 M4 topics. Students check each item they can genuinely do. Module win textarea: "What's your biggest takeaway from Module 4? What will you change in how you use technology?" — earns b4 when submitted. Add capstone scores (0–10) to the leaderboard.

🎯 Global Feedback — M4

Priority correction for M4: agent confusion in passive. Students sometimes add an incorrect agent: "The app is used by the people too much" instead of "The app is used too much." Also watch for: "It was designed by the company for hook users" (infinitive error after passive). Correct once, clearly.

💡 Module Win Question

"What will you change in how you use technology?" — this question uses future simple, not passive. After they answer, reflect: "You just answered without passive — is that appropriate here?" Yes. Personal plans use active: "I will limit…", "I am going to turn off…" Passive would feel evasive here. That's the grammar intuition you want them to develop.

Preview — Module 5
⏱ 3 min · M5 overview · Second Conditional · Build anticipation
📋 Logistics

30 seconds maximum. Read the 3 preview questions aloud — don't answer them yet. Students should feel the new grammar immediately: these questions feel different from M3 and M4. They're imaginary, not real. That contrast IS the lesson hook for M5 S1.

🎯 Grammar Shift — M4 to M5

M4 = Passive Voice (descriptive: the world is doing something to you). M5 = Second Conditional (imaginary: what WOULD be different if…). The shift is intentional: from passive description of reality to active imagination of alternatives. Passive voice and Second Conditional are both B1 grammar milestones that require time to consolidate before moving forward.

💡 The Gap Is Intentional

"M5 arrives with a grammar shift: from real and descriptive (Passive Voice) to imaginary (Second Conditional). The gap between M4 and M5 grammar is intentional — it gives students time to consolidate passive before moving to conditionals." Use this language with students if they ask why the grammar changed.

📌 MODULE 5 PREVIEW

Module 5: Health, Habits & Wellbeing

Diet & nutrition · Exercise & fitness · Mental health · Sleep · Medical English

Grammar Preview — Second Conditional

If + past simple, would + base verb — for imaginary situations
"If I exercised more, I would feel better." | "If I slept 8 hours, I would be more productive."

Grammar shift: from real and descriptive (Passive Voice) to imaginary and reflective (Second Conditional). The gap is intentional — passive needs time to consolidate before conditionals.

THREE PREVIEW QUESTIONS — FEEL THE NEW GRAMMAR

Read these aloud — notice how they feel different from M4 questions
"If you could change one health habit right now, what would it be? Why?"
"If everyone exercised for 30 minutes a day, what would change in society?"
"If you were a doctor, what advice would you give yourself this week?"

These are Second Conditional questions — imaginary situations. You can't answer "yes it happened." You have to imagine. That's new. That's M5.

🎯 Homework Before M5 S1

Think about one health habit you want to change. Write one sentence using Second Conditional: "If I [changed this habit], I would [feel / look / work / sleep] better because…" Bring it to the first session of M5 ready to share.
