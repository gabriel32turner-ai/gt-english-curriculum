Wh- Generation Drill — Say the correct Wh- question

The teacher says a statement. The student produces the corresponding Wh- question INSTANTLY using the correct verb.

"Carlos went somewhere last week."
→ "Where did he go?"
"She met someone important at the conference."
→ "Who did she meet?"
"They made some kind of agreement."
→ "What did they make?"
"I wrote the report at some point."
→ "When did you write it?"
"He went there for some reason."
→ "Why did he go?"
"The presentation went well/badly."
→ "How did it go?"
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • Wh- Generation: teacher's answers are hidden in Student Mode — the student produces the question WITHOUT seeing the model. If they get the Wh- word wrong: pause, correct, repeat.
  • The most likely error: "What did she go?" (what → where). Reinforce: Where = place, Who = person, What = thing/action, When = time, How = manner, Why = reason.
  • Threshold for role play: 8/8 battle + 5/6 correct Wh- questions. If not → 3 extra minutes of drill.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Battle R2 is the speed version. If you can do 8/8 in 3 seconds, the verbs are already in your automatic memory."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "Generating Wh- questions is the highest level — not just answering questions, but CREATING them. That's what a fluent speaker does."

Carlos (Adam) · single-speaker monologue · Rate 0.82 · ~95 sec


Hi everyone, Carlos here with my full debrief from Dallas. Where did I go first when I arrived? Straight to the hotel, where I met Jim Carter, our regional contact. We had lunch and talked about the Q4 plan. Who did I meet from the client side? On Tuesday I met the Director of Operations. We gave our main presentation — forty people attended. We got excellent feedback from three companies. What agreement did I make with them? We made an agreement to schedule a follow-up call next month. They want a formal proposal. How did the presentation go overall? It went really well. I took notes on every conversation and wrote the full report on Wednesday evening. One small thing for Ana: I didn't have the exact follow-up date in writing yet, so I will send the details before noon today. Quick note on Wednesday — I didn't attend the networking dinner because I had an early flight Thursday morning, so I went back to the hotel early. Overall, two very productive days. End of memo.

ElevenLabs: Adam voice (Carlos). Single-speaker monologue framed as a recorded memo to the team. Rate 0.82, stability 0.65. Record in one take. Upload to Google Drive with iframe /preview.
"Count every irregular past verb you hear in this monologue. How many total?" (Target: 11+ — went, met, had, gave, attended, got, made, took, wrote, went, didn't have, didn't attend)
First listen: count only. Don't answer questions yet.
"How many Wh- questions did Carlos ask himself in the memo? Which Wh- word did he use for each?" (Answer: 4 — Where, Who, What, How)
Second listen: focus only on question words.
"Now YOU give the same kind of memo. Use the same 4 self-Wh- questions about your own week. Answer from memory — no notes. Use 4+ irregular verbs."
Minimum 4 irregular verbs in your answers.
"What two things did Carlos say he didn't do? Were both negative forms correct?" (Answer: "I didn't have that date in writing" / "I didn't attend the networking dinner" — YES, correctly BASE after didn't)
Grammar check: confirm the negative rule in context.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • Task 3 is the main diagnostic: can the student deliver a memo like Carlos with 4+ irregular verbs and no notes? If yes → ready for full role play. If not → 3 minutes of review with the §2 cards.
  • Task 4 reinforces the negative in real context: Carlos says "I didn't have / I didn't attend" — the student hears the correct form before producing it.
  • Carlos uses 4 self-Wh- questions to structure the memo. Listen pass focused on question words.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Carlos uses 4 self-questions to structure his memo in 95 seconds. That's the rhythm of real professional English. Could you follow all 4?"
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "In S4 you add 'I didn't go because…' and duration expressions. Your debrief will go from good to excellent."

🎭 Role Play 1 — Manager Debrief (Carlos + Ana)
You are Carlos. You just returned from the Dallas Sales Conference. Ana (the teacher) wants to hear the full summary — without reading the trip report. Use the 8 irregular verbs in real context.
Carlos (Student)
Report spontaneously: where you went, who you met, what you did each day, what agreement you made, what you couldn't do and why. Minimum 5 irregular past verbs. No notes.
Ana (Teacher) — Wh- Questions
Let the student speak for 30 seconds, then interrupt with 5 Wh- questions: Where / Who / What / How / When. Force complete answers, not just "yes/no".
[Ana]: "Welcome back Carlos! How did the conference go?"
Goal: the student uses went/met/had/gave/got/made spontaneously + answers 5 Wh- questions with correct irregular forms (BASE after did/didn't). If they make the "didn't went" error → immediate correction, the student repeats correctly before continuing.
🎭 Role Play 2 — Director Review (Carlos + Roberto)
Director Roberto (the teacher) wants more detail about the 3 potential clients. Carlos (you) must respond accurately using the vocabulary from the §3 audio.
Carlos (Student)
Use these facts: gave presentation to 40 people · got feedback from 3 companies · made agreement for follow-up call · wrote report on Wednesday · took notes on all conversations. Invent an additional detail that wasn't in the audio.
Roberto (Teacher)
"What did the companies say exactly?" / "Who did you give the presentation with?" / "How did the Director react when you made the agreement?" / "When did you take notes — during or after?"
[Roberto]: "Carlos, I read your report. I want to understand the client feedback better. What exactly did they say after your presentation?"
Goal: the student answers Wh- questions with irregular forms without hesitation. Specifically evaluate: "gave" (not gived), "took" (not taked), "wrote" (not writed).
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • RP1: if the student runs out of verbs to report → ask them to use the email from §5 of S3A as a mental script (they read it in the previous session). Don't use the document physically.
  • RP2: the "invented additional detail" activates truly free production with irregulars. If they invent something grammatically incorrect → correct after they finish, not during.
  • Most likely error in both RPs: "When did you went?" / "I met with him and we maked…" → register, correct, repeat.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You just gave a complete debrief in English about a real business trip. Carlos did it in the audio — you did it with your real work. That's harder and more valuable."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • Upsell: "In S4 you add 'I didn't go because…' + 'for 3 hours' — time expressions. Your debrief will go from good to executive-level."
§ 06
Debate — Business Trips vs Video Calls
8–10 min
🗣 Free Speaking — Debate with Irregular Past
"Carlos went to Dallas, gave a presentation, and made a deal in person. Was the trip necessary — or could he have done the same with a video call?"
✈️ Business trips are necessary
"I went to a meeting last year and made a deal that we couldn't make by email."
"When I met the client in person, we got better results."
"The feedback we got was only possible because we were there."
"You can't make real agreements through a screen."
💻 Video calls are enough
"Last month I had 5 client calls online and made 3 agreements — no travel."
"We gave our best presentation ever via Zoom last quarter."
"The time I spent traveling, I could have met 10 more clients."
"We got the deal without going anywhere."
Last time I went to… We made a deal when… I met the client and… We got better results because… I didn't have to go because…
🎤 Optional Extension — Trip Story · 90 seconds
"Tell me about a real business trip or important meeting you had. Where did you go? Who did you meet? What did you make or get? How did it go?"
Minimum 6 sentences. At least 6 different irregular verbs. At least 2 answers to spontaneous teacher Wh- questions. Minimum 1 negative.
Teacher Follow-Up — After Debate
"When did you last go on a business trip? What happened exactly — who did you meet?"
"What was the best agreement you ever made in a meeting? How did you make it?"
"Have you ever gave a presentation that didn't go well? What happened?" (Catch error: "gave" vs "given" — focus on past)
"What did your team get from the last important client call — positive or negative feedback?"
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • The debate activates irregular verbs in argumentative discourse — the highest level of production. Rule: the student MUST use at least 4 irregular verbs during the debate. If they don't appear naturally: "Now say the same thing with a past-tense example."
  • The phrases in the "debate-phrase" cards are models — the student should adapt them to their real experience, not repeat them verbatim.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You just debated business trips vs video calls in English using went, met, made, got. That's A2 executive English."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • Testimonial: "If your director asked you in English 'Carlos, how did the Dallas trip go?' — could you respond in English with went/met/made/got? Yes. That's what you learned today."
§ SP
Speaking Practice
10–12 min
🗣 Guided Speaking — Trip Debrief, No Script
Where Did You Go? — Free Wh- Q&A
No role cards. No script. Your teacher asks Wh- questions — you answer about your real work experiences. Use all five irregular verbs: went · had · made · gave · met. Then switch: you ask the questions.
Question 1
"Where did you go for work in the last six months? What was the purpose of the trip?"
Hint: I went to… for a meeting / conference / training… / The purpose was to…
Question 2
"Who did you meet there? How many meetings did you have?"
Hint: I met with… / I had three meetings… / I met the client's team… / We had a dinner with…
Question 3
"What decisions did you make on that trip — or in your last big project?"
Hint: We made a decision to… / I made a plan for… / We didn't make a final decision — we needed…
Question 4
"Did you give any presentations or reports? How did they go?"
Hint: Yes, I gave a presentation on… / I gave a report to… / No, I didn't give one — I attended instead…
Question 5
"Your turn — ask me THREE Wh- questions about my last work trip or project. No hints."
Hint: Where did you…? / Who did you meet…? / What did you make / give / have…? / How many…?
⏱ 10–12 min total 📋 5 questions 🎯 All 5 irregular verbs 👥 Pair or teacher-led
⚡ Speaking Challenge — Closing
"Give me a 60-second debrief of a real work trip or project — no notes. Use all five verbs: went, had, made, gave, met. Tell me what happened, who you met, what decisions you made."
Teacher evaluates: all 5 target irregular verbs produced correctly · at least 1 negative with base form (didn't go / didn't make) · at least 1 Wh- question formed by student in Q5 · narrative flow (not just a list). Compare to S3A §SP: are they producing the irregulars faster and with less hesitation? That speed gain is mastery evidence.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • S3B students have done a full Role Play (§5) with scripts and a Debate (§6). By this §SP they have had extensive exposure to the 5 irregular verbs — the goal now is speed and automaticity, not just accuracy.
  • Q5 is the highest-level task: forming 3 Wh- questions independently. Many students will still invert incorrectly ("Where you went?"). Recast once — "Where did I go? I went to…" — then ask them to repeat the correct form. Don't dwell; keep momentum.
  • The Speaking Challenge should feel like a real debrief, not a grammar exercise. If the student finishes in 30 seconds, push: "And then what happened?" / "Who made that decision?" / "What did your manager say?"
  • Watch for over-reliance on "went" — if student only uses went and had, prompt for the others: "Did you give a presentation there?" / "What decisions did you make?"
  • Negative forms: if no "didn't" appears naturally, ask: "Was everything perfect on the trip, or was there something that didn't go well?"
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "In §5 you had a script. Right now you had nothing — and you told me a complete story. That is the difference between knowing English and using English."
  • After Speaking Challenge: "Which verb came out the fastest? Which one still needs a beat of thinking? That's your personal practice target."
  • When student asks 3 Wh- questions correctly in Q5: "You just interviewed me in English. That is an advanced skill — most learners at your level can't do that yet."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "In S4A you'll add duration — 'I worked on it for 3 hours / from Monday to Thursday / all morning.' That one layer makes your debrief sound completely professional."
  • Upsell: "By S5 you'll combine everything — regular verbs, irregulars, negatives, Wh- questions, AND duration — in a single 90-second standup. Today is a major piece of that."
  • Testimonial trigger: "If your manager asked you right now — in English — 'How did the project go?' what would you say? Try it in one sentence."
