Open-ended speaking — pick a photo, tell your story

Pick a photo — the one that most resembles a career change you know (yours or someone else's). Answer the questions using what you learned in S2A. No notes — just speak.

🎙 Question 1: Look at your photo. What do you think this person USED TO do before this change? What habits or routines did they probably have?
Scaffold: "They used to work in ___ every day. They didn't use to ___. Their routine used to be ___."
🎙 Question 2: What specific event probably changed their career? When did it happen? Use Simple Past.
Scaffold: "They got a promotion / switched companies / joined a new team in ___ (year). That was the moment when ___."
🎙 Question 3: Now connect it to YOUR story. Tell me one thing you USED TO do professionally and one specific event that changed your career. 3 sentences minimum.
Scaffold: "I used to ___ every ___. Then I got / switched / joined ___. Now I ___."
Teacher follow-up questions
"What challenge did you use to face in your old role? Did it disappear or just change?"
"Did you use to work overtime regularly before your change? What about now?"
"When exactly did the change happen? Give me the year — or at least 'about X years ago.'"
"What colleague influenced your career change the most? Tell me about them."
Layer 1 · Pedagogy
  • This section is free production. Don't correct mid-stream — just listen and note errors for a later recast. The goal is for the student to ACTIVATE the grammar without scaffolding.
  • If the student uses "used to" with a specific event (e.g. "I used to get promoted last year") — note it and correct after the full response. Never mid-production.
  • Photo C is ideal for students who experienced remote work post-pandemic — very real, emotional context.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Pick a photo that means something to you. Not the prettiest — the one closest to a real moment in your career."
  • If the student is energized: "Can you tell me that story in 5 consecutive sentences? No pauses." — push toward fluency.
Layer 3 · Retention
  • "You just opened a job interview in English — spontaneously. That's exactly what a hiring manager asks you in the first 2 minutes."

Layer 1 · Pedagogy
  • Card 4 is the trap. "Last March" with "used to" = classic error. If the student hesitates — perfect, that's the point. If they say "flew" instantly — celebrate the automaticity.
  • Card 5 requires 4 distinct sentences. Time it: under 30 seconds = automaticity. Over 60 seconds = needs more oral production today.
  • Do at least 2 rounds. Round 2 at real conversation pace — no pause between cards.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Round 2 — I want you to answer before I finish showing the card. Can you do it?"
Layer 3 · Retention
  • "These 5 cards are the core of S2. If you produce them without thinking — the used to / Simple Past contrast is already in your brain, not your conscious memory."

3 progressive rounds · scaffold decreases each round
12–15 min

3 progressive rounds. Each round reduces the scaffold — Round 1 you have a script, Round 3 you only have the context. The goal: in Round 3 you produce the conversation completely naturally.

Round 1 · Guided
You have the script — follow the lines
3–4 min

Context: Carmen (HR Manager at Vertex Solutions) is interviewing you for a senior position. She wants to know your career background. You play: yourself (or any professional).

"Tell me — what did you use to do before your current role?"
"And when did you make the change? Was it a promotion or a switch?"

Student plays → Interviewee

Round 3 · Free
90 seconds · No scaffold · Real conversation
90 sec

Final challenge: Carmen asks for an extended response to one single question. The student speaks for 90 seconds without stopping. No pauses longer than 3 seconds allowed.

Carmen's Final Question
"Tell me your career story from the beginning until today — what you used to do, what changed, and where you are now. I want the full picture."
Timer — 90 seconds. Start it when the student says the first word.
Goal: 4-part structure · used to + Simple Past + present + contrast
1:30
Layer 1 · Pedagogy
  • Round 1: the teacher reads Carmen's lines literally. The pace is slow — the student has time to read and think. Goal: activate vocabulary and tenses in a real context.
  • Round 2: the teacher improvises follow-ups based on what the student said in Round 1. This makes the conversation authentic and personalized.
  • Round 3: if the student pauses more than 3 seconds — the teacher says "Carmen: please continue" without giving the answer. Only after 6+ seconds may the teacher give a minimal prompt: "What about your habits?"
  • Exit standard: the student produces the 4-part structure (habits → event → now → contrast) continuously in 90 seconds. That's the S2B benchmark.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "That was a job interview in English — with correct grammar, career vocabulary, and professional structure. Not B1. A2.2. That's what we do here."
Layer 3 · Retention
  • Protect the 90 seconds of Round 3 — it's the highest-value pedagogical moment of the whole session. Don't shorten it for time.

Layer 1 · Pedagogy
  • 5-second rule: the student must answer before you count silently "one, two, three, four, five." If no response — mark wrong and show the model answer.
  • Partial answer: if the student uses the correct tense but the sentence is incomplete — mark as ✓ but ask them to complete. Priority is the tense choice, not the length.

§ 06
Debate — Career & Change
3 real discussion questions · open opinions
8–10 min

3 real discussion questions. No right answer — only justified opinions. The student uses all the vocabulary and grammar from S2A freely. Minimum 2–3 sentences per turn.

Debate Question 1
"Is it better to stay in one company for many years — or to change companies every 3–5 years to grow faster professionally?"
PRO — Stay
You build deep expertise · you get promoted from within · stability matters · you become irreplaceable
CON — Change companies
New companies = new skills · higher salary increases · broader network · avoid stagnation
💡 Use: "I used to think ___, but now I believe ___." · "In my experience, people who ___ tend to ___." · "The biggest risk used to be ___, but nowadays ___."
Debate Question 2
"Working overtime was seen as dedication in the past. Is it still a sign of commitment today — or just poor time management?"
PRO — Overtime = Dedication
Some industries require it · shows commitment · can accelerate promotion · acceptable short-term
CON — Overtime = Problem
Leads to burnout · poor work-life balance · not sustainable · better to work smarter not longer
💡 Use: "Companies used to expect overtime, but now ___." · "I used to work ___ hours, and I noticed ___." · "The challenge used to be ___ — now the challenge is ___."
Debate Question 3
"Think about YOUR career. If you could go back and change ONE decision — what would it be? Why? What would be different today?"
Open Reflection — No sides
Personal career decision · what changed as a result · what you know now that you didn't know then · regret vs growth mindset
💡 Use: "I used to believe ___. But now I think ___." · "If I had known ___, I would have ___." · "The decision I made in ___ (year) changed everything because ___." (Simple Past)
Layer 1 · Pedagogy
  • Q1 and Q2 are two-sided debates — the teacher can take the opposite side to force the student to defend their position with more vocabulary and complex sentences.
  • Q3 is deliberately personal and emotional. This is the type of question that produces the most fluent oral output because the student is talking about something they really know. Don't interrupt — let it flow.
  • Post-turn correction: if the student used "used to" with a specific event (e.g. "I used to make a bad decision in 2019") — correct it at the end of the turn. "That was a specific decision in 2019 — what tense do you use?" — let the student self-correct.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Q3 has no right answer — only YOUR answer. And in English, your story is always interesting."
Layer 3 · Retention
  • "You just talked about your career — with opinions, examples, and contrasts — completely in English. That's real communication. That's the GT method."
