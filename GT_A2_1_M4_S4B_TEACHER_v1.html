§1 — Hot Take
Hot Take — “Planning is Overrated.”
Provocative business statement · Take a position using going to · 60 seconds, no preparation
5 MIN
Hot Take: Your manager just said this in a meeting: “We don’t need a project plan. We’re just going to figure it out as we go.”
“Companies that skip planning are going to fail. Good projects always need a plan with clear going-to actions. Agree or disagree — 60 seconds, right now.”
You must use going to at least 3 times and take a clear position. Partner counts every going-to usage.

💡 Pedagogical (B session): This is Session B — no scaffolding, no warm-up cue. The student starts on the timer tick. Do NOT pre-teach vocabulary. Cold start is the point. 📈 Retention cue: After the timer, ask: “How many times did you use going to naturally? How many pauses did you need?” This metacognitive moment is high-retention.
§2 — Pitch Debate
Pitch Debate — Plan A vs Plan B
Two competing project pitches · Audience votes · Min 5 going-to sentences + 1 Wh- question + 1 negative per pitch
12 MIN
Scenario: Grupo Nexo — a mid-size logistics company — needs to reduce delivery delays. Two project managers are pitching different solutions to the CEO. You are assigned one side. Defend it. The audience votes on the better pitch.
Side A — Tech Platform
“We’re going to implement a new tracking platform. We’re going to train the drivers by March. We’re not going to increase costs — we’re going to use our current budget.”
  • “We’re going to reduce delays by 30% in Q2.”
  • “We’re not going to need extra staff.”
  • “What are you going to do about driver training?”
Side B — Process Redesign
“We’re going to redesign the route system. We’re going to work with the team leads directly. We’re not going to invest in new software — we’re going to fix the process first.”
  • “We’re going to see results in 6 weeks, not 6 months.”
  • “We’re not going to waste money on tools that don’t work.”
  • “How many drivers are going to be affected by the new system?”
Pitch Checklist — Each pitcher must use:
✅ ≥5 going-to sentences
❌ ≥1 negative (not going to)
❓ ≥1 Wh- question
📅 ≥1 future time expression
💰 Benefits clearly stated
🏆 Audience votes the winner

🏃 Engagement: Assign sides randomly — this breaks comfort zones. If solo student: pitch Side A, then Side B (switching roles). Post-pitch: teacher plays “CEO” and fires one follow-up Wh- question to each side. Count going-to uses out loud as the pitcher speaks. Track on the board.
💡 Error focus: The most common B-session error here is over-relying on “we going to” (missing are) and “we’re going to to implement” (double to). Note both without interrupting.
§3 — Relay Pitch
Relay Pitch — Build the Pitch Together
Team relay · Each student adds ≥2 going-to sentences · Pitch grows from opener to close
10 MIN

How it works: Each student picks up the pitch where the previous person left off. You cannot repeat the same going-to sentence. Click “Show Model” only to verify — not to read first.

Layer 1 — Opener
Student A opens the pitch: “We’re going to present our plan for improving client communication at Grupo Nexo. First, we’re going to...”
Target: 2+ affirmative going-to sentences introducing the project and first action step.
“We’re going to present our plan for improving client communication at Grupo Nexo. First, we’re going to survey 50 clients next week. We’re also going to meet with the sales team on Monday to review the feedback.”
Layer 2 — Plan Details
Student B continues: “After that, we’re going to...” Mention what the team is NOT going to do and explain why.
Target: 2+ sentences — at least 1 affirmative and 1 negative going-to. Add a Wh- question to the imaginary client.
“After that, we’re going to redesign the follow-up email template. We’re not going to change the CRM system right now — that’s a Q3 decision. What changes are you going to make to the reporting process?”
Layer 3 — Close & Timeline
Student C closes the pitch: “Finally, we’re going to...” Include a future time expression and a closing Yes/No question to the audience.
Target: 2+ going-to sentences, 1 time expression, 1 question to close.
“Finally, we’re going to launch the new protocol in Q2. By the end of April, we’re going to have results from the first client survey. Are you going to support this project? We need a decision by next Friday.”
Solo student option
If working solo: deliver all 3 layers without stopping. Use a 90-second timer. No pausing between layers — treat it as one continuous pitch.
💡 Pedagogical: The relay reveals whether students can pick up mid-pitch without reverting to Spanish. Common slip: starting Layer 2 with “So, we are going to...” (correct but formal) vs the natural contraction. Both acceptable. Watch for connector transitions: After that / Then / Finally.

§5 — Solo Challenge
Solo Challenge — Your Full Pitch
90-second project pitch · Rubric: ≥8 going-to sentences · ≥2 Wh- questions · ≥1 negative · ≥2 time expressions
10 MIN
You are the project lead. The executive board of Grupo Nexo has given you 90 seconds to pitch your plan for the “2025 Delivery Improvement Initiative.” No notes. No slides. Just you and your going-to language. Deliver a full pitch: problem, plan, team, timeline, and result.
✅ Affirmative
Minimum 8 going-to sentences with different subjects and verbs.
I’m / We’re / They’re / She’s going to...
❌ Negative
At least 1 clear “not going to” sentence with a reason.
“We’re not going to rush this — we need to get it right.”
❓ Wh- Questions
At least 2 Wh- questions directed at the audience.
“What are you going to do with the results?”
📅 Time Expressions
At least 2 future time expressions — specific, not vague.
next week / by Q2 / in two months / this Friday...
No Spanish filler rule
Any use of Spanish (este, o sea, como, éh...) = 1-point deduction from the going-to count. The goal: full English production under pressure.
90
💰 Retention & upsell: This is the capstone moment of M4. After the pitch, ask: “Imagine delivering this pitch in real life, in your next project kickoff. Could you do it?” Students who feel yes here are your strongest retention candidates. Frame M5 (“will”) as the natural upgrade: “Now that you master going to, next module you’ll add ‘will’ — which gives you two future tools instead of one.”
🏅 Rubric scoring: Count going-to sentences on your fingers during the pitch. Call them out after: “I counted 7 — you need one more. Go again for 30 seconds.” The re-attempt is the most powerful B-session moment.
§6 — Source Analysis
Source Analysis — Going To in a Real Pitch
Read a business pitch excerpt · Spot structures · Evaluate and improve
8 MIN

Read the pitch excerpt below. Answer the analysis questions out loud. There are no multiple-choice options — this is pure spoken production.

🎤 Project Pitch Excerpt — Grupo Nexo Q1 Review
“Good morning. We’re going to present three key changes for our delivery operations. First, we’re going to redesign the routing system by end of March. We’re not going to touch the driver schedules yet — that’s a Q2 decision. The IT team is going to deliver the first prototype on the 15th. Are you going to need a full demo before the board meeting?”
🔍 Q1 — Count: How many “going to” structures appear? Identify each one by subject and form. Which subjects are used?
🔍 Q2 — Identify: Which sentence is negative? Which is a Yes/No question? What is the time expression?
🔍 Q3 — Evaluate: Is this a strong pitch opening? What would you add or change to make it more persuasive using going to?
🔍 Q4 — Produce: Close this pitch with 2 more going-to sentences. Use at least one future time expression. Say them out loud now.
✓ Answer Key (teacher only)
Q1: 5 going-to structures. We’re (x3 — affirmative + 1 negative), The IT team is, Are you going to (question).
Q2: Negative: “We’re not going to touch the driver schedules yet.” Yes/No question: “Are you going to need a full demo?” Time expressions: “by end of March” / “on the 15th” / “Q2.”
Q3: Open answer. Strong points: clear structure, multiple subjects. Possible addition: a Wh- question, more audience engagement.
Q4: Open production. Accept any grammatically correct going-to sentences with a time expression. Correct errors immediately.
💡 Pedagogical: Q4 is where the metacognitive loop closes. The student has just analysed a pitch — now they produce. The analysis makes the production more intentional. This pattern (observe → analyse → produce) is the highest retention sequence in A2.1.

🎯 3 Phrases You Can Use Starting Tomorrow
Phrase 1
“We’re going to deliver this by [date].”
The most powerful project commitment in English. Use it in standups, kickoffs, client calls. It signals a plan already in motion — not a wish.
Phrase 2
“We’re not going to do X. We’re going to do Y instead.”
Pivoting under pressure in English. Use when a plan changes or a stakeholder questions your approach. Negative + positive going to = decisive leadership language.
Phrase 3
“What are you going to need from us?”
The most professional question to ask a client or stakeholder at the end of a pitch. Shows initiative. Opens the conversation to their needs. Practice asking it naturally.
