S-S · Free Role Play
Partner A calls Solaris Manufacturing to report a delivery problem — the wrong products arrived. Partner B receives the call, handles it professionally, asks 2 questions using "Could you…?", and promises to call back.
No script. Use everything from S2A. 3 minutes. Switch roles after.
"I'm afraid I can't resolve this immediately, but I'll look into it right away." / "Could you tell me your order number?" / "Could you send us a photo of the wrong products?" / "I'll ask our logistics team to call you back before 3 PM." / "I'm sorry for the inconvenience."
Class · Challenge Call
The hardest call: An international client calls, speaks too fast, and you don't understand. How do you handle it professionally? Class brainstorm — then 2 students demonstrate live with teacher playing the fast speaker.
Key: "I'm sorry, could you repeat that more slowly, please?" / "Let me make sure I understood…" / "I'll transfer you to my colleague who can help you better."
Step 1: Stay calm — say "I'm sorry, could you speak more slowly, please?" Step 2: Repeat what you understood: "Let me make sure I understood — your name is [X] and you're calling about [Y]?" Step 3: If still unclear: "I'm sorry, I'm not sure I understood correctly. Could you hold the line? I'll transfer you to my colleague." This is 100% professional. Never pretend to understand when you don't.
TEACHER NOTE — S2B Role Play Management

Round 1: teacher plays the caller — be slightly difficult: speak a bit fast, give a long company name, ask a question the student didn't expect. This is authentic pressure. Round 2: walk the room, listen for object pronouns. The most common error is "I'll ask to she to call you" → I'll ask her to call you. Badge b1 for the pair that completes Round 2 with the fewest hesitations. Round 3 is cathartic — it acknowledges the real fear students have and gives them 3 concrete phrases to handle it. This is the most emotionally valuable moment of S2B.

Colloquial — Apply Under Pressure
From S2A: Hold on · Put you through · Take a message · Not sure, let me check
5 min
S2B Colloquial Drill — Say it in 5 seconds or less
Situation → What do you say?
Caller asks for your manager. You need to check if she's there.
Answer: "Hold on, please." (not "Wait a moment" or "Espérate")
Situation → What do you say?
You found the person. The caller is waiting. You're about to connect them.
Answer: "I'll put you through now." (not "I transfer you" or "I connect you")
Situation → What do you say?
Caller asks: "Is the delivery for Tuesday confirmed?" You're not sure — need to check the system.
Answer: "I'm not sure — let me check and I'll get back to you."
Situation → What do you say?
She's not available. You offer to write down the caller's information.
Answer: "Can I take a message?" (then: "Of course, go ahead.")
TEACHER NOTE — Colloquial Speed Drill

Run this as a physical drill — no looking at notes. Teacher describes the situation, students respond immediately. If they hesitate more than 5 seconds, the team loses a point. The pressure is intentional: on a real phone call, there is no time to think. Students who freeze on "I'll put you through" in the drill will freeze less on a real call. Run 3 full rounds with different students. By the third round, the response should be automatic.

Quick Reference — S2A Grammar (from memory)

"COULD YOU…?" PATTERN
Could you + verb + object?
Could you call her back?
Could you repeat that?
Could you send us the invoice?
OBJECT PRONOUNS
me · him · her · it · us · them
Tell her I called.
I'll ask him to call you.
Send it to us.

Activity A — Transform: Direct → Polite + Object Pronoun

Drag each sentence into the correct sequence: polite "Could you…?" version first, then identify the object pronoun used.

  • ⠿"Call the manager." → Could you call him?
  • ⠿"Send the report to Ana." → Could you send it to her?
  • ⠿"Repeat your name." → Could you repeat it, please?
  • ⠿"Tell Mónica I called." → Could you tell her I called?
  • ⠿"Transfer this call to us." → Could you transfer it to us?
  • ⠿"Give me your number." → Could you give it to me?

Order them from simplest object pronoun (1 pronoun) to most complex (2 pronouns). Then check.

🗣️ MICRO-SPEAKING
Say 3 "Could you…?" requests to your partner as if you are on a phone call right now. They answer yes or no. Use: him / her / it / us — at least one pronoun per request.

GT ORIGINAL — The "Could you…?" Ladder

There are 3 levels of politeness in corporate English phone calls, and students should know all 3: (1) Imperative + please: "Please hold." — minimum standard. (2) "Could you…?": "Could you hold the line?" — professional. (3) "Would it be possible to…?": "Would it be possible to call back tomorrow?" — very formal, for senior clients or complaints. At A1.2, Level 2 is the target. Level 3 is exposure only — tell students it exists, they'll encounter it with international clients.

Listening — Extended Call: Complaint Handling
ConnectPro International · Guadalajara · ~60 seconds
8–10 min
Complaint Call — ConnectPro International, Guadalajara
A1.2 · M5 · S2B · Voice: Sarah & James (American English) · Speed: 0.80
ElevenLabs Script — ~165 words · ~62 seconds at 0.80

[Phone rings]

Receptionist: Good morning, ConnectPro International. Andrea speaking. How can I help you?

Caller: Hello Andrea. My name is Robert Wilson. I'm calling from Pacific Imports in San Diego. I need to speak with someone about a delivery problem — we received the wrong products last Tuesday.

Receptionist: I'm sorry to hear that, Mr. Wilson. I understand completely. Could you hold the line, please? I'll transfer you to our Logistics team. They can help you directly.

Caller: Of course — but I've been waiting for three days for a response. This is urgent.

Receptionist: I completely understand, and I apologize for the delay. I'm afraid our Logistics manager is in a meeting right now, but I'll ask her to call you back before noon today. Could you confirm your direct number?

Caller: Yes — it's 619 555 0184. Extension 22.

Receptionist: Perfect. So — Robert Wilson, Pacific Imports, San Diego, 619 555 0184, extension 22, delivery problem, callback before noon. Is that correct?

Caller: Yes, that's right. Thank you, Andrea.

Receptionist: Thank you for your patience, Mr. Wilson. I'll make sure she calls you within the hour. Have a good day.

Comprehension — True or False

1. Robert Wilson is calling from Los Angeles.

He's calling from Pacific Imports in San Diego, not Los Angeles.

2. Andrea offers to transfer the call to the Logistics team.

✅ "I'll transfer you to our Logistics team."

3. The Logistics manager will call back before 5 PM.

"I'll ask her to call you back before noon today" — not 5 PM.

4. Andrea confirms all the message details back to the caller.

✅ "Robert Wilson, Pacific Imports, San Diego, 619 555 0184, extension 22, delivery problem, callback before noon. Is that correct?"

5. Robert Wilson has been waiting for 3 days with no response.

✅ "I've been waiting for three days for a response." He's frustrated — and Andrea handles it professionally.
🗣️ MICRO-SPEAKING
From memory: What 3 things did Andrea do well in this call? Tell your partner. Then: What would YOU have done differently?
TEACHER NOTE — Listening Analysis S2B

This call is harder than S2A — the caller is frustrated and pushes back ("I've been waiting for three days"). After T/F: ask "Was Andrea professional? What exactly made her professional — not just 'she was nice'?" Expected answers: (1) She apologized for the delay specifically. (2) She gave a concrete callback time — "before noon," not "soon." (3) She confirmed all message details back. (4) She thanked him for his patience. These 4 behaviors are the A1.2 professional phone benchmark. Badge b4 awarded when all 5 T/F answers are correct.

TEACHER NOTE — Speaking Practice S2B

Q2 (voicemail) is the most valuable individual production in S2B. Tell students before they start: "Pretend you're leaving a voicemail on a real phone. Your partner will NOT answer — they will only listen and take notes. This is exactly what happens with international clients." Q3 (note-check) reveals whether the voicemail was clear. If a student got fewer than 4/5 pieces of information, they redo the voicemail once. Q6 (cold call) is the S2B climax — select students who've been quieter, not the most confident ones. The reaction of the class when a quiet student handles the call well is worth more than any explanation.

🎯 This Week at Work — Use These 3 Phrases
"Hold on, please. I'll put you through."
The complete transfer sequence in 8 words. Use it on the next English call you receive. Don't say "espérate" — say this.
"Could you confirm your number?"
End every message you take with this. A confirmed number = a complete message. International contacts expect it.
"I'll make sure she gets your message."
The professional close of any message-taking call. More than "OK" — it's a commitment. Your caller hears reliability.
