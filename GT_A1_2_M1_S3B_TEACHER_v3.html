§ 01
Picture Prompt — What's Their Routine?
5–7 min
Pick a photo. Describe the routine of the people you see. What do they do always / usually / sometimes? Use 3rd person — he/she. Start talking from the first second, no prep. Minimum 3–4 sentences.
⚡ Quick Win — 20 seconds
Challenge your colleague: "What's the date today? When is your next deadline?" Answer: "It's [Monday, March 15th]. My next deadline is on [April 5th]."

👉 Describe the routine of the person/people in the photo. Use 3rd person: "He always…" / "She usually…" / "They often…" · Minimum 3 sentences · Minimum 3 different frequency adverbs.
Visible scaffold:
"He/She always ___."  ·  "She usually ___ at ___ AM."  ·  "He sometimes ___."  ·  "They never ___ after ___."  ·  "She rarely ___ because ___."
Teacher Push — Push for 3rd person
"What does this person do first in the morning? Use 'always' or 'usually' — with -s on the verb."
"Does she sometimes work from home? Say: 'She sometimes works from home because...'"
"When does his day end? 'He usually wraps up at ___'"
"What does this person NEVER do? 'She never ___' — invent something logical."
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Show all 3 photos at once — student picks one and starts talking the moment they point. No reading the scaffold first.
  • S3B has a particular trait: the photo forces 3rd person from the first moment. That's deliberate — it's the structure that was hardest in S3A. The photo creates context for "he/she" so the student doesn't have to invent it.
  • If the student says "He check emails" (no -s) → immediate recast: "He checks emails, yes! Every morning." Don't interrupt, but the audible recast is enough to trigger self-correction.
  • Minimum 3 different adverbs per photo. If they only use "always" → push: "And sometimes? And never?"
  • Timing: strict 5–7 min. §4 is the core of S3B.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "In S3A you read Ana García's profile. Now you're creating someone else's profile from scratch, out loud, without text. That's exactly what you need in a real conversation."
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "In 50 minutes you'll describe YOUR full routine in an HR interview without notes. We start here."

§ 04
Role Play — 3 Rounds · HR Routine Interview
12–18 min
HR Interview · Performance Review
Round 1 — Guided
With scaffold · 4–5 min
Scenario: You are Ana García. Your company's HR is conducting a performance interview in English. You must describe your full routine: arrival, morning, midday, and end of day — using at least 4 different frequency adverbs.
"I usually arrive at ___ AM. I always ___ first thing."
"At ___, I often/usually ___. Our team always meets at ___."
"At lunchtime, I sometimes ___. I rarely ___ because ___."
"At the end of the day, I always ___ and usually wrap up at ___. I never ___ after ___."
Teacher — HR Questions (Round 1)
"What time do you usually arrive at the office?"
"Does your team always attend the Monday meeting?"
"Do you often work from home?"
Round 2 — Semi-free
Prompts only · 5–6 min
Scenario: Now you are YOURSELF. Same HR interview, but with your name, your company, your real schedule. HR has a list of questions — answer them using YOUR real data and the correct adverbs.
What time do you arrive? What do you do first? How often do you have meetings? Do you sometimes work from home? Do you check work at night?
Teacher — Tougher HR (Round 2)
"Interesting. Does your manager usually work from home too, or always in the office?"
"How often does your team miss the Monday meeting?"
"Do you think you always meet your deadlines?" — Expect an answer with an adverb and justification.
Round 3 — Free
No help · Timer · 3 min
Scenario: David Park just met one of your new colleagues at a conference. He's asking about YOU — your routine, your habits. Your colleague describes you in 3rd person. You play that colleague: describe a real (or invented) colleague using he/she, frequency adverbs, and 3rd-person verbs. 3-minute timer — don't stop.
Timer
3:00
Teacher — Natural interruptions as David Park
"Oh interesting! Does she always skip lunch? That sounds stressful."
"Wait — he never checks emails after 8? I wish I could do that!" (prompts more description)
"Does he usually meet his deadlines? I need reliable partners." (real corporate context)
Correction protocol: ZERO during the round. Only at the end: 1 celebration + 1 improvement point. Prioritize fixing 3rd-person -s if it comes up.

"My colleague Carmen usually arrives at 8 AM. She always checks the ERP system first thing and often attends three or four meetings a day. She sometimes goes to the cafeteria with her team, but she rarely works from home — she prefers the office. She never works past 7 PM. She's very strict about work-life balance."

🗣️ 3 Phrases for This Week
Three model phrases with dates and ordinals. Use them in emails and meetings this week. 💬
1
"The next deadline is on April 22nd."
La próxima fecha límite es el 22 de abril.
✅ ON + month + ordinal. The gold formula for deadlines.
2
"Our annual conference is in October."
Nuestra conferencia anual es en octubre.
💼 IN + month. For broad events or long periods.
3
"The meeting is on Thursday at 2 PM."
La reunión es el jueves a las 2 PM.
🏆 ON + day + AT + time. Perfect combo of time prepositions.
✅ Self-Evaluation — After Round 3
  • I used at least 4 different adverbs in Round 2.
  • I described my colleague with he/she + verb +s in Round 3.
  • I positioned adverbs correctly (before the main verb).
  • I answered David's unexpected questions without pausing too much.
  • I completed Round 3 without reading any notes.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: §4 is the CORE of S3B. Protect at least 12 minutes here. Don't cut it short.
  • Round 3 has a key difference vs. S1B and S2B: the student talks about a THIRD PERSON in 3rd person for 3 minutes. That's sustained production of he/she + adverb + verb+s — exactly what S3A measured in §5.
  • If the student forgets the 3rd-person -s in Round 3 → it's NORMAL at this level. Discreet recast, don't stop. Accuracy comes with cumulative practice.
  • S3B A1.2 mastery standard: 90 seconds of fluent colleague description with at least 3 adverbs and 3 correct verb+s = full S3 mastery.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You described your colleague in English for 3 minutes. With adverbs. In 3rd person. No notes. That's A1.2 in action — and you just did it."
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "In S4B and S5B of the module you use the same 3-round format — more complex vocabulary and context. The method stays the same."

Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Student reads the clues silently for 20 seconds → then reports them to David (the teacher) in 3rd person WITHOUT reading. The teacher asks questions about the profile.
  • This WOW is fundamentally different from the S3A Frequency Spinner: here the input is written (clues) and the output is oral 3rd person → reporting. It's production at a higher abstraction layer.
  • "Show 3rd Person Report": use it AFTER the student has tried it. The model report has perfect structure — the student compares it with what they said and self-corrects.
  • 6 rounds are enough. If the student is in flow → run 7 or 8. If they're tired → 4 is enough.

§ 06
Debate — Work-Life Balance · Free Opinion
7–10 min
Work-Life Balance · Opinion Time
GT rules: Minimal correction during the flow. The goal is for the student to hold an opinion in English — with adverbs and 3rd person when natural. Only 1 improvement point at the end, 1 celebration. Prioritize flow over accuracy.
Question 1
"Do you think checking emails after 8 PM is necessary? Or should companies never contact employees outside work hours?"
Push with: "How often do YOU check emails at night? What does your manager usually do?" — Get them using adverbs from real life.
Question 2
"Working from home: does it always improve productivity, or does it sometimes create problems?"
Push with: "In your company, how often do people work from home? Does your manager always allow it?" — Natural 3rd person in the answer.
Question 3
"Describe your ideal workday — what would you always do, rarely do, and never do?"
Personal, creative question. The student uses all 6 adverbs freely with THEIR ideal routine. Perfect closure for the S3A-S3B cycle: from comprehension to free, personal production.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: One question at a time. Listen for 60 seconds without interrupting. Note the single error you'll correct at the end.
  • Question 3 closes the module's thematic cycle: the student who started S1A talking about hobbies now describes their ideal workday using all the adverbs they learned. Celebrate that arc explicitly.
  • If the student spontaneously uses 3rd person in Question 2 ("my manager usually allows it") → flag it: "Did you notice you used 'allows' with -s? That's automaticity. That's A1.2."
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Debating in English. About your real job. Describing your ideal routine. Not textbook adverbs — YOUR adverbs, YOUR company, YOUR life. That's no longer basic A1."
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "In M2 the debate is about your company: 'Should your company expand internationally?' Same free-opinion structure, new company-and-product vocabulary."
  • If the student is motivated: "You finished the S3A + S3B block. You've got imperatives, dates, logistics AND work routine in English. That's 60% of M1's Exit Task already mastered."
