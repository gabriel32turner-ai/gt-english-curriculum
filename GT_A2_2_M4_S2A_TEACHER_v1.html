Warm-Up — It's Confirmed. Now What?
From uncertainty to certainty · Activate the language shift · Connect to real calendars · 5–8 min
⏱ 5–8 MIN
📅 Look at your calendar right now. What do you have confirmed for next week? Not "might happen" — what IS happening? Tell your partner 2 confirmed events with day and time.
💡 Teacher: Listen for "I will have" or "I'm going to have" — both are common errors for confirmed arrangements. Collect on board for §5.
🏢 Translation challenge: "Tengo una reunión con el director el jueves a las 10." How do you say this in English? Don't use "I will have" — there's a more natural, professional structure. Try it!
💡 Key: "I'm meeting the director on Thursday at 10." — Present Continuous. The meeting is already arranged, already in the calendar.
📞 Last class you used "might": "I might travel to Guadalajara." Now imagine the ticket is booked, the hotel confirmed, the meeting is in the calendar. What changes in the English — and why?
💡 The grammar changes because the certainty changed. "Might" = 30–50%. Confirmed = 100%. Different certainty → different structure.
⚡ Andrea update: Her Guadalajara trip is now 100% confirmed. The director called. The ticket is booked. The hotel is reserved. What is Andrea doing next week? Tell your partner using Andrea's schedule.
💡 Teacher: Push for "She's flying / She's meeting / She's presenting" — this is the target form for the whole session.

§4
Colloquial — It's Locked In
5 natural phrases for confirmed arrangements at work · 5 min
⏱ 5 MIN
"It's in the diary."
British/international corporate English for "está en el calendario y confirmado." Signals 100% certainty — no may/might needed. Very professional register.
"Are you joining Thursday's call?" — "Yes — it's in the diary."
"It's all set."
Everything is organized, confirmed, and ready. Use this after booking a flight, reserving a room, or confirming a meeting. Replaces a long list of confirmations with one phrase.
"Hotel, flight, meeting — it's all set for Friday."
"It's locked in."
More emphatic than "confirmed" — implies there's no going back, no changes. Very common in project management and sales contexts. Strong and professional.
"The client presentation is locked in for Monday at 11 — no rescheduling."
"Count me in."
Used to confirm your own attendance at an event. More energetic and engaging than "Yes, I'll attend." Shows enthusiasm and commitment. Very common in team communication.
"We're doing the team lunch on Friday." — "Count me in!"
"Pencil it in."
The opposite of "locked in" — tentative, not yet confirmed. Used when you want to hold a slot but not commit fully. Contrast: "pencil it in" (may/might) vs "it's locked in" (Present Continuous).
"Can we meet Tuesday?" — "Pencil it in — I need to check with my manager."
💬 Teacher Drill — Pencil vs Lock
This is a golden contrast for A2.2: "pencil it in" = might / uncertain / going to (personal plan) and "locked in / it's all set / it's in the diary" = Present Continuous for confirmed arrangements. Run a quick drill: give a situation, students say which phrase fits. "The client may call Thursday" → pencil. "The director confirmed our 10 AM" → locked in.
§5
Grammar — Present Continuous for Confirmed Future
When might becomes confirmed · am/is/are + -ing · Contrast with going to and will · 12–15 min
⏱ 12–15 MIN
🚨 Real Work Situation
It's Monday. Your manager in Chicago asks: "What are you doing on Thursday?"

You want to say: "Tengo una reunión con el cliente a las 10, y después vuelo a Guadalajara." Everything is confirmed — in the calendar, booked, arranged.
❓ "I will have a meeting" sounds odd. "I'm going to have a meeting" sounds planned but not committed. What sounds fully confirmed and professional?
✅ The Tool: Present Continuous for Confirmed Arrangements
"I'm meeting the client at 10, and then I'm flying to Guadalajara."

Rule: When a future event is confirmed — in the calendar, booked, arranged with another person — use am/is/are + verb-ing.

Why not "will"? "I will meet" = spontaneous decision made right now. "I'm meeting" = pre-arranged commitment already in place.
Why not "going to"? "I'm going to meet" = personal intention. "I'm meeting" = confirmed arrangement with another party.
🔗 Confirmation Chain — Click to Confirm Each Arrangement
Uncertain → Confirmed · Watch the language change
💡 Each card starts with an uncertain "might" sentence. When Andrea receives confirmation, click ✓ Confirmed! — the sentence upgrades to Present Continuous and the card turns green.
UNCERTAIN
❓ "Andrea might visit the client on Thursday — we're waiting for their response."
✅ "Andrea is visiting the client on Thursday at 10 AM — confirmed this morning."
Might + base verb → am/is/are + -ing · Same event, new certainty
UNCERTAIN
❓ "The team might have a training session next Wednesday — HR hasn't confirmed yet."
✅ "The team is having a training session next Wednesday at 9 AM — the room is booked."
Might have → is having · "have" with events uses -ing in confirmed arrangements
UNCERTAIN
❓ "Andrea may present the Q2 results to the board — it depends on whether they add it to the agenda."
✅ "Andrea is presenting the Q2 results to the board on Monday at 2 PM — it's on the official agenda."
May present → is presenting · Added to official agenda = confirmed arrangement
UNCERTAIN
❓ "The director might take the team to dinner on Friday — she mentioned it but didn't confirm."
✅ "The director is taking the team to dinner on Friday at 8 PM — the restaurant is reserved."
Might take → is taking · Restaurant reserved = social arrangement confirmed
🎉 All arrangements confirmed! Every "might" became Present Continuous. That's the grammar point.
FormCertaintyStructureCorporate example
Present Continuous 100% · Confirmed am/is/are + verb-ing I'm meeting the client at 10. (In the calendar.)
Going to Personal intention am/is/are + going to + base I'm going to call him. (My plan — not yet arranged.)
Will Spontaneous / offer will + base verb I'll send the report now. (Decided this second.)
May / Might 30–50% · Uncertain may/might + base verb I might attend — depends on my schedule.
⚠️ Top errors — correct immediately:
✗ "I will have a meeting on Thursday." → ✅ I'm having a meeting on Thursday. (It's in the calendar.)
✗ "She is going to fly to Guadalajara." → ✅ She's flying to Guadalajara. (Ticket booked = confirmed arrangement.)
✗ "We will meet the client tomorrow." → ✅ We're meeting the client tomorrow. (Appointment confirmed.)
✗ "I am meeting to the client." → ✅ I'm meeting the client. (No "to" — meet takes a direct object.)

GT Original — Present Continuous Future in Mexican Corporate English
  • 🇲🇽 Real context: When a Mexican professional says "voy a tener una reunión" in English, they almost always say "I will have a meeting" — which sounds odd to native speakers. "I'm having a meeting" is the natural, professional form when the meeting is confirmed.
  • 💼 Register note: "I'm meeting the client" (Present Continuous) sounds more committed and professional than "I'm going to meet the client" — it signals that the arrangement is real, not just intended. Teach students this register difference explicitly.
  • ⚠️ The test question: Is it in the calendar? Was it arranged with another person? → Present Continuous. Is it a personal plan you haven't told anyone yet? → going to. Decided just now? → will.
  • 🔊 Pronunciation: Contractions are essential — "I'm meeting / she's flying / we're presenting" — the full forms ("I am meeting") sound unnatural in spoken English.

Production — Your Confirmed Week
Real calendars · Present Continuous in authentic context · Mix with may/might · 12 min
⏱ 12 MIN
💬 How to run this: Students answer using Present Continuous for confirmed plans. Partner listens and marks: (1) any "will have" instead of Present Continuous, (2) missing -ing ending. The key test: "Is it in your calendar? Then use -ing." Switch after Q3.
Question 1 — Your Confirmed Week
Describe 3 confirmed events from your calendar next week. Use Present Continuous + day + time. Partner asks: "Is it in the calendar?" You confirm: "Yes — it's scheduled for [day]."
💡 Use: "On [day] I'm [verb-ing] [object] at [time] — it's confirmed / it's in the calendar / it's locked in."
Question 2 — "What are you doing next…?"
Partner A asks: "What are you doing next [Monday/Tuesday/Wednesday]?" Partner B answers with confirmed events using Present Continuous. Switch after 4 questions each. No "will" allowed!
💡 Model: "I'm having the team standup at 9, and then I'm reviewing the budget with my manager at 11. In the afternoon I'm presenting to the client at 3."
Question 3 — Phone Role-Play: Arranging a Meeting
Partner A (manager): Call to arrange a meeting. Ask about availability next week. Partner B (you): Check your real calendar. Confirm what you're doing when, and suggest a free slot. Both use Present Continuous for confirmed times.
💡 "Are you free on Wednesday?" — "I'm meeting the supplier in the morning, but I'm free at 2 PM." — "Perfect — I'm setting up the call for 2 PM then."
Question 4 — Friday Manager Update
It's Friday 4 PM. Your manager asks: "What's your plan for next week — what do you have confirmed?" Give a 5-sentence update. Mix Present Continuous (confirmed) + might (uncertain). Your manager can't tell which is which without your language!
💡 Include: "I'm [confirmed], I'm also [confirmed], and I might [uncertain] if [condition]. The [event] is locked in — but the [other thing] is still TBD."
Question 5 — The Busiest Week
Describe the busiest week you've had at work — or a future week you're planning. Use Present Continuous for everything that IS in the calendar. Use might for anything that's still uncertain. Aim for 6+ sentences.
💡 Challenge: Use all 3 forms — Present Continuous (confirmed), going to (intention), might (uncertain) — in the same description. Your partner tracks how many times you use each.
Question 6 — 60-Second Brief ⭐ Challenge

3 Phrases You Can Use Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"I'm meeting [person] on [day] at [time]."
Replace "I will have a meeting with…" forever. Say this exact structure the next time someone asks about your schedule. It signals that the meeting is real, confirmed, and professional — not just a vague plan.
PHRASE 2
"We're presenting / flying / staying — it's all set."
Use this in your next email or WhatsApp update when something is confirmed. The Present Continuous + "it's all set" combination is a complete professional confirmation — no ambiguity, no "might," no "probably."
PHRASE 3
"What are you doing on [day]?"
Ask this instead of "What will you do on Monday?" to your colleagues — it's the natural question for confirmed schedules. Notice how they answer. If they're professional English speakers, they'll answer with Present Continuous too.

Wrap-Up — Session Checklist
3 key takeaways · I can now… · Session 2B preview · 5–8 min
⏱ 5–8 MIN
01
Confirmed = Present Continuous. If it's in the calendar, booked, arranged with another person → am/is/are + -ing. Not "will." Not "going to." This is the professional register for confirmed arrangements.
02
Three different futures, three different meanings. "I'm meeting" (confirmed) · "I'm going to meet" (intention) · "I'll meet" (spontaneous decision). Choosing the right one changes how professional you sound.
03
The test question. Before choosing your future form, ask: "Is it in the calendar? Arranged with someone?" → YES → Present Continuous. "My plan, not yet told anyone?" → going to. "Just decided now?" → will.
