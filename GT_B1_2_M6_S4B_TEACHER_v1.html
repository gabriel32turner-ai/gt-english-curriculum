⚡ Hot Take  ·  5 min

Remote Work — Where Do You Stand?

“Although companies talk about trust and flexibility, the real reason most managers want people back in the office is control — and everyone knows it, but no one says it.”
Voted. Justify using although / whereas / as a result + S4A vocabulary in 30 seconds.
This Hot Take is deliberately provocative — it names the subtext that most corporate learners recognise but rarely voice. The “both are true” option is most honest and most productive for debate. After voting, ask: “Has anyone ever said this out loud to a manager? In Spanish? In English? Why the difference?” The language gap between private belief and professional expression IS the session.
🗣 Pitch Round  ·  10 min

The Policy Proposal

Your company is setting its work policy for next year. Each pair argues for one model. 2 min prep — 2 min pitch — leadership board votes on most convincing argument structure.

POLICY A — FULL REMOTE
Argue that your company should go fully remote permanently. Although this challenges traditional culture, the productivity data, talent access, and cost savings make it the only sustainable model for knowledge workers in a global competitive environment.
Must use: although / despite / furthermore / as a result
POLICY B — FULL OFFICE
Argue for full office return. Whereas remote work offers flexibility, it erodes the trust, spontaneous collaboration, and shared culture that made your team successful. Furthermore, junior employees lose the mentorship and visibility they need to develop their careers.
Must use: whereas / furthermore / however / despite
POLICY C — HYBRID 3-2
Argue for a structured hybrid: 3 days office, 2 days remote. Although neither extreme satisfies everyone, as a result of this compromise, both collaboration and individual productivity are protected. Furthermore, it is the most defensible model to both employees and leadership.
Must use: although / as a result / furthermore / whereas
POLICY D — OUTPUT ONLY
Argue for a fully output-based model: employees work wherever and whenever they want, evaluated only on deliverables. Despite the operational complexity, this is the only model that treats adults as adults — and as a result, it attracts and retains the best talent.
Must use: despite / as a result / although / however
PREP TIME
PITCH TIME
Policy D (Output Only) is the most radical and generates the most creative linking word use — learners must make a case for a model most Mexican companies have never implemented. The leadership board vote should evaluate argument structure (linking words) not personal preference. Ask: “Which pitch used whereas most effectively to show both sides fairly?”
🏃 Relay  ·  8 min

The Negotiation Table

Four stakeholders are negotiating the company’s hybrid policy. Each has a fixed constraint AND a required linking word. In 45 seconds, state your position, acknowledge the previous speaker’s point, and push your agenda forward.

👤 The Employee
Constraint: Cannot accept less than 3 remote days
Must use: whereas
Argue from personal productivity, boundary, and commute data. Use specific numbers if possible.
👔 The Manager
Constraint: Must keep team visible to leadership
Must use: although
Argue from collaboration, culture, and mentorship. Acknowledge employee needs but protect team cohesion.
📋 HR Director
Constraint: Must reduce attrition rate
Must use: as a result
Argue from retention data, market competition, and wellbeing. Frame flexibility as a talent strategy, not a perk.
🏢 The CEO
Constraint: Must cut operating costs by 15%
Must use: furthermore
Reframe the whole debate: remote work reduces real estate costs. Furthermore, it expands the talent pool. The financial case IS the business case.
Relay rule: Each turn must begin by acknowledging the previous speaker: “I hear that [X], however…” or “Although [previous point], my position requires…” The negotiation must reach a written outcome by the end: what policy was agreed?
The Negotiation Table is the most structurally complex relay in M6 — each seat has a fixed constraint (non-negotiable) AND a required linking word. The CEO seat (furthermore) is the most powerful because it reframes the negotiation entirely around financial logic. The written outcome forces consensus language: “As a result of this discussion, the company will…” — an authentic closing formula for any real meeting.

30 Seconds — Work Preference Edition

What is the single most convincing argument FOR office work that even a remote-first advocate could not dismiss? Use although to acknowledge its limitation, then make the case.
Steelman: find what the other side gets genuinely right
Carlos says “presence has historically been confused with commitment” in Mexican culture. Do you agree? Give your honest opinion using whereas + a specific example from your workplace.
Whereas before in my company… now however…
Where do you do your deepest, most important work — and what conditions make it possible? Use despite to acknowledge the reality that may prevent those conditions.
Despite [constraint], I thrive most when [condition]. As a result, I…
Your company announced full return to office next Monday. In 30 seconds, respond professionally — not what you feel, but what you would say. Use linking words to make the response diplomatic but honest.
Although I understand the reasoning, as a result of this change… however, I’d like to discuss…
In 5 years, what does your ideal work arrangement look like — and how are you moving towards it now? Use whereas to contrast your current reality with your intended future.
This is the S5 Capstone warm-up. Think forward. Use vision vocabulary if it helps.

🌟 Solo Challenge  ·  8 min

The Policy Recommendation

Your Scenario
You have been asked by your Director to present a 90-second verbal recommendation on your team’s work arrangement for next year. You have the data. You have a clear position. This is not a discussion — it is a recommendation. Deliver it.
✅ State your recommendation clearly in the first sentence
✅ Use at least 3 M6 linking word families to support it
✅ Use S4A vocabulary: flexibility / productivity / collaboration / culture / boundary
✅ Anticipate the counterargument and address it
❌ No hedging. A recommendation is not a question.
90s SOLO TIMER
Delivering a clear verbal recommendation to leadership in English is one of the highest-value professional communication skills at B1.2. Confidence + structure + vocabulary = credibility.
💰 “The recommendation you just gave — clear position, structured argument, counterargument addressed — is a core skill for international leadership. At B2.1, you’ll develop this into a full written policy brief. But right now: you just completed M6. Next session is the Capstone — where do you see yourself in 5 years? Everything you’ve said today is part of the answer. Come ready.”

🎯 3 Phrases From This Session — Use Them This Week:
“Although I understand the concern, the data points in a different direction.”
Use in any meeting where you want to respectfully contradict a manager or senior colleague. “Although I understand” is the most professional way to acknowledge and then redirect in English. Never sounds aggressive.
“Whereas the old model relied on visibility, this approach measures output.”
Use when proposing any change in how work is measured or evaluated. The parallel structure makes the contrast crisp and professional. Works in change management, team meetings, and upward communication.
“Furthermore, this gives us access to talent we couldn’t reach before.”
The business case for flexibility — one sentence. Use it after any initial argument for hybrid or remote work. “Furthermore” signals structured thinking; the specific claim about talent is hard to argue against.
