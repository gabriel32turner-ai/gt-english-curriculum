🗣️ Speaking Topic — Social Media & Identity

8 min

Rodrigo Vega — LinkedIn vs Reality

Rodrigo is a process engineer at a manufacturing plant in Celaya. He has an impressive LinkedIn profile — but his daily reality is very different. He's been thinking about what his online identity says about who he actually is.

Round 1 · 60 seg T→S
"Rodrigo's LinkedIn says he's an 'innovative leader.' His boss says he's quiet and avoids conflict. Which version is real? Does it matter?"
Round 2 · 90 seg S→S
"Ask your partner: How do you describe yourself on LinkedIn? Does it match how your colleagues see you? What do you leave out?"
Round 3 · 90 seg Class
"Does social media affect how you see yourself? Do people present a fake version online, or just a curated one? Is there a difference?"
Model Answer

"I think social media definitely affects how we see ourselves — but I'm not sure it's always a bad thing. My LinkedIn is curated — I only post the good stuff. The failures and boring days are never shared. But I don't think that makes it fake — everyone does it. The problem is when you start comparing your reality to someone else's highlights. I've started to unfollow accounts that make me feel bad. My feed is mine — I can control what is shown to me."

🎯 Grammar — Passive Voice (Introduction)

12–15 min
Passive Voice — when the action matters more than who does it
🚨 Real Situation
You're talking to a colleague about your company's social media policy. You want to say:
"Los datos personales son recopilados por la app cada vez que la usas."
"Esta función fue diseñada para mantenerte en la plataforma."
❓ In English — who designed it? The company. But that's not the important part. The RESULT is. How do you focus on the result?
✅ The Tool: Passive Voice
"Personal data is collected by the app every time you use it."
"This feature was designed to keep you on the platform."

Present Passive: is / are + past participle
Past Passive: was / were + past participle
REFERENCIAL — NOT drilling. Show the table briefly, then move to the Passive Spectrum visual and activities. The goal: students recognize passive in context and start using it naturally in speaking.
Key insight for students: Passive voice is everywhere in tech journalism, business reports, and academic language. "Fue creado por…" / "Es usado por…" — they already understand it in Spanish.
FormStructureTech / Social Media Example
Present Passiveis / are + past participleYour data is collected every time you open the app.
Past Passivewas / were + past participleThis algorithm was built by a team of engineers.
Future Passivewill be + past participleNew regulations will be introduced next year.
Passive Negativeis not / wasn't + p.p.Your data isn't protected by default.
Passive QuestionIs / Was + subject + p.p.?Is this feature used by most people?
⚠️ Common Errors
✗ "This app is design to…" → ✅ "This app is designed to…" (need past participle)
✗ "Data is collected BY engineers" when engineers are obvious/unimportant → ✅ omit "by" if the agent adds nothing

✏️ Activity B — Error Correction: True or False?
❌ "This feature is design by engineers." → The correct form is: "This feature is designed by engineers."
"Your data is collected every day." uses the present passive correctly.
❌ "These apps were build by large companies." → The correct past passive is: "were built" (irregular past participle of "build").
In passive sentences, the "by" phrase is always required even if the agent is unknown.

GT Original — Passive in Mexican Business English
  • 🇲🇽 Real context: In manufacturing and corporate México, passive voice appears constantly in reports, audits, and quality documents: "El pedido fue aprobado" → "The order was approved." Students use it in Spanish daily but avoid it in English.
  • ⚠️ Algorithm pronunciation: /ˈælɡərɪðəm/ — NOT "algo-RITHM." Stress is on AL-. The 'th' sound /ð/ trips up Spanish speakers — like the Spanish 'd' in "cada."
  • 🧠 GT Cultural note: In tech journalism and corporate communications, passive voice is the standard — "The policy was implemented," "The budget was approved," "The system was updated." Teaching this unlocks reading comprehension of real business texts.
  • 📱 Discussion hook: Ask students: "La última vez que actualizaste una app, ¿leíste los términos y condiciones?" → "The last time you updated an app, did you read the terms? Your data collection preferences were changed without you realizing."

🎧 Listening — The Algorithm Knows You

8 min
A tech journalist explains how social media algorithms shape our identity
The Algorithm Knows You
B1.1 · M4 · S1A · Voice: Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.85 · Pitch: 1.0
"Have you ever noticed that after you search for something online, ads for that product are shown to you everywhere? That's not a coincidence. Your behavior is tracked, your preferences are analyzed, and content is selected for you by algorithms — all in real time.

The platforms we use every day were designed with one goal: to keep us engaged for as long as possible. Every scroll, every click, every second you spend on the app is recorded. This data is then used to predict what you want to see next.

But here's what most people don't realize: your identity online is also shaped by what you're shown. If you're shown a certain type of content repeatedly, you start to believe that's who you are — or who you should be.

This isn't accidental. These systems were built to be addictive. And the people who built them know it.

So the next time you look at your feed, ask yourself: was this content chosen by me — or was it chosen for me?"
📎 ElevenLabs: Voice — Sarah (American English) · Speed 0.85 · Pitch 1.0 · B1.1 M4 S1A

🎤 Production — Speaking Practice

12 min
💬 How to run this: Students answer in pairs. Partner listens for: (1) passive voice used correctly, (2) at least one word from today's vocabulary. Correct any active sentence that should be passive. Switch after Q3. Questions 5 and 6 are timed challenges.
Question 1 — Your Feed
Describe your social media feed right now. What type of content is shown to you most? Do you think it was designed to show you those things, or did you choose it?
💡 Use: "My feed is filled with… / Content about X is usually shown to me… / I think this was designed to…"
Question 2 — LinkedIn vs Reality
Think about your LinkedIn or work profile. What is shown there? What is NOT shown? Is the professional online version of you accurate — or is it curated?
💡 Use: "My profile is presented as… / My daily reality isn't shown… / I think online identities are curated because…"
Question 3 — Data & Privacy
Are you worried about how your data is collected and used? Do you read privacy policies? Should personal data be protected by law more strictly — or do companies already do enough?
💡 Use: "My data is collected by… / I think data should be protected by… / In Mexico, privacy laws are…"
Question 4 — Comparison & Self-Esteem
Do you compare yourself to people you follow? Has your self-image ever been impacted by what you saw online? Should these platforms be designed differently to protect users?
💡 Use: "I compare myself without realizing it… / My self-image has been impacted by… / These apps were designed to…"
Question 5 — Opinion Challenge
Someone says: "Social media was created to connect people, not to harm them." Do you agree or disagree? Give 3 reasons. Use passive voice at least twice in your answer.
💡 Challenge: Use "was created / is used / is designed / are impacted" at least twice. Your partner counts the passives.
Question 6 — ⭐ 60-Second Challenge
In exactly 60 seconds: describe how your digital identity is managed. What is shown, what is hidden, what has been designed by the platforms, and what you control. No notes. Partner times you.
💡 Model opening: "My digital identity is managed in two ways. On LinkedIn, a professional version of me is shown… Personally, my data is collected by…"

🎯 This week at work
3 Phrases You Can Use Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"This feature was designed to keep users engaged."
Use this when explaining how technology works — in meetings, reports, or when your manager asks why people spend so much time on an app. It's professional and uses passive voice naturally. Replace "feature" with any process: "This system was designed to track inventory."
PHRASE 2
"Personal data is collected every time you use this app."
Use this when talking to clients or colleagues about digital privacy. Works in IT meetings, HR briefings, or casual conversations. Instantly sounds professional because it uses present passive — the standard form in tech communication in English.
PHRASE 3
"I compare myself to people online without realizing it."
Use this in any honest conversation about social media and wellbeing — with colleagues at lunch, in an HR wellness session, or just chatting. It's natural, self-aware, and immediately relatable to anyone who has a smartphone. A perfect conversation starter.
