§1 Warm-Up — Your Social Media Moment Activate prior knowledge · Connect to personal habits · Surface natural passive ⏱ 5 MIN
📱 What social media platforms do you use every day? Which one is your favourite?
→ T→S. Collect answers — expect Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok, WhatsApp. Note which platforms dominate the class.
⏰ How many hours a day do you spend on social media? Is that too much?
→ T→S. Push for honest answers. Most adults underestimate. "Your phone knows — check your screen time."
📸 Do you post regularly, or just scroll? Why?
→ S→S. This surfaces identity: are they consumers or creators? No right answer — opens the session topic naturally.
🗑️ If you deleted all social media for a week, what would change in your life?
→ S→S. Best question — surfaces dependency, anxiety, comparison. Save good answers for §7 Production.
⚡
Quick Start — 90 seconds · Earns Badge ⚡
Timer starts now. Each student says ONE sentence about their social media use. No repetition. Natural, real, honest. Go.
📘 Layered Listening Layer 1 (natural passive): Listen for "My feed is filled with…" — if a student says it, highlight it immediately. Layer 2 (comparison): "People on Instagram look happier" — this surfaces the session's core psychological theme. Layer 3 (deeper): "What would be different about your relationships?" — push if time allows. M4 is the most psychologically rich module. Let students be honest.
§2 Speaking — Your Online Identity 3 rounds · Model answer with Passive Voice · Color-coded language ⏱ 8 MIN
Session Topic
Does Social Media Change Who You Are?
Round 1 — 60s · T→S
Describe your social media persona. Is it the same as the real you? What's different — and why?
Push for specifics: "My profile shows me travelling, but I work 60-hour weeks." Accept all answers — vulnerability here = engagement throughout. If students say "it's the same," challenge gently: "Really? Nothing is left out?"
Round 2 — 90s · S→S
Ask your partner: "What's the biggest lie you've seen on social media?" Then share one of your own.
S→S pairs. Give 90 seconds. This is rich territory for the vocab in §3: "curated," "comparison," "algorithm." Note good vocabulary use. Do NOT correct grammar here — fluency round.
Round 3 — 90s · Class
Is it possible to be completely honest on social media? Why or why not? What would a "real" social media profile look like?
Model Answer (B1.1 — Passive Voice):

"My online persona was created slowly over years. Photos are chosen carefully. Captions are written to seem confident. I'm not sure when my real self and my online self separated — but I think it happened gradually. The comparison with others is done automatically, without even realising it."

Color key: ■ vocabulary · ■ passive voice · ■ colloquial
💡 Grammar Preview The model answer in Round 3 previews §5 (Passive Voice). If any student produces a passive naturally, write it on the board immediately: "Was created — perfect. We'll study exactly that structure in §5." Connecting production to upcoming grammar = 3x retention.

§4 Colloquial English — Talking About Social Media 4 natural phrases · Psychological context · Ready to use this week ⏱ 5 MIN
"I compare myself to other people."
When describing social media's psychological effect. Natural, honest, universally relatable in professional Mexican adult contexts.
"After scrolling for an hour, I compare myself to other people — and I always feel worse."
"It's not always real life."
When questioning what's posted online. A gentle, socially safe way to express skepticism about curated content.
"Their vacation photos look perfect, but it's not always real life."
"I need to disconnect."
When talking about digital detox or stepping back from screens. "Disconnect" here is figurative — stepping away from social media mentally.
"After a stressful day, I need to disconnect — no phone, no notifications."
"My feed is full of…"
When describing algorithm-driven content. Note: this phrase uses natural passive voice — "is full of" implies something fills it without saying who. Preview §5.
"My feed is full of fitness ads — I clicked one and now that's all I see."
💡 Drill Note Quick drill: "Your colleague posts only perfect photos of their life. What do you say?" Target answer: "It's not always real life. Their feed is curated — everything is chosen carefully." Run 2 more: (1) "You've been on your phone for 3 hours." → "I need to disconnect." (2) "Why do you feel bad after Instagram?" → "I compare myself to other people — it happens automatically."
§5 Grammar — Passive Voice Introduction Referential approach · Technology writing · 2 activities · Quick Poll ⏱ 10 MIN
📘 Teacher Frame — REFERENTIAL, not drilling B1.1 grammar is a reference tool. The key insight for this session: in technology writing, the PASSIVE is everywhere. Who controls your data? The company. But we say "Data is collected" not "The company collects your data" — because the ACTION matters more than the ACTOR. Social media is the perfect context: "Your data is sold." "Your feed is curated." "You are shown content that keeps you engaged." The passive hides who's responsible. That's the critical literacy hook.
StructureFormTech Example
Present Passive is/are + past participle "Your data is collected every day."
Past Passive was/were + past participle "This app was designed to keep you scrolling."
Passive with by is/are + past part + by "These algorithms are built by large teams."
Negative Passive is/are + not + past part "Your privacy is not always protected."
Question Passive Is/Are + subject + past part? "Is your data sold to advertisers?"
#1 ERROR missing "be" verb ✗ "This app designed to keep you scrolling."
✅ "This app was designed…"
💡 The rule that matters most: Passive = be verb + past participle. Never drop the "be." "This app designed…" sounds like a headline — not a sentence. The be verb carries the tense: "is designed" (now) vs "was designed" (in the past).
⚠️ Correct immediately:
✗ "This app designed to keep you scrolling." → ✅ "This app was designed to keep you scrolling."
✗ "Your data collected every day." → ✅ "Your data is collected every day."
✗ "These algorithms are build by engineers." → ✅ "These algorithms are built by engineers."
⚡ Quick Poll — 3 Questions · 10 pts each · Score 30 to earn 🎯
Select the correct passive form for each sentence.
Q1: "This app ______ by a team of 200 engineers."
❌ "Builded" is not a word in English. "Build" is irregular: build → built → built. The past passive needs "was" + past participle: "was built."
✅ "was built" — past passive. "Was" (be verb, past) + "built" (past participle of build). Perfect.
❌ "Is building" is active voice, present continuous. The subject (app) cannot build itself. Passive needed: "was built."
Q2: "Personal data ______ every time you click."
✅ "is collected" — present passive. "Is" (be, present) + "collected" (past participle). It happens now, repeatedly. This is the most common tech privacy passive.
❌ Missing the "be" verb. "Collected" alone is just a past participle floating without a tense. Always need: is/are/was/were + past participle.
❌ "Was collecting" is active past continuous. The data doesn't collect — the company collects it. Passive: "is collected."
Q3: "_______ your messages _______ by the platform?"
❌ "Do" is for active questions. Passive questions use the be verb: "Are your messages stored?" — not "Do your messages store?"
❌ "Is" is singular — "messages" is plural, so we need "Are." Also "store" is the base verb; passive needs the past participle: "stored."
✅ "Are your messages stored?" — plural subject "messages" → "Are." Past participle "stored." Question passive = be verb + subject + past participle. Perfect.
✏️ Activity A — Complete the Passive
1. "Your location ______ every time you open the app." (track)
2. "This content ______ by millions of people last month." (share)
3. "Personal data ______ to advertisers by many platforms." (sell)
4. "These features ______ to maximize your screen time." (design)

§6 Listening — Rodrigo's Social Media Confession Single speaker monologue · AmE · Passive Voice throughout · 5 comprehension tasks ⏱ 8 MIN
Rodrigo's Social Media Confession
B1.1 · M4 · S1A · Voice: Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.82
I used to post on Instagram every few days. Photos from restaurants, travel, weekends. Everything was curated — the right filter, the right caption, the right moment. My profile was designed to look like I was living my best life. But honestly? Most of it was staged.

The thing I didn't realise at first is how much the algorithm controls what you see. My feed was filled with people who seemed more successful, more attractive, more interesting than me. And I started comparing myself automatically — without even choosing to.

About six months ago, I deleted Instagram from my phone. Not forever — just for a month. And I noticed something strange. I had more time, obviously. But I also felt less anxious. The constant comparison was gone.

Now I'm back on Instagram, but my relationship with it has changed. I post less. I follow fewer accounts. My feed is curated differently — I follow people who inspire me, not people who make me feel bad about myself.

My advice? Be careful what content your feed is filled with. The algorithm was designed to keep you engaged — not to make you happy. Those are two very different things.

§7 Production — Your Digital Identity Opinion meter · 4 speaking prompts · 90s challenge · Hot Seat Dice ⏱ 12 MIN
📊 Opinion Meter — Before the discussion
"Social media does more harm than good."
Strongly Disagree Strongly Agree 5
Ask students to set their position before discussion. Revisit at the end — did any minds change?
💬 How to run §7: Target 2 passive sentences per answer. Partner marks: (1) any missing "be" verb, (2) wrong participle form. Correct after each response. Progress: Q1–Q2 = structured, Q3–Q4 = open, challenge = extended.
Question 1 — T→S · Passive Structure Practice
Describe your relationship with social media in 3 sentences using passive voice. Start each with: "My feed is filled with…" / "I'm influenced by…" / "My data is collected by…"
💡 Scaffolded starters help. If students struggle, say: "Just complete the sentence — I'll add the passive for you first."
Question 2 — S→S · Pair Discussion
Ask your partner: "What content is posted most on your feed? Does it affect how you feel?" Partner answers with at least one passive: "I'm affected by…" / "Photos of [X] are posted…"
💡 90 seconds. Both partners answer. Listen for passive — celebrate when it's natural.
Question 3 — T→S · Open Question
If all social media was deleted tomorrow, what would change in your work? Your relationships? Your mental health? Be specific — use real examples from your life.
💡 This question intentionally uses Second Conditional. Accept it — and note the difference from what we practiced today. B1.1 awareness, not drilling.
Question 4 — Class Discussion
Who is responsible for social media addiction — the user or the company that designed the app? Use passive voice: "Users are manipulated by…" / "These features were designed to…"
💡 No right answer. Push for passive use: "The app was designed to…" / "Our attention is sold to…" These are naturally passive — that's the point.
