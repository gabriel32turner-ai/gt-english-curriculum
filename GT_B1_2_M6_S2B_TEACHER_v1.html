⚡ Hot Take  ·  5 min

Success — Where Do You Stand?

“Whereas society measures success externally, the only success that actually changes how you feel is internal — and most people spend their entire careers chasing the wrong one.”
Voted. Justify using whereas / although / as a result + S2A vocabulary in 30 seconds.
The Hot Take uses “whereas” as its opening word — model the parallel contrast structure explicitly before justification. The “both must coexist” option is the most philosophically rich and requires learners to produce the most sophisticated argument. Require the use of S2A vocabulary (fulfillment, legacy, purpose, ambition) in every justification.
🗣 Pitch Round  ·  10 min

The Success Reframe

Each pair takes a conventional definition of success and reframes it. 2 min prep — 2 min pitch — group votes on most convincing reframe.

REFRAME A — PROMOTION = SUCCESS?
Conventional view: getting promoted is success. Reframe: although a promotion changes title and salary, it often removes you from the work you love. Furthermore, many of the most fulfilled professionals you know are specialists who were never promoted into management — and made that choice deliberately.
Must use: although / furthermore / whereas / S2A vocabulary
REFRAME B — MORE MONEY = SUCCESS?
Conventional view: earning more money is success. Reframe: despite the appeal of higher salary, research shows that beyond a certain income level, additional wealth has almost no impact on life satisfaction. As a result, the pursuit of wealth beyond sufficiency may be a distraction from what actually drives fulfilment.
Must use: despite / as a result / however / S2A vocabulary
REFRAME C — BEING KNOWN = SUCCESS?
Conventional view: recognition and public profile equal success. Reframe: whereas visibility might build a brand, it creates expectations that constrain what you can be. The most impactful professionals in most industries are not famous — they are deeply trusted in small, specific networks.
Must use: whereas / although / furthermore / S2A vocabulary
REFRAME D — RETIRING EARLY = SUCCESS?
Conventional view: early retirement signals career success. Reframe: although financial independence is meaningful, purpose is not something you retire from. As a result, many people who retire early return to work — not because they need the money, but because meaning requires contribution.
Must use: although / as a result / however / S2A vocabulary
PREP TIME
PITCH TIME
The Reframe format requires learners to hold two things simultaneously: the conventional view AND its counter. This is structurally ideal for linking words — “although X is true, Y complicates it.” After pitches, ask: which reframe was most convincing — and which linking word made it work?
🏃 Relay  ·  8 min

The Success Spectrum Relay

The spectrum runs from Fully External (wealth, title, recognition) to Fully Internal (fulfillment, purpose, peace). Each learner has a position on the spectrum. Argue your position. Then the next person responds: “Whereas you prioritise [X], I believe [Y] — as a result, my definition of success looks like…”

🏆 Fully External ⚪ Balanced ✨ Fully Internal
POSITION 1
Success = salary & title. Measurable. Comparable. Real.
POSITION 2
Success = reputation & recognition. What others say about you matters.
POSITION 3
Success = balance. Neither extreme. Both and neither.
POSITION 4
Success = growth & impact. Not what you have but what you change.
POSITION 5
Success = purpose & peace. Internal alignment. No audience needed.
Relay rule: After each speaker, the group votes whether the argument moved the spectrum marker left (external) or right (internal). Use the interactive bar. Justify any movement using a linking word. This is the most dynamic relay of M6.
The Success Spectrum is S2B’s unique relay format. The interactive marker creates immediate accountability — “did your argument actually move anyone?” is a harder question than “was your argument correct.” The group consensus mechanism forces learners to evaluate argument quality, not just produce language. Positions 1 and 5 are the most extreme and easiest to steelman; Position 3 (balance) is the hardest to argue convincingly.
⚡ Speed Round  ·  6 min

30 Seconds — Success Edition

Recall Carlos’s definition of success: “a relationship between what you value and what you’re doing.” Apply it to yourself right now in 30 seconds. Use whereas to show any gap.
Whereas I value [X], I currently spend most of my time doing [Y]. As a result…
Name one thing you considered a success five years ago that you no longer do. What changed — and what does that tell you about how success evolves?
Use: whereas before / I now / as a result — the transformation structure
Your manager defines team success as hitting quarterly targets. You define it differently. In 30 seconds, argue for YOUR definition using at least two linking words.
Professional context + personal conviction + linking word structure = B1.2 debate
Design your “success score” for 5 years from now. Name 3 criteria — none of which can be a job title or salary. Use furthermore to connect them.
Seeds the S5 Capstone. Be specific: not “be happy” but “feel [X] when I wake up on Monday.”
Whose definition of success have you been living by — your family’s, your company’s, or your own? Use whereas to distinguish between them honestly.
Most honest card in M6. Model it yourself first if energy is low.
In 30 seconds, end with one M6 phrase. Any of the 5. Make it land: say something true that earns the phrase. “That’s the goal,” “I’ve come a long way,” “no looking back”…
The phrase should feel like a natural close — not a formula appended to the end
30s SPEED TIMER
Card 4 (success score for 5 years) is a direct seed for the S5 Capstone. Card 5 (whose definition) is the most emotionally challenging and often the most honest — in Mexican professional culture, family definitions of success (stable job, house, family) frequently conflict with personal definitions. Let the tension be named, not resolved. Card 6 (end with an M6 phrase) is the vocabulary integration challenge — the phrase must feel earned, not mechanical.
🌟 Solo Challenge  ·  8 min

The Mentee Letter

Your Scenario
A junior colleague has just been offered a high-paying job that will make them miserable. They ask for your honest advice. You have 90 seconds to give it — in English, honestly, without diplomatic vagueness. Use everything you’ve learned about success in S2A and S2B.
✅ Use at least 3 M6 linking words from different families
✅ Use at least 4 S2A vocabulary words accurately
✅ Give honest advice — not what they want to hear
✅ End with a single, memorable M6 phrase
❌ No reading, no hedging. Mentors speak from conviction.
90s SOLO TIMER
Mentoring language in English requires exactly what M6 has built: structured argument (linking words) + precise vocabulary (success, fulfillment, legacy) + personal conviction (no diplomatic vagueness).
💰 “The ability to give honest, structured advice in English — without translation lag, without hedging — is what makes you a trusted voice in international teams. At B2.1, you’ll develop your own coaching framework and deliver it in a formal presentation.”

🎯 3 Phrases From This Session — Use Them This Week:
“Whereas before I measured progress by title, I now measure it by impact.”
One of the most powerful “whereas” sentences in M6. Use it in any professional context where you want to signal intellectual maturity and changed priorities. Works in appraisals, interviews, and strategic conversations.
“Despite the results, I’m not sure this is the right definition of success for us.”
Use in team retrospectives and strategy meetings when good numbers are hiding a bad direction. Requires courage to say — but signals the highest level of professional honesty.
“That’s the goal — and I’m moving towards it, although the path isn’t fixed yet.”
Combines an M6 colloquial phrase with a linking word in one authentic sentence. Shows direction without overclaiming certainty. Useful in performance reviews, goal-setting conversations, and career discussions.
