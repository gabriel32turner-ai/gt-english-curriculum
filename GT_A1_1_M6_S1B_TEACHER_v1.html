§1 · Hot Take — 8 min
Warm-Up Opinions
Activate. React. Speak fast.
⏱ 8 min
⚡ Teacher Note — How to Run Hot Take

Read each statement aloud. Students respond immediately — Agree or Disagree — then give ONE reason in English. Don't wait for perfect sentences. The goal is FAST activation. Move through all 4 in under 3 minutes, then open the class discussion on the one with the most disagreement.

🔥 Hot Take #1
"Small talk in English is easy."
🔥 Hot Take #2
"English at the reception desk is very important."
🔥 Hot Take #3
"My job needs English every day."
🔥 Hot Take #4
"I feel nervous with my badge or pass in English."
💡 Engagement Layer

After the quick reactions, ask: "Who agrees with #2? Give me ONE word about why." Write the words on the board — it becomes the topic anchor for the whole session. Students feel heard immediately.

§2 · Pitch & Debate — 10 min
Real Work Situations
Role-play · Partner Production · Class Discussion
⏱ 10 min
⚡ Teacher Note — Pitch Setup

Put students in pairs. Student A = visitor to the office. Student B = receptionist. Run the scenario twice, then swap roles. Use the S_A phrases as reference if they need help, but encourage no-notes production. Common error to watch: "I am come for a meeting" → correct to "I have a meeting with..." or "I'm here for a meeting."

🏢 Student A — Visitor
You arrive at the reception desk at Solaris Manufacturing in Celaya. You have a meeting with Daniel Ochoa at 10:00 AM. Your name badge is not working. Ask for help.
Use: "Good morning. My name is... I have a meeting with... My badge isn't working. Can you...?"
📋 Student B — Receptionist
You work at the reception desk. Greet the visitor. Ask for their name and who they are meeting. Give them a visitor pass. Tell them the meeting room number and floor.
Use: "Good morning! Welcome to... Who are you here to see? The meeting room is on the... floor."
Class Discussion — Debate It
T–S
What do you say when you arrive at a new office in English? Say TWO sentences.
"Good morning. My name is Ana García." / "I'm here for a meeting with Mr. Ramírez."
S–S
Partner A: "The printer is broken." Partner B: respond and offer help using "Can I...?" or "I can..."
"Oh, really? Can I use the one on the second floor?" / "I can call IT for you."
📚 Upsell Moment

After the role-play, tell students: "This is exactly Module 6 — Real Interaction. In A1.2, you will do this with past tense and dates. You are almost there." This motivates continuation and retention.

§3 · Relay — 10 min
Vocabulary Relay
Unjumble · Build · Produce
⏱ 10 min
⚡ Teacher Note — Relay Rules

Two mini-activities back to back. First: the UNJUMBLE game — students order the words to form correct sentences from Module 6. Second: the Relay Chain — each student adds one sentence to build a story about arriving at an office. Keep energy high. Set a 90-second timer for the unjumble. Award badge b2 on completion of both activities.

🔀 Activity: Unjumble — Build the Sentence
Sentence 1 of 4
Put the words in the right order:
🔗 Relay Chain — Build the Story Together
Student 1Good morning! My name is...
→
Student 2I have a meeting with...
→
Student 3The meeting room is on...
→
Student 4There is / There are...
💡 Retention Layer

The relay chain mirrors a real office interaction. Tell students: "This is a real conversation. You will use this next week at work." Students who see immediate utility retain the language 3x more effectively. The unjumble fixes the top 4 word-order errors at A1.1: subject-verb inversion, negative formation, question word placement, and there is/are.

§4 · Speed Round — 8 min
Colloquial Flash Production
Click a card → say the phrase → earn the badge
⏱ 8 min
⚡ Teacher Note — Speed Round Rules

Each card has a work situation. Students click to reveal, then say the correct colloquial phrase from S_A OUT LOUD. No writing. Just speaking. If they get it right, they mark the card as done. Challenge: complete all 6 cards in under 3 minutes as a class. Award badge b3 when 5+ cards are completed.

Card 1
Your colleague says: "Is the 3PM meeting confirmed?" You want to say you'll look it up. What do you say?
✓ "Let me check."
Card 2
You sent the wrong file to your manager. You want to apologize informally. What do you say?
✓ "Sorry about that!"
Card 3
Your manager asks: "Can you send the report by Friday?" You are happy with the plan. What do you say?
✓ "Sounds good!"
Card 4
You need to leave the call for 2 minutes to get a document. What do you tell your team?
✓ "I\'ll be right back."
Card 5
You made a mistake but it is fixed now. Your colleague says "Don't worry." What do you reply casually?
✓ "No worries!"
Card 6
It is the end of the day. You see your colleague at the door. What do you say to say goodbye?
✓ "Have a good one!"
🏅 Badge Earned — Colloquial Master!
📚 False Friend Alert

Common Mexican error: "Sounds good" → students often say "Suena bien" word-for-word in structure (verb first). Make sure they hear the English rhythm: SOUNDS-good not it-SOUNDS-good. Both are correct but the elliptical form is more natural in real conversation.

§5 · Solo Challenge — 12 min
Grammar in Production
Solo timed challenge + Spin the Wheel
⏱ 12 min
⏱ Solo Challenge — 90 seconds
Describe YOUR typical workday — from arrival to lunch — in English. Use these structures:
  • There is / There are (in my building...)
  • I can / I can't (at my job...)
  • Simple Present + always/usually/sometimes/never
  • He/She works... / My team has...
⚡ Teacher Note — Solo Challenge

Give students 30 seconds to think silently first. Then start the timer. While they speak, note 2-3 errors on the board — WITHOUT stopping them. After the timer, correct the errors collectively. This is error correction without disruption — one of the highest-impact teaching moves at A1.1.

🎡 Extension: Spin the Wheel — Grammar Situation
▼
Your grammar situation:
Spin to get your situation!
⚡ Teacher Note

After spinning, give the student 30 seconds to make 2 sentences using the grammar from that situation. If they can't, team helps. Rotate through 4–6 students.

🚫 Error Watch — Top A1.1 Errors

  • ❌ "There is chairs" → "There are chairs"
  • ❌ "I am come for..." → "I'm here for..."
  • ❌ "She can to speak" → "She can speak"
  • ❌ "I like drinking" → "I like coffee"
