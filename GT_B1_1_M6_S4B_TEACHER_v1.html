Hot Take — Travel, Power & Perspective

10 min
Hot Take · Full toolkit required · 90 seconds · Final B-session opener
Today's Hot Take
"People who have never left their own city cannot make decisions that affect people who live differently — and most of our politicians and executives are exactly that problem."
✅ Agree
You cannot understand what you have never experienced. A leader who has never seen a different economic reality will always manage from their own bubble. Limited perspective produces limited decisions.
✗ Disagree
Empathy and good judgment can be built through reading, listening, data, and diverse teams — not just travel. Some of the most effective leaders have governed places they never visited.
T→S Round 1 — Your Hot Take (90 sec)

Full toolkit: PP (what has happened because of limited perspective) + FC (what will happen if leaders don't broaden their view) + 2 opinion phrases + S4A vocabulary: assumption, perspective, privilege, openness, humble.

S↔S Round 2 — The Counter (60 sec)

Counter with "That's a good point, however..." then PP + FC + opinion phrase. Required: at least 2 different opinion phrases across the full exchange.

Teacher note — Final Hot Take of M6 B-sessions: Students in manufacturing who report to executives who have never visited their facility will feel this immediately. The grammar is the vehicle. The experience is the fuel. This is what fluency looks like when connected to real life.
Session tone: S4B is the last B-session of B1.1. Set the tone: "Today is the final production session before your Exit Project. Everything you have learned — PP, FC, opinion language — you are going to use all of it today. This is your dress rehearsal."

Pitch / Debate — The Perspective Argument

15 min
Pitch · Full toolkit · PP + stacked FC + opinion · Board-level argument
T→S Pitch Round 1 — The Perspective Case (3 min)
You are presenting to your company's board. Argue that investing in employee travel — to suppliers, clients, other facilities — produces better professionals. Structure: (1) What has happened when perspective was limited — PP evidence — (2) What will happen if the company never invests in perspective — FC warning — (3) And if that happens, what competitive disadvantage follows — stacked FC — (4) Specific proposal. Full toolkit required: PP + double FC + 3 opinion phrases + S4A vocabulary.
Vocabulary: perspective, assumption, privilege, curiosity, challenge, appreciate, openness, humble.
S↔S Debate Round 2 — The CFO (90 sec)
Partner plays CFO: "If we send employees to visit suppliers, the cost will impact quarterly results. And if results drop, the board will ask why we are funding tourism." Pitcher responds: "That's a good point, however the data has shown... / If we don't invest in perspective, we will lose... / And if we lose that, our competitors will..."
Use PP to present evidence, then double FC to show the cost of NOT investing outweighs the cost of travel.
Class Debate Round 3 — Best Perspective Argument (2 min)
Class votes on the most convincing and well-structured argument. The winner delivers the complete PP + double FC + opinion chain one final time. Close: "Even short trips open your mind — and open minds build better companies."
Colloquial: "Not everyone has the chance to travel — and that is exactly why companies that invest in perspective have a competitive advantage."
Teacher note — CFO framing: The CFO objection is real. Students who have approved or rejected travel budgets know this negotiation. Best responses will be specific: "The Rodrigo example — he saved a key supplier relationship by visiting in person. What is the value of that relationship compared to the cost of one trip?" Specificity defeats abstraction.
Upsell note: "You just argued for professional development investment in front of an imaginary CFO — in English, with evidence, double FC, and professional language. That is exactly what a bilingual manager does in an international company."

Relay — The Assumption Collapse Chain

10 min
Relay · Assumption Collapse Chain · 4 voices · Assumption → Trip → Discovery → Changed worldview
🏃 Assumption Collapse Chain — 4 Voices, One Changed Mind
Voice 1
👤 The Assumption
States what they believed before travel — PP
→
Voice 2
✈️ The Trip
Describes what happened that challenged the assumption — PP narrative
→
Voice 3
💡 The Discovery
States what collapsed — PP + one FC
→
Voice 4
🌎 Changed View
States global consequence — PP + stacked double FC
How it works:
Voice 1: "Before I traveled to Monterrey for work, I had assumed that the way we worked in Celaya was the standard."
Voice 2: "During that trip, I saw a facility running a completely different process — faster, more collaborative, with less hierarchy."
Voice 3: "I discovered that my assumption had been limiting my thinking. If I had stayed in my own bubble, I would have continued proposing solutions that only worked in one context."
Voice 4: "Travel has humbled me professionally. If every manager visited a supplier outside their own region, they would make better decisions. And if they made better decisions, the companies they led would be more competitive — and more human."

Target: Voice 4 must use PP + stacked double FC. The chain moves from personal limitation to professional consequence to global argument.
Starter assumptions for Voice 1
"I had assumed that the way we did things in my company was the only efficient way."
"I had always assumed that people in another region of Mexico thought about work and family the same way we did."
"I had assumed that international clients wanted the same things Mexican clients wanted — until the first meeting proved me wrong."
Teacher note — Relay debrief: After Voice 4, connect to Rodrigo's Oaxaca story from S4A: "How is this chain similar to what Rodrigo described? What was his assumption? What collapsed it?" This connects listening from S4A to live production in S4B — full module integration.

Speed Round — 30 Seconds Each

10 min
Speed Round · 30 seconds · Full toolkit · Final speed round of B1.1

30 seconds, one student. Full toolkit required: PP + FC + one opinion phrase. Final speed round of B1.1 — no pausing, no Spanish.

Prompt 1
Use "I saw things differently after that trip" as your opener. PP of what changed + FC of what you would have missed if you had never gone.
Prompt 2
Recall Rodrigo's Oaxaca trip from S4A. Report using PP: "He had assumed... / The trip had shown him... / He said that if he hadn't made that visit..." Full reported + PP + FC.
Prompt 3
Argue: "Not everyone has the chance to travel — and that inequality has consequences." PP of the problem + stacked double FC of what happens when perspective is limited by access.
Prompt 4
Use "Even short trips open your mind." Give a real example from your experience + PP of what it changed + FC of what would be different if you had stayed in your bubble.
Prompt 5 — Exit Project
Exit Project warm-up: State your S5 topic + "I strongly believe that if people don't understand [topic], [consequence] will happen. And the evidence is..." PP + FC + opinion. 30 seconds of your Exit Project.
Prompt 6
Counter the Hot Take: "Great leaders can govern places they have never visited if they listen to diverse voices." PP evidence + FC + "I see both sides, but the reality is..." Full toolkit in 30 seconds.
Completed: 0 / 6
Teacher note — Prompt 5: Every student must do this one. After the full round, go back to Prompt 5 and ask: "Whose 30-second Exit Project argument was most complete?" This generates peer feedback and Exit Project anticipation before the Solo Challenge.

Solo Challenge — Exit Project Final Rehearsal

20 min
Solo Challenge · 90 seconds · Exit Project rehearsal · Full B1.1 toolkit · No notes
🎯 The Challenge Brief — Exit Project Rehearsal
This is your Exit Project rehearsal. Any topic. 90 seconds.

• Present Perfect — at least twice
• First Conditional — at least twice
• Opinion language — at least 3 of the 5 phrases
• 3+ B1.1 vocabulary words from any module
• No notes. No reading. No Spanish.

After you finish, the class gives one piece of specific feedback: "The strongest thing you said was..." Then you do it again — better. This is the final preparation for S5.
Teacher Rubric — B1.1 Exit Project Standard
Present Perfect x2
"has/have + past participle" at least twice. Natural, not forced. Evidence of real state or change.
First Conditional x2
IF clause = present simple (never will). Main clause = will + base verb. At least twice, different subjects.
Opinion Language x3
3+ of the 5 phrases: "I strongly believe / In my opinion / I see both sides / That's a good point / The reality is..."
Vocabulary Range
3+ B1.1 vocabulary words from any module — used naturally, not forced.
Fluency & Time
90+ seconds, no long pauses, no Spanish. Coherent: evidence then consequence then position.
Engagement
The speaker means it. The class listens. The argument is connected to something real.
Round 2 — Do It Again, Better (90 sec)

After class feedback, run the timer again. One fix only. Everything else stays.

Teacher note — Two-attempt format: Specific, immediate feedback followed by a second attempt produces the most significant learning gains. Class feedback must be specific ("The strongest thing you said was X"). Correction must be singular ("The only thing to fix is Y"). One thing at a time. One more attempt.
GT Original — S4B is the last production session before the Exit Project. Every student should leave knowing: (1) their topic, (2) their core PP + double FC argument, (3) the one thing to improve. If a student doesn't know these three things, they are not ready for S5. Identify them and give targeted guidance before next class.
