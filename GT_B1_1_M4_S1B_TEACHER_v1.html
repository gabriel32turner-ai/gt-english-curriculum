📘 How to run Hot Take Give the student 20 seconds to think silently, then start the timer. The key requirement: use passive voice at least once ("My profile was designed to…" / "I'm presented as…" / "My life is shown as…"). Listen for passive construction errors — especially missing the "be" verb ("My profile designed to…" — catch it, correct it after). The goal is spontaneous production under time pressure. No grammar input — pure output.
§2 Pitch Round — Assigned Sides Two assigned sides · Passive voice arguments · 90 sec per side ⏱ 10 MIN
💬 Motion: "Social media companies should be regulated by governments to protect user data." — Assign sides. Push for passive voice: "Data is collected without permission." / "Users should be protected by law."
Side A — Pro Regulation
"Users must be protected by law"
  • "User data is collected without real consent…"
  • "Young people are being harmed by these algorithms…"
  • "Social media platforms were designed to be addictive…"
Side B — Anti Regulation
"Innovation will be killed by control"
  • "Free expression should not be controlled by governments…"
  • "If regulations are imposed, innovation will be killed…"
  • "Users should be educated, not protected by laws…"
💡 Engagement tip After both sides, flip the question: "What's YOUR real position?" Ask them to summarize it in one passive sentence: "I think user data should be protected because…" or "I think innovation should not be limited by…" This converts debate practice into genuine opinion-forming — the passive voice becomes a tool for taking a position, not just a grammar exercise.
§3 Relay — Transform It 5 transformations · Active ↔ Passive · Error detection · Personal response ⏱ 8 MIN
🔄 How Transform It works: Each card shows a sentence to transform — active to passive, passive to active, tense change, or error fix. The student responds orally. Click "Show Answer" to reveal the model. Card 5 is always personal — no single correct answer.
1
Active → Passive
"The algorithm controls what you see."
What you see is controlled by the algorithm.
2
Passive → Active
"Your data is collected by the platform."
The platform collects your data.
3
Present → Past Passive
"This feature is designed to keep you scrolling."
This feature was designed to keep you scrolling.
4
Fix the Error
"This app designed by a team of engineers."
This app was designed by a team of engineers — the word "was" is missing! Passive voice always needs the verb "be" + past participle.
5
Your Reality
"Rodrigo said his profile was curated to look perfect. What about yours?"
Open response — student answers with: "My profile was/is designed to…" — no single correct answer. Listen for passive voice structure and encourage honest, personal content.
📘 Facilitation note For cards 1–4, push for speed — the transformation should come quickly (3–5 seconds). If the student hesitates, show the answer and move on. Card 5 should be given more time — 20–30 seconds. The personal connection is the most important moment of the relay. Write down their card 5 sentence verbatim — it becomes a model sentence for the session.
§4 Speed Round — Social Media Grammar 10 cards · Passive voice in social media context · Score 7+ for badge ⏱ 6 MIN
Score 0 / 10
1. Your personal data ______ by hundreds of companies.
2. This feature ______ to maximize engagement.
3. Millions of photos ______ every day.
4. The algorithm ______ to show you what keeps you scrolling.
5. Young users ______ by unrealistic beauty standards.
6. This notification ______ to bring you back to the app.
7. Private messages ______ to be encrypted, but…
8. Social media trends ______ overnight.
9. These influencers ______ by brands to promote products.
10. Content ______ for maximum emotional impact.
📘 Speed Round note Run this as call-and-respond — student says the answer aloud before clicking. Score 7 or more earns the Speed badge ⚡. After the round, ask: "Which one was hardest?" — that's the structure to revisit. Common errors: using bare past tense ("collected") without "is/was", and forgetting "are supposed to" vs "suppose to."
§5 Solo Challenge — 2 Minutes 120 seconds · Digital Identity Story · 3+ passives · No notes ⏱ 8 MIN
🎤 Solo Challenge — 2 Minutes
Your Digital Identity Story — describe your relationship with social media: how you use it, how it was designed to keep you engaged, how it impacts your identity, and what you have decided to do about it. Use at least 3 passive voice sentences.
01
Past habit: "Before, I was influenced by…" / "I used to be shown content that…"
02
Present reality: "Now, I am presented as…" / "My feed is filled with…"
03
Future intention: "I have decided to…" / "From now on, I want to be known for…"
04
Use at least 3 passive voice sentences — partner tracks each one.
05
No notes. Raw, honest, personal — not generic statements anyone could make.
📘 Assessment Partner tracks: (1) Passive count — 3+ passives with correct structure (0–3 pts), (2) Personal depth — is this their real story, not a generic one? (0–4 pts), (3) Timeline coherence — past / present / future structure is clear (0–3 pts). Max 10 points. Enter scores in Leaderboard §7. The most common error: forgetting "was" or "is" — "I shown content…" — note it and correct it after the timer ends.
§6 Source Analysis — Social Media Language in the Wild 4 source cards · Passive voice in authentic contexts · 4/4 correct earns badge ⏱ 8 MIN
📱 Tech Article
"Instagram's algorithm was redesigned in 2022. Users are now shown content based on their watch time, not just likes. Privacy advocates argue that personal data is collected without meaningful consent."
What was changed in 2022?
💬 Reddit Comment
"I've been using LinkedIn for 3 years. My profile was built carefully — every word was chosen to attract recruiters. I was offered my current job because of it. But sometimes I feel like a product that's being sold."
How does this person feel?
📧 Email from HR
"Your professional profile on LinkedIn is viewed by clients and partners. It is expected to be kept up to date. Personal opinions that could be misinterpreted are advised to be avoided."
What is the HR department asking?
🔍 Grammar Detective
"This feature was added last month. Users are encouraged to report problems. New accounts are reviewed within 24 hours."
How many passive sentences are there?
💡 Discussion anchor After the 4 cards, ask: "Which source uses passive voice most strategically — and why?" Push for answers that explain the grammar choice. Best answer target: "The HR email uses passive to avoid giving direct orders — 'opinions are advised to be avoided' sounds less aggressive than 'don't post opinions.' The passive voice hides the agent (HR/company) and makes the instruction sound like a universal rule." That's B1.1+ level analysis.

🎯 This week at work — use these 3 phrases
3 Phrases Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"This was designed to keep you scrolling."
Use passive about technology or systems. When a colleague asks why an app or tool behaves a certain way: "This notification was designed to keep you coming back." Passive voice to explain intent — sounds analytical, not conspiratorial. Works in any professional tech conversation.
PHRASE 2
"My feed is filled with…"
Describe algorithm effect in natural conversation. Present passive in everyday use: "My feed is filled with sports videos — the algorithm figured me out." Use it this week when anyone talks about social media, streaming, or recommendations. Shows you can use passive about technology without sounding formal.
PHRASE 3
"It's not always real life."
Question online appearances with confidence. Use it when someone compares themselves to what they see online: "Remember, it's not always real life — those photos were curated." The passive "were curated" adds nuance and vocabulary. A phrase that shows emotional intelligence and language awareness at the same time.
