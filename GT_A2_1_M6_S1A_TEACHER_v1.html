§2 Speaking — The Business Timeline 8–10 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — SETUP

Say: "Valeria Torres works at Solaris Manufacturing in Celaya. She's going to tell us about a project — but she uses three different tenses. Your job: notice which tense she uses and when." This primes students before the listening in §6.

T-S
Valeria's manager asks: "What happened with the supplier last week? What's your plan? And what will you do today?"

How would you answer if you were Valeria? Give one sentence for each question.

"Last week I called the supplier and we discussed the delay. Next month we're going to sign a new contract. Today I'll send them the updated specifications." — three tenses, three sentences, one professional answer.

S-S
Talk to your partner. Choose a real project from your own work. Take turns:
Partner A: "What did you do / work on last week?"
Partner B: Answer in Simple Past, then add your plans (going to) and one commitment (will).

A: "What did you work on last week?"
B: "Last week I prepared a quality report. Next week I'm going to present it to my supervisor. And I'll send a copy to the team today."

Class
Class challenge: One student gives the PAST sentence. The next student adds the PLAN. The third adds the COMMITMENT (will). Keep the same topic. Can you do it without stopping?

S1: "Last week we reviewed the production data."
S2: "Next month we're going to change the process."
S3: "I'll write the report this afternoon." — Perfect chain. That's A2.1 integration.

§4 Colloquial — Business Closing Phrases 5 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — CONTEXT

These 4 phrases are the "glue" of professional English — they appear at the end of meetings, emails, and calls. Students at A2.1 often know the grammar but miss these natural connectors. Say: "These phrases will make your English sound real." Drill each one with a quick T-S substitution.

"I'll keep you posted."
Use after making a commitment or giving an update. Signals you won't disappear.
"We're still waiting for approval — I'll keep you posted."
"We made real progress."
A natural way to summarize a productive meeting or project phase.
"The meeting was long, but we made real progress on the contract."
"Looking forward to it."
Expresses anticipation about a future meeting, event, or collaboration. Replaces "I will wait for it."
"Great — the presentation is on Friday. — Perfect, looking forward to it!"
"Let's pick this up next time."
Used when you run out of time in a meeting but want to continue the conversation.
"We didn't finish the budget topic — let's pick this up next time."
💰 RETENTION + UPSELL NOTE

After drilling these 4 phrases, ask: "Which phrase can you use TOMORROW at work?" Let 2-3 students commit out loud. This is the "I'll keep you posted" moment — they associate the phrase with their actual reality. Follow up next session: "Did anyone use it?"

§5 Grammar — Three Tenses in One Conversation 12–15 min
Real situation: Your manager asks: "Can you give me a quick update?"

You want to say: (1) what you did last week, (2) what you plan to do, and (3) what you promise to do right now.

You need three different structures — and you need to switch between them naturally. That's the tool: Simple Past + Going To + Will in the same conversation.
TenseFormUseExample
Simple Past Subject + V-ed / irregular
Did + subject + base?
Didn't + base (neg)		 Completed actions in the past "We reviewed the report last Monday."
Be going to Am/Is/Are + going to + base Plans and intentions (already decided) "We're going to launch next month."
Will Subject + will + base
Won't + base (neg)		 Spontaneous decisions, offers, promises, predictions "I'll send you the specs today."
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — CONTRAST DRILL

After the table, do a rapid contrast drill: give ONE work situation, ask students to produce it in all three tenses. Example: Topic = "meeting"
Past: "I had a meeting yesterday."
Going to: "I'm going to have a meeting tomorrow."
Will: "I'll be in a meeting soon — I'll message you after."
This 3-sentence pattern should become automatic by the end of M6.

ACTIVITY A — Choose the Correct Tense
1. "What ___ last week?" asked the supervisor.
2. We ___ a new contract with Apex Solutions next Thursday. It's already confirmed.
3. The phone is ringing — "Don't worry, ___ it." (spontaneous decision)
4. The conference ___ last week. It ___ very productive.
5. I ___ available on Monday — I ___ travel to León for a client visit. (planned)

🗣️ Quick: Tell your partner one thing using each tense: something you did, something you are going to do, and something you will do right now.

ACTIVITY B — DRAG TO REORDER: The Three-Tense Email

Put these sentences in the correct order for a three-tense business email. Drag to reorder.

  • ⠿I'll copy you on the confirmation email this afternoon.
  • ⠿Next month, we're going to start the new production cycle.
  • ⠿Hi Daniel, I hope you're doing well.
  • ⠿Last week, we reviewed the Q1 report and identified two key areas for improvement.
⚡ TEACHER NOTE

Correct order: Greeting → Past (what happened) → Future plan (going to) → Promise (will). This is the canonical three-tense email structure. Internalize this — it appears in the Exit Project for M6.

§6 Listening — Valeria's Project Update 8–10 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — PRE-LISTENING

Before playing, say: "Listen for the three tenses. Every time you hear a past verb, raise your hand. Every time you hear 'going to', nod. Every time you hear 'will', tap the table." This kinesthetic activation keeps the brain engaged throughout.

Valeria's Update — Solaris Manufacturing, Celaya
A2.1 · M6 · S1A · Voice: Valeria (Mexican accent) · Speed: 0.82
Hi, Daniel. I have a quick update for you about the Apex project. Last week was very busy. We had two meetings with the supplier — one on Monday and one on Wednesday. We reviewed the delivery schedule and discussed the main problem: three components arrived late. We didn't receive them until Thursday, which caused a small delay in production. But we solved the problem — we worked extra hours on Friday and completed the quality check by the end of the day. So, what's next? Next month, we're going to start the new production cycle. We're also going to present the Q1 results to the regional team in Guadalajara. I already scheduled that meeting — it's on the 18th. Finally, today I'm going to send you two documents: the updated production timeline and the Q1 summary report. I'll also call the Apex coordinator this afternoon to confirm the next delivery date. I won't forget — I put it in my calendar. That's everything for now. I'll keep you posted if anything changes. Looking forward to the Guadalajara presentation — I think it's going to be a great meeting.

TRUE / FALSE

1. Valeria had one meeting with the supplier last week.
2. The components arrived late and caused a delay in production.
3. The Guadalajara presentation is going to happen next week.
4. Valeria will call the Apex coordinator today to confirm the next delivery.
5. Valeria thinks the Guadalajara meeting is going to be difficult.

§SP Speaking Practice — Your Three-Tense Story 10–12 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — PRODUCTION FOCUS

This section is the real output of the session. Students move from understanding to producing. Pair them, and remind them: the goal is to use all three tenses naturally. Don't stop them to correct mid-sentence — note errors and address at the end in a brief class feedback moment (2 min max).

QUESTION 1 · T-S
What did you actually do at work last week? Tell me three things you completed.
Use: I reviewed / I attended / I finished / I sent / I called...
QUESTION 2 · T-S
What are you going to do differently this week based on what happened last week?
Use: I'm going to... / We're going to... + time expression
QUESTION 3 · S-S
Tell your partner about a project you worked on. Describe what happened AND what the plan is next.
Past first, then going to. At least 3 sentences each.
QUESTION 4 · S-S
Your partner is your manager. Give them a 30-second project update using all three tenses.
Start: "So last week we... Next month we're going to... And I'll..."
QUESTION 5 · Class
Who can say the longest correct chain? Start with past, add going to, add will — same topic.
Challenge: no stopping, no translating. Three tenses, one breath.
🎯 3 Phrases for This Week Use at work — starting tomorrow
"I'll keep you posted."
After every update you give this week — add this at the end. Your manager will notice the professional English.
"We're going to [verb] + time."
Describe any future plan using this structure. Works in meetings, emails, and conversations with your team.
"Last [week/month/Monday] we..."
Open any project update with this phrase. It signals professionalism: you track what happened, you report clearly.
§7 Wrap-Up — Module 6 Begins 5–8 min

30-second exit drill: each student says one sentence in each tense.

🗓️
Simple Past = Done
Use it to report completed actions: "Last week I reviewed, attended, and submitted."
📅
Going To = Planned
Use it for intentions already decided: "We're going to launch next month."
⚡
Will = Right Now
Use it for spontaneous offers, promises, and real-time decisions: "I'll call them today."

I Can Now...

  • Use Simple Past to describe completed work actions
  • Use going to for confirmed future plans
  • Use will for spontaneous decisions, offers, and promises
  • Give a 3-sentence work update in three different tenses
  • Use phrases like "I'll keep you posted" and "Looking forward to it"
