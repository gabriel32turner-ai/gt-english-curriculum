§1

🏫 Warm-Up — Is Education Keeping Up?

5–8 min
Module 6 · Session 2A — Is Education Keeping Up? · Did school actually prepare you for real life?
Q1 — Your honest answer
Did school actually prepare you for the job you have now? What is one thing you use every day at work that school didn't teach you?
Q2 — Exams vs. skills
Do you think exams are a good way to measure real ability? Or do they just measure the ability to memorize? What would you change?
Q3 — The gap
Think about your job right now. What is one skill you have that no school taught you? How did you develop it? What does this tell you about education?
Q4 — The First Conditional frame
"If the education system doesn't teach practical skills, students will enter the workplace unprepared." Agree or disagree? What will the consequence be?
⚡ Quick Win — 20 seconds
Say this out loud: "I strongly believe that education hasn't kept up with the real world. If the system doesn't change, students will keep graduating without the skills they actually need."
Present Perfect + First Conditional + Opinion Language. All three tools, one sentence.
Teacher note — Education is personal: This topic generates some of the most honest conversation at B1.1. Students in manufacturing and logistics in Mexico often have complex relationships with formal education — some have university degrees in fields unrelated to their current work; others built their careers through experience, not credentials. Both groups have strong opinions. Do not push personal disclosure, but do make space for it: "Has your education been useful for your career? Honestly?"
§2

🗣️ Speaking Topic — Did School Prepare You for Real Life?

8 min

Did school prepare you for real life and work? What's missing?

The education gap question. Both a personal question and a systemic one. Use First Conditional to express consequences and opinion language to frame your position.

Speaking Topic · 3 rounds · First Conditional + Opinion Language in every answer
T→S Round 1
What is the most useful thing school taught you? And what is the most important thing it didn't? Use: "In my opinion..." and "If schools don't teach [X], graduates will..."
S↔S Round 2
Partner debate: "Schools should focus more on practical skills and less on academic knowledge." Agree or disagree? Use at least one First Conditional each and the counter-phrase: "That's a good point, however..."
Class Round 3
Class verdict: "Education in Mexico has changed a lot — but has it changed enough? What has improved? What hasn't?" Use Present Perfect to describe change over time, then First Conditional for the next step.

In my opinion, school taught me discipline and how to learn — but very little about how to work. I've learned more in my first year on the job than in four years of school. The reality is that the education system was designed for a different economy. If it doesn't evolve to teach critical thinking and practical application, graduates will keep arriving with knowledge but without skills. That said, I see both sides — academic knowledge builds the foundation. But if we don't close the gap between school and the workplace, companies will have to invest more and more in training new employees.

Opinion · First Conditional · Present Perfect

Teacher note — Model Answer: Point to the two First Conditionals used back-to-back with different subjects: "if it doesn't evolve" (the system) and "if we don't close the gap" (all of us). This demonstrates that First Conditional subjects are flexible — you can use "the system / the government / we / companies / I." Ask students to identify both and then produce their own with different subjects.

💬 Colloquial & Opinion Language

5 min
"School didn't prepare me for everything."
When to use
Honest, non-bitter. Acknowledges a real gap without attacking the system. "School didn't prepare me for everything — but it gave me the discipline to keep learning."
"Practical skills matter just as much."
When to use
When arguing for skills-based learning. "I've seen people with no degree do a better job than people with one. Practical skills matter just as much — sometimes more."
"The system needs to change."
When to use
Strong, direct, opinion-driven. Follow immediately with a First Conditional: "The system needs to change — and if it doesn't, we will keep producing graduates who are not ready."
🎯 Opinion Language Repaso — M6 Range (all 5 phrases active)
I strongly believe that… — "I strongly believe that education hasn't kept pace with industry."
In my opinion… / From my perspective… — "In my opinion, the real problem is the curriculum, not the teachers."
I see both sides, but… — "I see both sides — academic knowledge matters AND practical skills matter."
That's a good point, however… — "That's a good point, however the system hasn't caught up with what the market needs."
The reality is… — "The reality is, most people learn their most important skills outside of school."
Teacher note: From S2A onward, opinion phrases should appear without prompting. If a student starts a speaking round with "I think..." — pause and ask: "How would you say that at B1.1 level?" They should upgrade spontaneously. This habit is the difference between a B1.1 student and a B2 student in professional contexts.
§5

⚡ Grammar — First Conditional + Present Perfect in Opinion-Giving

12–15 min
Grammar · Combining Present Perfect (evidence) + First Conditional (consequence)
🔧 The Combined Tool — Present Perfect + First Conditional

Present Perfect tells us what has already happened. First Conditional tells us what will happen if a condition is met. Together they make the strongest possible opinion argument at B1.1: "Here is the evidence (PP) → Here is the consequence if nothing changes (FC)."

Education hasn't kept up with industry — and if it doesn't change, the skills gap will keep growing.
The curriculum hasn't been updated in years — so if students don't learn these skills in school, companies will have to teach them.
Technology has changed the workplace completely — but if schools don't adapt, graduates will arrive unprepared.
Common errors with combined structure:
✗ "Education hasn't kept up — if it won't change,..." → No will in the IF clause. Use: "if it doesn't change..."
✗ "The system has failed — unless the gap doesn't grow." → "Unless" = "if not" so you don't need the negative. "Unless the gap grows" = "if the gap doesn't grow."
✗ "School didn't prepared me..." → Past simple with regular verb: "School didn't prepare me." No double past.

§6

🎧 Listening — Rodrigo on What School Didn't Teach Him

10–12 min
Listening · Rodrigo Vega reflects on his education · What he wished school had taught him

Rodrigo Vega, plant manager at Vertex Logistics, speaks informally to his team at a training session. He talks about his own education and what he has learned since. Listen for Present Perfect, First Conditional, and opinion phrases.

What School Didn't Teach Me — Rodrigo Vega
B1.1 · M6 · S2A · Voice: Adam (American English) · Speed: 0.85 · ElevenLabs
I want to be honest with you today, because I think it's important. I have a degree in industrial engineering. Five years of university. And I've been in this industry for eighteen years. And the honest truth is — most of what I use every day at work, I did not learn in school. School taught me the theory. It taught me how to follow processes, how to pass exams. And I was good at it. I memorized what I needed, I applied what was asked. But the curriculum never taught me how to lead a team. It never taught me how to have a difficult conversation with an employee. It never prepared me for the day a production line stopped and everyone looked at me for the answer. Those skills? I developed them here. On this floor. Through failure, honestly, more than through success. In my opinion, the biggest gap is in critical thinking. If we don't teach young people to analyze a problem, not just solve a formula, they will arrive at work completely unprepared for real decisions. Now, I see both sides. The academic knowledge I learned — the engineering principles, the systems thinking — that has been valuable. But if the curriculum doesn't evolve to include communication, leadership, and real-world application, we will keep producing excellent exam-passers who can't manage a conversation. The system needs to change. And if it doesn't, we're going to keep doing what we're doing here — training new graduates from scratch. Which is fine. But it's expensive. And we could be spending that time on things that actually move us forward.
📊 Comprehension — Multiple Choice
1. What does Rodrigo say is the biggest gap in education?
2. According to Rodrigo, where did he actually develop his leadership skills?
3. What does Rodrigo predict will happen if the curriculum doesn't change?
✅ True / False
Rodrigo says his university degree was completely useless for his career.
Rodrigo has worked in the industry for eighteen years.

🗣️ Production — Speaking Practice

12–15 min
Production · Opinion + Evidence + Consequence · PP + FC + Opinion Language
🎯 Class Opinion Meter
"The education system in Mexico has NOT kept up with what the modern workplace needs."
Strongly DisagreeStrongly Agree
Class: 50% agree
T→S Q1 — Your Experience
Use Present Perfect to describe your education journey and its gap: "School has / hasn't given me..." Then add a First Conditional: "If I hadn't developed [skill] on the job, I wouldn't / won't be able to..."
Colloquial: "School didn't prepare me for everything."
S↔S Q2 — Skills Debate
"Practical skills matter just as much as academic knowledge." Partner debate: one person argues the academic side, the other argues the practical side. Both must use Present Perfect + First Conditional together at least once.
"That's a good point, however..." required for counter-argument.
S↔S Q3 — Your Company
Has your company invested in training? What has it taught you that school didn't? Use: "The company has / hasn't..." + "If it hadn't invested in training, I wouldn't have..."
Vocabulary: develop, application, skill, gap, curriculum
Class Q4 — The Proposal
You are advising the Minister of Education. What ONE change would you make to the national curriculum? State it with a First Conditional consequence: "If we introduce [X], students will be able to [Y]." Convince the class.
Opinion: "I strongly believe that..." / "The reality is..."
⚡ Solo Challenge — 90 seconds

Your turn: give your complete opinion on whether education has kept up. Use Present Perfect (evidence from experience), First Conditional (consequence if nothing changes), and 2 opinion phrases. 3 vocabulary words minimum. No notes.

🎯 This week, you can use these 3 phrases at work
3 Phrases for This Week
PHRASE 1
"School didn't prepare me for everything."
Use this in any professional context where you are acknowledging a learning gap honestly — and then bridge to what you did about it: "School didn't prepare me for everything — that's why I've invested in continuous learning." Honest + professional + forward-looking.
PHRASE 2
"I strongly believe that..."
Use this whenever you want to express a position with full confidence. Today's topic: "I strongly believe that the education system hasn't kept pace with what industry needs." This week's challenge: use it in a real meeting or conversation — in English.
PHRASE 3
"If the system doesn't change, students will..."
First Conditional as advocacy. Fill in the consequence that matters most to you. You can adapt this to any professional context: "If we don't update our training program, employees will..." Use it this week when you need to make the case for a change at work.
