§2 Pitch Round — The Wh- Question Gauntlet 12 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — WH- GAUNTLET FORMAT

This is a rapid-fire Wh- question drill disguised as a debate. One student is "on the hot seat" — they must answer 9 Wh- questions (3 per tense) in under 90 seconds total. The class times them. Every answer that uses the correct tense earns 1 point. Every answer that uses a vocabulary word from S4A earns a bonus point. Run 3 students minimum.

T-S · HOT SEAT
WH- QUESTION GAUNTLET — 9 questions, 90 seconds
SIMPLE PAST
What did you do last week?
Who did you meet with?
When did you resolve the issue?
GOING TO
What are you going to present?
When is the team going to meet?
What initiative is going to launch?
WILL
What will you do today?
Who will address the concern?
How will you implement this?

Past: "Last week I resolved a quality concern with our supplier." / "I met with Daniel and the Guadalajara team." / "We resolved it by Thursday morning."
Going to: "I'm going to present the Q2 results next Tuesday." / "The team is going to meet on Friday — it's scheduled." / "The new initiative is going to launch next quarter."
Will: "I'll call the client this afternoon." / "I'll address the concern — that's my accountability." / "I'll implement it by end of the month."

S-S · PAIRS
PAIR GAUNTLET — Ask & Answer with Fillers
Same format, pairs. Partner A asks 3 rapid-fire Wh- questions (one per tense). Partner B must start EVERY answer with a filler from S4A before giving the real answer:

"That's a good point." / "Let me think about that for a second..." / "To be honest..."

No filler = no point. Natural filler = 2 points. Then switch.

A: "What did you work on last week?"
B: "That's a good point. Last week I worked on the supplier contract — we renegotiated the scope."
A: "What are you going to propose next month?"
B: "Let me think about that for a second... We're going to propose a new risk management initiative."
A: "What will you do if the client raises a concern?"
B: "To be honest, I'll address it directly — I'm accountable for the outcome."

90 sec: answer all 9 questions. Go.

§3 Relay — The Interview Simulator 12 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — INTERVIEW SIMULATOR

This is the unique visual relay for S4B — and the closest simulation of the Exit Project format. One student is the "presenter" (Valeria). Three others play different roles: manager, team member, international client. Each asks their scripted question. The presenter must answer naturally, use fillers, and add proactive information. Teacher marks each answer on the rubric (natural / correct tense / vocabulary used). Run 2 full rounds with different presenters.

🎤 INTERVIEW SIMULATOR — One presenter, three questioners
MANAGER Question 1 of 5
"Good morning. What did you accomplish last week? Give me the highlights."
Expected tense: Simple Past · Expected vocabulary: resolve, address, outcome
Presenter answers here — use a filler first, then Simple Past.
MANAGER Question 2 of 5
"What concern did you raise in last Tuesday's meeting, and how did you address it?"
Expected tense: Simple Past Wh- · Vocabulary: raise, concern, address
Wh- question about past — use "I raised... / I addressed..." structure.
TEAM MEMBER Question 3 of 5
"What initiative are we going to implement next quarter? I want to make sure I understand the plan."
Expected tense: Going To · Vocabulary: initiative, implement, propose
Going To answer — describe a pre-decided plan with time expression.
INTERNATIONAL CLIENT Question 4 of 5
"We need clarification on the delivery timeline. Who will handle our account going forward?"
Expected tense: Will · Vocabulary: clarification, accountable, commitment
Will answer — spontaneous commitment + accountability statement.
MANAGER Question 5 of 5 — Freeform
"Moving on — anything else you want to mention before we wrap up?"
Free choice of tense · Use: "Moving on..." / "To be honest..." / any colloquial from M6
Free production — use fillers, add proactive info, close naturally.

45 sec per answer. Click ✓ Done when finished.

§4 Speed Round — Meeting Vocabulary & Fillers 8 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — SPEED ROUND FORMAT

Front = a meeting situation. Back = the correct vocabulary word or filler phrase + a sentence. First correct call = 1 point. For an extra challenge, have the student use the word in a Wh- question (not just a statement) — 2 points for a correct question. This directly prepares the Wh- question production for the Exit Project.

You bring up a problem in the meeting for the first time
RAISE (a point) · "I want to raise a concern about the timeline."
You deal with a problem or question directly
ADDRESS · "We addressed the concern last Thursday."
You find the solution to a problem
RESOLVE · "We resolved the issue by end of the week."
You formally suggest a new plan or idea
PROPOSE · "I'm going to propose a new schedule on Friday."
You are responsible for results and must explain them
ACCOUNTABLE · "I'm accountable for this project."
You need 3 seconds to think — what do you say?
"Let me think about that for a second..." → then answer
You want to agree and add your perspective
"That's a good point. I also think that..."
You want to say something honestly and directly
"To be honest, I think we need more time."
You want to change topics in a meeting
"Moving on — let's talk about the Q3 targets."
§5 Solo Challenge — Exit Project Dress Rehearsal 14 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — DRESS REHEARSAL FORMAT

This IS the Exit Project — same format, same rubric, same time. The only difference: it's practice, not assessment. Run it exactly like S5. Give each student 4 minutes. After each presentation, give feedback using ONLY two sentences: "The strongest moment was [specific sentence/moment]" and "For S5, try [one specific improvement]." No grade. Just preparation. Students who do well here are already passing S5.

🎓 EXIT PROJECT DRESS REHEARSAL — 4 Minutes
This is your S5 practice run. Same rules, same time, same rubric.

✓ Simple Past — what you did last week / month (at least 3 sentences)
✓ Going To — your team's confirmed plans (at least 2 sentences)
✓ Will — your personal commitments right now (at least 2 sentences)
✓ Wh- questions — answer 2 spontaneous questions from the class after your pitch
✓ Vocabulary — use at least 4 words from any S4A vocabulary list
✓ Fillers — use at least 1 filler phrase when answering questions
✓ Colloquial — end with at least 1 phrase from any M6 session

No notes. Real project. Professional English. This is it.


💰 RETENTION + UPSELL NOTE

After the dress rehearsal, ask: "Who feels more ready for S5 now than before this session?" Hands will go up — even from students who struggled. This is the moment to reinforce: "You just did the Exit Project. S5 is the same thing — but it counts. And you already showed me you can do it." This is how you retain 90%+ of students into A2.2. The confidence moment matters more than the grammar.

§6 Open the Box — Final Spontaneous Round 8 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — FINAL OPEN THE BOX

This is the last unscripted production activity before S5. High energy, fast pace. Each box = one spontaneous prompt. Students have 30 seconds — no preparation. Class votes thumbs up (natural English) or thumbs down (forced/wrong tense). Award 2 points per thumbs-up. This simulates the Q&A portion of the Exit Project — the part students fear most.

📦 FINAL OPEN THE BOX — Last spontaneous production before S5
1 Your manager just asked: "What will you do if the client raises a concern about the scope?" Answer with will + accountable + filler.
2 PAST: "What did you resolve at work this month? Give me 3 sentences — no stopping."
3 GOING TO: "What initiative is your team going to implement? When? Who's accountable?" Answer all three questions.
4 A client from the US asks: "Are we on the same page about the timeline?" Reply naturally — include going to + will + one colloquial phrase.
5 FULL INTEGRATION: Give your Exit Project in 30 seconds — compressed. Past + going to + will + one filler + one closing phrase. Go.

🎯 3 Phrases for the Exit Project

"Let me think about that for a second."
Your safety net for Q&A. Use it every time the teacher asks a spontaneous question. Buys 3 seconds. Sounds professional.
"To be honest, I think..."
Opens your opinion or concern section. Signals confidence and directness. Use it once in your Exit Project presentation.
"I'll keep you posted on any changes."
The perfect closing line for your Exit Project. Ends with a will commitment + follow-up signal. Professional. Natural. Memorable.
