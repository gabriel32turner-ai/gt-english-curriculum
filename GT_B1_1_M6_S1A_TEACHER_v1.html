🗣️ Speaking Topic — Do Individual Actions Make a Difference?

8 min

Do individual actions make a difference, or is it down to governments and corporations?

The central question of environmental ethics — and one where both sides are right. The goal is to express a nuanced opinion in English, use opinion language, and connect to your own industry and daily life.

Speaking Topic · 3 rounds · Opinion language required in every answer
T→S Round 1
Give your opinion: do individual choices make a real difference? Use: "I believe / In my opinion / I think the reality is..." Then complete: "If individuals don't act, ___."
S↔S Round 2
Partner debate: one person argues "individual actions matter," the other argues "only systemic change works." Each must use at least one First Conditional: "If we don't change X, then Y will happen."
Class Round 3
Your industry verdict: "In manufacturing and logistics in Mexico, has the environmental conversation changed? What has your company done? What hasn't changed yet?"

In my opinion, both matter — but not equally. The conversation has changed a lot in the last five years, especially in industrial companies. Personally, I try to do my part — I recycle, I try to reduce plastic. But if companies don't change their production processes, individual habits won't be enough. I've seen my company make some changes, but honestly, I see both sides — individual responsibility and corporate accountability are both necessary. If the government doesn't create stronger incentives, nothing will change at scale.

Note: Opinion language · First Conditional · Present Perfect · Colloquial phrase

Teacher note — Opinion language setup: After Round 1, pause and introduce the opinion language box (see §4 Colloquial). Students need to hear why opinion phrases matter: "In English, saying just 'I think' sounds weak. 'I strongly believe' signals confidence. 'I see both sides, but' signals nuance. These phrases tell the listener who you are as a thinker." This framing makes the language immediately meaningful and memorable.

💬 Colloquial & Opinion Language

5 min
Colloquial English · 3 natural phrases + Opinion Language B1.1 range
"I try to do my part."
When to use
When acknowledging your own individual contribution while admitting it may not be enough. "I try to do my part — I recycle, I use less plastic — but if companies don't change, it won't make a real difference."
"Small changes help."
When to use
When defending the value of individual action. "I know it feels small, but small changes help — especially if millions of people make them." Authentic, practical, optimistic.
"We all need to do more."
When to use
When ending an argument or acknowledging shared responsibility. "Honestly, I think we all need to do more — individuals, companies, and governments. There is no one single solution."
🎯 Opinion Language — B1.1 Range
I strongly believe that… — Use for a position you are confident about. Stronger than "I think."
In my opinion… / From my perspective… — Standard, professional. Use to introduce any personal viewpoint.
I see both sides, but… — Use when acknowledging complexity before stating your position. Shows nuanced thinking.
That's a good point, however… — Use in debate to acknowledge a valid argument before disagreeing. Professional and respectful.
The reality is… — Use to state something you believe is true regardless of what others think. Confident and direct.
Teacher note — Opinion Language: These five phrases are the entire M6 grammar bonus. They appear in every session and every speaking activity. Post them on screen for the rest of the class. Require at least one per speaking turn from now on. The fastest way to raise a student's perceived fluency and sophistication in English is to give them a wider range of opinion openers — it signals B1 to a native speaker immediately.
Upsell note: "That's a good point, however…" is a phrase that almost no Spanish speaker learns in a standard English class. It is a high-value professional phrase used daily in meetings, negotiations, and debate. Students who master it stand out. Name that value explicitly.
§5

⚡ Grammar — First Conditional & Opinion Language

12–15 min
Grammar · First Conditional as an opinion weapon · "If we don't act now, things WILL get worse."
🎯 Real Work Situation

Your manager asks you in a team meeting: "What do you think about our company's new sustainability policy? Is it enough?" You want to give a clear, professional opinion that shows you have thought about the consequences.

How do you express your view and connect it to a real consequence?

🔧 The Tool — First Conditional as Opinion Weapon

The First Conditional connects a real condition to a real consequence. In opinion-giving, it makes your argument stronger: "If [condition], then [consequence] will happen." It turns a vague feeling into a logical claim.

If we don't invest in sustainability, we will lose clients who care about it.
If the government regulates emissions, companies will have to change.
If I don't reduce my consumption, my carbon footprint will keep growing.
If we switch to renewable energy, costs will fall over time.
IF clause (condition)Main clause (result)Use
If + subject + present simplewill + base verbReal, possible future result
If companies don't change,the planet will suffer.Opinion with consequence
If I choose green suppliers,my impact will be smaller.Personal decision + result
If we all demand better policy,governments will respond.Collective action argument
Common First Conditional errors:
✗ "If companies don't change, the planet would suffer." → Would = second conditional (hypothetical). Use will for real/likely situations.
✗ "If companies will change, things will get better." → Never use will in the IF clause. Use present simple.
✗ "If I don't will change my habits..." → Never. If clause = present simple always.
🗣️ Micro-Speaking: Give your opinion on the environment using the First Conditional as your argument: "In my opinion, if [X] doesn't [Y], then [Z] will happen." 30 seconds. Real position, real consequence.

GT Originals — Teaching Notes

🎧 Listening — Rodrigo's Sustainability Memo

10–12 min
Listening · Rodrigo Vega on the new sustainability directive · Vertex Logistics, Celaya

Rodrigo Vega, plant manager at Vertex Logistics, is briefing his team on a new corporate sustainability directive. Listen for his opinion, the First Conditional arguments he uses, and any Present Perfect phrases.

The Sustainability Directive — Vertex Logistics, Celaya
B1.1 · M6 · S1A · Voice: Adam (American English) · Speed: 0.85 · ElevenLabs
OK, everyone — I want to take ten minutes to talk about the new sustainability directive that came from corporate last week. Let me be direct: I think this is overdue. Our company has known about the environmental impact of our operations for years. We've reduced some packaging waste, and we've started tracking our energy consumption — but honestly, it hasn't been enough. In my opinion, if we don't take this seriously now, we will lose contracts with the international clients who are starting to demand green supply chains. The directive has three parts. First, if we reduce fuel consumption by ten percent this quarter, the company will invest in electric forklifts for this facility. That is a real incentive. Second, if we switch to certified sustainable suppliers for our packaging by June, we qualify for a government environmental credit. And third — this is the part I care most about — if we hit our carbon reduction targets for the year, every team here will receive a bonus. Now, I know some of you are skeptical. I hear that. But I strongly believe that if companies like ours don't change, the regulations will force us to anyway — and that will be more expensive and less flexible. I'd rather we lead this change than react to it. From my perspective, the real question is not whether we change — it's how fast we change. The planet hasn't waited. And our clients won't either.
Listening note: The script is dense with First Conditional and opinion language from §4 and §5 — every element of today's session appears in Rodrigo's speech. Before the second listen, ask students: "Count the First Conditional sentences you hear. Count the opinion phrases." Answer: 5 First Conditionals, 3 opinion phrases ("In my opinion," "I strongly believe," "From my perspective"). This develops active listening for grammatical structures.
📊 Comprehension — Multiple Choice
1. What does Rodrigo say will happen if the team reduces fuel consumption by 10%?
2. What is Rodrigo's main argument for acting NOW rather than waiting for regulation?
3. How does Rodrigo describe the company's previous environmental efforts?
✅ True / False

If the team hits carbon reduction targets, they will receive a bonus.

🗣️ Production — Speaking Practice

12–15 min
Production · Express, argue, and defend · First Conditional + Opinion Language in action
🎯 Class Opinion Meter
"Individual actions make a real difference to the environment — even without corporate or government change."
Strongly DisagreeStrongly Agree
Class: 50% agree
T→S Question 1 — Your Honest Position
Do you personally believe that what you do makes a difference? Give your honest opinion and support it with one First Conditional: "If I don't [X], then [Y] will happen."
Opinion language: "I strongly believe..." / "The reality is..." / "In my opinion..."
S↔S Question 2 — Your Company
Has your company taken any real steps toward sustainability? Use Present Perfect: "We have / haven't...". Then add a First Conditional: "If we don't [X] soon, [Y] will [Z]."
Present Perfect + First Conditional together = B1.1 opinion range
S↔S Question 3 — Debate: Who is Responsible?
Partner debate. Position A: "The individual is responsible." Position B: "Only corporations and governments can make real change." Use at least 2 First Conditionals and one counter-phrase: "That's a good point, however..."
Debate language: "That's fair, but..." / "I see both sides, but..." / "The reality is..."
Class Question 4 — Your Proposal
Propose ONE change — in your company, your home, or your city — that would have a real environmental impact. State it as a First Conditional argument: "If [this change] happens, [X] will improve because..." Make the class want to support your proposal.
Vocabulary: carbon footprint, sustainability, reduce, contribute, impact, policy
Class Question 5 — The Next Generation
PROGRAMA note: the First Conditional is the grammar of warning, hope, and advocacy. Final question: "What will happen to the next generation if we don't change?" 60 seconds. No notes. Full sentence every time.
Colloquial: "We all need to do more" — end with this phrase and mean it
⚡ Solo Challenge — 90 seconds

Give your complete opinion on personal vs. corporate environmental responsibility. Use: 2 First Conditionals, 2 opinion phrases, 3 vocabulary words from today. Connect naturally. No notes.

Teacher note — Question 5: The "next generation" frame forces students into First Conditional naturally because they are talking about future consequences of present inaction. Students who can answer this question fluently with First Conditional + opinion language are working at a B1.1 ceiling performance level. Celebrate it explicitly.
🎯 This week, you can use these 3 phrases at work
3 Phrases for This Week
PHRASE 1
"I try to do my part."
Use this when someone asks about your environmental habits. Honest, humble, and practical. Follow it with a First Conditional for extra impact: "I try to do my part, but if companies don't change, it won't be enough." Say it this week — in English.
PHRASE 2
"That's a good point, however..."
Use this in any meeting, discussion, or debate when you disagree with someone but want to acknowledge their argument first. This phrase is what separates B1.1 English from B1.2 English in professional contexts. It signals respect + critical thinking at the same time.
PHRASE 3
"If we don't act now, things will get worse."
The First Conditional as a professional warning. Use it in any context where there is a consequence for inaction: environmental, professional, organizational. Replace "act now" and "get worse" with whatever is relevant to your situation at work this week.
