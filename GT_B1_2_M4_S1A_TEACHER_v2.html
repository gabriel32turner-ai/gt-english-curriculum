§1

🔥 Warm-Up — Mental Health at Work

⏱ 7 min
Speaking first · No grammar drills · Real stories
Q1  T-S
On a scale of 1 to 10, how stressful is your job right now? What makes it that number?
Q2  S-S
Have you ever worked in a place where mental health was completely ignored? What happened?
Q3  S-S
If your company offered free therapy sessions, would you use them? Would you tell your manager?
Q4  Class
Is mental health still a taboo topic in Mexican workplaces? Has that changed in the last 5 years?
⚡ Quick Win — 20 seconds
Say this out loud right now: “If I had more support at work, I would be more productive.”
That's B1.2 grammar in action. You already know how to do this.
Say: “Before we start — number 1 to 10. What's your stress level right now? Write it down. We'll come back to it at the end.” This anchors the session emotionally and makes the grammar feel necessary, not abstract. Common reaction: some students laugh nervously. Good sign — they're engaged. Don't skip Q3 — the “would you tell your manager” question always triggers authentic Second Conditional without realizing it.
§2

🗣 Speaking Topic — “What would you do if...?”

⏱ 10 min
Carlos Mendez — Operations Manager, Celaya
Carlos has been working 60-hour weeks for three months. He hasn't slept well, he snaps at his team, and last week he had chest pain he ignored. If his doctor told him he had burnout, he wouldn't believe it. “I'm fine,” he says. “This is just work.”
Round 1  ·  60s  T-S
“If you were Carlos's doctor, what would you tell him? What would you recommend?” Use Second Conditional naturally.
Round 2  ·  90s  S-S
“Ask your partner: If your company had a real mental health policy, what would it include? Name 3 specific things.”
Round 3  ·  90s  Class
“If YOU were in Carlos's position, what would you do differently from day one? Be specific.”
Model answer (Second Conditional + vocabulary):
If I were Carlos, I'd start by talking honestly with my manager. At the end of the day, burnout doesn't fix itself — if you ignore the symptoms, they get worse. If the company offered therapy, I'd take it — I'm not gonna lie, that takes courage. I'd also set boundaries: no emails after 8pm. If everyone did that, the wellbeing culture would shift. It's a red flag when chest pain feels “normal.”
■ Grammar: Second Conditional x3 ■ Vocab: burnout, ignore, set boundaries, wellbeing ■ Colloquial: at the end of the day / I'm not gonna lie / it's a red flag
Always reveal the model answer AFTER students have spoken — never before. The model is a target, not a script. Layer the rounds: Round 1 is observational (what would Carlos do), Round 2 is personal (what would YOUR company do), Round 3 is fully owned (what would YOU do). This progression from 3rd person to 1st person is intentional — it builds confidence before they speak from their own experience. Error to watch: “If I would be in his position” → correct warmly: “If I were in his position”.

📚 Vocabulary — Mental Health & Wellbeing at Work

⏱ 12 min
Nouns — 10 Core Words
burnout
/ˈbɜːrnaʊt/
Complete mental and physical exhaustion from chronic stress
Carlos didn't realize he had burnout until his body gave out.
anxiety
/æŋˈzaɪəti/
Persistent worry and nervousness about uncertain outcomes
Work anxiety was affecting her sleep every single night.
resilience
/rɪˈzɪliəns/
The ability to recover from difficulties and keep going
Building resilience is not about ignoring pain — it's about processing it.
stigma
/ˈstɪɡmə/
Shame or disgrace attached to a condition or situation
The stigma around mental health is slowly decreasing in corporations.
wellbeing
/ˈwelˌbiːɪŋ/
The state of being comfortable, healthy, or genuinely happy
Employee wellbeing programs reduce turnover significantly.
boundary
/ˈbaʊndri/
A limit you set to protect your time and energy
Setting boundaries at work is a sign of self-awareness, not laziness.
pressure
/ˈpreʃər/
Stress caused by urgency or high expectations from others
The pressure to perform was affecting his health in visible ways.
support
/səˈpɔːrt/
Help and encouragement given to someone who needs it
A supportive manager can change someone's entire mental health trajectory.
therapy
/ˈθerəpi/
Professional treatment to improve mental or emotional health
If therapy were free, more employees would ask for help.
stress
/strɛs/
A state of mental tension caused by demanding circumstances
Chronic stress damages both performance and physical health.
Verbs & Expressions — 10 Core Words
burn out
/bɜːrn aʊt/
To become physically and mentally exhausted from overwork
She burned out after 18 months without a real break.
struggle
/ˈstrʌɡəl/
To have great difficulty with something difficult or painful
He struggled to concentrate after the performance review.
cope
/koʊp/
To deal effectively with a difficult or demanding situation
How do you cope when work gets completely overwhelming?
acknowledge
/əkˈnɒlɪdʒ/
To recognize or accept that something exists or is real
If managers acknowledged stress openly, teams would feel safer.
normalize
/ˈnɔːrməlaɪz/
To make something accepted as normal in a community or culture
We need to normalize conversations about mental health at work.
set boundaries
/sɛt ˈbaʊndriz/
To define limits of what you will and won't accept
She finally set boundaries and stopped answering emails at midnight.
disconnect
/ˌdɪskəˈnekt/
To step away mentally or digitally from work
If he could disconnect properly on weekends, he would be more productive.
seek help
/siːk help/
To actively look for support or professional assistance
If the stigma disappeared, more people would seek help sooner.
trigger
/ˈtrɪɡər/
To cause a reaction or emotional response in someone
Micromanagement triggers anxiety in most introverted employees.
thrive
/θraɪv/
To grow and succeed, especially in challenging conditions
People thrive when they feel psychologically safe at work.

Micro-Speaking — 30 seconds
Pick 3 words from today's vocabulary. Say ONE sentence using each word about a real situation at your job. Partner: listen and give a thumbs up 👍 if the sentence makes sense.
Three words to distinguish clearly: “Stress” = temporary pressure you can recover from quickly. “Anxiety” = persistent worry, often without a clear cause. “Burnout” = a point of no return if untreated. 💬 Colloquial — M4 · How We Really Talk About This

⏱ 7 min
“It's a red flag”
Warning sign about behavior or a situation
"Your boss never gives feedback? That's a red flag for the whole team culture."
“That's messed up”
Something unfair, shocking or morally wrong
"They fired him the week after his surgery? That's messed up."
“I called it”
I predicted this would happen — and I was right
"She quit after 3 months? I called it — the culture was toxic from day one."
“I'm not gonna lie”
About to say something honest and possibly uncomfortable
"I'm not gonna lie, I've been struggling since the restructure."
“At the end of the day”
What ultimately matters most, regardless of everything else
"At the end of the day, your health is more important than any project."
🗣
Micro-Speaking — 45 seconds
Use 2 colloquial phrases from this section in one connected story about a real work situation you've witnessed or experienced. Make it specific: a company, a person, an outcome.
These phrases appear in the model answers throughout the session — students who use them in production get full credit in the exit task. Drill: give each phrase as a context and ask students to finish the story. Example: “My coworker worked late every day for 6 months, and then...” (expect: “I called it — burnout was coming” or “that's messed up, no one said anything”). “At the end of the day” is B1.2 gold — it signals opinion + conclusion together.
§5

🎯 Grammar — Second Conditional (Referential)

⏱ 15 min
Second Conditional
Imaginary situations · What you would do if things were different right now
Real situation first: Your manager asks: “What would you do if you had to tell a team member their performance is affecting others?” You want to answer but you're not sure of the structure. The tool you need: Second Conditional.
✅ Affirmative
If + Past Simple,
would + base verb
“If I had more support, I would perform better.”
❌ Negative
If + didn't / weren't,
wouldn't + base verb
“If he weren't so stressed, he wouldn't make so many mistakes.”
❓ Question
What would you do
if + past simple?
“What would you do if your manager ignored your concerns?”
⚠️ Critical error: “If I would have more support…” ➡ “If I had more support…” — NO “would” in the if-clause. Ever.
⚠️ Were vs. was: “If I were in his position…” is grammatically correct at B1.2. “Was” is informal/acceptable but coach them toward “were.”
Activity A — Choose the Correct Form
If the company _____ mental health days, the team would feel more valued.
If I _____ in his position, I would have an honest conversation with HR.
What _____ you do if your manager ignored your wellbeing concerns?
If she _____ set boundaries, she wouldn't be so overwhelmed right now.
🗣
Micro-Speaking — 30 seconds
Tell your partner one thing using Second Conditional about YOUR job right now: “If I could change one thing about my work environment, I would...”
Activity B — Unjumble the Sentence

Click words in the correct order to build the Second Conditional sentence:

SENTENCE 1 — Click words in order:
if I were the manager, I would prioritize team wellbeing
Key contrast at B1.2: Second Conditional (imaginary now) vs First Conditional M3 (real future). “If I pass the exam, I will celebrate” = real, possible. “If I had more time, I would study more” = not true now. M4 is entirely hypothetical — this emotional distance makes it perfect for sensitive topics like mental health. Students say things they might not say directly.

🎧 Listening — “If I could start over, I wouldn't hide it” (Carlos)

⏱ 10 min
Carlos Mendez
Operations Manager · Celaya · B1.2 Character · M4
0:001:10
B1.2 · M4 · S1A · Voice: Carlos (Male, Mexican English) · Speed: 0.85 · ElevenLabs
I've been thinking about that conversation with my doctor for months now. She told me I had burnout. Burnout. I said: impossible. I'm fine. I just need a weekend. But if I'm honest with myself, I knew something was wrong for a long time. The chest pain. The irritability. The way I couldn't disconnect even on Sundays. If I had sought help earlier, I wouldn't have lost three months of my life recovering. My manager was supportive, actually. I was surprised. He said: 'If more of us acknowledged this openly, the team would be stronger.' And he was right. The stigma is the real problem. If there weren't so much shame around mental health, people would seek help before they crash. I didn't set boundaries. I didn't cope well. I just pushed through, and that's the mistake. Now I prioritize my wellbeing. Not because I'm weak. Because I want to thrive, not just survive.
Comprehension Questions
What did Carlos's doctor tell him?
What was Carlos's first reaction?
What surprised Carlos about his manager?
What does Carlos say is the real problem?
True / False
“Carlos set clear boundaries before he burned out.”
“Carlos regrets not seeking help earlier.”
“Carlos now believes mental health is a sign of weakness.”
Play the script using browser TTS (click the script toggle and read aloud at a natural pace) while students follow along. Key Second Conditional lines to highlight in the script: “If I had sought help earlier, I wouldn't have lost three months” / “If there weren't so much shame, people would seek help”. After comprehension questions, ask: “Which sentence from Carlos hit closest to home for you?” This transitions naturally into §SP.

🗣 Speaking Practice — Deep Dive Discussion

⏱ 10 min
Guided discussion · Real opinions · No wrong answers
T-S
If you could redesign your company's approach to mental health from scratch, what would you change first?
Use: If I could... I would... / The most important thing would be...
S-S
Have you ever felt burned out? If that happened again, what would you do differently now?
Use: If it happened again, I would... / I'd probably...
Class
If the stigma around mental health completely disappeared overnight, how would your workplace change?
Use: People would... / There would be more... / It would be easier to...
S-S
Carlos said 'I just pushed through and that's the mistake.' Do you agree? What would you do instead?
Use: I think... / If I were him... / The mistake wasn't... it was...
T-S
What's one boundary you wish you could set at work right now? If you set it, what would happen?
Use: If I set this boundary... / My manager would / wouldn't...
Challenge
60-second challenge: Explain the difference between stress, anxiety, and burnout using a real example from your industry.
Use all 3 words + at least one Second Conditional sentence
Start with Q1 (T-S) as a class opener — it generates the most ambitious answers. Move to Q2 for a more personal S-S pair. Q3 Class is best as a debate: divide the class and ask them to argue a side. This Week — 3 Phrases You Can Use at Work

⏱ 3 min
🎯 This week, you can use these 3 phrases at work:
“If I were in charge, I would...”
Next team meeting. If someone asks for your opinion on a process, open with this. It signals initiative without aggression.
“At the end of the day, what matters is...”
In any work conversation about priorities. Use it to redirect to what truly counts. Sounds professional and calm.
“I need to set a boundary here...”
When someone asks you to do something beyond your capacity or role. It's assertive, adult, and completely acceptable at B1.2 level.
This is the “this is why I came to class” moment. Read each phrase out loud. Ask: “Where exactly this week would you use this?” Make students commit to a specific situation. If they can't, it means the language hasn't connected to their real world yet. Go back and make it more personal.
