§1
Business Travel — Confirmed or Uncertain?
Activate thinking about certainty · Connect to real corporate travel situations
⏱ 5 MIN
✈️ Travel check: Do you have any business trips planned for the next month? Are they 100% confirmed — or still uncertain?
💬 Partner activity: Tell your partner 2 things that are confirmed for next week and 2 things that are uncertain. How do you express the difference?
🧠 Quick think: What's the difference? "I'm flying to Guadalajara on Monday" vs "I might fly to Guadalajara." Which one is booked? Which one is just an idea?
⚡ Teacher note: S3 focuses on the contrast between present continuous (confirmed arrangement) and may/might (uncertain possibility) in business travel context. Core target: "I'm meeting the client on Thursday" vs "I might visit the factory — it depends." If students produce both forms correctly in context by §7, the session succeeded.
⚡ Quick Win — 20 seconds
Say this out loud: "I'm meeting [someone real] on [a real day]. But I might also [something uncertain]." Use your real schedule!
1:00
This warm-up surfaces the core contrast of the session: confirmed vs uncertain future. Don't correct grammar yet — just get students talking about what's "definite" vs "maybe." Collect 3–4 student sentences on the board for later correction in §5. Key insight: students naturally use "going to" for everything — today they learn that Present Continuous signals a CONFIRMED arrangement (ticket bought, meeting in calendar).
Ask who's traveled for work recently. Get them to describe the trip planning: what was certain, what changed last minute. This creates genuine context. Students in manufacturing often travel Celaya→CDMX or Celaya→Monterrey — use these real routes.
Business travel English is a high-value topic for HR managers evaluating program ROI. Students who can confirm appointments, express uncertainty about schedules, and update itineraries in English are immediately more valuable. Reference this when sending progress reports to companies.
§2
Business Travel — Your Next Trip
Andrea Soto's travel dilemma · 3 speaking rounds · Grammar in real context
⏱ 8 MIN
Andrea Soto's Situation
Andrea is planning a business trip to Guadalajara next week. Some things are confirmed — some things are still uncertain. How does she communicate this to her manager?
Focus: "I'm [verb]-ing…" = confirmed  ·  "I might / I may…" = uncertain  ·  "I'm not sure yet" = honest doubt
Round 1 — Confirmed Plans
What business trips or meetings do you have confirmed for the next 2 weeks? Tell your partner using "I'm [verb]-ing…" — as if the appointment is in your calendar.
💡 "I'm flying to CDMX on Tuesday." · "I'm meeting the supplier at 10 AM." · "We're visiting the plant on Thursday."
Round 2 — Uncertain Possibilities
What might happen next week that isn't confirmed yet? A possible trip? A meeting that depends on someone else? Use "I might / I may…" + a reason for the uncertainty.
💡 "I might travel to Monterrey — it depends on the client." · "The meeting may be cancelled."
Round 3 — Mix: Confirmed + Uncertain
Give your partner a 60-second travel update mixing confirmed and uncertain plans. Can your partner identify which is which?
💡 "I'm checking in at the hotel on Monday — that's confirmed. But I might need to extend my stay — we'll see."
"Next week I'm flying to Guadalajara on Tuesday morning — the tickets are booked. I'm meeting the regional manager at 2 PM. But I might also visit the new warehouse — it depends on the schedule. And the dinner with the client? I'm not sure yet. It may happen on Wednesday, but she might have a conflict. I'll keep you posted."

§4
Everyday English — Certainty Phrases
5 phrases native speakers use to express certainty and uncertainty at work
⏱ 5 MIN
"We'll see."
= Not certain yet, I'll decide later
"Are you flying business class?" — "We'll see. It depends on the budget."
"It depends."
= The answer changes based on conditions
"Will you stay an extra day?" — "It depends on the client meeting."
"I'll keep you posted."
= I'll update you when I know more
"Is the flight confirmed?" — "Not yet. I'll keep you posted."
"TBD."
= To Be Determined — used in emails and calendars
"What time is the dinner?" — "TBD. We're waiting for the client."
"It's in the diary."
= It's confirmed and scheduled
"Is the meeting definite?" — "Yes, it's in the diary."
Practice mini-dialogues: Student A asks about a plan, Student B uses one of these 5 phrases to respond. Rotate through all 5. Then reverse. Goal: these become automatic responses. Note: "It's in the diary" is more common in British English; "It's on my calendar" is the American equivalent — teach both.
§5
May/Might vs Present Continuous — The Certainty Scale
When to use each form · The visual certainty gauge · Interactive practice
⏱ 10 MIN
🚨 Real Situation — Monday Planning Email
Your manager asks: "What's happening this week? Give me an update on travel and meetings."

Some things are 100% confirmed (tickets bought, calendar invites sent). Other things are still uncertain (waiting for client reply, budget not approved).
How do you communicate the difference in English?
✅ The Tool: Certainty Scale
100% CONFIRMED → Present Continuous: "I'm flying to GDL on Tuesday."
~50% POSSIBLE → May: "The client may call tomorrow."
~30% LESS CERTAIN → Might: "I might need to extend my stay."
0% NOT HAPPENING → Won't / Not -ing: "I won't travel this week."

⚠️ Top errors — correct immediately:
✗ "He might to come" → ✅ He might come — no "to" after any modal
✗ "I'm going to meet him at 10" (when ticket is booked) → ✅ I'm meeting him at 10 — confirmed = present continuous
✗ "She mays call" → ✅ She may call — no conjugation on modals
✗ "I might will travel" → ✅ I might travel — never two modals together

Travel Update Call — What's Confirmed?
Andrea updates her manager on next week's travel · Certain vs uncertain throughout
⏱ 8 MIN
Andrea's Travel Update — Next Week
A2.2 · M4 · S3A · Voice: Lily (American English) · Speed: 0.82
Hi Carlos, here's my travel update for next week.

OK, so Tuesday is confirmed. I'm flying to Guadalajara at 7 AM — the tickets are booked. I'm checking into the Fiesta Americana hotel around noon. The reservation is confirmed for two nights.

On Tuesday afternoon, I'm meeting the regional sales team at 3 PM. That's in the calendar already. But the dinner with the Monterrey client? That's still TBD. She might not be available on Tuesday. She may prefer Wednesday evening — I'm waiting for her reply.

Wednesday is a bit uncertain. I may visit the new warehouse in Zapopan — it depends on whether the operations manager is there. If he's not available, I might stay at the hotel and work on the Q4 report instead.

My return flight is on Thursday morning at 10 AM — that's booked. But I might need to extend my stay if the client meeting moves to Thursday. I'll keep you posted.

So: Tuesday and the return flight are confirmed. The client dinner and the warehouse visit might change. I'll send you an update by Monday evening.

§8
Session Wrap-Up
What you learned · 3 phrases for this week · My Win today
⏱ 5 MIN
01
Present Continuous = CONFIRMED. "I'm meeting the client at 10" means it's in the calendar, booked, definite. Use it for arrangements.
02
May (~50%) vs Might (~30%). Both express possibility but might is less certain. "She may call" vs "She might call" — use both at work.
03
No "to" after modals. Ever. "He might come" ✅ · "He might to come" ✗ · This rule applies to may, might, will, can, should, must — all of them.
✅ I Can Now… — Click each one when confident
I can use present continuous for confirmed future arrangements: "I'm flying on Tuesday."
I can express possibility with may and might: "She might not be available."
I can describe a business travel itinerary mixing confirmed and uncertain plans.
I can use certainty phrases: "We'll see" / "It depends" / "I'll keep you posted" / "TBD"
I know the bare infinitive rule: no "to" after may/might/will/can/should/must.
🎯 This week at work
3 Phrases You Can Use Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"I'm meeting [person] at [time] on [day]."
Use this in emails, WhatsApp, and standups to confirm appointments. It sounds professional and signals that you're prepared and organized.
PHRASE 2
"I might need to [verb] — it depends on [factor]."
Perfect for uncertain situations. Honest, professional, and shows you're aware of variables. Use in updates and planning calls.
PHRASE 3
"I'll keep you posted."
The go-to phrase when something is uncertain and you'll update later. Works in every context: email, WhatsApp, meetings, calls.
