🔥 Warm-Up — Screen Time & Digital Wellness

5–8 min
Module 4 · S2A — How much of your life is spent staring at a screen?
Q1 — The number
Check your phone's screen time right now (or estimate). How many hours a day do you spend on your phone? What app takes most of your time?
Q2 — Sleep connection
Do you check your phone before sleeping? Does your phone stay in the bedroom? Has technology ever seriously affected your quality of sleep?
Q3 — Work impact
Does your phone distract you at work? Have you ever been in a meeting and secretly checked your phone? Is checking notifications a habit you can't control?
Q4 — Digital detox
Have you ever tried to reduce your screen time? What happened? Is a digital detox actually possible for someone who works with technology every day?
⚡ Quick Win — 20 seconds
Say this out loud: "If I reduce my screen time, I sleep much better — but I haven't managed to do it yet."
Is this true for you? Tell your partner.
Teacher note: Q1 is powerful — have students actually check their phones right now. The real number is always a conversation starter. Average in Mexico: 4–5 hours/day. Use this as the hook for passive voice: "This data is recorded by your phone automatically." Point to the Screen Time feature and say: "This information was collected without you thinking about it."

🗣️ Speaking Topic — Screen Time & Digital Wellness

8 min

Rodrigo Vega — The Honest Screen Time Check

Rodrigo is an engineer at a Celaya plant. He just checked his screen time for the first time and was shocked: 5.5 hours a day. He uses his phone for work — emails, WhatsApp, SAP — but also spends 2 hours a day on TikTok and YouTube. His manager mentioned that the team's productivity has been affected by phone use during meetings.

Round 1 · 60 seg T→S
"Rodrigo's manager said productivity has been affected. Is that fair? Can a company tell employees how to use their phones?"
Round 2 · 90 seg S→S
"Ask your partner: How do you use your phone at work — for work or for personal use? Do you track your screen time? Has it ever been reduced on purpose?"
Round 3 · 90 seg Class
"How much screen time is too much? Is it different if notifications are turned off? Should screen time be monitored — or is that too controlling?"
Model Answer

"Honestly, I checked my screen time last month and I was embarrassed. Almost 6 hours a day. A lot of it was used for work — but 2 hours were wasted on videos I don't even remember watching. I've started turning off notifications during meetings and it actually helps. My attention is better. It affects my sleep more than anything else — if I check my phone at midnight, I can't sleep for another hour. I feel better when I disconnect. But it's hard to reduce it when your whole job is on the phone."

💬 Colloquial — Talking About Screens & Habits

5 min
Everyday English — phrases you'll actually use this week
"I check my phone all the time."
When: admitting a compulsive digital habit
"I know I should focus in meetings but I check my phone all the time — even when nothing important is coming."
"It affects my sleep."
When: connecting tech use to physical health
"I know it affects my sleep but I still scroll before bed. I can't seem to stop."
"I feel better when I disconnect."
When: describing the positive effect of switching off
"On weekends I try to leave the phone in another room. I feel better when I disconnect — calmer, more present."
"If I reduce my screen time, I sleep much better."
When: talking about cause and effect of digital habits
"I tested it for a week. If I reduce my screen time before bed, I sleep much better — it's a clear difference."
Drill: Ask students: "¿Cuál de estas frases describes tu situación más honestamente?" The goal is authentic personal use, not just repetition. The last phrase ("If I reduce…") is functional English from the program — note it uses both passive context AND a conditional. Point this out: "This is First Conditional — we studied it in M3. It's still active grammar."

🎯 Grammar — Passive Voice: The "By" Phrase & When to Drop It

12–15 min
Passive Voice — the next level: when "by" matters and when to drop it
🚨 Real Situation
You're writing an email about your company's new digital wellness policy. You want to say:
"Los datos de uso del teléfono son rastreados automáticamente. Los empleados fueron informados la semana pasada. Las notificaciones serán desactivadas durante las reuniones."
❓ Three passive sentences. In each one — do you mention WHO does the action? Does it matter?
✅ The Tool: Passive + "By" — Use It or Drop It
"Phone usage data is tracked automatically." (no "by" — the agent is obvious or unimportant)
"Employees were informed by HR last week." ("by HR" adds useful information)
"Notifications will be disabled during meetings." (no "by" — policy language, agent irrelevant)

Rule: Include "by + agent" only when it adds new, useful information. Drop it when the agent is obvious, unknown, or unimportant — which is most of the time in business writing.
REFERENCIAL. Key B1.1 insight: native speakers almost never say "by someone" in passive unless it genuinely adds information. This is what separates textbook passive from real passive. "My phone was stolen" ← no "by" needed. "The report was approved by the CEO" ← the CEO matters here.
SituationKeep "by"?Example
Agent adds information✅ KeepThe policy was approved by the board of directors.
Agent is obvious❌ DropThe meeting was cancelled. (obviously by someone in charge)
Agent is unknown❌ DropMy phone was stolen last week.
General / impersonal❌ DropScreen time is tracked automatically.
Agent is "people" / "they"❌ DropThese apps are used by millions. (ok) → are used (cleaner)
⚠️ Common errors — correct immediately:
✗ "My data is tracked by the app by the company" → Never double "by" — pick ONE agent or drop it
✗ "The report was written by someone" → ✅ "The report was written" — "by someone" adds nothing
✗ "Notifications are turned off by me every morning" → ✅ "I turn off notifications" — active is better when YOU are the agent
✗ "The phone is checked by me 80 times" → ✅ "I check my phone 80 times" — passive with "by me" is almost always wrong

✏️ Activity B — True or False?
"My phone was stolen" is a correct passive sentence even without "by someone."
"The meeting was cancelled by someone" is a natural and professional sentence in English.
"Notifications are turned off by me every morning" is better expressed as "I turn off notifications every morning."
"The new policy was approved by the CEO" — keeping "by the CEO" is justified because it adds relevant information.

GT Original — Passive in Mexican Corporate Communication
  • 🇲🇽 Real context: HR emails, policy updates, and quality reports in Mexican companies use passive constantly: "Se informa que…" → "Employees are informed that…" / "Se aprobó el presupuesto" → "The budget was approved." Learning to recognize this unlocks formal business reading.
  • ⚠️ "By me" trap: Students often add "by me" to every passive — "The report was written by me." In English, this sounds odd. Active is almost always better when YOU are the agent. Passive is for when the agent is NOT you or NOT important.
  • 🔊 Pronunciation: "notification" /ˌnoʊtɪfɪˈkeɪʃən/ — 5 syllables: no-ti-fi-CA-tion. Stress on the 4th syllable. Students say "no-ti-fi-CA-ción" (Spanish influence) — close, but the vowels are different.
  • 🧠 Discussion hook: "¿Tu empresa tiene una política de uso del teléfono en reuniones?" → "Does your company have a screen policy? Has one been introduced?" Perfect passive question for real corporate context.

🎧 Listening — The 30-Day Screen Detox

8 min
A professional describes what happened when screen time was reduced for 30 days
The 30-Day Screen Detox
B1.1 · M4 · S2A · Voice: Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.85 · Pitch: 1.0
"I decided to try something last January. My screen time was averaging almost seven hours a day. I was embarrassed when I saw the number. So I set a rule: my phone would be put in a drawer by 9 PM every night. Notifications were turned off for everything except calls. Social media was deleted from my phone — not from my account, just from my phone.

The first week was hard. I noticed how often I was reaching for something that wasn't there. My attention was so fragmented — I couldn't focus on a book for more than ten minutes.

By week two, something shifted. My sleep was improved dramatically. I was getting almost an hour more of actual sleep. My wellbeing felt different — calmer. Things that had been ignored for months — hobbies, exercise — were being picked up again.

By the end of the month, my screen time had been reduced to under three hours a day. Half of that was work. I felt like my attention had been returned to me.

I didn't go back to seven hours. Today it's about four. Small steps. But I noticed one thing: I was never actually happier when I was scrolling. I just couldn't stop."
📎 ElevenLabs: Voice — Sarah (American English) · Speed 0.85 · Pitch 1.0 · B1.1 M4 S2A

🎤 Production — Speaking Practice

12 min
Speaking practice — passive voice in real conversations
💬 How to run: Pairs. Partner A asks — Partner B answers using passive voice at least once. Partner A tracks: (1) passive structures used correctly, (2) vocabulary from today. Switch after Q3. Question 6 is timed solo.
Question 1 — Your Data
Describe your phone habits right now. What is tracked? What has been wasted? What could be reduced? Use passive voice for at least 2 of your sentences.
💡 Use: "My [noun] is/was [verb]ed… / Time has been wasted on… / My [habit] could be reduced if…"
Question 2 — Notifications
How are notifications managed in your life? Have they ever been turned off? Should they be controlled more at your company — in meetings, during focus time, or after work hours?
💡 Use: "Notifications are… / They have been turned off… / I think they should be managed by…"
Question 3 — Wellbeing & Sleep
Has your sleep ever been affected by phone use? What was noticed when you disconnected? What habits have been developed — good or bad — because of technology?
💡 Use: "My sleep was affected when… / I noticed that… / A habit has been developed — I [do x] without realizing it."
Question 4 — Company Policy
Should screen time be regulated at work? Has a digital wellness policy been introduced at your company? What rules do you think should be implemented — and who should they be enforced by?
💡 Use: "A policy has/hasn't been introduced… / Rules should be implemented by… / Screen time should/shouldn't be monitored because…"
Question 5 — The 30-Day Challenge
If you tried the speaker's 30-day experiment, what would be changed? What would be turned off, deleted, or reduced? What do you think would be improved — and what habits would be missed?
💡 Use passive + conditional: "If I tried it, [X] would be turned off… / I think my sleep would be improved… / What would be missed most is…"
Question 6 — ⭐ 60-Second Challenge
In 60 seconds: describe your ideal digital wellness policy for your team at work. What would be required? What would be banned? What data would be tracked? Use passive voice at least 3 times. No notes.
💡 Model: "In my ideal policy, phones would be put away during… / Notifications would be disabled… / Screen time data would be reviewed monthly…"

🎯 This week at work
3 Phrases You Can Use Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"If I reduce my screen time, I sleep much better."
Use this in any conversation about health, habits, or wellbeing at work. It uses conditional AND implies a passive consequence. Natural, relatable, and immediately useful in HR sessions, team check-ins, or casual lunch conversation.
PHRASE 2
"Notifications were turned off — it helped a lot."
Perfect for sharing a tip in a standup, a productivity conversation, or a team message. Pure past passive, no "by" needed, completely natural. Replace "notifications" with anything: "WhatsApp was muted," "The app was deleted."
PHRASE 3
"I feel better when I disconnect."
Use this whenever someone asks how your weekend was, or in a wellbeing conversation at work. Simple, honest, human. No passive — but it pairs perfectly with "My sleep was improved when I disconnected." Two sentences, powerful together.
