Warm-Up — M5 Quick-Fire: All 5 Will Functions
⏱ 8 min · 90 sec per student · Full M5 activation · Badge ⚡
Layer 1 · Logistics

90-second Quick-Fire activates ALL 5 will functions from Module 5: spontaneous offer (S1), spontaneous decision (S1), promise / won't refusal (S2), prediction (S3), and the will vs. going-to contrast (S4). Run as rapid round-robin — never correct mid-answer. Badge ⚡ awarded when timer completes.

Layer 2 · Pedagogical Focus
  • Q1: Spontaneous offer — "I'll help you with that." / "I'll take care of it."
  • Q2: Promise + won't — "I promise I'll…" / "I won't let the team down."
  • Q3: Prediction — "I think it will…" / "Revenue will probably…" + adverbs
  • Q4: Will vs. going to contrast — students must choose the right system and justify it
Layer 3 · S5 Purpose

This is the performance session — not a new lesson. Students should feel the accumulated weight of 5 sessions. If Q1 triggers "I going to help" errors, note for debrief only — never re-teach in S5. If Q4 is weak, that is the main focus of §3. Redirect: "What did we say about the difference in S4?"

⚡ QUICK-FIRE · 90 SECONDS PER STUDENT

🔵 Offer + Spontaneous Decision — S1 Review
Your colleague just said: "I can't finish the client report before the meeting." React with 3 spontaneous will offers: "I'll…". Each must be a real action you could take. Then add 1 spontaneous decision triggered by what you see on your desk right now.
Push: "Is that offer or decision? How do you know it's 'will' and not 'going to'?"
⭐ Promise + Won't Refusal — S2 Review
Make 2 professional promises using "I'll…" and 2 firm refusals using "I won't…" — all business-realistic. Then say 1 sentence using "I promise…" explicitly. At least 1 promise and 1 won't must relate to a real project at your company.
Push: "What's the difference between 'I'm not going to…' and 'I won't…'? Which feels stronger?"
🟢 Prediction — S3 Review
Make 3 business predictions about your industry or company this year. Use: "I think… will…" / "…will probably…" / "…will definitely…". Each sentence must include an adverb of certainty. Vary between optimistic and cautious predictions.
Push: "Can you rank them? Which is most certain? Use 'definitely' vs. 'probably' vs. 'might'."
🟣 Will vs. Going To Contrast — S4 Synthesis
Describe tomorrow using both systems in the same story: "Tomorrow we're going to present to the board — that's been planned for two weeks. But if there's a problem, I'll handle it immediately." Produce ≥3 going-to sentences (pre-planned) + ≥3 will sentences (spontaneous / prediction).
Push: "Which sentence would break if you swapped will and going to? Why?"

Grammar Review — Master Integration Table + Will vs. Going To
⏱ 16 min · Integration table · 1 MC poll · 2 error boxes · 2 drag-and-drop · Hot Seat Dice · Badge 🎯
Layer 1 · Logistics

Master integration table shows all 5 will functions + full will vs. going-to contrast in one view. Use this as the S5 reference anchor — students should be able to explain any row without looking by end of session. Poll earns Badge 🎯. Error boxes target the 2 most common M5 production errors. Drag-and-drop activates will vs. going-to classification. Dice = 6 hot-seat production prompts.

Layer 2 · Top M5 Errors to Watch
  • Error 1: Using going-to for spontaneous offers — "I'm going to help you." ✗ → "I'll help you." ✓
  • Error 2: Missing adverb in predictions — "It will grow." ✗ → "It will probably grow." ✓ (more natural)
  • Error 3: Won't confusion — "I won't to do that." ✗ → "I won't do that." ✓ (no "to" after modal)
  • Error 4: Mixing signals — if it was pre-planned, use going-to regardless of the conversational moment.
Layer 3 · The Capstone Contrast

The will vs. going-to contrast is the A2.1 graduation test. Students who can explain the contrast in their own words are ready for B1.1. Celebrate any student who can say: "I use going-to because I decided before this moment — I use will because I decided right now."

All 5 Will Functions + Going-To Contrast — Master Table

Function Form Signal Words Example (Business)
Spontaneous Offer I'll + base verb at the moment of speaking "I'll send that report right now."
Spontaneous Decision I'll / We'll + base verb decided just now, not before "I'll take the Monday slot — it's free."
Promise I'll + base verb / I promise I'll… commitment to another person "I'll have it ready by 9 AM. I promise."
Won't Refusal won't + base verb firm refusal or commitment not to do "I won't approve that budget increase."
Prediction will / won't + base verb + adverb probably, definitely, I think, I believe "Revenue will probably grow next quarter."
Going To — Pre-Planned am/is/are going to + base verb decided before speaking; evidence visible "We're going to present to the board Friday."
Poll: "Ana just walked in with a broken laptop. Your colleague asks for help — you decide to fix it right now." Which sentence is correct?

Error Correction — Spot and Fix

Error 1: "The client called — I'm going to call them back immediately."
Fix: "I'll call them back immediately." — Spontaneous reaction to new information = will, not going to. The decision was made at the moment of speaking.

Error 2: "Costs will to increase next year, probably by 10%."
Fix: "Costs will increase next year, probably by 10%." — Modal verbs (will / won't) are NEVER followed by "to". Will + base verb only.

Sequence Builder — Will in Order + Will vs. Going To
⏱ 14 min · 2 ordering exercises · Oral retell
Layer 1 · Logistics

2 drag-and-drop ordering exercises. Exercise A: a client complaint call using will only — students sequence the steps a manager takes when reacting in real time. Exercise B: a project update meeting mixing will (spontaneous reactions) and going-to (planned agenda items). After each exercise, student retells the sequence orally using "First… Then… After that… Finally…" — connecting M2 connectors with M5 grammar.

Layer 2 · Oral Retell Protocol
  • After each exercise: give 60 seconds for student to retell the full sequence orally.
  • Monitor for: correct will use (spontaneous language) vs. going-to slippage.
  • Push: "Was that decision already in the plan? Then going-to. Was it triggered by what just happened? Then will."
Layer 3 · Bridging M4 + M5

Exercise B is the key M4+M5 integration moment. Students must hold two systems simultaneously. Tip: draw a line on the board — left side = planned (going-to), right side = spontaneous (will). Each sentence in the retell should be classified before being spoken.

Exercise A — Client Complaint Call (Will Only)

A client just called with an urgent complaint. Drag the manager's responses into the correct logical order. All responses use will — spontaneous real-time actions.

  • ⠿"I'll look into the delay immediately and call you back in 20 minutes."
  • ⠿"I'll escalate this to the operations manager right now."
  • ⠿"I'll send you a written confirmation of the resolution by end of day."
  • ⠿"I'll personally make sure this doesn't happen again."
  • ⠿"I won't close this case until you confirm you're satisfied."

Oral Retell — Exercise A

Retell the complete complaint call sequence orally using: First… Then… After that… Then… Finally… — using will for each step. Partner counts the will structures. Target: 5 will sentences minimum in under 60 seconds.

Exercise B — Project Update Meeting (Will + Going To Mixed)

A manager is running a project update meeting. Some steps are pre-planned (going to) — some are spontaneous reactions (will). Drag into the correct logical meeting flow order.

  • ⠿"We're going to review the Q3 milestones — that's agenda item 1." (planned)
  • ⠿"I'll handle that issue with procurement — I'll call them after this meeting." (spontaneous)
  • ⠿"We're going to present the budget proposal to the CFO on Thursday — confirmed." (planned)
  • ⠿"I'll take notes on that risk — we'll address it in the next session." (spontaneous)

Oral Retell — Exercise B

Retell the project update meeting orally. For each item, say aloud: "This is going-to because…" OR "This is will because…". Partner confirms or challenges. Both students must agree on the reasoning before moving to the next item.

Role-Play — 3-Round Integration: Will + Going To
⏱ 16 min · 3 rounds · Color-coded model answers · Boardroom simulation
Layer 1 · Logistics

3-round integration progression. Round 1: will only (client complaint). Round 2: will + going-to mixed (project update). Round 3: full 3-min boardroom simulation mixing all 5 will functions + going-to. Color-coded model: cyan = I'll offers, red = won't promises, gold = predictions, green = going-to planned.

Layer 2 · Round Structure
  • Round 1 (4 min): Student A = angry client. Student B = manager responding with spontaneous will offers + won't promise. No going-to allowed.
  • Round 2 (5 min): Swap. Student A = team member with a mid-meeting problem. Student B = manager using will (spontaneous) + going-to (pre-planned items).
  • Round 3 (7 min): Full boardroom simulation. Both students share the room — A presents planned items (going-to), B asks questions, A responds with spontaneous will + predictions.
Layer 3 · Color-Code Debrief

After Round 3, replay one student's key sentences and ask the class to identify the color: cyan (offer), red (won't), gold (prediction), green (going-to). One common Round 3 failure: students revert to going-to for everything under pressure. Remind: "If you decided it before this conversation started — going-to. If you just decided — will."

Cyan = I'll offer / spontaneous decision
Red = won't promise / refusal
Gold = will prediction + adverb
Green = going-to pre-planned
🎤 3-Round Will + Going-To Integration
Round 1 — Client Complaint (Will Only)

Student A (Client): "I ordered 500 units two weeks ago and they still haven't arrived. This is unacceptable. What are you going to do about it?"

Student B (Manager): Respond with ≥4 will sentences: 1 offer, 1 promise, 1 won't refusal, 1 spontaneous decision. No going-to allowed in Round 1.

"I'll contact logistics right now — give me one moment. I won't let this situation continue without a full explanation. I'll personally track the shipment and call you back within 30 minutes. I'll also send you a written update by end of day. I won't close this ticket until you confirm the units have arrived and you're satisfied."
Round 2 — Project Update (Will + Going To Mixed)

Student A (Team member): "We just found out the supplier can't deliver the materials until next month. What do we do?"

Student B (Manager): Use ≥2 going-to sentences (pre-planned items still on track) + ≥3 will sentences (spontaneous reactions to this new problem).

"We're going to present the timeline update to the board on Thursday — that hasn't changed. We're going to keep the testing schedule in August as planned. But for this supplier issue — I'll call the backup vendor right now. I'll also renegotiate the delivery date directly — I won't accept a one-month delay without a penalty clause. Revenue will probably take a hit this quarter — but I'll handle the CFO conversation personally."
Round 3 — Full Boardroom Simulation (All Functions · 3 min)

Student A (General Manager): Present the monthly review. Include: ≥2 going-to items (planned), ≥2 I'll offers or decisions (spontaneous), ≥1 won't, ≥2 predictions with adverbs. 3-minute time limit. No notes.

Student B (Board member): Ask ≥3 questions — at least 1 about a prediction, 1 about a plan, 1 spontaneous problem ("The projector just broke — what do we do?").

"We're going to launch the new client portal in August — that's confirmed. We're going to present the full Q3 report to the board next Friday. Revenue will definitely improve this quarter — I'm confident. The new portal will probably increase client retention by 15%. I won't approve any scope changes without a full team review."

Listening — Alejandro Vargas: Monthly Review
⏱ 14 min · ~215w monologue · 4 MCQ + 4 T/F
Layer 1 · Logistics

Single-speaker monologue (~215 words): Alejandro Vargas, General Manager, delivering a monthly team review. Voice: Sarah AmE at 0.82× speed (ElevenLabs). Play twice minimum. First listen: general comprehension. Second listen: note every will sentence and every going-to sentence — students classify them as they hear them.

Layer 2 · Target Structures in Monologue
  • Going-to (pre-planned): portal launch August, annual report Friday, QA review
  • Will — Promises: "I'll personally lead…", "I'll handle any issue…"
  • Will — Won't refusal: "We won't launch without a full QA review"
  • Will — Predictions: "next quarter will be our strongest", "revenue will probably grow", "will definitely improve retention"
  • Will — Spontaneous decision: "I'll print them at the corner shop" (triggered by the broken printer)
Layer 3 · Post-Listening Production Bridge

After MCQ/T-F: "Which of Alejandro's going-to sentences could NOT be changed to will? Why?" (Answer: going-to items were pre-planned — weeks on the calendar.) Then: "Which of his will sentences could NOT be changed to going-to?" (Answer: the printer decision was triggered in the moment.) This contrast discussion is the S5 grammar integration test.

Voice: Sarah AmE · 0.82× · ~215 words · Monthly Team Review — Will + Going To Integration

"Good morning, team. Let me give you a quick overview of where we are and where we're going. First — the planned items. We're going to present the annual report to the board next Friday — that's been on the calendar for three weeks. We're also going to launch the new client portal in August, as scheduled. Now, I want to make some commitments. I'll personally lead the portal launch. I promise the team will have all the resources they need. We won't launch without a full QA review — that's non-negotiable. Now, let me share some predictions. I think next quarter will be our strongest yet. Revenue will probably grow by 20 percent. I'm confident the new portal will definitely improve our client retention rate. Will there be challenges? Yes, there will. But I'll handle any issue that comes up personally. One more thing — Ana just told me the printer is broken and we need the meeting handouts now. I'll print them at the corner shop and be back in ten minutes. That's a spontaneous decision. That's will. For everything else — the board presentation, the portal, the QA review — that's going to. Two systems. One team. Let's have a great quarter."

Multiple Choice — 4 Questions

1. When is the annual report going to be presented?

2. What does Alejandro promise about the portal launch?

3. By how much will revenue probably grow?

4. Why does Alejandro decide to go to the corner shop?

True / False — 4 Statements

TF1: "The annual report presentation has been planned for three weeks."
TF2: "Alejandro says they will launch the portal without a QA review."
TF3: "Revenue will probably grow by 20 percent next quarter."
TF4: "Going to the corner shop was a pre-planned decision."

Exit Task — Will + Going-To Integration Pitch
⏱ 20 min · 90 sec timer · 3 scenarios · Rubric · Badge 🌟
Layer 1 · Logistics

Student selects one of 3 scenarios and has 90 seconds to speak. Rubric: ≥5 will sentences (at least 1 offer, 1 promise, 1 prediction) + ≥2 going-to sentences + ≥1 adverb of certainty + ≥1 won't. Badge 🌟 awarded after attempting the task. No notes during the pitch.

Layer 2 · Evaluation Criteria
  • Will functions: offer + promise + prediction all present — 3 functions minimum
  • Going-to contrast: at least 2 going-to sentences that are clearly pre-planned
  • Adverb use: "probably" or "definitely" in at least 1 prediction
  • Won't: at least 1 firm won't refusal or non-negotiable commitment
  • Fluency: no going-to slippage on spontaneous reactions (the most common error)
Layer 3 · Debrief Protocol

After each pitch: (1) peer counts will structures out loud; (2) teacher gives ONE correction — the most impactful error only; (3) student re-says that sentence correctly; (4) move on. Never give a list of 5 corrections in S5 — it destroys confidence at the performance session. Celebrate quantity and system separation first.

Capstone Game — Will & Going To Final Builder
⏱ 10 min · 5 slots · Badge 🏆 on 5/5 · Confetti
Layer 1 · Logistics

5 sequential slots that unlock one by one. Each slot requires a sentence that meets specific will or going-to requirements. Progress bar fills 20% per slot. 5/5 earns the Module 5 Graduate badge 🏆 and confetti. Validation is structural, not semantic — any plausible sentence passes if it meets the grammar rules.

Layer 2 · Slot Validation Rules
  • Slot 1: ≥3 words + "I'll" or "We'll" — spontaneous offer
  • Slot 2: "won't" present — won't promise or refusal
  • Slot 3: "will" + ("probably" or "definitely") — prediction with adverb
  • Slot 4: "going to" present — pre-planned intention
  • Slot 5: "will" + "going to" both present — both systems in one sentence
Layer 3 · Graduation Moment

The 5-slot builder is the A2.1 future system graduation gate. Students who complete 5/5 have demonstrated: spontaneous offer, won't commitment, adverb prediction, going-to planning, and the ability to hold both systems in one sentence. That final slot is a B1.1-adjacent skill — celebrate it explicitly.

Module Close — I Can Now…
⏱ 8 min · Checklist + Badge Showcase + My Win + M6 Preview
Layer 1 · Logistics

Module 5 close ceremony. 6-item "I can now…" checklist — students tick each item as a self-assessment. Badge showcase reveals all earned badges. "My Win" textarea captures one moment of success from the session. M6 preview card teases the next topic: Full A2.1 Integration — Past + Future.

Layer 2 · Reflection Protocol
  • Read each checklist item aloud as a class. Student raises hand only if they feel confident — not just "covered it."
  • Items 3 (predictions with adverbs) and 6 (contrast going-to vs. will in one sentence) are typically the weakest — flag them for M6 recycling.
  • "My Win" should be specific: not "I learned will" but "I made a spontaneous offer in the boardroom simulation without thinking."
Layer 3 · M6 Preview Framing

M5 graduation unlocks the full A2.1 future system. M6 integrates past + future — students tell stories that move between what happened (past simple) and what will happen or is going to happen next. Frame M6 as: "You now have the complete A2.1 toolkit. M6 is where you use everything at the same time — in one real business conversation."

I Can Now… — Module 5 Self-Assessment

  • Use "I'll" to make spontaneous offers and decisions in real professional situations.
  • Make promises with "I'll…" and firm commitments with "I won't…" in business contexts.
  • Make predictions using "will probably…" and "will definitely…" with correct adverbs.
  • Explain the difference between will (spontaneous) and going to (pre-planned) in my own words.
  • Use both systems in the same conversation: going to for my agenda, will for unexpected reactions.
  • Respond to a client complaint, project update, or boardroom situation using will for at least 90 seconds.
