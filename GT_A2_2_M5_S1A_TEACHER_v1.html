§3
Colloquial — Decision Language
4 real-world phrases for making and justifying choices · 5 min
⏱ 5 MIN
"It's a no-brainer."
Use when one option is so clearly better that no thinking is needed. The choice is obvious.
"Option B is faster, cheaper, and more reliable — it's a no-brainer."
"There's no comparison."
Use when one option is significantly better than another. Strong, confident language.
"Between the two suppliers, there's no comparison — we should go with Vertex."
"The pros outweigh the cons."
Use when an option has disadvantages, but the advantages are more important. Balanced reasoning.
"Yes, it's more expensive, but the pros outweigh the cons — we save time and reduce errors."
"It's worth the extra cost."
Use when you're recommending a more expensive option that has clear advantages.
"The premium package costs more, but it's worth the extra cost — the support is much better."
⚡ Teacher Note — Colloquial Drill
Say: "Click each phrase to hear it — repeat it with me." Then: "Tell your partner about a work decision using one of these phrases." Anticipated errors: "It's no-brainer" (missing article) → "It's A no-brainer." / "pros overcome cons" → "outweigh." Cultural note: In Mexican Spanish, the equivalent of "no-brainer" is "está clarísimo" or "es obvio" — help students feel the register. "The pros outweigh the cons" is a key boardroom phrase that appears in presentations — worth drilling for B1.1 bridge.
§4
Grammar — Comparatives: Short & Long Adjectives
Problem → Tool format · Full system · 2 interactive activities · 12–15 min
⏱ 12–15 MIN
🚨 Real Work Situation
Your manager asks you to compare two logistics providers: Vertex Logistics (fast but expensive) and Lumina Express (slower but cheaper). You want to recommend Lumina Express. But how do you explain the comparison in English?
You think: "Lumina es más barata que Vertex... but how do I say that?" / "Y Vertex es la más rápida... how do I form that?"
🛠 The Tool — Comparative Adjectives
English uses two different rules to compare, depending on the adjective length:
Short adjectives (1 syllable): add -er + than → cheap → cheaper than
Long adjectives (2+ syllables): use more + adj + than → reliable → more reliable than
So: "Lumina Express is cheaper than Vertex, but Vertex is more reliable than Lumina."
Adjective TypeRuleExample (+than)Notes
Short (1 syllable)adj + -erfast → faster than / cheap → cheaper than / big → bigger thanDouble final consonant if CVC: big → bigger / hot → hotter
Ends in -ychange -y to -ierheavy → heavier than / noisy → noisier than / busy → busier thanCommon in descriptions of environments or people
Long (2+ syllables)more + adjreliable → more reliable than / efficient → more efficient thanNEVER say "more cheaper" or "more faster" — both are wrong
IrregularLearn the formgood → better than / bad → worse than / far → farther thanThese forms must be memorized — no rule applies
As … as (equality)as + adj + asThis product is as reliable as our current one.Use for equality. "Not as fast as" = slower.
⚠️ Common Errors — Mexican learners:
✗ "This option is more fast than the other." → ✓ faster than (short adjective = -er)
✗ "Our supplier is more better." → ✓ better (never "more" + irregular comparative)
✗ "Option A is as cheaper as Option B." → ✓ as cheap as (use base adjective with as...as)
✗ "It is more heavier than expected." → ✓ heavier (adjective ending in -y → -ier)

GT Originals — Teacher Notes
  • 💼 Business application: ask students "in your job, what do you compare most — prices, suppliers, or candidates?" Then drill comparatives in that exact context so the grammar sticks to a real memory.
  • ⚠️ Mexican learner pattern: students often say "it is more better" because in Spanish "es más mejor" sounds natural in informal speech. The fix: "more" + irregular = always wrong — show it with a red X on the board and make students correct each other.
  • 🔊 Pronunciation tip: comparative -er endings reduce in natural speech → "cheaper" sounds like /ˈtʃiːpər/ not /ˈtʃiːpɛr/. Drill the schwa: fast-ER, cheap-ER, big-GER.
  • 🌍 Cultural note: in professional English, you always justify a comparison — "Option A is cheaper BECAUSE it has a shorter delivery chain." The reasoning is as important as the comparison itself. Teach this as a unit: comparison + reason.

Andrea Soto
Regional Sales Coordinator · Celaya, Guanajuato
Internal Report
Module 5 · Reading Practice
🔑 Grammar in this text: Comparatives (orange) · As…as / Not as…as (cyan) · Superlatives (green)

My manager asked me to compare two logistics providers for our Q3 operations. After researching both companies, I have a clear recommendation.

Vertex Logistics is faster than Lumina Express — their average delivery time is two days, compared to four. However, their prices are significantly higher than Lumina's. For our current budget, Vertex is not the best option.

Lumina Express is more affordable than Vertex, and their customer service is more flexible than any provider we have used before. Their response time is almost as fast as Vertex for domestic routes.

In my opinion, Lumina Express offers the best value for our current situation. It is more reliable than our previous supplier and cheaper than Vertex. I recommend we sign a six-month contract with Lumina Express starting in September.

Comprehension — True or False?

1. Vertex Logistics is faster than Lumina Express.
2. Lumina Express is more expensive than Vertex Logistics.
3. Andrea recommends Vertex Logistics for Q3.
4. Lumina Express has more flexible customer service than any previous supplier.
5. Andrea wants to sign a one-year contract with Lumina Express.

Speaking Practice — Your Comparisons
Guided questions · Pair and group discussion · 10–12 min
⏱ 10–12 MIN
T-S Question 1 — Recall
What are the two logistics providers in the listening? Which one is more expensive? Which one is faster?
Grammar hint: Use "is more [adj] than" — e.g., "NovaTrans is faster than Lumina Express."
S-S Question 2 — Apply
Think of two products or services you use at work. Compare them using at least 3 adjectives from today's vocabulary.
Grammar hint: Mix short and long adjectives — "it's cheaper" (short) + "it's more reliable" (long).
S-S Question 3 — Justify
Your partner proposes Supplier A. You prefer Supplier B. Give 2 reasons using comparatives to support your choice. Convince them!
Grammar hint: Use "because" after the comparison — "I prefer B because it's more affordable than A."
Class Question 4 — Discussion
In your opinion: is price always more important than quality in business? Or is quality more important than price? Defend your answer using comparatives.
Grammar hint: Try using "as important as" for balance — "price is just as important as quality."
T-S Question 5 — Challenge
Andrea had to write a supplier report. Have you ever had to compare options at work and make a recommendation? What was harder — collecting the data or deciding which option was better?
Grammar hint: Use past tense + comparative together — "It was harder to decide because the prices were similar."
Class Question 6 — Production
Compare Celaya with another city in Mexico for doing business. Which is better for companies? Which is more convenient for workers? Use at least 4 comparatives.
Grammar hint: Use superlatives too — "Monterrey is the most industrial city in Mexico." Preview next session!

Wrap-Up — Module 5 Start
Key takeaways · Checklist · My Win Today · Next session preview · 5–8 min
⏱ 5–8 MIN
☐ Rule 1 — Short adjectives
Add -er + than: fast → faster than, cheap → cheaper than. Double the final consonant if CVC: big → bigger.
☐ Rule 2 — Long adjectives
Use more + adj + than: more reliable than, more efficient than. NEVER "more faster" or "more better."
☐ Rule 3 — Irregulars & As…as
Learn these forms: good → better, bad → worse. For equality: as [adj] as. For inequality: not as [adj] as.

I Can Now...

☐Compare two products or suppliers using "-er than" for short adjectives
☐Use "more [adj] than" correctly with long adjectives like reliable, efficient, affordable
☐Use irregular forms: better than, worse than, farther than
☐Say "as [adj] as" and "not as [adj] as" to express equality or inequality
☐Use collocquial phrases: "it's a no-brainer", "the pros outweigh the cons"
