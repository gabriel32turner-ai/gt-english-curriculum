§1

🔥 Hot Take — Screen Time & Passive Voice

5 min
60 seconds. Your opinion. Passive voice required.
🔥 Hot Take
“Screen time should be monitored and limited by companies — not just at work, but on personal devices too. If your phone use is tracked by your employer, your productivity will improve. Period.”
Rule 1: Agree OR disagree — commit fully Rule 2: Use passive voice at least twice Rule 3: 60 seconds, no notes Rule 4: Drop “by me” — use active if YOU are the agent
Model Answer — Disagree

“I strongly disagree. Personal devices should not be monitored by any employer — that’s a basic privacy right. Yes, screen time can be a distraction at work. But the solution isn’t surveillance. Notifications can be turned off voluntarily. Wellbeing is improved when people feel trusted, not watched. I feel better when I disconnect on my own terms — not because I’m forced to by a company policy.”
■ Vocabulary ■ Passive Voice ■ Colloquial
After the model answer: Count passive structures together — there are 4. Then ask: “¿Quién usó más pasivas en su Hot Take?” The student with the most correct passives earns 3 pts for the leaderboard. Note: “by a company policy” — here the “by” IS kept because it tells us the agent causing the forced disconnection. Good discussion point.
§2

⚡ Pitch / Debate — Should Screen Time Be Limited at Work?

10 min
Two sides. Every argument uses passive voice. Timer: 3 minutes.
🟣 Side A — Screen Time Should Be Limited
“Productivity is damaged by phone use at work. Rules are needed.”
  • Meetings are disrupted by notifications — this has been proven in studies.
  • Company time is wasted when phones are used for personal content.
  • Employees’ wellbeing is impacted by constant digital overload.
  • Clear limits have been introduced at top companies and results are positive.
  • If screen time is reduced during meetings, quality will be improved.
🟢 Side B — Personal Freedom Should Be Respected
“Trust cannot be replaced by surveillance. People manage themselves.”
  • Privacy rights are violated when personal devices are monitored.
  • Motivation is reduced when employees feel they are watched constantly.
  • Results should be measured, not screen time — output matters, not method.
  • Self-regulation is developed through trust, not control.
  • Better tools are needed — wellness training, not digital surveillance.
“Notifications have been shown to reduce…” “Employees are affected when…” “If limits are introduced…” “Trust is built by…” “I think both sides…”
How to run: Split class in two. 90 seconds per side, then 30 seconds rebuttal each. Award 1 pt per correct passive used. Count out loud together after. Winner by passive count, not by opinion.
Key grammar point to reinforce: Notice Side A uses “by notifications / by studies” — the “by” is kept because it adds meaningful information. Side B uses “when monitored / when watched” — no “by” needed. Both are correct. Point this out explicitly.
§3

🎤 Relay — Keep or Drop the “By”?

10 min
Read the sentence. Keep or drop “by”? Say your answer before revealing.
Keep “by” only when it adds real information — decision time
Revealed: 0 / 5
Card 1 — Obvious Agent
“Screen time data is recorded by the phone automatically.”
→ Should we keep “by the phone”? Why or why not?
❌ Drop “by the phone” — it’s completely obvious. The phone records it. Better: “Screen time data is recorded automatically.” Clean, professional, no unnecessary words.
Card 2 — Informative Agent
“The new digital wellness policy was introduced by the HR director last Monday.”
→ Should we keep “by the HR director”? Does it add information?
✅ Keep “by the HR director” — it tells us WHO is responsible for the policy. In corporate communication, knowing the decision-maker matters. The “by” phrase adds real, useful information.
Card 3 — Unknown Agent
“My phone was stolen while I was in the meeting.”
→ Is this sentence complete without “by”? What would we add?
✅ Complete without “by” — the agent is unknown (we don’t know who stole it). This is one of the most natural uses of passive: when the agent can’t be named. Adding “by someone” would sound absurd.
Card 4 — “By me” Trap
“Notifications are turned off by me every morning before my first meeting.”
→ What’s wrong? How would a native speaker say this?
❌ “By me” = almost always wrong. When YOU are the agent, use active: “I turn off notifications every morning before my first meeting.” Passive + “by me” sounds unnatural and is one of the most common errors at B1 level.
Card 5 — General / Impersonal
“Sleep quality has been shown to improve when screens are turned off one hour before bed.”
→ Is “by” needed here? What is the agent, and does it matter?
✅ No “by” needed — this is general research / impersonal passive. “Has been shown” implies scientific evidence without needing to name a specific study. Very common in business reports, wellness documents, and academic language.
Relay mechanic: Student A reads the sentence, Student B says “keep” or “drop” BEFORE the reveal, with a reason. Then reveal together. Award 2 pts for correct answer + valid reason, 1 pt for correct answer only.
The big lesson: By the end of these 5 cards, students should have internalized the decision tree: Is the agent obvious? Unknown? “By me”? General? → Drop. Does it add new, useful information? → Keep.

⚡ Speed Round — Keep or Drop? 10 Seconds Per Card

6–8 min
Say “Keep” or “Drop” out loud before clicking. 10 seconds per card.
Completed 0 / 10
“The report was written by me.”
DROP → use active
“Sleep was improved by the experiment.”
KEEP → experiment caused it
“Notifications are turned off automatically.”
FINE → no “by” needed
“The policy was approved by someone.”
DROP → “someone” adds nothing
“My phone was broken by my son.”
KEEP → “my son” is the point
“The meeting was cancelled.”
FINE → no agent needed
“Screen time is tracked by the app by the company.”
ERROR → double “by” is wrong
“Wellbeing was impacted by digital overload.”
KEEP → cause matters
“The email was answered by me in 5 minutes.”
DROP → “I answered” is better
“The app was designed by a team of engineers.”
KEEP → who designed it matters
How to run: Set a 10-second rule. Student calls “Keep” or “Drop” out loud, gives a 1-sentence reason, then clicks to see the answer. 1 pt per correct call + reason. The double “by” card (card 7) is a trap — the error is structural, not just the “by.” Use it as a mini-lesson moment.

🎤 Solo Challenge — 2 Minutes

8 min
Two minutes. Your company’s digital wellness policy. No notes.
🎤 SOLO — Design Your Company’s Digital Wellness Policy (2 min)
You are presenting to your company’s leadership team. Describe the digital wellness policy you would implement:
What would be restricted or monitored? What would be turned off or limited? By whom would it be enforced? (decide: keep or drop “by”) What improvements would be expected? Passive voice at least 3 times
Scoring (announce verbally, log in leaderboard): 3 pts — correct passive structures (min 3) · 2 pts — correct “by” decisions · 2 pts — vocabulary from S2A · 1 pt — fluency (no long pauses) · 1 pt — colloquial phrase used naturally. Max: 9 pts.
Tip: Model opening: “In my policy, notifications would be turned off during core hours. Screen time data would be tracked voluntarily. Employees would be trusted to manage…”

🔍 Source Analysis — Digital Wellness & Work

8 min
Read four real-world sources. Find the passive structures. Answer the questions.
📰 HR Industry Report
“In a 2023 study, productivity was found to be 23% higher in teams where phones were placed face-down during meetings. Focus was improved significantly. These results were replicated across four countries.”
❓ According to the study, what happened to focus when phones were face-down?
💬 Employee Survey — Celaya Plant, Anon.
“I was told by my manager that my phone had to be left at my desk during production hours. I felt monitored. But after one month, I noticed that my concentration was improved. I didn’t expect that.”
❓ Why was the employee surprised by the end of the month?
📋 Mexican Labor Law Excerpt (adapted)
“Personal devices may not be monitored by employers without explicit written consent from the employee. Data collected from personal phones is protected by federal privacy law. Violations will be sanctioned by the relevant authority.”
❓ Under Mexican law, when can a personal device be monitored?
💻 Tech Company Wellness Blog
“Our screen time pilot was launched last year. Notifications were turned off during ‘deep work’ hours. Employee satisfaction scores were raised by 18%. No one was forced to participate — it was introduced as a voluntary option.”
❓ How was the screen time pilot introduced at this company?
Extension: After answering, ask students: “¿Cuántas estructuras pasivas encontraron en total?” There are at least 14. Then ask: which “by” phrases were kept and which were dropped? Let students justify each decision — this is the S2 grammar point applied to authentic reading.
