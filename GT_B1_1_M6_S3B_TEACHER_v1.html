Hot Take — Food, Identity & Globalization

10 min
Hot Take · Pick a side · Stacked FC required · 90 seconds on the clock
Today's Hot Take
"Fast food and globalization have done more damage to cultural identity than any war or political system in history — because they make people choose to lose their own culture."
✅ Agree
A military occupation ends. A McDonald's stays forever. When people voluntarily choose fast food over traditional cooking, they participate in erasing their own heritage. The damage is invisible because it feels like freedom.
✗ Disagree
Cultures have always borrowed and mixed food. Globalization gives people access to more — it doesn't take away. If a tradition has real meaning, it will survive. What dies was already weakening from inside.
T→S Round 1 — Your Hot Take (90 sec)

State your position + stack two First Conditionals: "If [fast food culture continues], then [X] will happen — and if [X] happens, [Y] will follow." Open with an opinion phrase. Use S3A vocabulary: identity, tradition, heritage, preserve.

S↔S Round 2 — The Counter (60 sec)

Counter with "That's a good point, however..." + PP evidence of cultural food survival: "Mexican cuisine has been recognized by UNESCO precisely because people have continued to value it." Stack a FC: "If communities preserve their recipes, the tradition will outlast any fast food chain."

Teacher note — Hot Take tone: This is the most emotionally charged Hot Take of M6 for Mexican students. The fast food vs. traditional food tension is real and immediate — tamales vs. a burger is not an abstract debate for them. Let the heat in. The strongest argument will be the one grounded in personal memory. Listen for the colloquial phrase "Some things you just can't give up" — when it appears naturally, without prompting, it is a fluency breakthrough. Celebrate it.
PROGRAMA note: S3 food and identity is documented as one of the best sessions of B1.1 for Mexican students. The B session should carry that same energy. If the Hot Take runs long because students are genuinely arguing, let it run. Authentic production is always the priority over timing.

Pitch / Debate — The Cultural Heritage Case

15 min
Pitch · Make the case for cultural food heritage · Stacked FC + PP · UNESCO-level argument
T→S Pitch Round 1 — The Heritage Case (3 min)
You are presenting to an international cultural committee arguing why one food tradition from your region deserves official protection or recognition. Structure: (1) What this tradition has meant to your community — PP evidence — (2) What will happen if it is not protected — FC warning — (3) And if that happens, what will be lost — second FC — (4) What specific protection or action you propose. Required: stacked double FC + PP + 3 opinion phrases + personal memory or example.
Vocabulary: identity, tradition, heritage, preserve, associate, memory, recipe, celebrate. Colloquial: "That dish reminds me of home."
S↔S Debate Round 2 — The Modernist (90 sec)
Partner plays a modernist economist: "You said this tradition has deep meaning — but if we invest in protecting food culture instead of industrial development, we will miss real economic opportunities. Culture can survive without subsidies." Pitcher responds: "I see both sides, but the reality is..." Use PP + stacked FC to defend: "If we lose this tradition, we will lose... and if we lose that, we will never..."
Key: double FC chain must be logically connected. The second consequence must follow from the first.
Class Debate Round 3 — Best Heritage Argument (2 min)
Class votes on the most emotionally compelling AND logically structured argument. The winner presents their full double FC chain to the class. Then: class reports what they heard using PP: "She argued that... / She demonstrated that the tradition has..." Close with: "Some things you just can't give up."
This round produces natural PP reporting of arguments just made — grammar consolidation through genuine communication.
Teacher note — Heritage Case: The best pitches in this round will be specific: not "Mexican food" in general, but "mole negro from Oaxaca" or "the way my grandmother makes pozole" or "the tortilla-making process in rural Michoacán." Specificity is the difference between a B1.1 argument and a B1.2 argument. Push students toward the particular. "Which dish? From where? Who makes it? What will be lost if no one learns the recipe?"
Upsell note: "You just made an argument for cultural preservation in English that a diplomat or UNESCO representative would make. The vocabulary, the structure, the emotional grounding — that is professional advocacy. That is what this program builds toward."

Relay — The Food Memory Chain

10 min
Relay · Food Memory Chain · 4 voices · Personal → Family → Community → Universal
🏃 Food Memory Chain — 4 Voices, One Tradition
Voice 1
👤 Personal
Shares a food memory using PP + one FC about what would be lost
→
Voice 2
👪 Family
Expands to the family tradition: "If that recipe disappeared from our family..."
→
Voice 3
🏠 Community
Scales to the community: "If families stop making this dish, the community will..."
→
Voice 4
🌎 Universal
States the global cultural loss with PP + stacked double FC
How it works:
Voice 1: "That dish reminds me of home. My grandmother has made it every Christmas for as long as I can remember. If she had never taught my mother the recipe, I would have lost that connection."
Voice 2: "If that recipe disappears from our family, the children won't know where their identity comes from. Food has always been the way we pass our story to the next generation."
Voice 3: "If families stop making traditional dishes, communities will lose the rituals that bring them together. The festivals, the celebrations, the shared meals — if they disappear, the community will fracture."
Voice 4: "Cultures that have preserved their food traditions have held onto something irreplaceable. If communities don't actively preserve these practices, they will vanish. And if they vanish, we will have less humanity in the world — not more convenience."

Target language: The chain must move from intimate to universal. Each voice must include at least one FC. Voice 4 must use PP + stacked double FC. The emotional arc is the point.
Starter memories for Voice 1 (teacher chooses)
"That dish reminds me of my grandmother's kitchen on Sunday mornings when I was very young."
"There is a smell from my mother's cooking that I have never found anywhere else in the world."
"Every time I eat this dish, I remember a specific moment — a person, a place, a feeling that no other food can recreate."
Teacher note — Food Memory Chain debrief: This relay works best when Voice 1 takes a real risk — shares a genuine memory, not a safe invented one. The more personal Voice 1 is, the more authentic the chain becomes. After Voice 4 speaks, ask: "Did the emotional arc build? Did the argument feel true? Was the double FC logically connected?" Then ask Voice 1: "Do you recognize your memory in what Voice 4 said?" The gap between the personal and the universal is the cultural insight of this session.

Speed Round — 30 Seconds Each

10 min
Speed Round · 30 seconds · Stacked FC + PP + opinion · No pausing

30 seconds, one student. Each response must include at least one stacked FC (double) OR PP + FC. Click Done when complete.

Prompt 1
Use "That dish reminds me of home" as your opener. Then: stack two FC about what happens if that food tradition disappears. Real dish, real consequence.
Prompt 2
Describe one specific food tradition from your family or region using PP: "This dish has always been..." Then: "If we don't preserve it, we will lose..." + "And if we lose that..."
Prompt 3
Argue: "I love trying new food — but some things you just can't give up." Use PP to say what you have discovered through food + one FC about what you would lose without your cultural food anchor.
Prompt 4
Recall Rodrigo's story about the Japanese dinner from S3A. Summarize what he said using PP: "He had never experienced food as a philosophy before that night. He said..." + one FC from his argument.
Prompt 5
Defend fast food: "I see both sides, but the reality is..." Use PP + one FC. "Fast food has made food more accessible to people who... / If fast food hadn't existed, some communities would never have..."
Prompt 6
Use "I strongly believe that..." to make the case for UNESCO-level protection of a specific Mexican food tradition. Stack double FC: "If it is not protected... and if that happens..."
Completed: 0 / 6
Teacher note — Speed Round timing: In S3B, the 30-second constraint is deliberately harder because students are expected to produce stacked FC, not just single FC. Expect some students to only get one FC in 30 seconds — that is acceptable. What matters is that the attempt is genuine and the structure is present. After the round: "Did anyone manage a double FC in 30 seconds? That is B1.2-range production. Under pressure."

Solo Challenge — The Cultural Heritage Pitch

15 min
Solo Challenge · 2 minutes · Heritage pitch · Stacked FC + PP + personal memory + opinion language
🎯 The Challenge Brief
An international cultural foundation asks you: "Tell us about one food tradition from your culture that deserves to be preserved — and why the world would be poorer without it."

Answer in 2 minutes. Include:
• What this tradition has meant to you personally — PP + personal memory
• What will be lost if it disappears — First FC
• And what will happen as a consequence of that loss — Second FC (stacked)
• At least 3 opinion phrases — including "I strongly believe that..."
• At least 4 S3A vocabulary words
• At least one colloquial phrase used naturally
• Close with genuine emotion — this is a pitch, not a grammar exercise

No notes. This is the most personal Solo Challenge of Module 6.
Teacher Rubric — Click when met
PP + Personal Memory
"This tradition has always been... / I have associated this dish with..." Specific, honest, real.
First FC (Warning)
"If this tradition disappears, [specific loss] will happen." Logical and particular, not vague.
Second FC (Stacked)
"And if [that loss] happens, [deeper consequence] will follow." Connected to the first, not a new topic.
Opinion Phrases
3+ phrases including "I strongly believe that..." Used naturally, integrated into argument.
Vocabulary
4+ S3A words: identity, tradition, heritage, preserve, associate, memory, comfort food, celebrate.
Engagement & Emotion
The speaker clearly cares. The class listens. The argument feels true, not performed.
Teacher note — Cultural Heritage Pitch: Give students 3 minutes of silent preparation before this challenge. This one earns it. After the talk, give feedback in this specific order: (1) Name the exact moment that was most powerful — quote the student's own words back to them. (2) Identify whether the double FC was present and connected. (3) State what level the talk represents. For a student who uses stacked FC naturally with genuine emotion: "That is a B1.2 performance. You just gave a heritage argument in English that has real emotional and logical weight. That is rare."
GT Original — S3B is the emotional peak of M6 B-sessions. The food topic generates more authentic language than any other in this module because the emotional investment is real. Students who speak slowly here because they are choosing words carefully are working harder, not worse. Fluency at the service of truth is the highest form of language production. Name that when you see it.
