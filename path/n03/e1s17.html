Teacher notes · §2 Vocabulary

Pedagógica

  • Step A: tú dices la palabra, el alumno repite 2 veces; luego el alumno inicia y tú respondes.
  • Describe It, Don't Say It (OCULTAMIENTO, A2): el describidor NO puede decir la palabra ni traducirla — solo da pistas EN ORACIONES con "You have to…". Ejemplo válido para "dishes": "You have to do this after dinner, with soap and water." Ejemplo inválido: solo decir "plato" o "dishes".
  • Escalada: ronda 1 = 40s por palabra; cada ronda nueva baja el tiempo (el botón "Next round" lo reduce). Regla de oro: el punto es por PRODUCIR la oración con have to/has to, no por adivinar rápido.

Engagement

  • "Ten clues, ten sentences with have to — let's clear the board."

Crecimiento

  • Estas 10 palabras regresan en §3 (Tic-Tac-Toe), §4 (Reading) y la Mission.

Grammar · Have to / has to

13–25 min · Discover → understand → compete
New pointGRM-15 Tic-Tac-Toe · colección
Discover

Read the message. What two words show an obligation?

🚪🔑

You have to take off your shoes at the door.

Really? Every time?

Yes! And she has to wash the dishes tonight.

OK, I can help her.

Count them. How many do you see?(Respuesta: 2 · have to, has to)
The tool

Have to = obligation

1 · I / you / we / they

have to + verb

We have to be quiet after ten.

2 · he / she / it

has to + verb

She has to wash the dishes.

3 · The verb

base form, never -s

He has to clean (not "cleans").

Sentence
I/you/we/theyI have to take off my shoes. · We have to share the bathroom.
he/she/itHe has to take out the trash. · The guest has to give back the key.

Hoy solo practicamos el afirmativo. Don't have to (no es obligatorio) se practica en la Sesión 18, y Do I have to…? en la Sesión 19. No usamos must ni had to en este nivel.

¿Qué es? Have to significa "tener que" — expresa una obligación o una regla.

¿Cómo se usa? Con I / you / we / they: have to. Con he / she / it: has to. Después siempre va el verbo en forma base: has to wash (nunca "washes").

¿Cómo suena? have to se pronuncia /ˈhæf tə/ (la "v" suena como "f"); has to se pronuncia /ˈhæz tə/. El acento cae en la palabra que nombra la regla: RULE, KEY, DISHES.

¿Para qué hoy? Para explicar las reglas de una casa compartida — lo que se necesita en la Misión.

Practice A · Mechanical

Choose: have to · has to · has

Practice B · GRM-15

Tic-Tac-Toe — claim a square with a correct sentence

1. Read the icon and the subject on a square. 2. Say a COMPLETE sentence with have to / has to for that subject, out loud. 3. If it's correct, your team claims the square. Three in a row wins.

Team A's turn
Watch out

Mistakes at this level

She have to wash the dishes.She has to wash the dishes.he/she/it → has to, not have to.
He has to washes the dishes.He has to wash the dishes.Base form after "to" — never add -s.
You has to take off your shoes.You have to take off your shoes.you/we/they → have to.
Teacher notes · §3 Grammar

Pedagógica

  • Punto NUEVO del día. Da tiempo real al Discover antes de nombrar la regla. Practice A es mecánica (máx. 1/3 del bloque).
  • Tic-Tac-Toe es COMUNICATIVA: el alumno dice la oración completa en voz alta ANTES de que reclames/marques la casilla por él — no hagas clic sin escuchar la oración correcta.
  • Nivel: solo afirmativo con have to/has to. Nunca introduzcas must ni had to, aunque un alumno los proponga — recast con have to.

Engagement

  • "Three in a row — but only with real sentences, not guesses."

Crecimiento

  • "Have to" regresa en S18 (don't have to), S19 (Do I have to…?), S20 (avisos) y S30 (mediación).

HOUSE RULES

Welcome to our home! Please follow these simple rules. First, you have to take off your shoes at the door. Second, everybody has to wash their own dishes after eating. We also have to keep the kitchen clean after cooking. Third, we have to take out the trash on Mondays and Fridays. We have to be quiet after ten p.m. because our neighbors work early. A guest has to give the key back before leaving. Everybody has to share the bathroom in the morning. Thank you for helping us clean up!

Teacher notes · §4 Reading

Pedagógica

  • Primera lectura en silencio (1 min) buscando SOLO "have to" / "has to" y la regla que sigue. Segunda lectura en voz alta, un alumno por regla.
  • ~90 palabras, nivel A2 — ninguna palabra fuera del léxico de la sesión.

Engagement

  • "Real house rules, day 17 — you can read every single one without help."

Crecimiento

Third, we have to be quiet after ten p.m. Fourth, you have to leave the front door open all night. Fifth, the guest has to give the key to me before leaving. Any questions?

Instruction Trap — which rule is wrong?

Nota: "Can you repeat…?" es un chunk de reconocimiento (ya se escuchó en Básico 2) — no se evalúa como punto nuevo hoy.

Pronunciation

have to / has to · stress on RULE and KEY

Teacher notes · §5 Listening

Pedagógica

  • Guion de 2 hablantes, para grabar: HOST (hombre, inglés irlandés, velocidad medio-lenta) y GUEST (mujer, inglés estadounidense, lenta) — ambos personajes NUEVOS del nivel.
  • Answer key — Global: (a) explaining the house rules. Detalle: la regla 4 ("leave the front door open all night") es incorrecta; la correcta es "You have to close (lock) the front door at night."
  • IMPOSTOR: el alumno debe señalar la regla Y proponer la corrección — no basta con decir "está mal".

Engagement

  • "A good guest listens for the rule that doesn't make sense."

Crecimiento

  • "Have to" regresa en la Mission con un reglamento nuevo que el propio grupo escribe.

Teacher notes · §6 Speaking

Pedagógica

  • El sorteo es REAL: el alumno no elige su postura — eso es lo que hace la dinámica incómoda y útil. No dejes que cambien de postura antes de defenderla 60s.
  • Grupo: parejas, uno defiende, el otro reta con preguntas usando have to/has to. 1:1: tú retas las dos posturas.
  • El veredicto final es personal y puede no coincidir con ninguna de las dos tarjetas — eso es correcto.

Engagement

  • "You didn't choose your side — now make it sound true."

Crecimiento

  • Cuenta cuántas veces usó have to/has to correctamente en las dos posturas — insumo directo para la Mission.

Mission · House Rules Meeting

Real-life taskEvidence
Your mission

Three roommates, three complaints. Negotiate 6 house rules and give 2 responsibilities to real names.

Success: 6 correct rules with have to / has to (3 with he/she) + 2 responsibilities assigned by name + 1 negotiated rule with a simple reason.

Group: each roommate card goes to one student in a breakout room. Present the final rulebook in 60s.

1:1: the teacher plays the three roommates, one complaint at a time.

Teacher notes · §7 Mission

Pedagógica

  • Marca cada casilla SOLO cuando el grupo dice la oración completa en voz alta, sin leerla de la pantalla.
