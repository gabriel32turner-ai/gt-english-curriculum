§1
Warm-Up — Question Recall
4 quick prompts · Teacher fires, student forms the question instantly · No preparation
⏱ 5 MIN
💡 Setup: Spin or dice to select a card. Teacher reads the cue; student must produce the full going-to question AND a short answer — no pausing. This is a diagnostic to calibrate today's session.
Card 1 — Yes / No Question
Cue: "Daniel / attend / the product launch / tomorrow"
Student must ask: "Is Daniel going to attend the product launch tomorrow?" and give a short answer: "Yes, he is." or "No, he isn't."
Q: "Is Daniel going to attend the product launch tomorrow?"
A+: "Yes, he is."    A-: "No, he isn't. He's going to join remotely."
Card 2 — Wh- Question (WHAT)
Cue: "Valeria / prepare / for the client call / next week"
Student must ask: "What is Valeria going to prepare for the client call next week?"
Q: "What is Valeria going to prepare for the client call next week?"
A: "She's going to prepare the sales report and the timeline."
Card 3 — Wh- Question (WHO)
Cue: "someone / lead / the project meeting / on Monday"
Student must ask: "Who is going to lead the project meeting on Monday?"
Q: "Who is going to lead the project meeting on Monday?"
A: "Carlos is. He's going to lead it."   Note: WHO = subject → Is going to, not did.
Card 4 — Yes / No + Negative (ARE THEY?)
Cue: "the suppliers / confirm / the order / in two weeks"
Student asks the Y/N question, then gives a negative short answer + reason with "going to".
Q: "Are the suppliers going to confirm the order in two weeks?"
A: "No, they aren't. They're going to postpone it until next quarter."
Card 5 — Bonus — WHEN question
Cue: "the finance team / review / the budget / ?"
Student must form: "When is the finance team going to review the budget?"
Q: "When is the finance team going to review the budget?"
A: "They're going to review it next Thursday at 2 PM."
🎮 Error watch: Common mistakes today: "When they are going to…?" (missing inversion), "Is she going to presented?" (adding -ed), "Yes, she going to." (missing verb). Note any errors silently — use silent recast.

§3
Negotiation Debate — Who's Going to Handle It?
Two roles · Negotiate the project plan using going-to questions · Reach agreement
⏱ 12 MIN
Scenario: Valeria (Project Manager) and Carlos (Sales Lead) must divide responsibilities for the Q2 client launch at Solaris Manufacturing. Neither wants to handle the client communication AND the logistics. Use going-to questions to negotiate who does what.
Role A — Valeria (Project Manager)
You're going to handle the internal timeline and budget review. You don't want to manage the client calls — ask Carlos to do it.
  • "Are you going to contact the client directly?"
  • "Who is going to prepare the presentation?"
  • "I'm not going to manage the client emails — I'm going to focus on the budget."
  • "When are you going to confirm the schedule with them?"
Role B — Carlos (Sales Lead)
You're going to lead the client relationship. But you need Valeria to handle logistics. Negotiate — use questions to clarify responsibilities.
  • "What are you going to do about the delivery schedule?"
  • "Is the team going to be ready by next Friday?"
  • "I'm not going to organize the venue — are you going to do that?"
  • "Who is going to send the confirmation email to the client?"
Agreement target — end the negotiation with:
"OK, so I'm going to handle [X], and you're going to handle [Y]."
"And we're going to meet on [day] to confirm everything. Agreed?"
📚 Pedagogical: The goal is sustained question production in a realistic negotiation. Count how many going-to questions the student produces (target: 5+). Watch for: question word order ("What you are going to do?" vs "What are you going to do?"), and whether they use short answers fluently mid-dialogue.

§5
Solo Challenge — Project Brief
Student delivers a 90-second brief · Ask AND answer 6 going-to questions · No notes
⏱ 10 MIN
Scenario: You are presenting a new project plan to your manager. Your manager asks you six questions. You must both ask the questions (as your manager) AND answer them (as yourself) — monologue style — in 90 seconds.
❓ 3 Y/N questions "Is the team going to…? / Are we going to…?" + short answers
❓ 3 Wh- questions What / Who / When + going to + full answer
❌ 1 negative "We're not going to…" with a reason
📅 2 time expressions next week / at 10 AM / in two weeks / tomorrow
Sentence starters to use:
"Are we going to launch on schedule?" — "Yes, we are."
"Who is going to handle client communication?" — "Maria is."
"What are we going to present at the review?" — "We're going to present the Q2 results."
"Is the budget going to be approved?" — "No, it isn't — we're not going to exceed €50,000."
"When are we going to confirm with the client?" — "We're going to call them tomorrow at 10 AM."
90
💰 Retention & upsell: This is the mastery moment. After 3 sessions on going-to questions, the student can self-ask and self-answer a realistic project brief. Frame it: "This is what your next standup in English is going to sound like." Massive confidence signal.
§6
Fluency Booster — Going To vs Fixed Schedule
Advanced contrast · 4 source texts · Spot the difference in real professional language
⏱ 8 MIN
📚 Context: This section deepens the going-to vs Simple Present distinction introduced in S3A. Students read 4 authentic-style texts and answer analysis questions. Teacher leads the discussion — this is not silent work.
💌 Email — Project Update
Hi Carlos, quick update: the kick-off meeting is on Monday at 9 AM (room B2). We're going to discuss the Q2 launch timeline and assign roles. Are you going to bring the client brief? Let me know. — Valeria
🤔 "The meeting IS on Monday" (Simple Present — fixed schedule, already booked). "We're going to discuss" (going to — planned action). Can you find the Y/N question? What's the short answer Valeria is expecting?
💬 Chat — Team Message
Daniel: The supplier call starts at 11. Are you going to join, Valeria?
Valeria: Yes, I am. I'm going to prepare three questions for them first.
Daniel: Perfect. Who else is going to be on the call?
Valeria: Carlos and the logistics team. They're not going to miss this one.
🤔 Count: how many "going to" uses? Which one is a Wh- question? Which uses Simple Present and why? What do the short answers reveal about formality?
📋 Meeting Minutes — Action Items
Q2 Launch Planning — Action Items:
• Valeria is going to confirm delivery dates with Proveedor Norte by May 20
• Carlos is going to send the client presentation by end of week
• The logistics team is NOT going to process new orders until the system update is complete
• Next review meeting: Thursday at 2 PM (Room A1)
🤔 Why do action items use "going to" (not will / not Simple Present)? Which item uses Simple Present — and why is it different from the others?
Quick Reference — Going To vs Simple Present
Situation Going To (planned intention) Simple Present (fixed schedule) Note
Team discussion plan "We're going to discuss the budget." — Planned action — use going to
Meeting on the calendar — "The meeting is at 9 AM." Fixed timetable — use Simple Present
Asking about intentions "Are you going to attend?"
"Yes, I am."		 — Question about plan — use going to
Train / flight departure — "The flight leaves at 6 AM." Fixed schedule — Simple Present
Personal future plan "I'm going to prepare three questions." — Intention already decided — going to
💡 Pedagogical: At A2.1 the key distinction is not nuanced — it is binary. If it is on the calendar (timetable / schedule), Simple Present. If it is a plan, intention, or decision already made, going to. The question form "Are you going to attend?" ALWAYS uses going to — never Simple Present for this meaning.

🎯 Use These at Work This Week
3 Going-To Questions You Can Use Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"Are you going to handle that?"
The most powerful delegation question. Use in any meeting when responsibilities are unclear. Short, professional, forces a clear "Yes, I am." or "No, I'm not." response.
PHRASE 2
"What are we going to do about this?"
The problem-solving question. Use when a deadline is missed or a client is unhappy. Opens the discussion and shows leadership — even as a junior team member.
PHRASE 3
"Who is going to confirm that with the client?"
The accountability question. Use at the end of any meeting to clarify action items. The "who" forces a name — not a vague "we will." Sounds senior and structured in English.
