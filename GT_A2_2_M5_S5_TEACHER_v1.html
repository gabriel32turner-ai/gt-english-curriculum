Short adjective — -er than
1–2 syllables: faster, cheaper, higher, closer, better, worse
"Option A is faster than Option B."
✗ "more faster" / "more cheaper"
Long adjective — more...than
3+ syllables: more qualified, more collaborative, more expensive
"She is more qualified than the other candidate."
✗ "qualifieder"
Irregular forms
good → better → the best · bad → worse → the worst
"This is the best option. That was the worst commute."
✗ "more better" / "the most best"
Intensifiers
much/far (big) · slightly/a little (small) · significantly (measurable)
"The salary is significantly higher. The commute is only slightly longer."
✗ "very more expensive"
Equal comparison — as...as
Same level: as + adjective + as · Less than: not as + adj + as
"The new job is not as secure as my current one."
✗ "as more comfortable as"
Superlative — the most
Best/worst of 3+: the most + adj · the -est · always with THE
"Of all the options, this is the most cost-effective."
✗ "the most best" / "most expensive" (no THE)

⚡ Teacher Note — Module Recap
Start S5 with a full class oral review: teacher points to each domain card and asks one student to produce a sentence from memory. No notes. "Products: give me a sentence with a superlative." "Travel: give me an intensifier + comparative adverb." "Career: give me as...as." This diagnostic reveals who has mastered which structures before the Exit Task. Colloquial Strip: each student chooses their favorite phrase from the module and explains when they would use it in their real job. This is the emotional anchor of S5 — it reconnects the grammar to their professional identity. Typical time: 7 minutes maximum.

Every sentence has exactly ONE error. Find it, choose the correction, then say your own correct sentence using the same structure. These are the most common mistakes from M5 sessions 1–4.

S1 · PRODUCTS: "Supplier B is more cheaper than Supplier A by 15 percent."
S2 · CANDIDATES: "Of all the applicants, Maria is the most qualify for this position."
S2 · CANDIDATES: "Roberto is more experience than Valeria in cross-functional projects."
S3 · TRAVEL: "The bus arrives more punctual than the flight on this route."
S3 · TRAVEL: "The flight is as more expensive as three times the bus fare."
S4 · CAREER: "The new job is more better for my career than my current position."
S4 · CAREER: "The salary at the multinational is significantly more higher than my current one."
S4 · CAREER: "Of all the candidates, she is the most strong fit for the leadership role."
⚡ Teacher Note — Error Correction Sprint
Errors 1, 6, 7 are the double comparative errors (more + -er, more better, more more). Error 3 is the S2 IS/HAS distinction — the most persistent error in the module. Error 4 is the adj/adverb confusion after action verbs (punctual vs punctually). Error 8 is the adjective length confusion: "strong" has 1 syllable so uses -er, not "most." Run as teams: 1 point per correct answer, 2 bonus points for correctly naming the error TYPE before choosing. End with: "Which error was the most common in your group? Why?" This metacognition is B1.1 level thinking — celebrate it.

⚡ Teacher Note — Exit Task
The exit task is the most important 5 minutes of Module 5. Before students begin: "You have used this grammar to compare products, people, travel options and careers across 8 sessions. Now you have 30 seconds to choose your domain and plan your comparison. Then you present without notes." Scoring guidance: 15–20 = outstanding A2.2 performance; 10–14 = solid A2.2, ready for M6; 6–9 = passing but needs review before B1.1; below 6 = recommend one more practice session before M6. Rubric point rb10 (real professional context) is the most differentiated: students who use their actual work experience score higher not just linguistically but because their delivery is more confident and connected. Celebrate every student who completes the 5 minutes — this is the full M5 grammar in one real production moment.
GT Originals — M5 Exit Notes
  • 📚 M5 full arc summary: Students entered with "more + adj" and left with 6 structures across 4 domains. The colloquial toolkit (9 phrases) is the most immediately usable language in the program — they can use every phrase at work this week.
  • ⚡ Most persistent error: "more better" / "more higher" — double comparative. If a student still produces this in the exit task, it is the single highest-priority focus for M6 review.
  • 🔹 IS/HAS distinction (S2): the most sophisticated grammar point in M5. Students who have mastered this are already operating at the top of A2.2. Note these students by name — they are ready to accelerate into B1.1.
  • 🌟 Upsell: "You just completed Module 5 of A2.2. In Module 6, you integrate all A2.2 structures in one networking conversation. After M6, you move to B1.1 — Present Perfect, First Conditional, Passive Voice. Real intermediate English starts there. And everything you built in M5 will be the foundation."
  • 🌎 LinkedIn moment: ask students to write one LinkedIn-ready sentence using today's language before they leave. Example: "I am looking for a role that is significantly more challenging and far more international than my current position." This is the sentence that starts their B1 journey.
