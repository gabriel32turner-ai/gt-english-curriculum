⚡ Hot Take  ·  5 min

Immigration & Belonging — Where Do You Stand?

“If México invested in truly welcoming skilled immigrants, the Bajío industrial corridor would grow twice as fast.”
Voted. Justify in 30 seconds — use Second Conditional + S4A vocabulary.
This Hot Take is grounded in the real economic context of learners’ daily lives — BMW, Audi, Hella, Toyota are all present in the Bajío. The “local talent first” position often wins initial votes but loses in the second round when learners think about skill gaps. Let the tension run. Do not resolve it. Move at 5 minutes regardless.
🗣 Pitch Round  ·  10 min

The Integration Policy Debate

A multinational company is opening a plant near Celaya. They need to hire 200 people, 40 of whom will come from abroad. Each pair argues one approach to integration. 2 min prep — 2 min pitch.

APPROACH A — IMMERSION FIRST
You believe international hires should be placed directly into Mexican teams from day one, with no special treatment. If they adapted quickly, integration would be faster and more authentic. Sink or swim builds real belonging.
Must use: adapt, integration, belong, Second Conditional
APPROACH B — STRUCTURED ONBOARDING
You believe the company should design a 3-month integration program with language support, cultural orientation, and mentors. If the company invested in proper onboarding, the contribution of international talent would be visible within 6 months.
Must use: contribution, diversity, acceptance, Second Conditional
APPROACH C — COMMUNITY INTEGRATION
You believe workplace integration is not enough — the company should also help immigrants connect with the local community. If immigrants felt welcomed outside work too, they would stay longer and contribute more.
Must use: prejudice, acceptance, opportunity, Second Conditional
APPROACH D — CULTURAL EXCHANGE
You believe integration should be mutual: Mexican employees should also learn about the cultures of their international colleagues. If the exchange went both ways, diversity would create genuine innovation rather than just filling a quota.
Must use: stereotype, diversity, integrate, Second Conditional
PREP TIME
PITCH TIME
Approach D (Cultural Exchange) is the most innovative and often surprises learners who hadn’t considered that integration is mutual. After pitches, ask: “Which approach would actually work at YOUR company right now?” Ground the abstract in the real. This produces the most authentic production of the session.
🏃 Relay  ·  8 min

Journey Map Relay

Each node on the map represents one stage in an immigrant’s first year at a company in Celaya. Each learner takes ONE node and speaks for 40 seconds: what is this person experiencing? What would help them at this stage?

✈
Arrives in Mexico. First week. Overwhelmed.
💬
First team meeting. Language barrier. Feels invisible.
☕
Month 2. First real connection with a colleague.
😊
Month 6. Starts to feel like they belong.
🏠
Year 1. Mentors a new international hire.
For each node: Describe what the person is thinking and feeling. Then say: “If the company had done _____ at this stage, this person would have…”
▶ The final node (Year 1 mentor) is the most powerful — this person is now the integration bridge. Assign it to the strongest speaker.
The Journey Map is S4B’s unique relay format — distinct from all previous B-session relays. It maps a human arc of belonging, activating empathy and Second Conditional simultaneously. The visual structure (origin → destination) helps learners sequence their ideas. After all 5 nodes, run a 2-minute group close: “What was the turning point in this person’s journey?”
⚡ Speed Round  ·  6 min

30 Seconds — Immigration Edition

Pick a card. 30 seconds. Click when done.

Name 3 barriers an immigrant might face in their first month at a Mexican company — be specific, not generic.
Language, culture, bureaucracy, social dynamics…
If you had to move to Japan for work, what would be the hardest thing to adapt to? Be honest and specific.
Personal + Second Conditional + vocabulary
What is the difference between “tolerance” and “acceptance” in a workplace context? Make the distinction clear.
“Tolerance = I allow you. Acceptance = I include you.”
Someone says: “Immigrants should adapt completely — it’s their choice to come here.” Respond constructively in 30 seconds.
Concede + counter. Use: integration, contribution, adapt
If your team suddenly included colleagues from 5 different countries, what ONE team norm would you suggest to help everyone belong?
Practical + Second Conditional: “If we agreed to…, everyone could…”
Describe someone you know or have heard of who successfully immigrated and contributed significantly. What made the difference?
Real story. Use: adapt, belong, contribute, opportunity
30s SPEED TIMER
Card 3 (tolerance vs acceptance) is a vocabulary-precision distinction that B1.2 learners often conflate. Reward those who articulate it clearly. Card 6 (real story) produces the most personal and authentic language — this is the card that reveals whether learners have truly internalised the vocabulary. If someone has personal immigration experience (family, friends), this card often produces unexpected depth. Let it breathe.
🌟 Solo Challenge  ·  8 min

The Welcome Message

Your Brief
Your company is welcoming a new international team member next Monday. You have been asked to write and deliver a 90-second welcome speech on behalf of the team. The goal: make this person feel genuinely included from day one — not just tolerated.

Speak directly to this new colleague. Use “you” and “we.” Be warm, professional, and specific about what your team has to offer and what you hope to learn from them.
✅ Address the new colleague directly (“you” / “we”)
✅ Use Second Conditional at least once: “If you ever need…, we would…”
✅ Use at least 3 words from S4A vocabulary
✅ Include one specific thing the team is excited to learn FROM this person
❌ No clichés: not “Our door is always open” — be genuine and specific
90s SOLO TIMER
This is the most human challenge of Module 4. The language is direct, warm, and professionally essential. A welcome speech is a real deliverable in international workplaces.
💰 Close the session with this: “You just delivered a welcome speech in English. That’s not intermediate English. That’s professional English. At B2.1 you’ll be facilitating multilingual meetings, writing formal reports, and presenting to international leadership. The gap is smaller than you think — and you’re already crossing it.”

🎯 3 Phrases From This Session — Use Them This Week:
“If you ever need anything, we would be happy to help.”
Use it when welcoming someone new — a colleague, a client, a supplier. Second Conditional makes it warm and non-presumptuous.
“At the end of the day, diversity makes us stronger.”
Use it to close a business case for inclusion, or to reframe a team conversation about differences. Works in any professional context.
“That hit different for me — I hadn’t considered that perspective.”
Use it when someone shares a view that genuinely changes your thinking. Shows intellectual honesty and openness. Rare in professional English — powerful when used.
