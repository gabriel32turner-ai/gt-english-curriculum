§1
Warm-Up — Who would you hire?
Activate HR comparison thinking · Connect comparatives to people decisions · 5–8 min
⏱ 5–8 MIN

Remember from S1: cheaper → cheaper than · reliable → more reliable than · good → better than / the best. Today: same tools, new context — people and jobs.

👔 Have you ever been involved in a hiring decision? Did you compare candidates? What did you look at — experience, education, personality, salary expectations?
💡 Say: "Tell your partner about one hiring situation you were part of — or one time you were the candidate. What made someone the best choice?" Collect Spanish words for qualities (experiencia, calificado, disponible) → write on board.
⚖️ Quick ranking: When your company hires someone, what is the most important quality? Rank in order: experience / qualifications / personality / salary expectations / availability.
💡 Let students debate the ranking — they will naturally use "more important than" and "the most important." Do NOT correct the grammar yet — collect examples and use them in §4 Grammar to show "you were already using this — here is the rule."
🤖 Translation challenge: How do you say in English: "El candidato A tiene más experiencia que el B" and "La candidata B está más calificada para este puesto." Try before any notes!
💡 Collect attempts. Note "more experience than" (noun comparison) vs "more experienced than" (adjective). This contrast surfaces in §4 Grammar — save it for then.
👩‍💼 Andrea's new challenge: After her supplier win in S1, Andrea's manager now asks her to help choose between two candidates for a new Logistics Coordinator role. She has two CVs and 24 hours. What would you look for first?
💡 This sets up the narrative for the whole session. Andrea is the character — her situation drives the reading, listening and grammar. Ask: "What is more important for a logistics coordinator — experience or education?" Plant the debate.
👔
Quick Win — 20 seconds
Say this out loud: "Candidate A is more experienced than Candidate B, but Candidate B is better qualified for this role."
You already know the structure. Today you get the vocabulary to make this sentence yours — in any real HR conversation.
⚡ Teacher Note — Warm-Up
S2A shifts context from products/suppliers (S1) to people/candidates — same grammar, new domain. This is deliberate: show students that "more reliable than" works for a machine AND a person. Layer 1 (A2.2 standard): let students discuss in pairs, collect Spanish → English on the board. Layer 2 (stronger): challenge them to say the translation challenge in under 10 seconds. Layer 3 (fastest): ask "what is the difference between having more experience and being more experienced?" — this is the noun vs adjective contrast that surfaces in §4.

⚡ Teacher Note — Vocabulary
Key pronunciation note: "strengths" /streŋkθs/ — the consonant cluster at the end is difficult for Spanish speakers (they will say "strengs" or "strengths-es"). Drill it 3x before moving on. Pair drill after cards: "Name a quality from the vocab list that describes YOU as a worker — then name one that describes a colleague." This makes the vocabulary personal and memorable. False friend: "available" ≠ "voluble" (Spanish for talkative). "Available" = disponible. Always clarify.

§3
Colloquial — Hiring & People Language
4 real phrases used in HR conversations and performance reviews · 5 min
⏱ 5 MIN
"She ticks all the boxes."
Use when a candidate meets every requirement on your list. Strong endorsement in a hiring context.
"Candidate B ticks all the boxes — bilingual, experienced, and available next month."
"He is a strong fit for this role."
Use when a candidate's profile matches the job well. More professional than "he is good for the job."
"After reviewing both CVs, I think Candidate A is a stronger fit for this role."
"She stands out from the others."
Use when one candidate is clearly better or more impressive than all others. A superlative idea in a colloquial phrase.
"Of all the candidates we interviewed, she stands out the most — her experience is unmatched."
"It is a tough call."
Use when two options are very similar and the decision is difficult. Opposite of "it's a no-brainer."
"Both candidates are well qualified — it's a tough call. But I think Candidate B is slightly better."
⚡ Teacher Note — Colloquial Drill
Connect to S1 vocabulary: "It's a no-brainer" (S1) = one option is obviously better. "It's a tough call" (S2) = both options are similar — create the contrast on the board. Drill "tick all the boxes" — students often say "check all the boxes" (American English version is acceptable too, but "tick" is the more common corporate British English form used in Bajío multinationals). Role-play: teacher reads a candidate profile, students use one colloquial phrase to react. 30 seconds each.

🚨 Real Work Situation
Andrea has to send a comparison report to her manager by 5pm. She wants to write: "Roberto tiene más experiencia, pero Valeria está más calificada." But she is not sure: do you say "more experience" or "more experienced"? And is it "more qualified" or "more qualification"?
You think: "Is it: She is more qualified OR she has more qualifications? He has more experience OR he is more experienced?" — These are NOT the same in English.
🔧 The Tool — Two Ways to Compare People
With adjectives → use more + adjective + than (describes the person):
  "Valeria is more qualified than Roberto for this position."
  "Roberto is more experienced than Valeria in logistics operations."

With nouns → use more + noun + than (describes what they have):
  "Roberto has more experience than Valeria — 8 years vs 4."
  "Valeria has more qualifications than Roberto — degree and MBA."

Key rule: Adjective = describes the person (qualified, experienced). Noun = what they have (experience, qualification). Both use more … than.
What you compareStructureExample — Roberto vs Valeria
Adjectivemore + adj + thanValeria is more qualified than Roberto. Roberto is more experienced than Valeria.
Nounmore + noun + thanRoberto has more experience than Valeria. Valeria has more qualifications than Roberto.
Short adjectiveadj + -er + thanRoberto is cheaper than Valeria (salary). Valeria's English is stronger than Roberto's.
Superlative — groupthe most + adj / the + -estValeria is the most qualified candidate on our shortlist. Roberto is the cheapest option.
As … as (equality)as + adj + asRoberto's commitment is as strong as Valeria's — both want this role.
⚠️ Common Errors — Mexican learners:
✗ "She is more experience than him." → ✓ She is more experienced (adjective) OR she has more experience (noun)
✗ "He has more experienced than her." → ✓ He has more experience (noun after "has") OR he is more experienced (adj after "is")
✗ "She is the most qualify candidate." → ✓ She is the most qualified (past participle used as adjective)
✗ "He is more available as her." → ✓ more available than her — never "as" after comparatives
⚡ ACTIVITY A — Choose the Correct Form

Use the Roberto vs Valeria profiles above. Choose the correct comparative structure for each sentence.

1. Roberto _____ Valeria in years of logistics work. (more experience / more experienced)
2. Valeria _____ Roberto — she has a degree and an MBA. (more qualification / more qualified)
3. In terms of salary, Roberto is _____ Valeria — $18k vs $24k per month.
4. Of all the candidates we interviewed, Valeria is _____.
5. Roberto's commitment to the role is _____ Valeria's — both really want this job.

Micro-Speaking
Tell your partner: based on the profiles above, who would YOU hire — Roberto or Valeria? Give 2 reasons using comparatives. "I would hire [name] because [he/she] is more [adj] than [name] and [he/she] has more [noun] than [name]." 60 seconds — go!

GT Originals — Teacher Notes
  • 👔 The adjective vs noun contrast is the highest-value grammar point in S2A. Spanish does not distinguish between "está calificado" (adjective) and "tiene calificaciones" (noun) the same way English does. Make this explicit: draw two columns on the board — IS (adjective) / HAS (noun) — and ask students to sort the vocabulary from §2 into each column.
  • ⚠️ Watch for: "He is more experience" (mixing noun with "is") and "She has more experienced" (mixing adjective with "has"). These are the two most common errors from Mexican learners in HR contexts.
  • 💼 Cultural bridge: in Mexican corporate culture, "la actitud" (attitude/proactivity) is often valued as much as formal qualifications. Ask students: "At your company, is experience more important than attitude?" — this triggers the grammar in a culturally relevant way.
  • 🌟 Upsell: students who can write a candidate comparison report in English are already doing B1-level professional writing. Point this out — "what you are doing right now is exactly what a B1.1 student does." Creates aspiration and positions the next level.

Internal Hiring Report
Module 5 · Session 2A
🔑 Grammar: Comparatives — adj & noun (orange) · Superlatives (cyan) · As…as equality (green)

After reviewing both CVs and conducting phone interviews, I am ready to make my recommendation for the Logistics Coordinator position.

Roberto Morales has more experience than Valeria Sandoval in logistics — eight years compared to four. He is also more available than her, since he can start immediately. His salary expectation is significantly lower than hers, which is important for our current budget.

However, Valeria Sandoval is more qualified than Roberto in terms of formal education — she has a university degree and an MBA. She is also bilingual, which makes her more suitable than Roberto for our international accounts. Her references are stronger than his — three former managers confirmed her performance. Her English is almost as strong as a native speaker's.

In my opinion, Valeria is the most qualified and the most strategic hire for the long term. Yes, she is more expensive than Roberto, but her bilingual skills and qualifications make her the stronger fit for where our company is going. She ticks all the boxes for the international growth we are planning.

My recommendation: hire Valeria Sandoval.

Comprehension — True or False?

1. Roberto has more experience than Valeria in logistics.
2. Valeria is available to start immediately.
3. Andrea recommends Valeria because she is bilingual and more qualified.
4. Roberto's salary expectation is higher than Valeria's.
5. Andrea uses the phrase "she ticks all the boxes" in her report.
🗣️
Micro-Speaking
From memory: summarize Andrea's recommendation in 2 sentences. Use one comparative and one colloquial phrase from §3. Example: "Andrea recommends Valeria because she is more qualified than Roberto. She says Valeria ticks all the boxes." 30 seconds — go!
⚡ Teacher Note — Reading
Ask students to underline all comparison structures before checking answers — color-coding in the text makes this visual. Connection to real life: "Does your company write hiring reports or candidate summaries in English? After today, you can." This is a direct upsell moment for English in the workplace. For stronger groups: ask them to rewrite the final paragraph from Roberto's point of view — flipping the comparatives builds deeper grammar control.

⏱ 8–10 MIN
The Interview Call — Vertex Logistics, Celaya
A2.2 · M5 · S2A · Voice: Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.82 · ~55 sec
Hi, this is Andrea Soto calling from Vertex Logistics in Celaya. I am calling to follow up on the Logistics Coordinator position. I have reviewed both candidates, and I want to share my thoughts before the final decision. Candidate A, Roberto, is more experienced than Candidate B — he has eight years in logistics, which is two times more than Valeria. He is also available sooner and his salary expectation is lower than hers. Those are important advantages. However, Candidate B, Valeria, is more qualified in terms of education and bilingual skills. Her English is stronger than Roberto's, which is critical for our international accounts. She also has more references than him, and they are all very positive. After thinking about both profiles carefully, I believe Valeria is the stronger fit for our long-term goals. Yes, she is more expensive than Roberto, and she needs two weeks notice. But she ticks all the boxes for what we need. She stands out from all the candidates we have seen this month. My recommendation is Valeria. It is not an easy call, but I think it is the right one. I can send the full comparison report by tomorrow morning.
⚡ Teacher Note — Listening
ElevenLabs: Voice = Sarah, Speed = 0.82, Stability = 0.65, Similarity = 0.80. Before playing: "Count the comparatives — how many can you hear?" (Answer: more experienced, more than, lower than, more qualified, stronger than, more references, more expensive, stronger fit — 8 instances). After listening: "Did Andrea change her recommendation from the written report? What is the same?" — This builds listening-to-reading comparison as a skill. Note: script uses "it is not an easy call" — connect back to §3 colloquial phrase "it's a tough call."

Speaking Practice — Your Hiring Decision
6 guided questions · Pair and group discussion · Opinion Meter · 10–12 min
⏱ 10–12 MIN
T-S Question 1 — Recall
Based on the reading and listening, give 3 reasons why Andrea chose Valeria over Roberto. Use comparatives.
Grammar hint: "She chose Valeria because Valeria is more [adj] than Roberto / has more [noun] than Roberto."
S-S Question 2 — Disagree
Your partner thinks Roberto is the better hire. You disagree — you prefer Valeria. Debate for 90 seconds. Each person must use at least 2 comparatives and 1 superlative.
Grammar hint: "Yes, but Valeria is the most qualified candidate. Her English is stronger than his."
S-S Question 3 — Apply
Think of a real hiring decision at your company (or a role you know well). Compare two types of people who could do that job. Who is the better fit?
Grammar hint: Use "more [adj] than" + "has more [noun] than" + one colloquial phrase from today.
Class Question 4 — Discussion
In your industry, which is more important for a Logistics Coordinator: bilingual skills or years of experience? Defend your answer using comparatives. Then vote as a class.
Grammar hint: "Bilingual skills are more valuable than experience because…" / "Experience is more important than language because…"
T-S Question 5 — Personal
If YOU were the candidate — like Valeria or Roberto — what would be your strongest comparison point against a competitor for your job? What makes you the most [adj] person for your role?
Grammar hint: "I am the most [adj] person in my team because…" / "I have more [noun] than most of my colleagues."
Class Question 6 — Challenge
Class debate: "Experience is always more important than formal education in the workplace." Who agrees? Who disagrees? Each side presents one argument using a superlative and one comparative. 2 minutes.
Grammar hint: "The most important quality in any worker is…" / "Education is more valuable than experience because…"

⚡ Teacher Note — Speaking
Questions 1–2: quick, factual recall to lock the text content. Questions 3–4: real-world application — students should be speaking about their actual company, not the characters. Question 5 is the most personal and highest-value: ask students to think of themselves as candidates. This prepares them for real job interviews in English, which is a direct motivation driver for A2.2 learners in manufacturing and logistics. Question 6 Opinion Meter: let the class see the live percentage — use it as a conversation starter ("Why is it at 65%? Who voted agree?"). Expected errors to collect silently and review after: "more importanter," "the most experienced than," "as experience as."
🎯 This week, use these 3 phrases at work:
Practical English from today — ready to use in meetings, emails or performance reviews.
"[Name] is more experienced than [Name] in [area]."
Use when comparing two colleagues or candidates in a meeting. Replace [area] with: logistics / customer service / project management / operations.
"She ticks all the boxes."
Use when recommending a candidate or colleague who meets every requirement. Sounds professional and confident — much stronger than "she is good for the job."
"It's a tough call, but I think [option] is the stronger choice."
Use when two options are close and you need to make a decision anyway. Shows you considered both sides — the mark of a professional communicator.

Wrap-Up — S2A Complete
Key takeaways · Checklist · My Win Today · Next session preview · 5–8 min
⏱ 5–8 MIN
☐ Adj vs Noun comparison
"She IS more qualified" (adjective after IS). "He HAS more experience" (noun after HAS). Different verbs, different structures — both use "more … than."
☐ Superlatives in HR context
"The most qualified candidate" / "The strongest fit" / "The best choice for long-term growth." Always use THE before a superlative.
☐ Colloquial HR phrases
"Ticks all the boxes" · "Strong fit for the role" · "Stands out from the others" · "Tough call." These phrases make you sound like a native HR professional.

I Can Now...

☐Compare two candidates using adjectives: "more qualified than / more experienced than"
☐Compare using nouns: "has more experience than / has more qualifications than"
☐Use superlatives to identify the best option: "the most qualified / the strongest fit"
☐Use HR colloquial phrases: "ticks all the boxes", "tough call", "stands out from the others"
