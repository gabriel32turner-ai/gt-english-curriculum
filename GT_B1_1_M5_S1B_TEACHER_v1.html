Hot Take — Timed Debate Opener

10 min
Hot Take · Pick a side · Defend it · 90 seconds on the clock
Today's Hot Take
"Workplace stress is always the company's fault — never the individual's responsibility."
✅ Agree
Companies create the conditions: unrealistic deadlines, excessive workload, toxic management. The individual cannot fix a broken system.
✗ Disagree
Individuals have agency: they can set limits, communicate, prioritize, and recover. Blaming only the company removes personal responsibility.
Teacher note — Hot Take setup: Say: "You have 60 seconds to decide your position. Then 90 seconds to defend it. No reading — speak from the head. Ready?" Force commitment before the timer starts — wishy-washy non-positions destroy the energy of this round. If everyone agrees, assign half the class to the opposite side.
T→S Round 1 — Your Hot Take (90 sec)

Each student states their position AND gives one Reported Speech example: "My manager told me that..." or "A colleague said she had..." to support their argument. Vocabulary from S1A must appear.

S↔S Round 2 — Counter-Argument (60 sec)

Pairs: one person argues Agree, the other argues Disagree. Each must report what the other said and then challenge it: "You said the company was responsible — but I think..."

Teacher note — Corrección en tiempo real: During Round 1, listen specifically for: (1) Reported Speech tense shifts ("said she IS" vs. "said she WAS") — the most common error. (2) "Said me" instead of "told me." Write errors anonymously on the board, correct briefly between rounds. Do not interrupt during the 90-second timer.
Energy note: The Hot Take only works if the teacher commits to it too. Take a position yourself. Model the structure: "I agree with this statement, and here is why: my own manager once told me that..." This breaks the teacher-student power dynamic and makes the round feel real.

Pitch / Debate — Build the Case

15 min
Pitch · Make your case with evidence · Reported Speech is your evidence tool
T→S Pitch Round 1 — The Proposal (3 min)
You are presenting to your company's leadership team. Propose ONE concrete change to reduce workplace stress. Use Reported Speech to give evidence: "Employees have told us that... / A survey showed that colleagues said they had..." What is your proposal and why should they approve it?
Language structure: State the problem with evidence → Propose the solution → Predict the impact. Aim for 5–7 sentences minimum.
S↔S Debate Round 2 — The Challenge (2 min)
After hearing the proposal, your partner challenges it: "You said the main problem was the workload — but did you ask employees what they said about the management style?" Partners alternate: propose, challenge, defend. Use reported speech throughout to quote each other back.
Key pattern: "You said... but I think... / My colleague told me... / The data showed..."
Class Debate Round 3 — Best Proposal Vote (2 min)
Each person shares their proposal in one sentence. Class votes on the most convincing. The winner reports back: "The class agreed that my proposal would... They also said..."
This round produces natural Reported Speech in context — reporting what the class just decided.
Teacher note — Pitch Round: The Proposal format is a direct preparation for real workplace scenarios: employee feedback sessions, team meetings, HR conversations. Students who can do this in English at B1.1 are performing at B1.2-adjacent professional level. Flag this to them: "What you just did — in English — is exactly what a team leader does in an international meeting."

Relay — The Stress Report Chain

10 min
Relay · Pass it on · Reported Speech across 4 people
🏃 The Broken Telephone — Professional Edition
Student A
Says original statement
→
Student B
Reports to C
→
Student C
Reports to D
→
Student D
Reports to class

How to run it

Setup: Groups of 4. Student A whispers (or says quietly) a sentence about stress at work.

Relay starts: B reports to C: "She said she had been feeling burned out because the workload had doubled." C reports to D. D reports to the class.

Debrief: A reveals the original. Compare. What changed? What tense shifts happened? Did meaning drift?

Round 2: Rotate. New A. New starting sentence. This time, include a reported question: "She said... and she also asked me if..."
Starter sentences for Round 1 (teacher chooses one)
"My manager told me I need to reduce my workload this month or I will burn out."
"She said the company hadn't done enough to protect employee wellbeing last year."
"He told me he had been feeling so much pressure that he couldn't recover on weekends."
Teacher note — Relay debrief: The most valuable moment is comparing the original sentence with what D reports. Tenses will have shifted further. Information will have been added or lost. This is a real-world demonstration of why precision in Reported Speech matters — made viscerally clear through play. Ask: "What changed? What stayed the same? Why?" This generates 3–5 minutes of high-quality metalinguistic discussion at zero effort.

Speed Round — 30 Seconds Each

10 min
Speed Round · 30 seconds · No pausing · Click Done when complete

Each prompt: 30 seconds, one person, speaking without stopping. Click Done when the student finishes. Complete all 6 prompts to earn the badge. Rotate through the class — no one sits this one out.

Prompt 1
Report what your manager said to you last week — real or invented. Use "She told me that..." at least once.
Prompt 2
Describe your workload right now in 4 sentences. Use: workload, pressure, boundary, and "I try to..."
Prompt 3
What did someone tell you about stress or burnout recently? Report it with a tense shift.
Prompt 4
Give 3 things you do to recover from a stressful week. "First... Second... Third..." Use vocab from S1A.
Prompt 5
Say the colloquial phrase 'I have too much on my plate' — then extend it with a Reported Speech sentence about why.
Prompt 6
Report a company announcement you heard recently — any topic. "The company told us that... They also said..."
Completed: 0 / 6
Teacher note — Speed Round: The 30-second constraint is intentional — it forces students past their internal editor and into fluency mode. Errors are expected and acceptable. After the round, pick ONE error pattern you heard most and address it in 60 seconds: write on the board, correct, move on. Do not correct during the timer.

Solo Challenge — 2-Minute Monologue

15 min
Solo Challenge · 2 minutes · Real story · Reported Speech throughout
🎯 The Challenge Brief
Tell the class about a real (or realistic) stressful situation at work. In 2 minutes, cover:
• What happened (context, workload, pressure)
• What someone said to you about it — reported statement
• What someone asked you — reported question
• What you did to recover or manage
• What you would tell a colleague in the same situation

Use at least 4 vocabulary words from S1A. Use at least one colloquial phrase. No notes.
Teacher Assessment Rubric — Click each criterion when met
Reported Statement
Uses "said / told me" + past tense correctly at least once
Reported Question
Uses "asked me if / what / whether" with correct word order
Vocabulary
4+ S1A words used naturally: workload, burnout, boundary, pressure, recover, prioritize
Colloquial
Uses at least 1 natural phrase: "too much on my plate," "disconnect," "hard to..."
Fluency
Speaks for 90+ seconds without long pauses or switching to Spanish
Connectors
Uses linking language: "She also told me... / Then she asked... / Because of that..."
Teacher note — Solo Challenge: This is the session's performance moment. Give 2 minutes of silent preparation before starting. While the student speaks, use the rubric to track — do not write visible notes (it creates anxiety). After the student finishes, give ONE specific piece of positive feedback and ONE specific improvement. "I noticed you said 'she told me she IS stressed' — remember the tense shift: 'she WAS stressed.' Everything else was excellent, especially your use of 'boundary.'"
Upsell / retention: After the best monologue of the class, say: "That is exactly the level you are working toward. That sentence — right there — you just said that in English without notes. That is B1.1 production." Name the achievement out loud. Students need to hear that they have crossed a threshold — it is the single most powerful retention driver you have.
