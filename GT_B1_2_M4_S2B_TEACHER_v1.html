⚡ Hot Take  ·  5 min

Gender Roles — Where Do You Stand?

“If gender roles truly disappeared from the workplace, men would lose as much as women would gain.”
You voted. Justify your position in 30 seconds using Second Conditional.
This Hot Take is designed to be genuinely ambiguous — there is no “right” answer. Men who feel constrained by expectations (e.g. “must be strong, must provide”) may choose Agree. Those who feel men have structural advantages may choose Disagree. Both positions produce rich Second Conditional language. Let the room argue. Do not resolve it — move on at 5 minutes.
🗣 Pitch Round  ·  10 min

The Gender Policy Debate

Your company is considering a new gender equity policy. Each pair argues one position. 2 min prep — 2 min pitch — group votes.

POSITION A — FOR QUOTAS
If the company did not implement gender quotas in hiring and promotions, the current leadership imbalance would never change. Structural problems need structural solutions. Build the case for mandatory targets.
Must use: equality, discrimination, promote, Second Conditional
POSITION B — AGAINST QUOTAS
If the company hired based on merit alone, and if biases were removed from processes, quotas would be unnecessary and could even undermine confidence in women who are promoted. Make the meritocracy argument.
Must use: bias, stereotype, challenge, Second Conditional
POSITION C — CULTURE FIRST
If a company changed its daily culture — how meetings run, who interrupts whom, whose ideas get credit — formal policies would be less necessary. You believe culture is the root problem.
Must use: norm, evolve, expectation, Second Conditional
POSITION D — INDIVIDUAL RESPONSIBILITY
You believe that if each person — regardless of gender — took personal responsibility to challenge biased thinking in their immediate team, the collective result would be faster than any top-down policy.
Must use: identity, progress, question, Second Conditional
PREP TIME
PITCH TIME
Position C (Culture First) is the most nuanced and often the most convincing. Position D (Individual Responsibility) may provoke disagreement from the group — this is intentional. After all pitches, open a 2-minute free discussion: “Which argument changed your mind?” Peer challenge drives language production.
🏃 Relay  ·  8 min

Opinion Spectrum Relay

Each learner places themselves on the spectrum for ONE of the statements below — then justifies their position in 45 seconds using Second Conditional. The next learner must agree, disagree, or nuance the previous person’s point before stating their own.

Strongly Disagree Neutral Strongly Agree
Choose one — place yourself, then justify:

◆ “If men were given equal paternity leave, gender roles at home would change faster than any workplace policy.”
◆ “If women didn’t have to prove themselves twice as hard to get the same recognition, the gender pay gap would close within a decade.”
◆ “If I personally challenged one gender bias at my workplace this month, it would make a measurable difference.”
The Opinion Spectrum Relay is the S2B unique format — different from S1B’s story relay. Each session’s relay has its own visual logic. This one drives consecutive, connected speaking: learner 2 must reference learner 1 before taking their own position. This mirrors real professional discourse. The third statement is the most personal and often generates the most language — save it for the last round if possible.
⚡ Speed Round  ·  6 min

30 Seconds — Gender Roles Edition

Pick a card. 30 seconds on the timer. Click when done.

Name 3 gender stereotypes you’ve observed in a workplace — real ones, not textbook examples.
Be honest. Use: stereotype, norm, expectation
If you were promoted today and your team was surprised because of your gender, how would you respond?
Use Second Conditional + professional language
What’s the difference between “gender equality” and “gender equity”? Define both in English.
Try: equality = same rules / equity = fair outcomes
If your company announced a gender pay audit tomorrow, would you be surprised by the results? Why?
Use could/might + honest reasoning
Someone says: “Gender roles exist because they’re natural.” Challenge this in 30 seconds.
Use: evolve, challenge, It’s a red flag…
If you had to mentor a younger colleague on navigating gender bias at work, what would be your ONE piece of advice?
Use Second Conditional + wisdom from the session
30s SPEED TIMER
Card 3 (equality vs equity) is a vocabulary-precision card — many B1.2 learners conflate these. Card 5 uses “It’s a red flag” from M4 colloquial phrases — reward learners who use it. Card 6 (mentoring advice) is the most emotionally intelligent card — it produces personal, original language. Assign it to the learner who has been quietest in the session.
🌟 Solo Challenge  ·  8 min

The Interview

Your Scenario
You are being interviewed for a senior management position at a multinational company. The interviewer asks: “Our company is committed to gender equity. Can you tell us about a time you challenged gender bias in a professional context — and what you would do if you encountered it in a leadership role?”

You have 90 seconds to answer as if in a real interview. Use professional language, session vocabulary, and Second Conditional for the hypothetical part.
✅ Address both parts of the question (past experience + hypothetical)
✅ Use Second Conditional for the hypothetical section
✅ Use at least 3 vocabulary words from S2A
✅ Keep a professional interview register — no slang
❌ No reading from notes
90s SOLO TIMER
The “interview” format is a GT favourite for real-world language transfer. The language produced here is directly usable in actual professional English interviews.
💰 After the Solo Challenge, say this: “Everything you just said, you can say in a real interview with an international company. That’s not practice English — that’s real English.” Then mention: the B2.1 module covers interview preparation, salary negotiation, and formal email writing. Plant the seed for the next investment.
