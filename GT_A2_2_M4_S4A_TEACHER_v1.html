§1
What's in Your Calendar?
Confirmed vs planned · Activate the contrast · Real schedules in context
⏱ 5 MIN
✋ Open your real calendar. Look at next week. What's already in it — meetings, calls, visits? And what's something you're planning to do but haven't scheduled yet?
💬 Partner activity: Tell your partner 2 things that are in your calendar (confirmed) and 2 things you are planning to do but haven't arranged yet. Notice: do you use different words for each?
🧠 Quick think: "I'm going to call the supplier" vs "I'm calling the supplier at 10." Are these different? Which one is in your calendar? Which is just your plan?
⚡ Teacher note: S4 targets the Going to vs Present Continuous contrast — the subtlest distinction in A2.2. Both refer to the future, but: Present Continuous = confirmed arrangement (booked, calendar, ticket); Going to = personal intention (plan, decision, decision made). Collect student sentences on board for §5. Don't correct yet.
⚡ Quick Win — 20 seconds
Say out loud: "Tomorrow I'm [verb]-ing at [time]" (something in your calendar) AND "I'm going to [verb] [something] this week" (a plan not yet arranged). Two sentences. Go!
1:00
The Going to vs Present Continuous contrast is the most nuanced future form distinction at A2.2. The key insight: both refer to future events, but the grammar reflects the speaker's relationship with the event. Present Continuous signals external commitment — someone else is involved, a booking was made, a calendar invite was sent. Going to signals internal decision — you decided, you planned, you intend. Neither is "more correct" — they reflect different realities.
Ask students to actually open their phone calendar right now. Real data beats hypothetical scenarios. Someone will have a dentist appointment (confirmed → present continuous) and a plan to organize their desk (intention → going to). Use their real examples throughout §5.
This form distinction matters in professional emails: "I'm meeting you at 3 PM" signals preparation and commitment. "I'm going to try to meet you" sounds tentative. Teaching students to upgrade their language from "going to" to present continuous for confirmed events is a quick win they can apply this week.

3 speaking rounds · Calendar vs intention · Real professional context
⏱ 8 MIN
Andrea Soto's situation — Monday morning
Andrea has just returned from Guadalajara. Her manager asks: "What does your week look like?" Some things are in her calendar — others are just plans she intends to do. How does she communicate the difference?
Focus: Present Continuous = booked, calendar, external · Going to = intention, personal plan · Both refer to the future, but one has a ticket and one doesn't

Round 2 — What are you planning to do?
Tell your partner 3 things you intend to do this week — tasks you decided to tackle, not yet booked with anyone else. Use "I'm going to [verb]…"

Round 3 — Mix both: the full weekly briefing
Give your partner a 60-second Monday briefing mixing both forms. Partner listens and marks every time you use each form correctly. Can they tell which is which?

§4
Calendar English — How Professionals Talk About Plans
5 everyday phrases for scheduling and planning · Instantly usable at work
⏱ 5 MIN
"My calendar is packed."
= I have no free time this week
"Can we meet Tuesday?" — "Sorry, my calendar is packed. How about next week?"
"Let me check my availability."
= I need to look at my calendar before confirming
"Are you free Thursday?" — "Let me check my availability and get back to you."
"Can we find a slot?"
= Can we find a mutual free time?
"We need to discuss the proposal. Can we find a slot this week?"
"I have a conflict."
= I have two things at the same time
"The 2 PM works." — "Actually, I have a conflict then — I'm presenting to the board."
"Put it in the calendar."
= Schedule it officially — make it a confirmed arrangement
"So Wednesday at 3 PM?" — "Perfect. Put it in the calendar and I'll send a confirmation."
"Put it in the calendar" is the grammar trigger — once something is in the calendar, you use present continuous. Before it's in the calendar, going to. This real-world logic helps students remember the form distinction. Run mini role-plays: Student A proposes a meeting, Student B checks availability, they agree, and then one of them says "I'm putting it in the calendar now." At that moment, the grammar flips from "going to meet" to "we're meeting on [day]."

GT Original — Going To vs Present Continuous: The Professional Upgrade
  • 💼 Register insight: "I'm meeting you at 3 PM" signals preparation and commitment. "I'm going to try to meet you" sounds tentative. Help students upgrade confirmed events to present continuous in professional emails.
  • ⚠️ The calendar rule (easy to remember): If it's in the calendar → use present continuous. If it's only in your head → use going to. This maps directly to how students already think about their schedule.
  • 🌍 Cultural note: In US/UK business culture, sending a calendar invite is a commitment — it's the professional equivalent of confirming with a handshake. "I'm sending you a calendar invite" = this is now a confirmed arrangement.
  • 🔊 Pronunciation: "going to" /ˈɡoʊɪŋ tə/ reduces to "gonna" /ˈɡɒnə/ in casual speech. Teach students to recognize "gonna" in listening but produce "going to" in professional writing.

Weekly Briefing — What's Confirmed, What's Planned

Andrea's Monday Briefing — Confirmed vs Planned
A2.2 · M4 · S4A · Voice: Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.82
Good morning, Carlos. Here's my week at a glance.

Monday — that's today — I'm presenting the updated supplier analysis at 9 AM. The team is already in the meeting room. After that, I'm going to reorganize my project folders. I need to do that before end of week.

Tuesday I'm speaking with the Chicago office at 10. That call is confirmed and on the agenda. I'm also going to start the draft for the Q4 communication plan — just me, no deadline yet, but I want to have something by Thursday.

So: four confirmed arrangements and a few personal intentions mixed in. That's the week. Any questions?

Session Wrap-Up
3 key ideas · Checklist · Phrases for this week · My Win
⏱ 5 MIN
01
Calendar = Present Continuous. "I'm meeting the client at 10" — the invite is sent, they're expecting you. This is a confirmed arrangement, not just a plan.
02
Plan = Going To. "I'm going to write the report" — your decision, your task, no one else is waiting for you at a specific time.
03
Same event, different moment. "I'm going to call the client" → then you book it → "I'm calling the client at 3 PM." Grammar reflects reality.

🎯 This week at work
3 Phrases You Can Use Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"I'm [verb]-ing on [day] at [time] — it's confirmed."
Replace "I'm going to meet" with "I'm meeting" in any professional email or standup where the event is in your calendar. Instant upgrade.
PHRASE 2
"I'm going to [verb] [task] this week."
Use for personal tasks and solo plans you intend to complete. More professional than "I will try" — going to signals a real decision was made.
PHRASE 3
"Let me check my availability and get back to you."
The professional way to respond to any meeting request before confirming. Use it this week instead of "I don't know" or "maybe."
