Your supplier sent the wrong products on Tuesday. Today is Thursday. You need to notify your manager and the supplier. Write one email — choose the correct type, use "due to," an apology phrase, and 1 Wh-question. 3 minutes.
Type: Apology. "Due to an error in the delivery…" / "I regret to inform you…" / "Could you tell me when the correct items will arrive?"
"Subject: Delivery Error — Apology & Request. Dear Mr. Vega, I'm writing to inform you that the delivery received on Tuesday contained the wrong products, due to a packing error. I regret the inconvenience this causes our production schedule. Could you tell me when the correct items will be delivered? Also, could you confirm whether you will collect the incorrect items? I'll keep you posted once we have clarity on our end. Best regards, [Name] · Purchasing Coordinator · Solaris Manufacturing."
S-S · Situation B → C Chain
Partner A: Write a request email asking your supplier for an updated price list for Q4 — include 2 Wh-questions and 2 connectors. Partner B: Write the confirmation reply — all 4 connectors + "Please find attached." Exchange and read aloud. Total: 5 minutes.
A = Request. B = Confirmation. This is the full email chain from M5.
Request (A): Subject clear ✓ / "I'm writing to request…" ✓ / "Could you…?" ×2 ✓ / Connectors ×2 ✓ / "Thank you in advance" ✓ / Sign-off with title ✓. Confirmation (B): "I'm writing to confirm…" ✓ / First/Then/Also/Finally ✓ / "Please find attached" ✓ / "Let me know if…" ✓ / Sign-off with title ✓.
Class · Type Identification & Defense
Teacher reads 3 short email extracts — class votes on the email type. Then: 2 students defend their answer — "I chose [type] because the opening says X and the purpose is Y." Challenge: can anyone find an extract that could be 2 types?
Debate: a delivery delay email — is it apology OR request? (It can be both — discuss the primary purpose.)
The dual-purpose email insight: "I'm writing to apologize for the delay and to request a new delivery date." — This is both an apology AND a request. In professional English, you can combine types. The key is that the PRIMARY purpose determines the opening line and the subject line. The secondary purpose goes in the body. This is A1.2-level nuance — reward any student who identifies this.
TEACHER NOTE — S4B Integrated Production

Round 1 forces type identification under time pressure — the most important skill for S5. Round 2 is the most complex writing task of the S_B series: a request-confirmation chain where both emails must be coherent with each other. Walk the room during pair work. The class discussion (Round 3) is the S4B intellectual peak — a delivery delay email is genuinely dual-purpose, and recognizing this is the difference between A1.2 and B1 thinking. Celebrate the insight.

Activity A — Error Correction: M5 Full System

Each error comes from a different session of M5. Identify and correct.

S1A error — email structure

1. "Hi — I am writing to confirm the meeting on Friday at 10 AM. Regards."

✅ Both issues are present, but the primary error is the greeting. "Hi" is semi-formal — for a formal confirmation email to a client, use "Dear Mr./Ms. [Name]." AND the sign-off is incomplete — always include your name and title.

S2A error — phone/object pronoun

2. "I'll ask to her to call you back before noon."

✅ "ask her" — object pronoun directly after the verb, no "to." Pattern: ask + [object pronoun] + to + verb. "I'll ask HER to call you back."

S3A error — connectors

3. "First, please send the invoice. Also, please confirm the delivery date. Then, please let us know the total weight. Finally, sign the document."

✅ Steps 1→2→3→4 should use: First → Then → Then (or Also for extra info) → Finally. "Also" signals additional information at any point — not step 2 in a sequence. Correct: First / Then / Then / Finally OR First / Then / Also (extra info) / Finally.

S4A error — apology language

4. "I'm sorry for the inconvenient. The delivery is delayed because a transport problem."

✅ Two errors: (1) "inconvenient" is an adjective — the noun is "inconvenience." Fixed phrase: "I'm sorry for the inconvenIENCE." (2) "because + noun" is wrong — use "due to + noun" OR "because of + noun." "Due to a transport problem" is the most professional form.

S4A error — embedded Wh-question

5. "Could you tell me when will the new products arrive?"

✅ Inside "Could you tell me…" there is NO question inversion. The embedded question uses normal word order: subject + verb. "…when the new products WILL arrive" — NOT "when WILL the products arrive."
🗣️ MICRO-SPEAKING
From error 4 and 5: say the corrected sentences aloud 2 times each — fast. "I'm sorry for the inconvenience. Due to a transport problem…" + "Could you tell me when the new products will arrive?" These are your S5 target sentences.

Activity B — Complete Apology Email: Put in Order

This apology email uses structures from S1A, S3A, and S4A. Drag into correct order.

  • ⠿I'll keep you posted as soon as I have a confirmed new delivery date. Don't hesitate to contact me if you need more information.
  • ⠿Best regards, Mónica Herrera · Sales Manager · ConnectPro International · Tel: +52 33 1234 5678
  • ⠿Delivery Delay — Apology and Update
  • ⠿First, please note that the new estimated date is Thursday, June 5th. Then, the driver will contact you 30 minutes before arrival. Could you confirm if Thursday works for your team?
  • ⠿Dear Ing. Torres, I'm writing to inform you that the delivery scheduled for Tuesday, June 3rd is delayed due to a transport problem in Guadalajara. I regret any inconvenience this causes.
GT ORIGINAL — The S5-Ready Email

Once the drag activity is confirmed correct, point out which structure each part comes from: Subject (S1A) → "I'm writing to inform you" (S4A) → "due to" (S4A) → "I regret" (S4A) → "First / Then" (S3A) → "Could you…?" (S2A) → "I'll keep you posted / Don't hesitate" (S4A+S3A) → Sign-off (S1A). This email IS the S5 exit task template. Tell students: "Tomorrow in S5, you will write something very close to this — from scratch, in 5 minutes. Today's practice is your S5 preparation."

Listening — Full M5 Scenario: 3 Email Types in Context
Mónica Herrera · ConnectPro International · Guadalajara
8–10 min
M5 Full Scenario — ConnectPro International, Guadalajara
A1.2 · M5 · S4B · Voice: Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.80
ElevenLabs Script — ~168 words · ~63 seconds at 0.80 · All 3 email types

Hello. I'm Mónica Herrera from ConnectPro International. Today I want to share three short emails — one of each type from Module 5. Listen carefully and identify each type as you hear it.

Email 1 — Confirmation: Subject: Meeting Confirmation — Thursday, June 5th. Dear Mr. Park, I'm writing to confirm our meeting on Thursday, June 5th at 2 PM at our Guadalajara office. Please confirm your attendance by Wednesday. I look forward to seeing you. Best regards, Mónica.

Email 2 — Request: Subject: Request for Q3 Product Catalogue. Dear Ms. Chen, I'm writing to request your updated product catalogue for Q3. Could you send it to us as soon as possible? Also, could you confirm your new pricing? Thank you in advance. Best regards, Mónica.

Email 3 — Apology: Subject: Delivery Delay — Apology. Dear Ing. Torres, I'm writing to inform you that the delivery scheduled for Friday is delayed due to a logistics issue. I regret any inconvenience. The new date is Monday at 8 AM. I'll keep you posted. Best regards, Mónica.

Comprehension — Identify + Analyse

1. In Email 1, Mónica uses "I look forward to seeing you." What type of email is this?

✅ Confirmation — opener: "I'm writing to confirm…" / closing: "I look forward to seeing you."

2. In Email 2, Mónica asks two questions. Both use "Could you…?"

✅ "Could you send it to us?" + "Could you confirm your new pricing?" — both polite requests.

3. In Email 3, the delay is due to a meeting conflict.

"Due to a logistics issue" — not a meeting conflict. Delivery delays use logistics/transport/supply chain language.

4. Which email includes "I'll keep you posted"?

✅ Email 3 (Apology): "I'll keep you posted." — most common in bad news emails where the situation is still developing.

5. All three emails end with "Best regards, Mónica." — without her title or company.

✅ The listening script simplified the sign-off for clarity — but in a real professional email, always include: Name · Title · Company · Phone. Without title, the recipient doesn't know your authority level.
🗣️ MICRO-SPEAKING
From memory: Say the 3 opening lines — one for each email type Mónica used. "I'm writing to confirm…" / "I'm writing to request…" / "I'm writing to inform you that…" — Say all 3 without stopping.
TEACHER NOTE — Listening S4B — Integration Analysis

After Q5 discussion about the sign-off, play the listening one more time. Students listen ONLY for the opening line of each email. After: "You just heard three different opening lines — three different purposes — three professional emails — all from Mónica Herrera in the same week. This is what Module 5 enables. You can do this." Badge b4 when all 5 comprehension items are correctly answered.

TEACHER NOTE — Speaking Practice S4B

Q2 (S5 simulation) is the most important activity of the entire S_B series. The 5-minute email is essentially the S5 exit task under practice conditions. After peer review (Q3), collect the 3 most common errors from the class and address them in Q4. This targeted correction is more valuable than any pre-planned grammar explanation. Q5 (phone + email chain) integrates S2B and S4B — the most complex single task of M5. If only one pair completes it fully correctly, celebrate them — it's a high bar. Q6 (oral production only) removes the safety net of writing. Students who struggle here need one more pass through the M5 grammar map before S5.

🎯 M5 Complete — Your 3 Essential Phrases
"I'm writing to [confirm / request / inform you that]…"
Three words that change — everything else in the opening is the same. Master this pattern and you master all 3 email types. Use it this week.
"Due to [reason], I regret to inform you…"
The professional bad-news opener. Used by every international company in manufacturing, logistics, and finance. Your contacts already know this phrase — now you do too.
"I'll keep you posted."
Works in email AND on the phone. Promise of updates = reliability. Use it after any situation that is still developing — delivery, decision, project update.
