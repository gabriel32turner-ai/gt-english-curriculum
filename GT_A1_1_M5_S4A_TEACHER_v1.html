Real skills + preferences in a work context · Integrated production

Teacher Note — Speaking Setup

Context: Sofia Reyes is a new coordinator at Lumina Finance in Querétaro. She's meeting her team for the first time and talking about her skills and preferences. Students answer as themselves. Layer 1: get answers. Layer 2: check can + base verb (no "to"). Layer 3: push for 2-sentence answers — skill + example.

T–S
Sofia's manager asks: "What can you do well at work?"
Answer for yourself. Give 2 things you can do and 1 thing you can't do yet.

"I can use Excel and I can write reports in Spanish. But I can't speak English perfectly yet — I'm working on it!"

Check: can + base verb (no "to") · can't for negatives · 1 positive, 1 negative minimum

S–S
Ask your partner: "What do you like at work — the meetings, the lunch break, or something else?"
Use: "I like ___." or "I don't like ___ — I prefer ___." NO gerunds. Nouns only.

"I like the lunch break and I like coffee in the morning. I don't like long meetings — I prefer short meetings!"

Common error: "I like eating lunch" → ✗ "I like lunch" → ✓ (No gerund at A1.1)

Class
Class survey: "What do you need for your job today — right now, this week?"
Each student says 1 thing. Teacher writes on board. Count which need is most common. Use: "I need a ___ / I need ___."

"I need a charger for my laptop. I need paper for the meeting. She needs a pen. We all need coffee!"

Focus: need + noun (NOT "I need to have" — keep it simple at A1.1)

§4
Colloquial · Real English for Real Situations
4 phrases you'll use this week · Cafeteria, requests, preferences
5 min
"Sure, no problem!"
When someone asks "Can you help me?" or "Can you send this?" — this is the natural, professional American English response. "Yes, I can" is grammatically correct but sounds stiff.
A: "Can you print this for me?" B: "Sure, no problem!"
"I'm not really into [coffee]."
Fixed chunk for saying you don't like something — without saying "I don't like" (which can sound blunt). More natural in conversation. Always: "I'm not really into" + NOUN.
"Do you want a soda?" — "I'm not really into soda, thanks."
"Can I get a...?"
American English informal for ordering. More natural than "I'd like..." in casual settings (cafeteria, coffee shop). Both are correct — "Can I get a" is very common in US-influenced business environments.
"Can I get a coffee and a sandwich, please?"
"Anything else?" / "That's all, thanks."
The natural closing pair for any order or request situation. "Anything else?" = ¿Algo más? "That's all, thanks." = Eso es todo, gracias. Used in cafeterias, reception, stores — everywhere.
"Can I get a water?" — "Anything else?" — "That's all, thanks."
Teacher Note — Colloquial Drill

Drill sequence (2 min): Teacher plays the customer, students play the server. Say: "I'd like a coffee and a salad." Student responds: "Anything else?" Teacher: "That's all, thanks." Rotate 3-4 times. Then switch. Next step: students add "I'm not really into [X] — can I get [Y] instead?" to make it a real conversation.

Teacher Note — The Critical Errors

Error 1: "I can to speak" → ✅ "I can speak" — modals NEVER take "to" (can, can't, will, would, should, must). Elicit the rule from students: "After 'can', what form of the verb do we use?" Error 2: "I like drinking coffee" → ✅ "I like coffee" — at A1.1, only NOUNS after like. The gerund is A2.1. Write it on the board: "like + NOUN = ✓ | like + verb-ing = A2.1 (not today)". Error 3: "She need a pen" → ✅ "She needs a pen" — 3rd person -s rule from M2, it STILL applies here.

Listening · First Day at Lumina Finance
Sofia talks about her skills, likes and needs · Comprehension + True/False
8–10 min
"First Day at Lumina" — Lumina Finance, Querétaro
A1.1 · M5 · S4A · Voice: Sarah (AmE) · Speed: 0.78

Hi! My name is Sofia Reyes. Today is my first day at Lumina Finance here in Querétaro, and I want to tell you a little about myself.

I can speak English and Spanish — that's why I have this job. I can also use Excel and Word, and I can write reports in both languages. But I can't design presentations — that's not really my skill. I need to learn Photoshop, too. Maybe next year!

For lunch, I like salad and fruit. I don't like fast food — I'm not really into it. I want a coffee right now, actually. The office has a nice cafeteria on the second floor. I'd like a black coffee and a sandwich, please. That's all, thanks!

For my desk, I need a charger — my laptop battery is almost empty — and I need a notebook for meetings. My manager says I also need a folder for the client documents. Can you help me find one? Sure, no problem? Great. I like this team already!

Teacher Note — Opinion Meter

Start with the meter. Ask 3–4 students to justify their vote in English. Prompt: "Why do you agree/disagree? Can you give me ONE reason?" Expect: "Because I need English for my job." Correct to: "Because I need English for my job" — not "because I need the English." Accept very simple justifications — this is A1.1.
