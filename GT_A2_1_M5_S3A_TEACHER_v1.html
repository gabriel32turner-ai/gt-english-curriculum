A2.1 · Module 5 · Session 3A — Pivot Session
Will vs Going To:
The Complete Contrast
This is the pivot session of A2.1. Students have mastered going to (M4) and will (M5 S1A–S2A). Today both systems come together for mastery-level decision-making: pre-planned vs spontaneous, evidence vs opinion, commitment vs intention.

§1
Warm-Up — Will or Going To?
Instinctive classification before the rule · Activate both systems · Surface student confusion zones
⏱ 5–8 MIN
Layer 1 — Activation: Students have both systems in their heads. The goal of this warm-up is to make them choose instinctively — before any rule review. Write their answers on the board. Expect mix-ups. Celebrate the confusion: "That confusion is exactly why we have this session."
📅 Situation A: It is Monday morning. You arrive at the office. Your colleague asks: "What are your plans for today?" You have a full calendar. You say: "I _____ meet with the finance team at 10." Which word? Why?
⚡ Situation B: In the same meeting, your boss suddenly says: "Can anyone send this report to the client today?" You decide right now — nobody else is moving. You say: "I _____ send it." Which word? Why?
🌦 Situation C: You look out the window. The sky is completely dark, it's getting cold, and the forecast said rain. You tell a colleague: "It _____ rain this afternoon." Which word? Why?
⚡ Teacher note: Expected instinctive answers — A: going to (planned); B: will (spontaneous); C: going to (evidence). Situation D below is opinion-based. Don't confirm or deny yet — let §3 confirm. The discomfort here IS the pedagogy.
💭 Situation D: No evidence, no plan — just your gut feeling. You think the new project will succeed. You tell your team: "I think it _____ go well." Which word? Why?
Quick Win — 90 seconds
Tell your partner: one thing you are going to do this week (planned) and one thing you think will happen (opinion). Two sentences. Use both forms correctly.
90

§3
Grammar — The Complete Contrast System
6-function master table · Error correction · Multiple choice · Drag-and-drop classification
⏱ 18–22 MIN
Layer 1 — This is the heart of the session. The table below is the complete A2.1 contrast reference. Walk students through each row. Emphasize: the question is not "is this about the future?" — both are. The question is always: "When was the decision made / what kind of prediction is this?"
Will — Cyan System
Spontaneous decision (at the moment)
Offer ("I'll help you with that")
Promise ("I'll send it by Friday")
Opinion prediction ("I think it'll be fine")
Going To — Gold System
Pre-planned decision (decided before now)
Strong intention ("I'm going to resign")
Evidence-based prediction ("Look — it's going to rain")
Scheduled/arranged future event
Function Form Signal Words Business Example
Pre-planned decision going to already, scheduled, calendar, decided earlier, plan "We're going to launch in Q3 — it's been planned for months."
Spontaneous decision will / 'll suddenly, right now, at this moment, just decided, OK I'll... "The supplier cancelled? I'll call them immediately."
Offer will / 'll Shall I...? I'll... (no signal needed — context is an offer) "That looks heavy — I'll carry it for you."
Promise / Commitment will / 'll promise, guarantee, I assure you, definitely "I promise I'll have the report on your desk by 9 AM."
Evidence-based prediction going to Look at..., those numbers show, I can see, clearly, obviously "Look at those sales figures — it's going to be a record quarter."
Opinion-based prediction will / 'll I think, I believe, probably, I expect, I doubt, maybe "I think the new product will perform well in the market."
Layer 2 — Error Correction
These are the most common A2.1 errors. Read each aloud. Ask students: "What is wrong?" before revealing the correction. Make students explain the rule, not just the fix.
Error 1: "I will call you tomorrow — I have it in my calendar."
Why wrong: The decision was already planned → must use going to.
Correction: "I'm going to call you tomorrow — I have it in my calendar."
Error 2: "Are you going to help? The printer just broke!"
Why wrong: Spontaneous reaction to a new situation → must use will (offer/decision).
Correction: "Will you help?" or "I'll help — let me check it."
Error 3: "Look at those dark clouds — it will rain."
Why wrong: Visible evidence is present right now → going to, not will.
Correction: "Look at those dark clouds — it's going to rain."
Error 4: "I think the project is going to succeed — but I have no data, just a feeling."
Why wrong: Opinion prediction without evidence → will, not going to.
Correction: "I think the project will succeed."
Layer 2 — Multiple Choice Poll
You are in a meeting. A colleague says: "Nobody has booked a room for Friday's review." You decide this second that you will do it. What do you say?
B is correct — spontaneous decision made at this exact moment = will ('ll).
Your manager shows you a graph: costs are rising sharply month by month. She says: "Look at this trend." What is the natural response?
B is correct — "Look at this" signals visible evidence = going to (evidence-based prediction).

§4
Controlled Practice — 8 Context Exercises
Choose will or going to · Explain why · Fix errors · Identify the function
⏱ 15–18 MIN
Layer 1 — For each item, students must (1) choose the form, (2) say the function aloud: "This is a [spontaneous decision / promise / evidence prediction / etc.]." The function label is as important as the correct form.
1. Your calendar shows: "10:00 — client call with Torres." You tell your team: "I _____ speak to Torres at 10 — it's in the calendar."
Correct: am going to speak — pre-planned, scheduled decision.
2. Your colleague looks stressed and can't find a document. Without thinking, you decide to help right now. You say: "Don't worry — I _____ find it for you."
Correct: will find — spontaneous offer made at this exact moment.
3. You look at the project timeline and see every deadline has been missed. You tell your manager: "At this rate, we _____ miss the final deadline too."
Correct: are going to miss — evidence-based prediction (visible data supports it).
4. A client asks: "Do you think the economy will improve?" You have no data — just an optimistic feeling. You say: "Yes, I think things _____ get better."
Correct: will get — opinion-based prediction with "I think" as the signal.
5. You made a commitment in last week's meeting. Now you confirm it: "As I said last Tuesday, I _____ send the full proposal by end of this week."
Correct: will send — reconfirming a promise/commitment = will. (Note: going to is also acceptable here — both work, but will is more natural for promises.)
6. The company announced a restructuring plan three months ago. A journalist asks you about it. You say: "Yes, we _____ restructure the sales department in April."
Correct: are going to restructure — the plan was decided and announced months ago = pre-planned intention.
Layer 2 — Fix the Error
7. Error: "I will prepare the agenda before the meeting — I planned it yesterday." Fix it:
Correct: "I am going to prepare the agenda" — planned yesterday = pre-planned decision = going to.
Layer 3 — Identify the Function
8. "The technician just called — the system is down. OK, I'll handle the client communication." What function is this use of will?
Correct: Spontaneous decision — the trigger (system down) just happened; the decision is formed at this moment.
§5
Listening — Ramón's Monday Morning
Single-speaker monologue · Will vs going to in real time · 4 MCQ + 4 True/False
⏱ 18–22 MIN
Layer 1 — Pre-listening task: tell students "You will hear a manager describe his Monday morning. Count how many times he uses going to and how many times he uses will. Do NOT write them down — just count." First listen = global comprehension. Second listen = analysis.
Ramón Castillo — Operations Director
Sarah AmE voice · 0.82× speed · ~180–200 words · Single speaker
ElevenLabs TTS — Sarah (American English) · Rate: 0.82 · Generate via ElevenLabs dashboard before session
"It's Monday morning and everything is happening at once. Let me walk you through it. I was already going to present the Q3 budget this afternoon — that's been scheduled for two weeks. But then Ana called: the main supplier cancelled. So right now, in this moment, I've decided — I'll handle it personally. I'll call their director immediately. I'm also going to meet with the logistics team at 3 PM — that's been on the calendar. But the client from Guadalajara just sent a message: they need a response today. I'll write it now. I'll make sure they feel supported. We were going to launch the new pricing model next Monday — that's confirmed. But I just realized we need one more review. I'll organize an emergency call this afternoon. So: going to is for everything I planned. Will is for everything I'm deciding right now, as I speak. Two systems. One morning. And I promise — it'll all be under control by five o'clock."
Layer 2 — After listening: ask "How many going to? How many will?" Answer: going to = 3 planned items (budget presentation, logistics meeting, pricing launch); will = 5 spontaneous/promise items (handle it, call director, write message, support client, organize call). The final "it'll" is a promise.
Comprehension — Multiple Choice (4 Questions)
Q1. What was Ramón already going to do this afternoon?
C — "I was already going to present the Q3 budget this afternoon — that's been scheduled for two weeks."
Q2. Why does Ramón decide to call the supplier director?
B — "Ana called: the main supplier cancelled. So right now... I'll handle it personally. I'll call their director immediately."
Q3. When was the new pricing model going to launch?
B — "We were going to launch the new pricing model next Monday — that's confirmed."
Q4. What does Ramón promise at the end?
C — "And I promise — it'll all be under control by five o'clock."
True / False (4 Statements)
Layer 2 — For each TF, ask students to find the exact line in the script that proves or disproves the statement. This is a precision comprehension task.
TF1: "The Q3 budget presentation was a spontaneous decision."
TF2: "Ramón decides to handle the supplier issue himself."
TF3: "The pricing model launch was already scheduled."
TF4: "The logistics meeting at 3 PM was arranged this morning."
TF answers: TF1 = FALSE (it was scheduled for two weeks — planned); TF2 = TRUE ("I'll handle it personally"); TF3 = TRUE ("that's confirmed"); TF4 = FALSE ("that's been on the calendar" — pre-arranged, not this morning).
§6
Role-Play — Plan + React
3 rounds · Solo narration · Pair contrast · Full 2-minute scenario
⏱ 15–18 MIN
Layer 1 — This is structured output. Students are not free-talking — each round has a constraint. In Round 1, they must produce going to only. In Round 2, they must produce will only. In Round 3, they must produce both in one narrative. Monitor for cross-contamination errors.
Round 1 — Solo · 90 sec · Going To Only
Narrate your own Monday morning — but only use going to. Describe at least 3 planned actions. Use time signals: "At 9 I'm going to...", "After lunch I'm going to...", "This afternoon we're going to..."
Focus: pre-planned decisions, scheduled meetings, confirmed intentions. No will allowed in this round!
Round 2 — Pairs · 2 min · Spontaneous Reactions
Student A presents a problem (e.g., "The system just crashed", "The client wants a meeting in 10 minutes", "We're out of coffee"). Student B reacts spontaneously using will: an offer, a decision, or a reassurance. Then swap roles.
Student A: create realistic workplace surprises. Student B: react with will — no thinking time allowed!
Round 3 — Full Scenario · 2 min · Both Systems
You are a project manager. Narrate a project update that includes: (1) what you planned to do this week (going to), (2) what you are deciding right now because of a new problem (will), (3) a prediction based on evidence (going to), and (4) a promise to your team (will).
Aim for 6–8 sentences. Switch between will and going to naturally. Your partner listens and counts each form used correctly.
Model Answer — Round 3
"OK, so here's the update. We were going to finalize the budget proposal on Thursday — that was on the project plan. We were also going to send the client a full report by Friday. But this morning I got an email: the client moved the deadline to Wednesday. So I've decided — I'll rework the timeline today. I'll ask the finance team for their numbers by tomorrow noon. Looking at our current progress, I think we're going to meet the new deadline if we focus. The data looks positive. And I promise — I'll personally review the final document before it goes out."

Coming Up Next
M5 · Session 4A — Will in Extended Business Discourse
Students use both systems across longer texts: project proposals, meeting minutes, client emails, forecast reports. Fluency focus: natural switching between will and going to in authentic business writing and speaking.
End-of-session note: Remind students that the contrast is now a tool they own. In every future session, when they produce a future sentence, they should ask themselves: "Was this planned before now?" If yes → going to. If no → will. This question is the entire system.
