§2 · Pitch & Debate — 14 min
The Full Scenario
Reception → Small Talk → Problem → Solution — all in one
⏱ 14 min
⚡ Teacher Note — Integrated Role-Play

This is the capstone role-play for Module 6. Students must use language from ALL three previous sessions in one continuous scenario. Run it twice (swap roles). While they speak, track: (1) Did they greet formally? (2) Did they use small talk? (3) Did they report the problem with the full frame? (4) Did they use at least one colloquial phrase? These 4 criteria = the oral assessment rubric for this session.

🎬 The Scenario
An international visitor arrives at Solaris Manufacturing in Celaya for a 9 AM meeting with the operations manager. At the reception, there is a problem: the visitor's pass is not working and the meeting room on floor 3 is occupied. The receptionist must help, make small talk, and find a solution.
🌐 Student A — International Visitor
Step 1 (Reception): Greet. Say your name. Say you have a meeting at 9 AM with [manager name].

Step 2 (Small talk): While waiting, make 2 exchanges of small talk with the receptionist.

Step 3 (Problem): Your pass doesn't work. Report it. Ask for help.
Use: "Good morning, my name is..." / "How's your week?" / "My pass isn't working. Can you...?"
🏢 Student B — Receptionist at Solaris
Step 1 (Reception): Welcome the visitor. Ask for their name and who they are meeting.

Step 2 (Small talk): Respond naturally. Add ONE piece of information about the building or the week.

Step 3 (Problem): The pass is broken AND the meeting room is full. Say: "Let me check." Offer an alternative.
Use: "Welcome to Solaris!" / "Let me check." / "There is no room available on floor 3, but there is one on floor 2." / "Sorry about that!"
📚 Assessment Rubric — 4 Criteria

While pairs perform, score each student 0–1 on each criterion: (1) Formal greeting with name — 1 pt / (2) Small talk exchange (at least 1 question + answer) — 1 pt / (3) Problem reported with PROBLEM + LOCATION + SOLUTION frame — 1 pt / (4) Colloquial phrase used naturally — 1 pt. Enter scores in the leaderboard in §6.

§4 · Speed Round — 8 min
30-Second Full Integration
Each situation uses language from 2+ sessions
⏱ 8 min
⚡ Teacher Note — Speed Round

Each situation requires integrated language. The student must use at least 2 different M6 grammar structures. Class counts: 1 point per structure, 2 points for using a colloquial phrase. The teacher tracks on the board. Fastest class to hit 20 total points wins. Award badge b3 when the class hits 15+ points collectively.

Sprint 1 — Reception + Problem
You arrive at a new client's office. Greet the receptionist, say who you are meeting, AND report that your visitor pass is not working. All in 30 seconds.
Sprint 2 — Small Talk + Routine
You meet a foreign colleague in the cafeteria. Make small talk AND describe your typical morning routine. Use at least 2 frequency adverbs.
Sprint 3 — Problem + Team
There is a problem in your production area. Report it to your manager AND describe what your team can and cannot do because of it.
Sprint 4 — FULL INTEGRATION
BONUS: Describe your ENTIRE workday from arrival to end. Reception, small talk, routine, one problem, one solution. Use all 5 colloqial phrases from Module 6. 30 seconds. No notes.
M6 Colloquial Phrases — All 5
"Sounds good!" "No worries!" "I'll be right back." "Let me check." "Sorry about that!"

🎯 Module 6 — Your 3 Most Important Phrases:
The Reception Phrase
"Good morning. My name is ___. I have a meeting with ___."
Use every time you arrive at any office in Mexico or internationally. This one sentence opens every professional interaction in English.
The Problem Phrase
"The ___ is broken. I can't ___. Can you fix it before ___?"
Report + impact + deadline. This is the complete English formula for reporting any workplace problem to anyone, anywhere.
The Lifesaver Phrase
"Let me check."
When you don't know the answer, this phrase buys you time professionally. It works in every situation: calls, meetings, face-to-face. Three words. Infinite use.
