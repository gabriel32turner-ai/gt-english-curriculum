§1
Hot Take — Timed opinion opener
Remember from S1A · Activate integration structures under pressure · 8 min
⏱ 8 MIN
⚡ Remember from S1A
S1B builds on S1A content: integration of all A2.2 structures (used to / have to / should / might / comparatives), networking vocabulary (background, connection, opportunity, impression, reach out, stay in touch), and the four networking closers ("That's a great opportunity" / "Let's stay in touch" / "It was great meeting you" / "I'll reach out next week"). Students should NOT need their notes from S1A — the Hot Take deliberately activates what they retained spontaneously. If they blank, that is data — note which structure or phrase is missing and target it in §4 Speed Round.
🔥 Hot Take — 60 seconds each
"The most important thing you can do at a professional networking event is NOT what you say — it's how you CLOSE the conversation."
Agree or disagree in 60 seconds. Use at least 2 A2.2 structures and 1 networking phrase from S1A. No notes. Pure fluency.
🔥 Hot Take Round 2 — 60 seconds
"In Mexico's Bajío region, speaking English at a trade fair is more valuable than having 5 extra years of experience in your field."
Agree or disagree using a comparative + should/could + one colloquial phrase from S1A. The best argument wins a point for their team.
⚡ Teacher Note — Hot Take
The Hot Take is the fluency thermometer for S1B. You are NOT teaching here — you are measuring. Two things to listen for: (1) do students combine structures spontaneously (e.g., "I should focus on my closing because it might be more important than my opening") or do they use isolated grammar? (2) do the S1A networking phrases appear naturally or do they need prompting? Students who use "Let's stay in touch" or "It was great meeting you" unprompted in their argument have internalized S1A. Those who don't get targeted in §4 Speed Round. Round 2 is intentionally provocative for Bajío professionals — the comparison of English vs experience generates strong opinions and rich language. Collect the best 2–3 arguments for the leaderboard in §6.
§2
Pitch & Debate — The Networking Pitch
3 rounds · T-S · S-S · Class · Model answers · 20 min
⏱ 20 MIN
T-SRound 1 — 30-Second Pitch
You are at the Bajío Trade Fair right now. You have 30 seconds to introduce yourself to a potential new client. Include: your name + company + one thing you used to do + what you currently do. Go — no preparation. Teacher scores on: structures used + naturalness.
Model (Andrea Soto, Vertex Logistics):
"Hi, I'm Andrea Soto. I work as Regional Sales Coordinator at Vertex Logistics in Celaya. I used to manage a small territory in Irapuato, but now I'm responsible for the whole Bajío region — Guanajuato, Querétaro, and Jalisco. It's a great opportunity to be here today."

Structures used: M1 (used to) + M6 vocab (responsible for, opportunity) + S1A colloquial. Goal: 3 structures in 30 seconds.
S-SRound 2 — Networking Conversation (2 min)
Student A: you are a procurement manager from Monterrey looking for new suppliers. Student B: you are a sales coordinator from Celaya pitching your company. Have a real 2-minute networking conversation using: used to (background), might (future possibility), should/could (recommendations to each other), one comparative, and ALL FOUR S1A closers. Then switch roles.
Language target for each student:
Used to: "I used to work with a different supplier, but…"
Might: "We might be interested in a trial order — it depends on the timeline."
Should: "You should talk to our logistics team — they can give you a better quote."
Comparative: "Our delivery time is faster than most providers in the region."
Closers: "That's a great opportunity." / "Let's stay in touch." / "It was great meeting you." / "I'll reach out next week."

Correction protocol: Do NOT interrupt. Write errors silently. Review after the round ends.
ClassRound 3 — Class Debate
Class debate: "At a networking event, it is more effective to talk to many people briefly than to have one long, deep conversation." Each student makes one argument using a comparative + at least one A2.2 structure. Teacher tracks: which team uses the most integrated language (comparatives + modals + past/future).
Scoring for leaderboard:
1pt: any comparative or superlative used correctly
2pts: comparative + because/so (full justification)
3pts: comparative + A2.2 modal (should / might / going to / used to)
4pts: ALL of the above + one S1A colloquial phrase

Best argument to listen for: "You should have one deep conversation because it might lead to a more lasting connection than ten brief ones — and that's a no-brainer." (3pts + colloquial = 4pts)
⚡ Teacher Note — Pitch & Debate
Round 1 is the S1B equivalent of the S1A Quick Win — it establishes the production baseline at the START of the B session, not the end. Students who struggled with the 20-second quick win in S1A should be able to do 30 seconds now. Note who has improved. Round 2 is the core S1B task: the full networking conversation with 5 structure targets. 2 minutes per pair. Use the timer in §3. The switching of roles is essential — both the procurement and sales perspective use different A2.2 structures naturally (procurement: should we / might we / used to work with; sales: you could try / it might be / this option is more). Round 3 generates leaderboard points that feed directly into §6. Write student names and points on the board as they speak — the visible scoreboard creates energy and motivates even the quietest students to participate.

⚡ Teacher Note — Relay Spin
Scoring: 1pt per A2.2 structure used correctly, 2pts for a comparative, 3pts for a S1A networking phrase used naturally, BONUS 2pts if they close with all four networking closers in sequence. Run as teams — spin once per team, 60 seconds to respond. Teacher keeps score on the board. Wheel segments are 8 real-world networking scenarios from the Bajío business context. §4
Speed Round — Structure Recall Under Pressure
6 prompts · 15 seconds each · No hesitation · 10 min
⏱ 10 MIN

15 seconds per prompt. No notes. Pure production. Teacher reads the prompt — student responds immediately. 1pt per structure used correctly, 2pts if they include a comparative or modal. Speed matters.

Prompt 01
Introduce yourself to a new contact using used to and currently.
Target: M1 + M6 vocab in 2 connected sentences.
Prompt 02
The new client asks about your company's future plans. Use going to for a confirmed plan + might for a possibility.
Target: M4 distinction — 100% certain vs 50% possible.
Prompt 03
Give this client ONE piece of advice about working with your company. Use should + a comparative.
Target: M3 advice + M5 comparative in one sentence.
Prompt 04
Compare your company to a competitor using TWO comparatives. End with "It's a no-brainer."
Target: M5 double comparative + S1A colloquial closer.
Prompt 05
The conversation is ending. Close it using ALL FOUR S1A networking closers in natural sequence.
Target: That's a great opportunity → Let's stay in touch → It was great meeting you → I'll reach out next week.
Prompt 06
Final challenge: combine ALL FIVE structures in ONE sentence. Past + present + modal + comparative + future.
Example: "I used to work in sales, but now I might move into operations, which should make our team more efficient than before."
15-SECOND RESPONSE TIMER
⚡ Teacher Note — Speed Round
The Speed Round is the fluency stress test. The 15-second timer creates productive pressure that reveals processing automaticity — students who truly internalized S1A structures respond in under 5 seconds. Those who are still translating mentally use most of the 15 seconds. Prompt 06 (5 structures in one sentence) is intentionally very challenging — it is the A2.2 ceiling task. Any student who produces it naturally in under 15 seconds is demonstrating B1-approaching competency. That is your strongest upsell moment: "What you just did in 10 seconds? That is the base unit of B1.1 English. One module away." Points: each prompt worth up to 4pts (1pt per structure + 2pts for comparative/modal combo). Add to leaderboard after each prompt.
§5
Solo Challenge — 3-Minute Networking Pitch
Full A2.2 integration · Individual performance · Rubric assessment · 20 min
⏱ 20 MIN
🎯 Solo Challenge — The Bajío Trade Fair Pitch
You have 3 minutes. You are at a trade fair in Guadalajara and you have just met a potential new business contact. Deliver your complete professional introduction and networking conversation covering all five elements:

1. Introduce yourself with past (used to) + present (responsible for / currently)
2. Describe a confirmed future plan (going to) + one possibility (might)
3. Give your contact one piece of advice using should + a comparative
4. Compare your company to a competitor using at least 2 comparatives
5. Close the conversation using all 4 S1A networking phrases naturally

Your partner plays the contact. The class watches and scores using the rubric below.
Past + present introduction: used to + responsible for / currently
3 pts
Future forms: going to (confirmed) + might (possible)
2 pts
Advice with comparative: should/could + more/[adj]-er + than
2 pts
Two comparatives used correctly (short or long adjective rules)
2 pts
All 4 closers in natural sequence — sounds fluent, not memorized
3 pts
Bonus: at least one sentence combining 3+ A2.2 structures with a connector
3 pts
Total: 0 / 15 pts
3-MINUTE NETWORKING PITCH TIMER
⚡ Teacher Note — Solo Challenge
This is the M6 S1 summative task. The rubric is deliberately scored by the CLASS (peer assessment) not by the teacher alone. Peer scoring has three benefits: (1) it keeps the whole class engaged during individual pitches, (2) it reinforces the grammar structures in the listeners (they have to identify them to score), (3) it creates accountability for the speaker. Run protocol: speaker pitches, class listens with rubric open on their screen, teacher reveals class scores after each pitch. Collect all rubric scores for the leaderboard in §6. Any student who scores 12+/15 is performing at the top of A2.2. Score of 9–11: solid A2.2 — ready for S5. Score below 9: identify the specific rubric items that failed and address in the wrap-up My Win. The bonus point (rb6: three structures in one sentence with connector) is the B1 preview: "What you just did? That is B1.1 integration. One module from now, that is your baseline."
