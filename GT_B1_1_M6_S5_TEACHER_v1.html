🎯 The B1.1 Opinion Toolkit — Final Repaso
Present Perfect: "has/have + past participle." Used for evidence — what has happened up to now. "This problem has existed for years. The situation has changed. We have tried..."
First Conditional: "If + present simple, will + base verb." Used for real future consequences. "If we don't act, things will get worse. If the system changes, students will benefit."
Opinion language (5): "I strongly believe that..." / "In my opinion..." / "I see both sides, but..." / "That's a good point, however..." / "The reality is..."
B1.1 vocabulary: From any module. Use 5+ words naturally. The Exit Project judges range, not repetition — if you use the same word 3 times, it doesn't count as 3.
Teacher note — Rapid repaso: Go through the 5 opinion phrases out loud as a class — everyone says them together. Then: "What is the most common error with First Conditional?" (will in IF clause). "What is the most common error with Present Perfect?" (using past simple instead). 3 minutes. Sharp. Then move to Flash Cards.

Flash Cards Repaso — M6 Key Language

10 min

Click to flip. Front = phrase/word. Back = how to use it in the Exit Project. Work through all 16 cards. Click "All reviewed" to earn badge.

Opinion
I strongly believe that…
Opens with full confidence. Use when you are certain. "I strongly believe that education hasn't kept up with industry needs."
Opinion
In my opinion…
Standard professional opener. Use it to introduce any personal viewpoint without sounding aggressive.
Opinion
I see both sides, but…
Shows nuance. Use when the topic has genuine complexity. Signals B1.1+ thinking to a native speaker immediately.
Opinion
That's a good point, however…
The professional disagreement phrase. Acknowledge + counter. "That's a good point, however the reality of manufacturing is different."
Opinion
The reality is…
Strong, direct. "The reality is that most people never challenge their assumptions — and if they don't, the world stays the same."
S1 — Env.
If we don't act now, things will get worse.
The FC as warning. Replace "act" and "get worse" with your topic: "If schools don't change, graduates will keep arriving unprepared."
S1 — Env.
"I try to do my part."
Honest, humble, practical. Use it to acknowledge your own role before making a bigger argument. Then: "but if companies don't change..."
S2 — Educ.
Education hasn't kept up.
PP for evidence of an ongoing gap. "The curriculum hasn't been updated in years. The system hasn't adapted." Then: "If it doesn't change, the gap will grow."
S2 — Educ.
"Practical skills matter just as much."
Use to argue for balance between academic and practical. "I've seen people with no degree outperform people with one. Practical skills matter just as much — sometimes more."
S3 — Food
If we stop preserving recipes, we lose identity.
Stacking FC: "If [A] doesn't happen, [B] will occur — and if [B] occurs, [C] will follow." Two conditionals = complete cultural argument.
S3 — Food
"That dish reminds me of home."
The most personal phrase of M6. Use to connect food to identity and memory. Follow with PP: "This food has always been a part of who I am."
S4 — Travel
Travel has changed the way I think.
PP for personal growth. "Travel has taught me / I have discovered / My assumptions have changed." Then FC: "If you never travel, you will always see through the same lens."
S4 — Travel
"I saw things differently after that trip."
Works with any experience. "I saw things differently after that project / conversation / course." Human, direct, B1.1. Follow with what changed.
M5 — Balance
Work-life balance is different for everyone.
Use when making a nuanced argument. "I see both sides — balance is different for everyone. But if companies don't create the conditions for it, the conversation stays empty."
Grammar
IF clause = present simple (never WILL)
Zero tolerance error: "If we DON'T act" (correct) vs. "If we WON'T act" (wrong). IF clause ALWAYS present simple. Main clause: WILL + base verb.
Grammar
PP = has/have + past participle
Common error: "Education HAS CHANGE" (wrong) vs. "Education HAS CHANGED" (correct). The past participle form, not the base verb. Always.

📋 CEFR B1.1 Assessment Rubric — Click each criterion when achieved
Present Perfect
PP used naturally at least twice. "has/have + past participle" with correct form. Not just "I have" as filler.
First Conditional
FC used at least twice. IF clause = present simple (no will). Main clause = will + base verb. Logical consequence.
Opinion Language
At least 3 of the 5 phrases used naturally — not just tacked on at the start. Integrated into the argument.
Vocabulary Range
5+ B1.1 words from any module used naturally. Range counts — not repetition of the same word.
Fluency & Time
Speaks for 3+ minutes. No long pauses. No switching to Spanish. Coherent structure throughout.
Engagement
The class listens. The speaker makes eye contact, modulates their voice, and seems to actually care about the topic.
Per-Student Score Tracker

After each talk: score 1–6 (one per rubric criterion met). Add to leaderboard. Give feedback: ONE specific compliment + ONE specific correction.

⌛ Delivery Timer — 3 Minutes
Teacher note — Delivery order: Stronger students first. This sets the standard and gives nervous students one more model. 2 minutes of silent preparation before each talk (no notes — just thinking). During the talk: never interrupt. After: one compliment first, then one correction, then the score. Public scores are motivating — but frame them as achievement, not ranking. "A 5 today, from where you started, is extraordinary." Name the progress.
GT Original — Exit Project format: If time allows, after all talks: ask one student "What was the best Exit Project argument you heard today — and why?" This generates one more round of natural PP + FC + opinion language with zero preparation, because they are talking about what they just witnessed. Best unrehearsed production of the session.
