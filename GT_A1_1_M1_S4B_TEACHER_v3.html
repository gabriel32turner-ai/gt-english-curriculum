§ 01
Warm-Up — Rapid Fire: Reversed Questions
5 min

Dynamic shift: This time the teacher gives the ANSWER and the student says the corresponding QUESTION. No time to think. The reverse format is the most valuable skill in S4B. ⚡

R1
"My name is Ana García."
Question: "What is ___?"
✓
R2
"My office is on the 3rd floor."
Question: "Where is ___?"
✓
R3
"I am a coordinator at Grupo Norte."
Question: "What is ___?"
✓
R4
"My manager is Carlos Ramírez."
Question: "Who is ___?"
✓
R5
"I am fine, thank you!"
Question: "How ___?"
✓
R6
"Her email is [email protected]."
Question: "What is ___?"
✓
⚡ Quick Win — 30 seconds

Zero prep. Your teacher fires 5 rapid questions — you answer with only short answers: "Yes, I am / No, I'm not / Yes, she is / No, he isn't / Yes, they are." Five correct short answers = you have mastered the hardest part of verb "to be."

Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • The reversed format (answer → question) activates active production from minute 0. This is harder than S4A §1 — and that is intentional.
  • Accept variations: "What is your name?" or "What is her name?" depending on perspective — both are valid.
  • If the student fails 3+ cards: pause 2 minutes and mentally review the Wh- table from S4A before continuing.
  • Optional Round 2: the STUDENT gives the answer and the TEACHER asks the question — the student verifies whether the question is correct.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "In S4A you were answering questions. Now you are creating them. That is twice the skill. And you did it in the first 5 minutes."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "In a real networking situation, you need to ASK questions, not just answer them. That is what you just practised."

Recap — Wh- Word Flashcards
7–8 min
GT Flashcard Wall — Wh- Word → Full Question
Say the full question BEFORE flipping. Remember the structure: Wh- + is/are + subject.
WHAT
name / job / email?
"What is your name?"
"What is your job?"
"What is your email?"
WHERE
office / from?
"Where is your office?"
"Where are you from?"
WHO
manager / contact?
"Who is your manager?"
"Who is your contact?"
HOW
are you?
"How are you?"
→ "I'm fine, thanks!"
"Very well, thank you."
Question Generation Drill: The teacher says a noun (email, office, manager, job, name, phone, nationality). The student produces the complete Wh- question INSTANTLY. Example: "email" → student: "What is your email address?" — 3 rounds, increasing pace.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • The Generation Drill is the real diagnostic before the role play: can the student generate questions without a model? If not: review the corresponding flashcard before §5.
  • Trick nouns: "floor" → "Where is your office?" (not a direct question about floor). The student must connect noun → context → question.
  • Target: 6/7 correct in 3 rounds. That is the threshold for free role play.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You are producing questions without looking at any table. That is automaticity. That is how English works in a real meeting."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "In S5 (module review) you will repeat this drill with all sessions mixed together. Today you build it. Tomorrow you show it."

At a business event in Celaya, Ana García meets David Chen for the first time. Ana says: "Hello! Nice to meet you. My name is Ana García. I am a coordinator at Grupo Norte. I am from Celaya. What is your name? Where are you from? Who is your contact here?" David is from Vancouver, Canada. He is Canadian. His email is david.chen@grupovancouver.ca. His phone number is 461 147 2119. ElevenLabs: Single narrator — Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.85 · ~35 sec. This narration uses ALL the Wh- words from S4A in natural context — students hear them as conversation tools, not grammar exercises. Upload to Google Drive with iframe /preview.
Listening Task — Oral Response
Listen once. No writing. Teacher asks a question — student responds by ASKING a question back, not giving an answer.
"What question did David use to get Ana's email? Say it exactly."
"What Wh- question did Ana use to ask about David's origin? Say it."
"How many different Wh- questions did you hear? Name them: What, Where, Who…"
"Now be David — greet Ana and ask her 3 questions using What, Where, Who."
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • This audio shows the 4 Wh- words IN REAL COMMUNICATIVE USE — not in an exercise. The student hears how they sound in context, not in a table.
  • Task 4 is the most important: the student TAKES ON David's role and asks 3 questions of their own. That activates active production after auditory input.
  • If the student did not identify all Wh- words: replay the audio and ask them to raise their hand each time they hear a Wh- question.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You just listened to a real networking event in English. That is exactly what happens at business events with international clients."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "In §5 of this session, YOU are David or Ana. No script. No model. Just you and the questions you already know how to ask."

Round 4 — Student Creates
Now the student CREATES an answer about themselves or a real colleague — and the teacher forms the question. If the teacher forms the question correctly, the student confirms. If the teacher gets it wrong, the student corrects them. The student is the expert now.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • Round 4 reverses roles: the student gives the answer, the teacher asks the question. This is the most sophisticated exercise in the module — the student acts as the evaluator.
  • The key to Round 4: the teacher must make an intentional error at least once ("What is your manager?" when it should be "Who"). The student must correct it.
  • If Wordwall is not available: go directly to Rounds 1–4. The oral game is the most valuable part.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You just corrected me. That means you are already more of an expert than I am on this specific point."

🎭 Role Play 1 — The Plant Visit
A Canadian engineer arrives at your plant in Celaya for the first time. You are the host. Welcome them, introduce yourself, ask for their contact information, and introduce them to your colleague.
Role A — You (student / host)
You are [your real name], [your real job] at [your real company]. You start: greet them, introduce yourself, ask for their email, introduce an imaginary colleague.
Role B — Teacher (visitor)
You are James Park, an engineer at Toyota Canada. You are from Toronto. Your email is [email protected]. Answer the student's questions — and ask them AT LEAST 2 Wh- questions.
"Hello! Welcome to [company]. Nice to meet you! My name is ___."
Minimum goal: the student uses What (email/job), Where (office/from), Who (manager) — at least 3 different Wh- questions. And says "Nice to meet you too!" when the teacher greets them.
🎭 Role Play 2 — The Zoom First Contact
First video call with a new contact from Brazil. They do not know each other. They must exchange: names, jobs, company, email, and ask at least 2 questions each.
Role A — You (student)
You are [your real name and job]. You start. Ask at least 3 different Wh- questions. At the end: exchange a fictional email address.
Role B — Teacher
You are Gabriela Souza, a coordinator at Vale (Brazilian mining company). You are from Belo Horizonte. Your email is [email protected]. You ask 2 questions as well.
[Teacher starts as Gabriela] "Hello! Nice to meet you! I am Gabriela Souza, a coordinator at Vale. Where are you from?"
Goal: the student answers Gabriela's question correctly, then asks 3 questions of their own (What job, What email, Where from — or variations). At the end they give their email address in English.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • Role Play 1 uses the student's REAL data — maximum transfer. If the student does not know their job title in English: they learned it in S3. That is the verification moment.
  • Role Play 2 introduces a Brazilian contact (Gabriela) — this connects with the nationalities from S2A. The student may say "She is Brazilian" spontaneously.
  • Do not interrupt during the role play to correct. Note errors and review them at the end. Fluency first.
  • "Yes, I'm." errors in short answers: correct immediately AFTER the role play by showing the S4A rule.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You just had TWO complete professional conversations in English. One by video, one in person. That is exactly your real job with international clients."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • Upsell: "In S5 you do the same thing but with ALL the module vocabulary mixed together — your final presentation lasts 90 seconds and uses no script."
§ 06
Presentation — Your Professional Introduction
8–10 min
🎤 Free Presentation — 60 Seconds · No Script · No Notes
"Deliver your complete professional introduction — as if meeting a senior executive from a Japanese automotive company for the first time."
5 steps, no visible structure, no notes:
(1) Greeting + name · (2) Job + company + article A/An · (3) Origin + nationality · (4) Introduce a colleague with His/Her · (5) Contact + elegant close

After: the teacher asks 3 random Wh- questions. The student answers without hesitating more than 3 seconds.
Teacher Q&A Round — 3 Wh- Questions After Presentation
Q1."What is your colleague's job? Use AN if applicable."
Q2."Where is your company? What city?"
Q3."Is your manager Mexican? What is his/her name?"
Bonus."How are you feeling about your English right now? Answer in English."
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • This presentation is the CAPSTONE of Module 1. It integrates: to be (S1) + nationalities (S2) + a/an + his/her (S3) + Wh- questions (S4).
  • First delivery: with 2 min of mental preparation. Second delivery: no preparation — it is always better. That is what demonstrates real retention.
  • If the student completes all 5 steps with fewer than 3 errors: they are ready for S5 (module review).
  • Internal teacher assessment: A/AN correct, His/Her correct, at least 1 Wh- in the presentation or Q&A — those are the 3 mastery indicators.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You just gave a 60-second professional presentation in English. Four sessions ago you couldn't say hello. Look at where you are now."
  • "Your A/AN was perfect. Your greeting was natural. That is real progress — not luck."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • Testimonial trigger: "How would you describe your English level today compared to 4 sessions ago? Tell me in English."
  • Upsell: "S5 is your consolidation session. The whole module in a single session. After that — A1.2: routines, schedules, and your first real work conversation."

Speaking Practice
10–12 min
🗣 Guided Speaking — Free Production
Network Like a Pro — Real Conversation
Use What / Where / Who / How + "to be" freely. No template — this is real conversation.
Question 1
"You meet someone at a trade show in Guadalajara. Ask 2 Wh- questions to start the conversation."
Hint: What / Where / Who / How + is/are + noun?
Question 2
"Now answer: your name, job, company, city — 4 sentences using 'to be.'"
Hint: I am ___. My company is ___. I am from ___.
Question 3
"Who is the most important person in your team? Describe them — job, nationality, personality."
Hint: He/She is ___. He/She is from ___. He/She is very ___.
Question 4
"How is business at your company right now? What are the challenges?"
Hint: Business is ___. The main challenge is ___.
Question 5
"Ask me 3 questions in a row — as if you just met me at a conference. Keep it natural."
Hint: What / Where / Who / How — pick freely. No translating, just go.
⏱ 10–12 min total 📋 5 questions 🎯 Min. 6 uses of is/are 👥 Teacher-led
⚡ Speaking Challenge — Mastery Close
"60 seconds. You are at a conference. Introduce yourself AND ask me 3 questions. Go — no template."
S4B mastery standard: student introduces themselves (4 sentences) AND asks 3 Wh- questions naturally. Total 6+ uses of is/are. After: 1 celebration naming a specific sentence, 1 forward focus for S5 review.

Rapid fire drill: "Are you Mexican? — Yes, I am. Are you the CEO? — No, I'm not. Is your company big? — Yes, it is. Is your manager American? — No, he isn't — he's Mexican. Are your colleagues friendly? — Yes, they are!"
Target: zero hesitation on short answers. No "Yes, I'm." No "She is from Mexican." Clean, fast, correct = M1 complete.

Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • This is the cumulative production moment of all 4 sessions in Module 1. Student should feel the difference from S1A: vocabulary is broaright, questions are confident, sentences are longer.
  • Q5 is the peak task — 3 questions in a row without prompting. If student hesitates after the first: "Keep going — What else do you want to know?" Light scaffolding only.
  • Watch for: mixing up "What is you?" — still the highest-frequency error. At S4B it should appear less frequently than S4A. If it still appears at the same rate, note it for S5.
  • Challenge debrief: count uses of Wh-words aloud. "I counted What × 2, Where × 1, How × 1. Four different Wh- words. That is module 1 mastery."
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "S1A: you said your name. S4B: you just ran a 60-second networking conversation in English. That is Module 1."
  • "Every sentence you said is real English. Not textbook English — real professional English."
Layer 3 · Growth & Retention
  • "S5 is the module review — all vocabulary, all grammar, all skills in one session. Then you start Module 2 with a completely new topic."
  • Gentle preview. No hard upsell at A1.1.

3 Phrases — M1 Complete
01
"Yes, I am." — NEVER "Yes, I'm."
The most common A1.1 error. Affirmative short answers never use a contraction. Drill it until automatic.
02
"No, she isn't." / "No, they aren't."
Negative short answers DO use contractions. isn't / aren't / I'm not — all correct and natural.
03
"Are you...?" — "Is she...?" — "Are they...?"
You now know ALL question forms of "to be." This is the complete foundation of English grammar.
