📘 How to run Hot Take Give the student 20 seconds to recall a real situation (or invent one), then start the timer. The key requirement is passive voice for de-escalation: "That was misread — let me clarify" / "My intention was misunderstood" / "The message should have been written differently." Listen for whether they use passive to avoid blame — that is the pragmatic goal here, not just the grammar. If they say "I wrote it badly" instead of "It was written badly," pause after the timer and note the difference: passive removes the subject and sounds more professional when managing a conflict.
§2 Pitch Round — Verbal vs. Written Two assigned sides · Passive voice arguments · 90 sec per side ⏱ 10 MIN
💬 Motion: "All serious workplace conversations should be held by phone or in person — not by text or email." — Assign sides. Push passive: "Important decisions should be communicated verbally." / "Meetings are wasted when agreements aren't written down."
Side A — PRO VERBAL
"Tone is preserved face-to-face"
  • "Tone is preserved when conversations are held face-to-face…"
  • "Conflicts are more easily resolved when both parties can be heard…"
  • "Important agreements should be confirmed in person before being written…"
Side B — PRO WRITTEN
"Written communication can be reviewed later"
  • "Written communication can be reviewed and referenced later…"
  • "Verbal agreements are often misremembered and later disputed…"
  • "Time is saved when decisions are communicated efficiently by email…"
💡 Engagement tip After both sides, flip the question: "What's YOUR real preference — do you prefer to handle difficult conversations verbally or in writing?" Push for a passive rule: "In my team, difficult feedback should be delivered in person" or "In my company, decisions are communicated by email first." That converts the debate into a genuine professional opinion. If they say "it depends on the situation" — that is fine, but push for specifics: "Should be communicated verbally WHEN…" — making the rule contextual is a sign of B1.1 fluency maturity.
§3 Relay — Transform It 5 transformations · Active ↔ Passive · Modal passive · Error detection · Personal response ⏱ 8 MIN
🔄 How Transform It works: Each card shows a sentence to transform — active to passive, passive to active, adding a modal, or fixing an error. The student responds orally. Click "Show Answer" to reveal the model. Card 5 is always personal — no single correct answer.
1
Active → Passive
"My colleague misread my email as criticism."
My email was misread as criticism by my colleague.
2
Passive → Active
"The meeting was called by the team leader."
The team leader called the meeting.
3
Modal → Passive Modal
"We should handle conflicts face-to-face." → Make it a rule.
Conflicts should be handled face-to-face. — (Also accept: "must be handled" / "need to be handled")
4
Fix the Error
"The misunderstanding resolved with a quick call."
"The misunderstanding was resolved with a quick call." — missing 'was'! Passive voice always requires the auxiliary verb 'be' + past participle.
5
Your Reality
"Rodrigo said feedback should be given by phone, not email. Do you agree? Describe your communication rule using passive."
Open response — "In my team, [type of message] should be communicated by…" — no single correct answer. Listen for modal passive structure (should be / must be / can be + past participle) and encourage genuine, workplace-specific content.
📘 Facilitation note For cards 1–4, push for speed — the transformation should come in 3–5 seconds. Card 3 (modal passive) is often the hardest — students will say "we should handle" instead of "should be handled." That is the core B1.1 error: modal + passive requires "should be + past participle," not "should + bare infinitive." Card 4 targets the missing auxiliary — "The misunderstanding resolved" sounds complete to many students. Drill the rule: passive = be + past participle; without 'be' it is active simple past. Card 5 should get 20–30 seconds. Write down their exact sentence — it becomes the model for §5 Solo.
§4 Speed Round — Communication Grammar 10 cards · Passive voice in workplace communication · Score 7+ for badge ⏱ 6 MIN
Score 0 / 10
1. The email ______ to the wrong person by mistake.
2. Her tone ______ as aggressive, but she was trying to be direct.
3. The conflict ______ with a 10-minute phone call.
4. All feedback ______ in writing and confirmed by both parties.
5. The message ______ before it was sent — but the damage was done.
6. Important decisions ______ in person, not by email.
7. Three drafts ______ before the email said what I actually meant.
8. Difficult news ______ over the phone or face-to-face.
9. The meeting ______ by a misread Slack message.
10. Sarcasm ______ very easily in written communication.
📘 Speed Round note Run this as call-and-respond — student says the answer aloud before clicking. Score 7 or more earns the Speed badge ⚡. Key errors to watch for: (1) Q4 — "should be given" not "give" — the modal passive requires "be + past participle"; (2) Q7 — "written" without "were" is the classic missing auxiliary error; (3) Q8 — "should be delivered" not "deliver" — same modal passive rule. After the round, ask: "Which question showed you something you still need to practise?" That question becomes the homework focus.
§5 Solo Challenge — Digital Communication Policy 120 seconds · Workplace context · 3+ passives · 2+ modal passives · No notes ⏱ 8 MIN
🎤 Solo Challenge — 2 Minutes
Your Digital Communication Policy — describe how miscommunication happens in your workplace, what rules should be followed, and what you personally do to avoid being misunderstood. Use at least 3 passive sentences and 2 modal passives.
01
Describe how miscommunication happens in your workplace — give a real or typical example.
02
State what rules should be followed in your team — use modal passives ("should be communicated / must be confirmed").
03
Explain what you personally do to avoid being misunderstood — use passive where possible.
04
Use at least 3 passive sentences — teacher tracks each one.
05
Use at least 2 modal passives — "should be / must be / can be + past participle."
📘 Assessment Track silently during the 2 minutes: (1) Passive count — 3+ correct passive structures (0–3 pts), (2) Modal passive count — 2+ correct modal passives (0–2 pts), (3) Content quality — workplace-specific, not generic (0–3 pts), (4) Fluency — sustained talk without long pauses (0–2 pts). Max 10 points. The most common errors: "should communicated" instead of "should be communicated" — modal passives require "be"; and "the rule was change" instead of "was changed" — past participle required. Note errors during the talk and correct them after the timer ends using the student's own words.

Best follow-up for the company guideline (card 3): "The guideline uses passive throughout — 'is to be delivered', 'may be provided', 'should not be communicated'. 🎯 This week at work — use these 3 phrases
3 Phrases Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"That was misread — let me clarify."
De-escalate with passive. Use it whenever a message causes a conflict: "That was misread — let me clarify what I meant." Passive voice removes personal blame — "I was misread" sounds like an accusation, but "That was misread" makes the problem impersonal. This is the most professionally powerful use of passive voice in B1.1. Works in email replies, Slack, and face-to-face.
PHRASE 2
"Conflicts should be resolved by phone, not text."
Modal passive rule. Use it to propose a team communication norm: "In my opinion, conflicts should be resolved by phone, not text." Modal passive turns a personal preference into an objective-sounding rule. The subject is removed — it is not "I think we should call" but a policy statement. Perfect for proposing communication guidelines in any professional setting.
PHRASE 3
"I think we need to talk — not text."
Switch channels professionally. Use it when a written conversation starts to escalate: "I think we need to talk — not text." Simple and direct, but strategic — it signals that the channel is the problem, not the person. Pair it with a passive: "This is getting complicated — it should probably be discussed in person." Use this phrase this week the moment any message thread gets longer than five exchanges.
