§1
Hot Take — Career & Future opinions
Remember from S2A · Spontaneous production under pressure · 8 min
⏱ 8 MIN
⚡ Remember from S2A
S2B produces the full career arc from S2A: used to (past) + responsible for/currently (present) + going to/might (future). Colloquial phrases: "Back in the day," "I am moving up," "It is a stepping stone," "I am looking forward to it." Key grammar: going to (confirmed plan, ~100%) vs might (possibility, 30–60%) vs will (spontaneous). Errors to watch: "I might probably change" (double uncertainty marker), "We going to expand" (missing be). The Hot Take activates S2A retention without notes — what students produce spontaneously NOW is the real measure of their internalization.
🔥 Hot Take Round 1 — 60 seconds
"In professional English, how you describe your PAST career says more about you than how you describe your future plans."
Agree or disagree in 60 seconds. Use used to (past) + going to / might (future) in your argument. Which do employers find more persuasive? Use evidence from YOUR career.
🔥 Hot Take Round 2 — 60 seconds
"In Mexico's Bajío region, most professionals are going to change careers at least once in the next 10 years — and that is a good thing."
Agree or disagree using going to + might + at least one S2A colloquial phrase. Bonus point: use "back in the day" + "stepping stone" in the same argument.
⚡ Teacher Note — Hot Take
Round 1 targets the past vs future contrast — the core of S2A. Listen specifically for: (1) do students use going to and might distinctly (confirmed vs possible) or do they use "will" for everything? (2) do they produce "used to" spontaneously in an argument, or only when prompted? Round 2 is designed to force real going to + might production in a debate context — the future form distinction is most visible when students have to argue a specific position. The bonus point for using "back in the day" + "stepping stone" in the same argument rewards students who integrated the S2A collocations into their active vocabulary. Collect the best 2 arguments for the leaderboard. Students who use 3 different time frames in one 60-second argument (past + present + future) are performing at the S2B ceiling — name them explicitly.
§2
Pitch & Debate — The Career Pitch
3 rounds · T-S / S-S / Class · Model answers · 20 min
⏱ 20 MIN
T-SRound 1 — 2-Minute Career Introduction
Deliver your complete career introduction to the class using ALL THREE time frames: past (used to / back in the day) + present (responsible for / currently) + future (going to for a confirmed plan + might for a possibility). Include at least one S2A colloquial phrase. 2 minutes. No notes. Timer below.
Model (Andrea Soto, Vertex → Company B):
"Back in the day, I used to work in customer service in Irapuato. It was a good role, but it was not where I wanted to be long-term.
Now I am responsible for regional sales at Company B in León — the largest territory I have ever managed. I am really looking forward to this chapter.
As for the future, we are going to expand to three new cities in the next 12 months — that is confirmed. Personally, I might also move into a director role if the results continue. This position is definitely a stepping stone."

Structures: M1 + M6 vocab + M4 (going to vs might) + 3 colloquial phrases from S2A. Target for students: minimum 4 structures in 2 minutes.
S-SRound 2 — Career Interview Roleplay (3 min)
Student A: you are an HR manager interviewing for a senior coordinator position at your company. Ask 4 questions about the candidate's past, present responsibilities, and future plans. Student B: you are the candidate. Answer using the full career arc — used to + responsible for + going to + might. Then switch roles.
HR Manager question bank (choose 4):
1. "Tell me about your professional background. Where did you start and how did you get here?" → target: used to
2. "What are you currently responsible for in your role?" → target: responsible for / currently
3. "What does your company plan to do in the next year?" → target: going to (confirmed plan)
4. "Where do you see yourself in 3–5 years?" → target: might / going to
5. "What was the most important change in your career so far?" → target: used to + but now
6. "What might change in your industry in the next 2 years?" → target: might / may

Correction protocol: collect errors silently. Do NOT interrupt the interview flow. Review the top 3 errors after both roles complete.
ClassRound 3 — The Ambition Debate
Class debate: "Is it better to have ONE clear career plan (going to) or stay open to many possibilities (might)?" Each student defends their position in 2 sentences using: going to OR might + a reason using because/so + at least one S2A colloquial phrase. Teacher tracks: most integrated language production wins a bonus point.
Scoring for leaderboard:
1pt: going to OR might used correctly in an opinion
2pts: both going to AND might in the same argument with correct distinction
3pts: going to + might + because/so (complete argument with reason)
4pts: all of the above + one S2A colloquial phrase

Best 4-point argument: "Back in the day, I used to think you needed one clear plan, but now I believe you should be going to pursue your goals AND staying open to what might come. It is a stepping stone mentality, and I am looking forward to where it takes me." (4pts + bonus colloquial = 5pts)

Option Meter idea: Use the debate to feed the opinion meter in §4 Open the Box — write the split (e.g., 6 for plan, 4 for flexibility) on the board.
2-MINUTE CAREER PITCH TIMER (Round 1)
⚡ Teacher Note — Pitch & Debate
Round 1 (2-minute career introduction) is the S2B equivalent of the S1B networking pitch — but now with the full three-time-frame arc. Listen for three things: (1) is the transition from past to present smooth (connector: "but now" / "these days"), (2) does the student distinguish going to (confirmed) from might (uncertain) without prompting, (3) do the S2A colloquials appear naturally or do they sound inserted? Round 2 (career interview) generates the richest language because the questions force specific structures. The HR manager must ask questions that require the target grammar — coaching students to write good interview questions IS grammar practice. Round 3 (ambition debate): use the class vote to set the opinion meter in §3 Open the Box. The most engaged session 2B dynamics happen when the class is genuinely split on this question — it maps to real professional values students have.

Relay — Open the Box: Career Scenarios
6 numbered boxes · Click to reveal a career prompt · Respond using full career arc · 10 min
⏱ 10 MIN
🏭 Open the Box — Career Arc Prompts
Teams choose a box · Reveal prompt · Respond in 60 sec using past + present + future
1
🔒
You used to work in a completely different industry. A new contact asks: "How did you end up in your current field?" Tell your career change story using used to + but now + going to.
2
🔒
Your company is going to launch a new product line next quarter. Your manager asks you to update the team on what is confirmed and what might still change. Use going to vs might clearly.
3
🔒
Back in the day, your industry looked very different. Describe THREE changes: one used to (past habit), one present situation (responsible for), one future possibility (might). 60 seconds.
4
🔒
BONUS BOX: Use ALL FOUR S2A colloquial phrases in one 60-second career monologue. "Back in the day..." + "I am moving up..." + "It is a stepping stone..." + "I am looking forward to it."
5
🔒
You are being considered for a promotion. Your manager asks: "Why should we give you this role?" Use your career arc (used to + now + going to) to make the case. Include one comparative.
6
🔒
CHALLENGE BOX: Combine 5 A2.2 structures in ONE paragraph: used to + responsible for + going to + might + comparative. Your real career. Your real plans. 60 seconds. No notes.
⚡ Teacher Note — Open the Box
Run as teams: each team picks a number without seeing the prompt. The surprise element adds energy. Scoring: Box 1–3 = 3pts for correct grammar + 2pts for connector use (but now / so / because). Box 4 (Bonus) = 6pts for all 4 colloquials used. Box 5 = 4pts (career arc + comparative). Box 6 (Challenge) = 8pts for all 5 structures. Points feed directly into §6 Leaderboard. Key teacher move: if a team opens Box 6 and panics, give them 30 seconds of preparation. Then run the timer. The constraint of spontaneous production is the pedagogical value — but a 30-second prep window removes the anxiety without removing the challenge. Teams who open Box 4 or 6 voluntarily are performing above A2.2 baseline.
§4
Speed Round — Future Forms Under Pressure
6 prompts · 15 seconds each · Going to vs might vs will · 10 min
⏱ 10 MIN

15 seconds per prompt. Teacher reads the scenario — students choose the correct future form and say a sentence. No notes. 1pt per correct form, 2pts if they add because/so, 3pts if they use a S2A colloquial phrase.

Prompt 01
Your company confirmed last week that it will open a new branch in Leon. How do you tell a colleague?
Target: going to — decision already made, evidence exists.
Prompt 02
You are not sure if you will get the promotion. You think there is about a 40% chance. How do you describe this to a friend?
Target: might — possibility under 50%, uncertain outcome.
Prompt 03
Your client just asked for your company brochure right now. You decide instantly to email it to them. What do you say?
Target: will — spontaneous decision made in the moment.
Prompt 04
Describe your career change in one sentence: what you USED TO do + what you ARE GOING TO do next.
Target: M1 (used to) + M4 (going to) combined with a connector.
Prompt 05
Use "back in the day" + "I am looking forward to it" in ONE sentence about your real career trajectory.
Target: combine two S2A collocations with a career arc structure naturally.
Prompt 06
Final: say your complete career arc in under 15 seconds. Past (used to) + present (responsible for) + future (going to + might). All three time frames. Go!
This is the S2 ceiling task. Students who do it in under 10 seconds are ready for S5 exit project.
15-SECOND RESPONSE TIMER
⚡ Teacher Note — Speed Round
Prompts 01–03 target the core S2A grammar distinction: going to (confirmed evidence) / might (uncertain possibility) / will (spontaneous). These three prompts are the fastest diagnostic you have for whether students internalized the certainty scale. Prompt 04 combines M1 and M4 in one sentence — the connector is the key (but now / so / and then). Prompt 05 forces collocational production: "Back in the day" + "looking forward to it" in one natural sentence is genuinely difficult under 15-second pressure. Prompt 06 is the ceiling: 3 time frames in under 15 seconds. Students who do this fluently are above A2.2 floor and approaching B1 readiness. That is your strongest S2B upsell moment: "What you just did in 10 seconds? That is B1.1 entry-level English. You are there."
§5
Solo Challenge — The Investor Pitch
Full A2.2 career arc · Individual performance · Peer rubric · 20 min
⏱ 20 MIN
🎯 Solo Challenge — Pitch to the Investors
You have 3 minutes. You are pitching yourself to a panel of "investors" — your classmates — who will decide whether to hire you, partner with you, or invest in your idea. Deliver a professional presentation covering all five elements:

1. Career background: where you started (used to) and what you did between then and now
2. Current role: what you are currently responsible for — with concrete scope (team size, region, numbers)
3. Confirmed plan: one thing your company or you are going to do in the next 12 months (with evidence it is decided)
4. Possibility: one thing that might happen — clearly marked as uncertain, not guaranteed
5. Close: use at least 2 S2A colloquial phrases to make your close memorable and natural

The panel scores using the rubric. The highest scorer of the session earns a bonus 5 points on the leaderboard.
Career background: used to used correctly with a connector (but now / back in the day)
2 pts
Current role: responsible for with concrete scope — numbers, region, or team size
2 pts
Confirmed plan: going to used correctly — sounds decided, not tentative
2 pts
Possibility: might clearly distinct from going to — conditional language present
2 pts
Close: 2+ S2A colloquial phrases used naturally (not memorized-sounding)
3 pts
Bonus: all three time frames in one connected sentence using a connector
4 pts
Total: 0 / 15 pts
3-MINUTE INVESTOR PITCH TIMER
⚡ Teacher Note — Solo Challenge
The "Investor Pitch" framing elevates the stakes above a standard speaking exercise. Students who would give a mediocre career introduction suddenly perform better when they are "pitching" to a panel. The rubric bonus (rb6: all three time frames in one connected sentence) is now worth 4pts instead of 3 — this is the S2B capstone item. Peer scoring: have students hold up 1–5 fingers for each rubric item immediately after the pitch (no discussion — instant gut response). Average the class score. This runs faster than written rubrics and generates more energy. Key observation: rb4 (might clearly distinct from going to) is the hardest item consistently. Students who score 2/2 on rb4 have definitionally mastered the S2A grammar. Those who score 0 need the Speed Round prompt 01–03 review before S3A. Add Solo Challenge scores directly to the leaderboard in §6.

    🎯 This week, use these 3 phrases at work:
    From today's session — production-ready career English for any professional conversation.
    "We are going to [confirmed plan] — it's already decided."
    Use in any meeting or update where the plan is confirmed. The phrase "it's already decided" signals that this is going to, not will or might. Instantly upgrades your English from casual to professional. Use it in emails too: "We are going to launch in Q3 — the board confirmed it last week."
    "Back in the day, I used to [past] — but now I am responsible for [present]."

    Honest, professional uncertainty. Use when someone asks about your plans and you are not 100% sure. Sounds much more professional than "I don't know." The condition clause ("it depends on the results / the budget / the timeline") shows strategic thinking, not indecision.
