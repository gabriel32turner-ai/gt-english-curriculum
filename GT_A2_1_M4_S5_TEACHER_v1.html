Warm-Up — Module 4 Quick-Fire
⏱ 8 min · 90 sec per student · Full M4 activation · Badge ⚡
Layer 1 · Logistics

90-second Quick-Fire activates ALL of Module 4: going-to affirmative (S1), negative (S2), Wh- questions (S3), short answers and schedule contrast (S4A). Run as rapid round-robin — never correct mid-answer, mark errors and address after. One badge ⚡ awarded when the timer completes.

Layer 2 · Pedagogical Focus
  • Q1: Plan announcement — going-to affirmative: "We're going to…"
  • Q2: Negative — "We're not going to…" or "They aren't going to…"
  • Q3: Wh- question — "What/When/Who/How long are they going to…?"
  • Q4: Schedule contrast — going-to intention vs. Simple Present fixed schedule
Layer 3 · S5 Purpose

This is not a new lesson — it is a performance session. Students should feel the accumulated weight of 4 sessions. If warm-up reveals gaps (e.g., "We are going launch" — missing "to"), note them for targeted correction during the Exit Task debrief. Never re-teach in S5 — redirect: "What did we say about the verb form after 'going to'?"

⚡ QUICK-FIRE · 90 SECONDS PER STUDENT

🔵 Going-To Affirmative — S1 Review
Announce 4 plans for your team this quarter. Use going to with a subject and base verb: "We're going to launch / implement / assign / organize…". Each sentence with a time marker.
Push: "Who is going to lead that? When exactly are you going to start?"
⭐ Going-To Negative — S2 Review
Tell me 3 things your team is NOT going to do in the new project. Use: We're not going to… / They aren't going to…. Include a reason for at least one.
Push: "Why aren't you going to do that? Who decided that?"
🟢 Wh- Questions — S3 Review
Interview your partner about their next project. Ask 4 going-to Wh- questions: What / When / Who / How long + are/is + subject + going to + base verb?. Partner answers in full going-to sentences.
Push: "What are you going to do first? How long is that going to take?"
🟣 Schedule Contrast — S4A Synthesis
Describe Monday's plan using both going-to (intentions) AND Simple Present schedule (fixed events): "The kickoff starts at 9 AM — we're going to cover the roadmap. The client call is at 2 PM — we're going to confirm the budget." ≥3 contrasts.
Push: "Which of those is a fixed schedule? Which is still a plan?"

Grammar Review — Going-To Full System
⏱ 18 min · Table + Poll + Error Correction + Drag-and-Drop + Hot Seat · Badge 🎯
Layer 1 · Logistics

Full going-to paradigm table: affirmative (all persons), negative, yes/no questions, Wh- questions, short answers, and contrast with Simple Present for schedules. One MC poll, one error-correction box, two drag-and-drops (A/B), then Hot Seat Dice with 6 going-to prompts. Badge 🎯 after completing dice or drag exercises.

Layer 2 · Key Error Patterns to Watch
  • Missing "to": "We're going launch" → "We're going to launch"
  • Wrong verb form: "She's going to launched" → "She's going to launch"
  • Inverted question word order: "When going they to finish?" → "When are they going to finish?"
  • Short answer confusion: "Yes, I going to." → "Yes, I am."
Layer 3 · Schedule Contrast Insight

The contrast row (gold border) is the key S4A insight: Simple Present for fixed scheduled events ("The meeting starts at 9") vs. going-to for intention/plan ("We're going to discuss the roadmap"). At A2.1 this distinction is often blurry — validate approximations and build precision gradually.

Error Correction — Find and Fix


1. "We're going launch the product next week." → "We're going to launch the product next week."


2. "She going to coordinate the event tomorrow." → "She's going to coordinate the event tomorrow."


3. "When they going to finish the testing?" → "When are they going to finish the testing?"


4. "We aren't going to implemented the new system." → "We aren't going to implement the new system."

Sequence Builder — Project Plans in Going-To
⏱ 14 min · 2 clickable-order exercises + oral retell
Layer 1 · Logistics

Two sequencing exercises: (A) 5-step product launch in going-to sentences — students drag into logical order; (B) 4-step client onboarding in going-to sentences. After checking, students retell the sequence aloud without looking at the screen. Time oral retell at 60 seconds max.

Layer 2 · Pedagogical Focus
  • Exercise A: product launch — introduces "First… Then… After that… Finally…" as oral sequencers for going-to chains
  • Exercise B: client onboarding — shorter, faster, focuses on confirm / coordinate / deliver / organize vocabulary
  • Oral retell: students must produce going-to from memory — the text is scaffolded, the speech is not
Layer 3 · Bridge to Exit Task

The sequence builder mirrors the Exit Task structure: students will need to present a multi-step plan in order using going-to. Students who can retell Exercise A and B from memory with no notes are ready for the Exit Task at full performance level.

Exercise A — Product Launch: 5-Step Sequence

Drag the going-to sentences into the correct launch order. Then retell aloud without looking.

  • ⠿We're going to confirm the launch date with the executive team.
  • ⠿We're going to build a self-service portal for top clients.
  • ⠿We're going to reduce support calls and exceed client satisfaction targets.
  • ⠿We're going to organize a kickoff meeting to assign roles.
  • ⠿We're going to run two testing sprints before the public launch.

Exercise B — Client Onboarding: 4-Step Sequence

Drag into the correct order. Partner listens and confirms the logic.

  • ⠿They're going to deliver access credentials and set up accounts.
  • ⠿They're going to schedule a 30-day check-in to confirm satisfaction.
  • ⠿They're going to coordinate the kickoff call with all stakeholders.
  • ⠿They're going to implement the onboarding training sessions.

Oral Retell Prompt

After checking both exercises: close the screen. Retell Exercise A in 60 seconds using First… Then… After that… Finally… + going-to sentences. Partner counts going-to structures used. Target: ≥ 5 going-to sentences, ≥ 2 connectors.

Role-Play — Going-To Pitch Progression
⏱ 16 min · 3 rounds · Color-coded model answers
Layer 1 · Logistics

3-round going-to pitch progression — each round adds a layer of complexity. Round 1: simple affirmative pitch. Round 2: add negatives and constraints. Round 3: full pitch with Wh- Q&A. Color-coded model answer: cyan = going-to affirmative, red = negatives, gold = Wh- questions, green = time expressions.

Layer 2 · Round Structure
  • Round 1 (5 min): Student A pitches using ≥5 going-to affirmatives. Student B listens and counts.
  • Round 2 (5 min): Swap roles. Add ≥2 negatives ("We're not going to…") and ≥1 constraint.
  • Round 3 (6 min): Student A pitches full plan. Student B asks ≥3 Wh- going-to questions. Both produce going-to in Q&A.
Layer 3 · Common Round 3 Errors

Students often revert to Simple Present in answers: "We start in July" instead of "We're going to start in July." Coach the going-to short answer pattern: Q: "When are you going to launch?" A: "We're going to launch in September." If time pressure: allow Round 1 only and save Rounds 2/3 for Exit Task warm-up.

🎤 3-Round Going-To Pitch Progression
Round 1 — Announce the Plan

Student A: You are presenting your team's Q3 initiative. Announce at least 5 things you are going to do. Use "We're going to…" / "The team is going to…". Time limit: 90 seconds. No notes.

Student B: Listen and count every going-to structure. Report the total at the end.

"We're going to launch the Nexo Client Portal in Q3. We're going to build it for our top 50 clients. They're going to track orders and submit tickets directly. We're going to run two testing sprints in August. We're going to invite ten pilot clients to validate the system. We're going to present the final version to the board by end of September."
Round 2 — Add Constraints

Student B: Swap roles. Present a revised version of the plan — include ≥2 negatives (things you are NOT going to do) and at least 1 constraint or budget limit.

Student A: Listen and note each negative going-to used.

"We're going to implement the portal in twelve weeks. We're not going to exceed the 80,000-peso budget. We're not going to launch without client validation — that's non-negotiable. We're going to assign a development team of four people. We're going to coordinate weekly check-ins with the project lead. The team isn't going to reschedule the kickoff — the July 15th date is confirmed."
Round 3 — Full Pitch with Q&A

Student A: Present the full plan (2 minutes). Then Student B asks ≥3 Wh- going-to questions. Student A answers in full going-to sentences.

Wh- question starters: What / When / Who / How many / How long / Why + are/is + subject + going to + base verb?

Q: "What are you going to build?"
A: "We're going to build a self-service portal for our top 50 clients."

Q: "When are you going to start testing?"
A: "We're going to start testing in August — two sprints."

Q: "Who is going to lead the project?"
A: "Sofía Ramírez is going to lead it. She's going to assign roles at the kickoff."

Q: "How many clients are going to test the portal?"
A: "Ten pilot clients are going to test it in September."

Listening — Sofía Ramírez: Nexo Client Portal
⏱ 14 min · ~85 sec monologue · 4 MCQ + 4 T/F
Layer 1 · Logistics

Single-speaker monologue (~200 words, ~85 seconds): Sofía Ramírez, Project Lead, presenting the Nexo Client Portal Q3 initiative. Voice: Sarah AmE at 0.82× speed (ElevenLabs). Play twice minimum. First listen: general comprehension. Second listen: note going-to structures for the MCQ and T/F tasks.

Layer 2 · Target Going-To Structures
  • Affirmative: "We're going to build / need / run / invite / present / achieve / reduce"
  • Negative: "We're not going to launch without client validation"
  • Wh- rhetorical: "What are we going to need? / What are we going to achieve?"
  • Note: speaker uses "going to" 12+ times — this is the density students need for the Exit Task
Layer 3 · Post-Listening Production

After the MCQ/T-F, ask: "Which going-to sentences from Sofía's monologue can you remember?" Target 5+ recalled sentences. Then: "Now change the subject — what is YOUR team going to do this quarter?" This bridges the listening directly to Exit Task production.

Exit Task — Going-To Project Pitch
⏱ 20 min · 90 sec timer · 3 scenarios · Rubric · Badge 🌟
Layer 1 · Logistics

Student selects one of 3 scenarios and has 90 seconds to deliver a going-to project pitch. Rubric: ≥10 going-to sentences / ≥2 Wh- questions (self-directed or answered) / ≥1 negative / ≥3 time expressions. Badge 🌟 awarded after attempting the task. No notes during the pitch.

Layer 2 · Evaluation Criteria
  • Structure: intro → plan → timeline → constraint → outcome (mirroring Sofía's monologue)
  • Accuracy: going-to + base verb (never "going to + -ed"); short answers ("Yes, I am" not "Yes, I going to")
  • Fluency: no long pauses at going-to junctions — the construction should feel automatic by S5
  • Vocabulary: at least 3 M4 words used (launch / implement / organize / confirm / assign / coordinate / deliver / reduce / exceed)
Layer 3 · Debrief Protocol

After each pitch: (1) peer counts going-to structures out loud; (2) teacher gives ONE correction — the most impactful error only; (3) student re-says that sentence correctly; (4) move on. Never give a list of 5 corrections in S5 — it destroys confidence at the performance session. Celebrate quantity of going-to production first.

Capstone Game — Going-To Plan Builder
⏱ 10 min · 5 slots · Badge 🏆 on 5/5
Layer 1 · Logistics

5 sequential slots that unlock one by one. Each slot requires a going-to sentence that meets length + structure requirements. Slot 4 specifically requires a negative ("not going to" / "aren't going to" / "isn't going to"). Progress bar fills 20% per slot. 5/5 earns the Module 4 Graduate badge 🏆 and confetti.

Layer 2 · Validation Rules
  • Slots 1–3 and 5: ≥4 words + "going to" in text + ends with period or exclamation mark
  • Slot 4: ≥4 words + "going to" + negative marker (not going to / aren't going to / isn't going to) + terminal punctuation
  • All responses case-insensitive. Accept any plausible going-to sentence — validation is structural, not semantic.
Layer 3 · Bridge to M5

The Plan Builder is the final production gate before Module 5. Students who complete 5/5 have demonstrated functional command of going-to as a planning tool. In M5, they will use "will" for offers and spontaneous decisions — a natural contrast with the planned, pre-meditated going-to they mastered in M4.

Module Close — I Can Now…
⏱ 8 min · Checklist + Badge Showcase + My Win + M5 Preview
Layer 1 · Logistics

Module 4 close ceremony. 6-item "I can now…" checklist — students tick each item as a self-assessment. Badge showcase reveals all earned badges. "My Win" textarea captures one moment of success from the session. M5 preview card teases the next topic: Offers & Decisions with "will".

Layer 2 · Reflection Protocol
  • Read each checklist item aloud as a class. Student raises hand only if they feel confident — not just "covered it."
  • Items 3 and 5 (negative + Wh- questions) are typically the weakest — circle them for M5 recycling.
  • "My Win" should be specific: not "I learned going-to" but "I pitched a 10-sentence plan about our Q3 initiative."
Layer 3 · M5 Preview Framing

Going-to (M4) = planned decisions made before the moment of speaking. Will (M5) = spontaneous decisions, offers, and predictions made at the moment of speaking. The contrast is intuitive in Spanish (voy a vs. futuro simple) but often confused in production. Frame M5 as: "You already know the planned future — next we learn the spontaneous future."

I Can Now… — Module 4 Self-Assessment

  • Use going-to to announce plans and intentions in professional contexts.
  • Form going-to negatives ("We're not going to…" / "She isn't going to…") with confidence.
  • Ask and answer Wh- going-to questions: "What / When / Who / How long are they going to…?"
  • Give correct short answers: "Yes, I am." / "No, she isn't." / "Yes, they are."
  • Contrast going-to (intention) with Simple Present (fixed schedule) in one sentence.
  • Pitch a complete multi-step project plan using going-to for at least 90 seconds.
