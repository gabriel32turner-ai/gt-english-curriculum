§ 01
Warm-Up — Small Talk Scenario
5–8 min
🎬 Scenario — International Call, Monday Morning
Imagine: It's 9:00 AM on Monday. You have a video call with David Park, Sales Director from Phoenix, Arizona. Before starting, he says:
"Good morning! Busy weekend? I went hiking — the weather here was perfect. What about you — any hobbies? What do you usually do on weekends?"
👉 What do you say? — Try to answer. It doesn't matter if it's not perfect. Anything you can say is already a starting point.
Expected output at A1.2: The student should be able to say something about their hobbies using A1.1 (Simple Present). If they say "I play football on weekends" or "I watch movies" — perfect. If they only say "I like…" — also valid. Accept everything. Correction comes in §2 and §4.
⚡ Quick Win — 20 seconds
20 seconds! Tell me your top 2 hobbies in English: "I usually [hobby] in my free time. On weekends I [hobby]."
Teacher follow-up questions (after student's first attempt)
"How often do you do that hobby? — Every day? Once a week?" (hint: this points to §4)
"What do you do in your free time at work? Any meetings? Training? How often?"
"Can you give David an instruction about the call? 'Please mute your mic' — try it!"
"When is your next meeting? Give David the date. Try: 'Our next call is on…'"
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Open with the image and the scenario read aloud before showing the questions. Let the student see the photo first — it activates the visual context.
  • A1.2 prerequisite: the student already knows Simple Present from A1.1. If they say "I play / I watch / I go" — that's the base of this session. Don't correct form yet.
  • If the student can't say "How often" — that's exactly what they learn today. Mental note for the teacher: that's the new grammar in §4.
  • Hobbies as corporate small talk ARE the content of M1. It's not just activation — it's the topic of the module. Tell the student: "This kind of conversation opens EVERY international call in your company."
  • Strict timing: 5–7 minutes max. Move to §2 with high energy.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "You just did small talk in English. That's the first thing your American colleague hears BEFORE the business. If you can talk about this, you can open any call."
  • Connect to the student's reality: "Does your company have contact with teams in the US, Canada, or UK? This is exactly that conversation."
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "By the end of M1 you'll be able to present next week's schedule, give instructions to a colleague, and open any call with small talk. You don't learn that from a book — you learn it here."

§ 04
Grammar — Imperatives & How often
12–15 min
🔴 The Problem

Your boss in Phoenix asks you to give clear instructions to your team. You try to say: "You must to close the file." / "Please you send the report." — In formal English, that sounds strange and confusing.

↓
🟣 The Tool — Imperative

In English, the imperative uses the base form of the verb — no subject, no auxiliary. It's direct, clear, and professional.

Type Form Corporate example
Affirmative Verb (base form) Open the file.
→ Abre el archivo.
Negative Don't + verb Don't close the app.
→ No cierres la app.
Polite Please + verb Please send the report.
→ Por favor envía el reporte.
Polite neg. Please don't + verb Please don't share this file.
→ Por favor no compartas este archivo.
Key rule: The imperative has NO subject. You don't say "You open the file" to give instructions — you just say "Open the file." With "Please" at the start or end, it sounds professional and polite.
🔴 The Problem

David asks you "How often do you have meetings?" — You only know how to say "always" or "never." Your real frequency goes unexpressed.

↓
🟣 The Tool — How often + Frequency Expressions

These expressions are used with Simple Present to describe work and personal routines precisely.

Expression Frequency Example
every day 100% I check my email every day.
once a week 1×/week We have a team meeting once a week.
twice a week 2×/week She works out twice a week.
three times a week 3×/week I attend training three times a week.
once a month 1×/month We travel once a month for business.
rarely very seldom I rarely miss a deadline.
Key question: "How often do you…?" → "I go to the gym twice a week." / "We have reports once a month." Position: at the end of the sentence or after the auxiliary.
⚠️ Common Errors — Correct them aloud
✗ "Please you open the file."→✓ "Please open the file."
The imperative never has an explicit subject. The "you" is implied.
✗ "In Monday I have a meeting."→✓ "On Monday I have a meeting."
Days of the week always with "on." Months and years with "in."
✗ "I go two times to the gym."→✓ "I go to the gym twice a week."
"Two times" sounds unnatural. Use "twice" for 2, "three times" for 3+.
✗ "Don't to share the file."→✓ "Don't share the file."
After "don't" the verb is in base form — never with "to."

      ⚡ Micro-Speaking — 2 min · No prep
      "Give me 3 instructions you use at work every week. Use imperatives." — Student speaks nonstop for 45 seconds.
      "How often do you attend meetings? How often do you go to the gym? Answer both." — Direct answer, no script.
      "Tell me: when is your next deadline? Use 'on' or 'in.'" — Student's real date.
      Layer 1 · Pedagogical
      • ACTION: Read the imperative table aloud first. Student repeats each form. Then ask for 2 of their own examples before moving to exercises.
      • Expected critical error: "Please you open…" — Spanish speakers insert the subject due to Spanish interference. Run the drill: "How do you say it in Spanish? 'Por favor abre.' Is there a 'tú' there? No explicit subject — same in English."
      • How often: connect to the §1 Warm-Up. "David asked you 'How often?' — now you have the tool. What do you tell him?"
      • Activity 3 (drag & drop): help by saying "The imperative always starts with the verb or with Please. What goes first?"
      • Micro-Speaking: strict 2-min timing. Don't correct during the talk — only at the end. The goal is flow, not perfection.
      Layer 2 · Engagement
      • "Now you can give instructions in English without sounding strange. 'Please send the report.' 'Don't share the link.' That's leadership in English — and you just learned it."
      Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
      • "In S1B you'll use these imperatives in role plays — boss giving instructions, colleague executing them. That's real practice. In M2 you'll use them in corporate purchase order emails."

      Layer 1 · Pedagogical
      • ACTION: Read the chat aloud yourself first, then have the student read aloud, assigning characters. The multi-voice chat makes dramatic reading easier.
      • Imperative count in the text: "Please open" / "Don't share" / "Please send" / "Don't miss" / "Please mute" = 5 imperatives. Have the student find and classify them (affirmative/negative/polite).
      • Dates in the text: March 15th / Wednesday / every Monday / once a month / April / April 5th. Quick drill: "Which preposition do you use with 'Monday'?" / "And with 'April'?" / "And with the specific date 'April 5th'?"
      • T/F #2 trap: the chat explicitly says "Don't share it externally." Students sometimes don't read "externally" carefully.
      • Production Trigger: have the student speak nonstop for 1 minute. Don't correct during the talk. Note 1 error to comment on later.
      Layer 2 · Engagement
      • "You just read a real Microsoft Teams chat in English. Does your company use Teams? This is exactly what you read and write in English when you reach B1."
      Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
      • "In S2A you'll learn to write emails with this same structure. You already understand the chat — the email is one step more formal, and you'll master it in the next session."
