📘 How to run Hot Take Give the student 20 seconds to think silently, then start the timer. Key requirement: use modal passive at least once ("AI should be controlled by…" / "Decisions are being made by systems that have no accountability"). The 3-reason structure is a scaffolded opinion format — listen for whether they use signpost language (first, also, and finally). If they produce passive voice naturally, note it aloud: "Did you notice you just used passive? — that was perfect." If they miss it, point to the prompt phrases after the timer ends.
§2 Pitch Round — AI Jobs Debate Two assigned sides · Passive voice arguments · 90 sec per side ⏱ 10 MIN
💬 Motion: "AI will create more jobs than it destroys." — Assign sides. Push for passive in arguments: "New jobs will be created by AI." / "Existing jobs are being eliminated faster than new ones are created."
Side A — PRO AI JOBS
"New jobs will be created by AI"
  • "New industries are being created by AI right now…"
  • "Workers will be freed from repetitive tasks and used for creative work…"
  • "History shows that technology-driven unemployment was always temporary…"
Side B — ANTI AI JOBS
"Jobs are being replaced too fast"
  • "Too many jobs are being replaced faster than training programs can keep up…"
  • "The workers affected are not being supported by governments or companies…"
  • "The benefits of AI are being captured by corporations, not workers…"
💡 Engagement tip After both sides, flip the question: "What's YOUR real position — do you think AI will create or destroy more jobs in your field?" Ask them to answer in one passive sentence: "I think jobs in my industry will be replaced by AI because…" or "I think new roles will be created by AI in the next 5 years." This converts the debate into genuine opinion-forming. If they say "it depends" — that is fine, but push for a passive hedge: "The impact of AI will be determined by how governments and companies respond."
§3 Relay — Transform It 5 transformations · Active ↔ Passive · Modal passive · Error detection · Personal response ⏱ 8 MIN
🔄 How Transform It works: Each card shows a sentence to transform — active to passive, passive to active, adding a modal, or fixing an error. The student responds orally. Click "Show Answer" to reveal the model. Card 5 is always personal — no single correct answer.
1
Active → Passive
"AI engineers designed this system to predict behavior."
This system was designed by AI engineers to predict behavior.
2
Passive → Active
"Bias was discovered in the hiring algorithm by researchers."
Researchers discovered bias in the hiring algorithm.
3
Add a Modal
"AI decisions affect people's lives." → Make it a recommendation using a modal passive.
AI decisions should be explained to the people they affect. — (Also accept: "must be explained" / "need to be explained")
4
Fix the Error
"This algorithm trained on biased data, so its decisions are unfair."
This algorithm was trained on biased data, so its decisions are unfair. — The word "was" is missing! Passive voice always requires the auxiliary verb "be" + past participle.
5
Your Reality
"Rodrigo said AI was used to handle repetitive work. What task in YOUR job could be automated?"
Open response — student answers with: "In my job, [task] could be automated by AI…" — no single correct answer. Listen for modal passive structure (could be / might be / should be + past participle) and encourage genuinely personal, industry-specific content.
📘 Facilitation note For cards 1–4, push for speed — the transformation should come in 3–5 seconds. Card 3 (modal passive) is often the hardest — students will say "should explain" instead of "should be explained." That's the core B1.1 error to catch: modal + passive requires "should be + past participle," not "should + bare infinitive." Card 5 should get 20–30 seconds. Write down their exact sentence — it becomes the model for §5 Solo.
§4 Speed Round — AI Grammar 10 cards · Passive voice in AI context · Score 7+ for badge ⏱ 6 MIN
Score 0 / 10
1. This AI model ______ on data from 500 million users.
2. Thousands of jobs ______ by automation in the last decade.
3. AI regulation ______ by the EU in 2024.
4. Your credit score ______ by an algorithm, not a human.
5. Medical diagnoses ______ by AI in some hospitals now.
6. New AI tools ______ every week by major tech companies.
7. Bias ______ in facial recognition systems that misidentified people of color.
8. These images ______ without the permission of the artists.
9. AI benefits should ______ more fairly across society.
10. Millions of data points ______ every second by AI recommendation engines.
📘 Speed Round note Run this as call-and-respond — student says the answer aloud before clicking. Score 7 or more earns the Speed badge ⚡. Key errors to watch for: (1) Q9 — "should be distributed" not "should distributed" — the bare infinitive "be" is required after a modal; (2) Q1 — "trained" without "was" is the classic error; (3) Q2 — "are eliminate" shows wrong past participle form. After the round, ask: "Which question showed you something you still need to practice?" That question becomes the homework focus.
§5 Solo Challenge — AI Position Statement 120 seconds · Industry context · 3+ passives · 2+ modal passives · No notes ⏱ 8 MIN
🎤 Solo Challenge — 2 Minutes
Your AI Position Statement — describe how AI is used in your industry, whether you think it should be regulated, and what you believe will happen to jobs in the next 10 years. Use at least 3 passive sentences and at least 2 modal passives ('should be / can be / must be').
01
Industry context: describe how AI is currently used or being introduced in your field.
02
Regulation opinion: say whether you think AI should be regulated — and by whom.
03
Future prediction: what do you believe will happen to jobs in the next 10 years?
04
Use at least 3 passive sentences — teacher tracks each one.
05
Use at least 2 modal passives — "should be / can be / must be + past participle."
📘 Assessment Track silently during the 2 minutes: (1) Passive count — 3+ correct passive structures (0–3 pts), (2) Modal passive count — 2+ correct modal passives (0–2 pts), (3) Content quality — industry-specific, not generic (0–3 pts), (4) Fluency — sustained talk without long pauses (0–2 pts). Max 10 points. The most common errors: "AI should regulated" instead of "should be regulated" — modal passives require "be"; and "AI was use" instead of "was used" — past participle required. Note errors during the talk and correct them after the timer ends using the student's own words.

📰 MIT Technology Review
"A study published last year found that AI hiring tools were biased against women in technical roles. The models had been trained on historical data that reflected past discrimination. The company responsible later admitted the system should have been tested more carefully before it was deployed."
Why was the AI biased?
💼 LinkedIn Post
"I used ChatGPT to write my cover letter. It was reviewed by three people — none of them noticed. I was offered the job. Does that say something about AI, or about how cover letters are evaluated?"
What is the person questioning?
📧 Company Email
"Starting next quarter, all client reports will be generated using our new AI tool. Templates have been created and approved. Your role will be focused on reviewing and adding personal insights. Training will be provided."
What change is being introduced?
🔍 Grammar Detective
"AI was introduced last year. Reports are now generated automatically. Employees have been trained. The system is updated weekly."
How many passive verb phrases are there?
💡 Discussion anchor After the 4 cards, ask: "Which source do you find most relevant to your own work — and why?" Best follow-up question: "The company email uses passive voice throughout — 'will be generated', 'have been created', 'will be provided'. Why do you think the author chose passive instead of 'We will generate' or 'We will provide'?" Target answer: passive voice hides the agent (management) and makes the change sound less threatening — inevitable rather than imposed. That is high-level B1.1 pragmatic awareness.

AI in Daily Life — Passive Voice & Opinion Language
Passive Voice Modal passives AI vocabulary Opinion language 2-min fluency
🎯 This week at work — use these 3 phrases
3 Phrases Starting Tomorrow
PHRASE 1
"AI should be regulated — but carefully."
Modal passive + hedged opinion. Use it when AI comes up in any professional conversation: "I think AI should be regulated — but carefully, so innovation isn't killed." The hedge "but carefully" turns a strong claim into a nuanced one. Perfect for business English — shows you can give opinions without being extreme. Works in emails, meetings, and casual conversation.
PHRASE 2
"That depends on how it's used."
Nuanced tech response. Use it any time someone asks a binary question about technology: "Is AI good or bad?" — "That depends on how it's used." Contains a hidden passive ("it's used" = it is used). Shows analytical thinking and avoids oversimplification. One of the most versatile phrases in professional English for discussing any technology topic.
PHRASE 3
"Jobs are being replaced — but new ones are being created too."
Present progressive passive — advanced construction. Use it when the AI/jobs topic comes up: "Yes, jobs are being replaced — but new ones are being created too." Two present progressive passives in one sentence. Shows grammatical confidence and a balanced view. Use it this week to describe any process of change in your industry — automation, restructuring, or any evolving situation.
