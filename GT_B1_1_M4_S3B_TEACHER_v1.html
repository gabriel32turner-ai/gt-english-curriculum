§1

🔥 Hot Take — AI & The Future of Work

5 min
One opinion. 60 seconds. Passive voice required. No notes.
🔥 Hot Take
"AI should be regulated by governments before it's too late. If it isn't controlled now, millions of jobs will be replaced and nobody will be held responsible."
Rule 1: Agree OR disagree — commit fully Rule 2: Use passive voice at least 3 times Rule 3: 60 seconds, no notes Rule 4: Use 1 word from S3A vocab
Model Answer — Agree, with nuance

"I agree that AI should be regulated — but I don't think it should be stopped. Automation has already been used in manufacturing for decades. The question isn't whether jobs will be replaced — some already have been. The question is: who is held responsible when it goes wrong? Right now, nobody is. I'm not sure I trust it completely — but regulation would at least create accountability. Bias in AI systems is already being detected in hiring and finance. That needs to change."
■ Vocabulary ■ Passive Voice ■ Colloquial
Count passives together: There are 7 passive structures in the model answer. Ask: "¿Cuántas notaron ustedes en sus propias respuestas?" Target: students used at least 3. Award 3 pts for 3+ passives, 2 pts for 2, 1 pt for 1.
Discussion bridge: "¿En tu empresa hay alguna tarea que ya esté siendo automatizada?" → "Is any task in your company already being automated?" Perfect passive present continuous — plant it here for §3 relay.
§2

⚡ Pitch / Debate — Should AI Be Regulated?

10 min
Two sides. Five arguments each. Every sentence uses passive voice.
🟣 Side A — AI Must Be Regulated Now
"If AI isn't controlled, people will be harmed — and nobody will be held responsible."
  • Biased decisions have already been made by AI systems in hiring and lending.
  • Personal data is being collected by AI tools without users fully understanding this.
  • Millions of jobs could be replaced without any compensation being offered.
  • Harmful content is generated by AI and spread faster than it can be removed.
  • In other industries — aviation, medicine — safety standards are enforced. AI should be treated the same.
🟢 Side B — Innovation Must Not Be Blocked
"If AI is over-regulated, progress will be slowed and competitive advantage will be lost."
  • Medical diagnoses are being improved by AI — regulation could slow life-saving tools.
  • If too many restrictions are introduced, innovation will be driven to less responsible countries.
  • Existing laws can be applied — new regulations aren't always needed.
  • Many AI benefits — accessibility tools, translation, education — would be limited unnecessarily.
  • The technology is being developed by companies that already are incentivized to avoid harm.
"AI should be regulated because…" "Jobs are being replaced by…" "Data is collected without…" "If limits are imposed…" "I'm not sure I trust it because…"
How to run: 90s per side, 30s rebuttal each. Award 1 pt per correct passive. Point out the present continuous passives in Side B — "is being improved / is being developed" — these are B1.1+ structures and excellent for strong students to attempt.
Key vocab to reinforce: "regulate / regulated / regulation" — students often confuse the noun and verb. Write all three on the board.
§3

🎤 Relay — Transform to Passive (AI Context)

10 min
Active → Passive. Real AI context. Say your answer before revealing.
Revealed: 0 / 5
Card 1 — Present Passive
Active: "AI tools generate thousands of fake images every day."
→ Transform. Focus on "thousands of fake images." Start with that.
✅ "Thousands of fake images are generated by AI tools every day." — Present passive. The images are the focus, not the tools. Drop "by AI tools" if the context is already clear.
Card 2 — Past Passive
Active: "The EU introduced strict AI regulation laws in 2024."
→ Transform. Start with "Strict AI regulation laws…"
✅ "Strict AI regulation laws were introduced by the EU in 2024." — Past passive. Keep "by the EU" — it adds important context about who is responsible for the regulation.
Card 3 — Passive Negative
Active: "Companies don't always disclose what data they collect."
→ Transform to passive negative. Start with "What data is collected…"
✅ "What data is collected is not always disclosed by companies." — Two passives in one sentence. Alternatively: "The data that is collected isn't always disclosed." Both are natural in tech journalism.
Card 4 — Future Passive
Active: "Governments will create new departments to oversee AI systems."
→ Transform to future passive. Start with "New departments…"
✅ "New departments will be created by governments to oversee AI systems." — Future passive. "by governments" is kept because it specifies who creates them — politically important information in this context.
Card 5 — Challenge: Present Continuous Passive
Active: "Engineers are currently testing the new AI detection system."
→ Transform to present continuous passive (is/are being + p.p.). Challenge level!
✅ "The new AI detection system is being tested by engineers currently." — Present continuous passive: is/are + being + past participle. This is a B1+ structure. Excellent for strong students — it describes an action in progress right now being done to something.
Card 5 is the extension point: Present continuous passive ("is being tested") is B1.1+. Strong students can attempt it. Don't require it from everyone — but name it, write it on the board, and say: "This is what B1.2 looks like." Seeds the next level.
Points: 2 pts per correct transformation + valid reason. Card 5 earns 3 pts.

🎤 Solo Challenge — 2 Minutes

8 min
Two minutes. AI and your job. No notes. Full passive voice.
🎤 SOLO — AI and Your Professional Life (2 min)
You are presenting to a colleague who knows nothing about AI. Explain how AI affects — or could affect — your specific job and industry:
What is already automated in your industry? What could be replaced by AI in the future? What cannot be replaced — and why? Should AI be regulated in your sector? Passive voice minimum 3 times
Scoring (log in leaderboard §7): 3 pts — correct passive structures (min 3) · 2 pts — vocabulary from S3A (automation, bias, regulation, data, tool) · 2 pts — clear opinion on AI in their sector · 1 pt — fluency · 1 pt — colloquial phrase used naturally. Max: 9 pts.
Strongest prompt for engineers: "In manufacturing, quality control is already being done by AI systems — defects are detected automatically. But decisions about shutdowns? That still requires a human." This uses present continuous passive AND simple present passive in one context they know.

🔍 Source Analysis — AI Around the World

8 min
Four real-world perspectives. Find the passive. Answer the questions.
🇪🇺 EU AI Act — Summary (adapted)
"High-risk AI systems must be certified before they are deployed. Transparency is required: users must be informed when AI is being used. Systems that are considered to pose unacceptable risk will be banned entirely."
❓ What must happen before a high-risk AI system can be used?
🏭 Industry Report — Bajío Manufacturing (adapted)
"In Celaya and Querétaro, AI-assisted quality control systems have been installed in over 40 plants since 2022. Defects are now detected at a rate 3x faster than manual inspection. Workers have been retrained for higher-value tasks. No significant layoffs have been reported."
❓ What has happened to workers in these plants?
🎙️ Tech Ethicist — Podcast (adapted)
"The problem isn't that AI is being used — it's that it's being used without enough transparency. When a loan is denied by an AI system, the applicant isn't told why. When someone is rejected for a job because of an algorithm, no explanation is given. That's not acceptable."
❓ What does the ethicist think is the main problem with current AI use?
💬 LinkedIn Post — Mexican Engineer (real quote, adapted)
"I use AI every day. My reports are generated in half the time. Patterns are detected that I would have missed. Am I worried about being replaced? Honestly — not yet. But I think the people who should be worried are the ones who refuse to learn how to use it."
❓ What is the engineer's opinion about job security and AI?
Passive count challenge: Ask students to count passive structures across all 4 sources. There are at least 16. Then ask: which ones could drop "by" without losing meaning? This bridges the S3 content with the S2 "by" lesson.
Discussion hook: The LinkedIn quote is the most relatable. "¿Están de acuerdo con este ingeniero? ¿Usan AI en su trabajo?" → "Do you agree? Is AI already being used in your job?" Present continuous passive — plant it again.
