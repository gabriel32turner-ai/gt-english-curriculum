🌟 Warm-Up — Hobbies & Personal Time

5–8 min
Module 5 · Session 2A — Hobbies & Personal Time · Remember from last session: Reported Speech
🔁 Remember from S1A: We practiced Reported Speech — "She said she was tired." / "He told me they could handle it." Today we use the same structure to talk about something more personal: what people say about hobbies and their free time.
Q1 — Opening
Do you have a hobby? When did you start it? Has a hobby ever taught you something you use at work?
Q2 — Time
How much personal time do you actually have in a normal week? Is it enough? What do you do with it?
Q3 — Balance
Do you feel guilty when you spend time on something that is just for you — not productive, not work? Why?
Q4 — Community
Do any of your colleagues share a hobby or interest with you? Has a shared interest ever improved a work relationship?
⚡ Quick Win — 20 seconds
Say this out loud right now: "My friend told me that playing guitar helped him disconnect from work completely."
Notice the structure. We will use exactly this pattern today — Reported Speech in a personal context.
Teacher note — Warm-Up: Q3 (guilt about personal time) is your emotional hook for the session. Most working adults in manufacturing/logistics feel this. Do not rush past it. If a student says "I don't really have hobbies anymore," that is your entry point: "What did you used to do? When did you stop?" Then: "What would your past self tell you right now?" This primes the grammar work in §5 naturally.

S1A connection: Before Q1, ask: "Does anyone remember one Reported Speech sentence from last class?" Take one answer, write it on the board, leave it there for reference.
Pedagogical layer: Hobbies is one of the most underestimated topics for B1 learners — it generates spontaneous, authentic language because students care about the content. It also expands vocabulary into areas (creativity, passion, community) that are directly transferable to professional self-presentation. A student who can talk about their passion fluently is a student who can do a job interview.

🗣️ Speaking Topic — Hobbies & Personal Time

8 min

Has a hobby ever taught you something useful for work?

Today's central question. Think beyond the obvious — patience, creativity, discipline, problem-solving. What has a personal activity given you that your job never could?

Hobbies & Personal Time · 3 Discussion Rounds
T→S Round 1
Tell me: what is one activity outside of work that genuinely recharges you? Why that specific thing?
S↔S Round 2
Ask your partner: "Has your hobby ever taught you a skill or quality you use at work?" Then report their answer to the class: "My partner said her hobby had taught her..."
Class Round 3
Hot debate: "Companies should give employees paid time for personal development and hobbies." Should this be part of the contract? Agree or disagree with a reason.

Hobbies & work connection — natural B1.1 response:

"My hobby is photography. I've been doing it for about three years, and I do it to disconnect completely from work. But honestly, it has taught me more than I expected. A friend of mine told me that photography had made him more patient — and I agree. You wait for the right moment. You develop an eye for detail. Those are exactly the skills I use in quality control. I lose track of time when I'm shooting — and that's rare for me."

Note: S2 vocabulary · Reported Speech · S2 colloquial phrase

Teacher note — Speaking: Round 2 is your Reported Speech drill disguised as a conversation. After students share, push: "How exactly did you say that? Use 'My partner said...'" If they use direct speech ('She said “I like cooking”'), gently redirect: "And in reported form?" This creates natural drilling without the student feeling drilled.

Error anticipation: Students will say "My hobby teach me..." → "My hobby has taught me" or "My hobby taught me." Both correct at B1.1 — but note which they use and build on it in §5.

💬 Colloquial English — Personal Time Phrases

5 min
3 colloquial phrases your colleagues actually use about hobbies and free time
"It helps me relax."
When to use
When explaining why you do a hobby or activity. "I started running last year — it really helps me relax after a stressful week." Simple, natural, immediately understood.
"I lose track of time."
When to use
When describing a hobby or activity that fully absorbs you. "When I'm cooking something new, I completely lose track of time — it's the best feeling." Shows passion naturally.
"I need that time for myself."
When to use
When explaining why personal time is non-negotiable for you. "Sunday mornings are mine — I need that time for myself to reset before the week." Assertive and healthy.
"It's something I picked up recently."
When to use
When mentioning a new hobby casually. "I picked up cycling recently — it's still new but I'm really enjoying it." Sounds natural and conversational.
Teacher note — Colloquial drill: "I lose track of time" is the richest one for speaking practice. Ask: "Which activity makes you lose track of time?" Take 4 answers. Then drill the Reported Speech version: "What did your colleague say?" → "She said that cooking made her lose track of time." Two-in-one: colloquial + grammar.

False friend alert: "I lost the count of time" is a direct translation from Spanish (“perdí la cuenta del tiempo”) — not natural in English. The correct phrase is "I lost track of time" or "I lost count." Point this out explicitly.
Upsell layer: The phrase "I need that time for myself" is something many students have never said confidently in English. After the drill, ask: "Could you say this to your manager or a colleague?" Take responses. This is the moment where language becomes real permission to set limits — connecting back to S1A vocabulary (boundary, protect). Name the connection.
§5

⚡ Grammar — Reported Speech: Questions & Requests

12–15 min
Grammar · Reporting questions and requests · The next level of Reported Speech
🎯 Real Work Situation

In your last performance review, your manager asked: "What do you do to recharge outside of work? Do you have any hobbies?"

Later, a colleague asks you: "What did the manager ask you?" You know how to report statements from S1A — but how do you report a question? The word order changes.

🔧 The Tool — Reporting Questions

When reporting a question, do not keep the question word order. The reported question becomes a statement (subject + verb), not a question. Use asked instead of "said."

“What do you do to recharge?” → She asked me what I did to recharge.
“Do you have any hobbies?” → He asked me if I had any hobbies.
“Can you describe your passion?” → She asked me to describe my passion. (request)
Direct QuestionReported QuestionKey rule
"Where do you work?"She asked me where I worked.No question word order · tense shifts
"Are you enjoying it?"He asked if I was enjoying it.Yes/No questions use "if" or "whether"
"Can you help me?"She asked me to help her.Requests use "asked + infinitive"
"What time will you arrive?"He asked what time I would arrive.Will → would · statement word order
Common errors:
✗ "She asked me what did I do." → She asked me what I did. (no inversion in reported questions)
✗ "He asked me if did I have hobbies." → He asked me if I had hobbies.
✗ "She asked me that I help her." → She asked me to help her. (requests use infinitive)
🗣️ Micro-Speaking: Think of a question your manager or a colleague asked you recently. Report it to your partner: "She asked me if..." or "He asked me what..." One sentence. Make it real. 45 seconds.
Teacher note — Grammar S2A: This is the natural progression from S1A. Students who mastered statement reporting find question reporting challenging because of the word order rule. The diagnostic error to watch for is inversion (“what did I do” instead of “what I did”). Use the board: write the direct question, then erase the auxiliary and re-order — visual transformation is faster than explanation.

GT Originals — Teacher Insights
  • The word order rule (no inversion) is the most memorable insight of the whole module. Make students write it on a sticky note: "Reported question = no question order." Physical writing helps retention.
  • Job interview connection: "The interviewer asked me what my strengths were" / "She asked me if I had experience with..." — this is immediately applicable and students feel it.
  • Reported requests ("She asked me to...") often feel strange to Spanish speakers — they expect a que + subjunctive structure. Point out the simplicity of the English pattern: asked + person + to + infinitive.

🎧 Listening — A Conversation About Personal Time

10–12 min
Listening · Rodrigo talks about his weekend hobby · Vertex Logistics, Celaya

Rodrigo Vega, plant manager at Vertex Logistics in Celaya, tells a colleague about a conversation he had with his daughter about his hobbies — and what he learned from it. Listen carefully for both Reported Speech statements AND reported questions.

Rodrigo and the Weekend Question — Vertex Logistics, Celaya
B1.1 · M5 · S2A · Voice: Adam (American English) · Speed: 0.85 · ElevenLabs
You know, something interesting happened last weekend. My daughter asked me what I did on weekends besides work. I thought it was a strange question at first. I told her I watched football. She said that was not a hobby, that was just watching other people do something. She asked me if I had ever had a real passion outside of work. I thought about it seriously. I told her that I used to play the guitar when I was younger. She said she had not known that. She asked me why I had stopped. I said I had stopped because work got very busy and I never found the time again. She told me that was exactly the problem. She said that her generation talked a lot about hobbies and personal time, and that her friends said it helped them recharge and be more creative at work. She asked me to think about one activity I could pick up again, even for just thirty minutes a week. I told her I would think about it. Last Saturday morning I found my old guitar in the closet. I played for about forty minutes. I have to say, I completely lost track of time. It was the first time in years that I had done something just for myself. My daughter called in the evening and asked how the day had been. I told her it had been a very good day. She asked if I had found the guitar. I said yes. She asked me what it felt like. I said it felt like I had found something I had been missing for a long time.
Listening note: This script is deliberately designed to contain both reported statements AND reported questions, naturally embedded in a personal narrative. Before the second listen, ask students: "Count how many questions Rodrigo reports." The answer is 6. This becomes a fluency check — students who notice the word order inversion rule will catch it.

1. What did Rodrigo's daughter ask him?
2. Why had Rodrigo stopped playing guitar?
3. What happened last Saturday morning?
✅ True / False

🌟 Production — Speaking Practice

12–15 min
Production · Talk about your personal life in English · Report it, own it
🎯 Class Opinion Meter
"Having a hobby outside of work makes you a better employee — not just a happier person."
Strongly DisagreeStrongly Agree
Class: 50% agree
T→S Question 1 — Your Hobby
Tell me about a hobby or activity you pursue outside of work. When did you start? Has it taught you anything? Speak for 60 seconds without stopping.
Vocabulary hint: use "pursue," "develop," "recharge," "disconnect," "creativity"
S↔S Question 2 — Interview Simulation
Partner A: ask Partner B three questions about their hobbies and personal time. Partner B: answer. Then switch. Afterwards, each person reports what their partner asked: "She asked me if..." / "He asked me what..."
Grammar hint: Report the questions, not just the answers — use the word order rule from §5
S↔S Question 3 — Reported Advice
Think of the best advice you ever received about work-life balance — from a friend, family member, or colleague. Report it to your partner: "She told me that..." or "He asked me to..." Did you follow the advice?
Grammar hint: statements use "said/told" · requests use "asked me to..."
Class Question 4 — Reconnect
Is there something you used to do — a hobby, a sport, a creative activity — that you stopped because of work? What happened? Could you pick it up again? Like Rodrigo, what would your past self say to you now?
Vocabulary hint: "I used to..." / "I gave it up when..." / "My past self would tell me that..."
Class Question 5 — Pitch Your Hobby
Convince the class to try your hobby. Give 3 reasons why it is worth their time. Use: "My friend told me that..." to add social proof, and at least one reported question someone asked you about it.
Grammar hint: Combine reported statements + reported questions in one pitch
⚡ Solo Challenge — 90 seconds

Describe a conversation you had recently — real or imagined — about hobbies and personal time. Use at least 2 reported statements AND 2 reported questions. Include what was asked and what was answered.

Teacher note — Q5 Pitch: This is the session's peak speaking moment. If time allows, let two or three students do the pitch and have the class vote on whose hobby they would most like to try. The voting creates authentic listening motivation — students need to actually understand to vote. Debrief: "Which Reported Speech structure did you hear most?"
Speaking layers — Q2: Layer 1: Did they use Reported Speech at all, or default to direct? Layer 2: Did they shift word order correctly in reported questions? Layer 3: Did they integrate both types (statements + questions) naturally? Use the board to show one student's sentence — upgrade it together as a class. This is speaking-as-grammar-instruction at its most efficient.
🎯 This week, you can use these 3 phrases at work
3 Phrases for This Week
PHRASE 1
"My colleague asked me what I did to recharge."
A natural Reported Question in conversation. Use any time you retell a workplace exchange. It is more professional than direct speech and signals English fluency immediately. Replace "recharge" with any topic: "what I thought about the project," "how I managed the deadline."
PHRASE 2
"I lose track of time when I do it."
The most authentic thing you can say about a passion. Use it in any casual conversation about a hobby or activity. It instantly tells the listener that you genuinely care about something — a rare and attractive quality in professional contexts.
PHRASE 3
"I need that time for myself."
A clear, confident boundary statement. Use it when explaining why you do not respond to emails on weekends, or why you protect certain hours. In English, this is not selfish — it is healthy self-management. Use it without apology.

🏆 Wrap-Up

5–8 min
Module 5 · S2A Complete — Hobbies & Personal Time · Well done
01
Reported questions = no inversion. "She asked me what I did." Not "what did I do." Remove the auxiliary, keep subject-verb order. This is the rule. Write it. Remember it.
02
Yes/No questions use “if” or “whether.” "Do you have a hobby?" → "She asked me if I had a hobby." The conjunction signals it is a yes/no type. Both "if" and "whether" are correct.
03
Requests = asked + to. "Help me" → "She asked me to help her." No "that." No "if." Just: asked + person + to + infinitive. Clean and professional.
