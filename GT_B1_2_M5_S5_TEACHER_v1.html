§2   Grammar Consolidation  ·  12 min

Modal Deduction — Complete System

THE COMPLETE PICTURE — ALL 5 FORMS
TimeCertaintyStructureM5 Example
PresentAlmost certain ✓must be / must verbShe must be sponsored — nobody recommends the same product 10 times for free.
PresentAlmost certain ✗can’t be / can’t verbThat story can’t be true — the source is completely anonymous.
PresentPossible / uncertainmight be / could beThe algorithm might be showing you only content that confirms your views.
PastAlmost certain ✓must have + past participleSomeone must have leaked the photos — they were private.
PastAlmost certain ✗can’t have + past participleShe can’t have known about the rule — the disclosure law only passed last year.
⚠ NEVER: “must to be” / “can’t to have” — no “to” after modal verbs.

💬 Quick: Create your own sentence about any M5 topic using must have OR can’t have. Say it out loud. Then your partner challenges: “Why are you so sure?”
Activity A spans all 4 module topics deliberately. The pattern shift (must → can’t → might → must have) is the full system in one exercise. Activity B Sentence 2 with “can’t have known” is the construction learners find most difficult — two auxiliaries plus a past participle. If errors persist here, write the formula: SUBJECT + can’t have + V3. The micro challenge (“Why are you so sure?”) forces learners to metalinguistically justify their modal choice — the highest order of grammar awareness at B1.2.
§3   Integrated Reading  ·  10 min

Carlos Mendez — Module Closing Reflection

Four sessions ago, I asked you a question that made some people uncomfortable: if algorithms decide what news you see, can you ever really trust your own opinion?

Over the past weeks, we’ve looked at four ways that media shapes what we think, feel, and buy. Influencers must be among the most powerful persuasion tools ever invented — because they feel like friends, not advertisements. Reality TV might be the most sophisticated behavioural experiment ever conducted at scale, with millions of willing participants who pay for the experience themselves.

Fake news can’t be solved by fact-checking alone, because the problem isn’t information — it’s emotion. And celebrity culture must have been with us since the first humans told stories around a fire — the technology changes, but the human need for narrative and admiration does not.

So what do you do with all of this? You learn to watch with better eyes. You ask: whose interest does this serve? What am I being sold? What am I comparing myself to? And then — and this is the part most people skip — you tell your own story. Not theirs.

That is what today’s session is about. Your pitch. Your narrative. Your words.

Carlos Mendez — Module 5 Closing:

I want to tell you something about stories. Every session of this module, you were exposed to someone else’s story — an influencer’s curated life, a reality show’s manufactured drama, a journalist’s headline, a celebrity’s carefully constructed persona. All of it, stories. All of it, designed to make you feel something and then act on that feeling.

The most powerful thing you can do in response is learn to tell your own. In English. With confidence. Without notes.

That’s what a pitch is. A pitch is not a presentation. It’s not a report. It’s a story that makes someone want to listen, and then want to know more. The best pitches I’ve seen — for products, for ideas, for films — all had the same thing: a clear character, a problem, and a reason to care.

When you pitch your film or series today, you’re not describing a plot. You’re making someone want to watch it. That’s a different skill. And it’s one of the most valuable things you can do in English in any professional context.

Two minutes. No notes. Your story. Go.

§5   Capstone — The Pitch  ·  20 min

Your 2-Minute Pitch — No Notes

🎬 Pitch Your Favourite Movie or Series
You have 2 minutes. No script. No notes. Sell your chosen film or series to the room — make them want to watch it tonight.

This is the GT Original S5 Capstone for Module 5. It should be one of the most memorable sessions of the level.
🎯 YOUR PITCH STRUCTURE (30 seconds to plan):
  • Hook: One sentence that grabs attention. Start with the twist, the theme, or an unusual detail — not the plot.
  • Setup: Genre + setting + central character. Keep it fast: 20 seconds maximum.
  • Conflict: What is the problem or tension that drives the story? This is what makes it compelling.
  • Theme: What is it really about, beneath the surface? (Use modal deduction: “It might seem like X, but it must be about Y.”)
  • Closer: Why must someone watch this? What can’t they miss? End with a reason to care — not a summary.
FLUENCY
Speaks for 2 full minutes with natural pace. No excessive pausing or repetition.
VOCABULARY
Uses S5 storytelling words accurately: plot, character, twist, theme, narrative, genre.
ENGAGEMENT
The pitch makes the listener genuinely want to watch. They’d remember this film tomorrow.
MODAL DEDUCTION
Uses must / can’t / might naturally to analyse or describe the film’s content or appeal.
COHERENCE
The pitch has a clear structure — hook, setup, conflict, theme, closer.
NATURALNESS
Sounds like genuine recommendation, not a book report. Emotional investment is visible.
2-MINUTE PITCH TIMER
PEER LISTENER CHECKLIST
GT Original: announce the topic at the end of S4A, not here. Learners who prepare will perform better. This is intentional — preparation in L2 is a skill. During pitches: do NOT interrupt or correct. Listen fully. After each pitch: 30 seconds of peer feedback using the listener checklist. Then teacher closes with 1-2 language observations from what they actually heard — not pre-planned, from real listening. This is the GT difference. Record if possible — learners are always surprised at how good they sound. That surprise is the moment of belief.
💰 After the last pitch: “You just pitched in English — without notes, for 2 minutes, to a real audience. That’s not intermediate English. That’s professional English. Module 6 is ‘Your Future, Your English.’ The final capstone is a 3-minute unscripted talk: where do you see yourself in 5 years? By then, you’ll be ready for it. And you should be. You’ve come a long way.”
