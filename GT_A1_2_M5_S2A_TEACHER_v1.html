T-S

Think: take a message / hold the line / call back
"I'm sorry, Mónica is in a meeting right now. Can I take a message? — Or: She's not available at the moment. Could you call back after 3 PM?"
S-S
Role play: Partner A calls your company and asks for Carlos in Logistics. Partner B receives the call. Transfer it — or take a message if he's not there.
Use: "Hold the line, please." / "I'll put you through." / "Can I take a message?"
B: "Good afternoon, ConnectPro. How can I help you?" A: "Hi, can I speak to Carlos in Logistics?" B: "Of course, hold the line, please… I'm sorry, he's not available. Can I take a message?" A: "Yes, please. Tell him Diego called. My number is 477 123 4567."
Class
At your company, what do you do when you receive a call in English and you don't understand the person? What is your strategy?
Tip phrases: "Sorry, could you repeat that?" / "Could you speak more slowly, please?"
"When I don't understand, I say: 'I'm sorry, could you repeat that more slowly, please?' And if I still don't understand, I say: 'I'm sorry, let me transfer you to my colleague who speaks better English.'"
TEACHER NOTE — Role Play Management

Round 2 is the key activity. Walk the room during pair role plays. Listen for: (1) students saying "Espérate" instead of "Hold the line" — correct warmly. (2) Students forgetting to say who they are when answering — remind them: company name + your name. After Round 3, do a class share: What is YOUR real strategy when you get an English call you don't understand? This generates authentic speaking and reduces phone anxiety — which is the biggest barrier at A1.2.

Colloquial — Real Phone English
4 phrases you hear on every corporate call
5 min
"Hold on, please."
The short version of "Hold the line." Slightly more casual but still professional. Used on fast-paced calls. "Hold on, please — let me check that for you."
"I'll put you through."
Natural way to say you are transferring the call. More fluent than "I transfer you." Example: "Of course — I'll put you through to the purchasing department now."
"Can I take a message?"
You offer to write down information for someone else. Polite and professional. "She's not in right now — can I take a message?" Then: name + number + brief reason for the call.
"I'm not sure — let me check."
Buys you time without sounding unprepared. Very natural. "Is Carlos available today?" — "I'm not sure — let me check and I'll get back to you." Use with confidence.
TEACHER NOTE — Colloquial Drill

Drill as a call script: Teacher is the caller, students are the receptionist. Teacher says: "Can I speak to Mónica?" → Student: "I'm afraid she's not available — can I take a message?" → "Yes, please." → "Of course, go ahead." → After name and number: "Thank you — I'll make sure she gets the message." Run the drill 3 times with different students. This exact sequence covers 80% of real corporate phone calls at A1.2 level.

5
Grammar — "Could you…?" for Polite Phone Requests
Problem → Tool format · Object pronouns in phone context
12–15 min
⚠️ REAL WORK SITUATION

You need to ask your supplier to send a document. You say: "Send me the invoice." The supplier feels it is rude — you lost the relationship.

❌ In English, direct imperatives on the phone sound aggressive. The tool that fixes this is "Could you…?" — one phrase, 100% professional. And then: object pronouns — so you say "send it to me" instead of "send the invoice to me every time."

"Could you…?" — Polite Phone Request

Too direct ❌Polite with "Could you…?" ✅
Send me the report.Could you send me the report?
Call her back.Could you call her back, please?
Give him my number.Could you give him my number?
Repeat that.Could you repeat that, please?
Transfer me to Sales.Could you transfer me to Sales?

Object Pronouns — Use Them on the Phone

PronounReplacesPhone example

TEACHER NOTE — Grammar Delivery

Use the PROBLEM card to establish why this matters — tone on the phone is critical in business English. Drill the transformation: say a direct request → students convert it using "Could you…?" Do 4-5 rapid examples before the activity. For object pronouns, the key error is "I'll ask to she" → "I'll ask her." Write on board: Subject + verb + OBJECT pronoun. The pronoun comes after the verb, not after "to" in this context.

Listening — A Corporate Phone Call
Mónica Herrera · ConnectPro International · Guadalajara
8–10 min
Corporate Phone Call — ConnectPro International, Guadalajara
A1.2 · M5 · S2A · Voice: Sarah & James (American English) · Speed: 0.80
ElevenLabs Script — ~160 words · ~60 seconds at 0.80

[Phone rings]

Receptionist: Good afternoon, ConnectPro International. This is Andrea speaking. How can I help you?

Caller: Hello. My name is Daniel Ochoa. I'm calling from Solaris Manufacturing in Celaya. Could I speak to Mónica Herrera, please?

Receptionist: Of course, Mr. Ochoa. One moment, please — I'll check for you. ... I'm afraid Mónica is in a meeting right now. She isn't available until 4 PM. Can I take a message?

Caller: Yes, please. Could you tell her I called about the Q3 contract? It's urgent. Could you ask her to call me back before 5 PM?

Receptionist: Of course. So — Daniel Ochoa, Solaris Manufacturing, about the Q3 contract, call back before 5 PM. Is that correct?

Caller: Yes, that's right. My direct number is 461 147 2119. Extension 305.

Receptionist: Perfect. I'll give her the message as soon as the meeting finishes. Thank you for calling, Mr. Ochoa.

Caller: Thank you very much. Goodbye.

Receptionist: Goodbye!

Comprehension — Choose the correct answer

1. Who is calling?

✅ Daniel Ochoa from Solaris Manufacturing in Celaya.

2. Why is Mónica not available?

✅ She is in a meeting and isn't available until 4 PM.

3. What does Daniel ask Mónica to do?

✅ "Could you ask her to call me back before 5 PM?"

4. What is Daniel's extension number?

✅ Extension 305.

5. What does the receptionist do with the message?

✅ She reads the message back: "Daniel Ochoa, Q3 contract, call back before 5 PM" — always confirm messages on the phone.
🗣️ MICRO-SPEAKING
From memory: Summarize the call in 2 sentences. What did Daniel want? What did the receptionist say she would do?
TEACHER NOTE — Listening Analysis

After the first listen for gist, play again and have students raise their hand each time they hear a phone phrase from §3. Second pass: count how many "Could you…?" requests Daniel makes (answer: 2). This shows students the grammar is natural and frequent. The receptionist confirming the message back is a key professional skill — discuss: "Do you confirm messages at work? In English? Why is it important?"

TEACHER NOTE — Speaking Practice Management

Q5 (voicemail) is the highlight — have students leave a real voicemail using the teacher's phone or a student's phone with speaker on. The class listens and evaluates: Did they include name + reason + number? Q6 (3-person role play) works best standing up — assign roles quickly and give 90 seconds. The "messenger" student is the most challenged — they must relay the message in English. This is exactly what happens in Bajío manufacturing companies every week.

TEACHER NOTE — Opinion Meter

This question always generates strong opinions. Ask 2-3 students who disagreed: "What makes phone calls easier for you?" Common answer: engineers say calls are faster and less formal. Ask 2-3 who agreed: "What makes it harder?" Common answer: can't re-read / accents / can't see the person. Use this to validate student fears AND show them that today's 4 phrases reduce that stress by 80%.
