Hot Take — Hobbies & Identity

10 min
Hot Take · Defend your hobby · 90 seconds · No mercy
Today's Hot Take
"Companies that invest in employee mental health will have better business results — and the data already proves it."
✅ Agree
Mental health investment reduces absenteeism, improves retention, and increases productivity. The ROI is documented. This is not charity — it is strategy.
✗ Disagree
Companies say they care but most initiatives are cosmetic. A poster about mindfulness does not address structural overwork, toxic management, or job insecurity. Real change requires structural investment.
T→S Round 1 — Your Hot Take (90 sec)

State your position + use ONE reported question as evidence: "A friend asked me once if I had a real passion outside of work — and I didn't have an answer." Use vocabulary: pursue, recharge, community, creativity, disconnect.

S↔S Round 2 — Ask & Report (60 sec)

Partner A asks Partner B: "Do you think your company takes mental health seriously?" Then A reports: "I asked my partner whether... She said that... She also told me that... Then I asked her if..." Use precision: do NOT say "she IS struggling" — say "she WAS."

Teacher note: Round 2 is the S2A grammar review in disguise — reporting a question you yourself asked. This is the productive inverse of S2A: students now generate the reported question, not just transform one. Listen for "I asked her if she had" (correct) vs. "I asked her if she has" (tense error). Correct between rounds, not during.

Pitch/Debate — The Mental Health Proposal

15 min
Pitch · Propose a real mental health initiative · Use Reported Speech as evidence from S4A
T→S Pitch Round 1 — The Wellbeing Proposal (2 min)
Pitch your hobby to the class. Convince them to try it. Structure: (1) What it is and how you started — (2) What it has given you: use vocabulary from S2A (creativity, community, skill, recharge) — (3) Social proof: "People who do this often say that..." / "A friend told me it had changed the way she..." — (4) Close: what you would ask someone to try first.
Grammar requirement: at least 2 reported statements + 1 reported question, with correct precision tense choices. Remember: "said she WAS," not "said she IS."
S↔S Debate Round 2 — The Leadership Challenge (90 sec)
After the pitch, your partner plays a skeptic: "You said this hobby helped you disconnect — but I want to know: did you ask yourself whether you actually had time for it? How do you manage?" The pitcher must respond and defend using more Reported Speech.
Skeptic pattern: "You said... but..." / "You told us... What did you mean by...?"
Class Debate Round 3 — The Company Vote (2 min)
Class votes: whose pitch was most convincing? The winner summarizes the class's decision using Reported Speech: "Several people said they would try my hobby because... Someone asked me whether... and I told them..."
S4A grammar victory lap: precision reported speech in a professional advocacy context.
Teacher note — Wellbeing Proposal: This round works best when students draw on real experiences from their companies. Do not push personal disclosure — professional observations are enough: "I have noticed that..." or "HR said at the last meeting that..." The precision element is critical here: flag any student who uses present tense in reported speech and correct immediately but gently.
Upsell layer: After the best pitch, ask: "If you were in a job interview and they asked 'What do you do outside of work?' — could you answer like that in English?" Most students will say they couldn't before this session. Now they can. Name that transformation.

Relay — The HR Report Chain

10 min
Relay · The HR Report Chain · Report the mental health meeting through 4 professional voices
🏃 HR Report Chain — 4 Professional Voices
Voice 1
📋 The Attendee
Was at the HR meeting
Voice 2
💬 The Colleague
Asks what happened
Voice 3
🆕 The Manager
Asks their team for feedback
Voice 4
🏆 The Director
Presents to company leadership
How it works:
Voice 1 reports the HR meeting: "The HR director said the company had acknowledged the problem."
Voice 2 asks: "Did they say what had caused it?" Voice 1 answers, Voice 2 reports to Voice 3.
Voice 3 (manager) asks their team: "What did Voice 2 tell you the HR director had said?"
Voice 4 presents to leadership: "The team reported that HR had said... / A colleague asked whether... / The manager told us that..."

Target language: Precision tense throughout. Sensitive content requires exact reported forms — this is where S4A grammar meets real professional stakes.
Starter sentences for Voice 1 (from S4A listening — teacher chooses)
"The HR director said the company had decided to take wellbeing more seriously."
"She said she knew that some people had been struggling in silence."
"She told us that from that month, every employee would have access to six free therapy sessions."
Teacher note — Relay debrief: After Role 4 presents, ask Role 1: "Is that what you said? Is anything different?" The accuracy comparison is the learning moment. Differences between what was said and what was reported surface both grammar errors (tense drift) and information distortion. Both are instructive. Two rounds minimum to give everyone a role.

Speed Round — 30 Seconds Each

10 min
Speed Round · 30 seconds · No pausing · Hobbies & Reported Questions

30 seconds, one student, no stopping. Rotate. All 6 must be completed. Timer below applies to each prompt.

Prompt 1
Report what the HR director said in the S4A listening — from memory. Use: "She said... She also told us... She acknowledged that... She asked us whether..."
Prompt 2
Report a question someone asked you about your free time. Use "She asked me if..." or "He asked me what..."
Prompt 3
Use the phrase "I lose track of time" in a sentence. Then say what someone told you about their hobby using Reported Speech.
Prompt 4
Report a request: "She asked me to try her hobby." Add what you said in response. "I told her that..."
Prompt 5
Why do YOU need personal time? Use: "I need that time for myself because..." and include one thing someone said about it.
Prompt 6
Say "It's okay to ask for help" in a sentence. Then report what someone said to you (or you wish someone had said) when you were having a hard time at work.
Completed: 0 / 6

Solo Challenge — The Mental Health Advocate

15 min
Solo Challenge · Mental Health Advocacy · 2 minutes · No notes
🎯 The Challenge Brief

Your company's leadership asks: “We are launching a mental health initiative. What have employees told you they need? And what have they said about the current culture?”

Advocate for your team to company leadership. In 2 minutes, cover:
• What employees have said they need — at least 2 reported statements with precision tense
• What the HR director said or asked in S4A — reported accurately
• What someone personally told you about stigma or wellbeing — precise past tense
• What initiative you propose and why — include a reported question as framing
• Connectors: "She said... She also told us... Then she asked... I told her that..."

No notes. Module 5 capstone production moment.
Teacher Rubric — Click when met
2 Precision Statements
"She said she had been feeling..." NOT "she said she is." Tense accuracy checked
Reported Question
"She asked me if / what / whether..." + no inversion
Vocabulary
3+ S4A words: stigma, anxiety, wellbeing, psychological safety, normalize, acknowledge
Colloquial
"I lose track of time" / "I need that time" / "It helps me relax"
Professional tone
Language is measured, not dramatizing. Represents others accurately and with care
Fluency & time
Speaks 90+ seconds, no long pauses, coherent structure
Teacher note — Job Interview framing: The interviewer framing is a masterstroke for adult learners. It gives the activity real stakes that feel immediately meaningful. Most students have never answered this question in English before. After the challenge, say: "You just answered a real interview question. In English. For 2 minutes. That is what this program is for." This moment justifies the entire session.
