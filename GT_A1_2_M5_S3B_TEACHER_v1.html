T-S · Guided Write
Situation: You need to request a document from a supplier. Write a complete email — subject line, all 5 parts, minimum 2 connectors, 1 "Could you…?", closing phrase. 3 minutes. Topic: request the updated delivery schedule for June.
Subject + Dear… + "I'm writing to request…" + body with connectors + "Could you…?" + Thank you in advance + Best regards
"Subject: Request for June Delivery Schedule. Dear Mr. Torres, I'm writing to request the updated delivery schedule for June 2025. Could you send it to us before Friday, May 23rd? Also, could you confirm the contact person for deliveries at our Celaya warehouse? First, we need the schedule to plan our production line. Then, we need to notify our logistics team. Finally, please let us know if any dates change. Thank you in advance for your help. I look forward to hearing from you. Best regards, [Name] · Purchasing Coordinator · Solaris Manufacturing."
S-S · Peer Review
Exchange emails with your partner. Use the checklist: (1) Subject line? (2) "I'm writing to request…" opener? (3) At least 2 connectors? (4) "Could you…?" present? (5) Closing phrase? Give 1 positive + 1 improvement. Say it aloud — don't just write it.
Say: "Your email works well because… One thing to improve: replace X with Y."
"Your email works well because the subject line is clear and you used 'Could you send it to us' correctly. One thing to improve: you wrote 'First, we need' but then 'Also, we need' — use 'Then' for the second step, and 'Also' only when adding extra information, not a step in a sequence."
Class · Read & Fix Live
3 volunteers read their request email aloud. Teacher selects the most common error for a live class correction. Class rewrites the sentence together on the board — correct version stays visible for the rest of class.
Common S3B errors: wrong connector order / "Could you to send" (no infinitive) / missing sign-off title
Error 1: "First… Also… Then… Finally" — wrong order. Correct: First → Then → Also → Finally. Error 2: "Could you to send us?" — no "to" after "Could you." Correct: "Could you send us?" Error 3: Subject line: "Hi" — not professional. Replace with a noun phrase: "Request for Delivery Schedule — June."
TEACHER NOTE — S3B Write-Review Cycle

The most valuable moment of S3B is Round 2 — when students say feedback aloud, not just write it. This forces them to articulate what makes a professional email, which consolidates their own understanding. Walk the room during Round 1. Common gap: students write "First / Also / Then" in the wrong order — this is a thinking pattern issue, not a language issue. Address it in Round 3: "'Also' adds information at any point — it doesn't signal the next step. 'Then' signals the next step." Write the sequence on the board and leave it up all class.

Colloquial — Write It in a Real Email
FYI · Per our conversation · Let me know · Please confirm — applied
5 min
S3B Colloquial — Write the phrase in a real email opening
You called a supplier yesterday. Today you email them to follow up. Start with:
"Per our conversation yesterday, I'm writing to confirm…"
Complete the sentence with a real situation from your job. Say it aloud.
You're sharing delivery information with a colleague — no action needed from them. Start with:
"FYI — the delivery from Monterrey has been moved to Thursday at 8 AM."
Why is FYI correct here? Because no action is needed — just information.
You sent a report. You close your email with:
"Let me know if you have any questions."
Write 1 more sentence before this phrase — the body of the email. Then say the full email aloud.
You scheduled a meeting by email. Your last line before the sign-off:
"Please confirm your attendance by Thursday."
This is a polite imperative. Not rude — professional. Use it this week.
TEACHER NOTE — Colloquial Application S3B

Situation 1 ("Per our conversation") is the most powerful — it creates a paper trail and references the phone call from S2B. Tell students: "Every time you call a supplier in English, send a follow-up email starting with 'Per our conversation.' This is what international professionals expect — and it protects you if there's a dispute." FYI situation: ask students to think of one thing at work this week they could send as a FYI email in English. Concrete task = real-world transfer.

Send the report. — too direct

Activity A — Fix the Email Body

This email has 4 errors. Click the correct version for each underlined part.

1. "I am write to request the delivery schedule for June."

✅ Present continuous: "I am writing to…" — always use the -ing form in this fixed email opener.

2. "First, we need the schedule. Also, we will plan the production. Then, we will notify logistics. Finally, please confirm."

✅ "Also" adds extra information — it's not a sequence step. Correct order: First (step 1) → Then (step 2) → Also (extra info) → Finally (last step).

3. "Could you to send it to us before Friday?"

✅ "Could you" is followed directly by the base verb — no "to": "Could you SEND it to us?" Never "Could you TO send."

4. "Don't doubt to contact us if you have any questions."

✅ False friend! In Spanish "no dude en contactarnos" = "don't doubt" — but in English the correct phrase is "Don't HESITATE to contact us." "Doubt" is never used this way in English.

GT ORIGINAL — The Real Email Test

After students complete the Email Builder, ask: "Would you actually send this email to a real contact at your job? Not to a test — to a real person." If the answer is yes → badge b1 earned. If no → ask what needs to change. This question is the ultimate S3B production check. Students who hesitate usually identify one of two issues: (1) they're not sure about the grammar, or (2) the email feels too formal for their workplace. Both are valid — discuss which register fits their specific context.

TEACHER NOTE — Speaking Practice S3B

Q5 (email chain) is the S3B peak — a coherent request → confirmation chain is the most complex writing task of A1.2. Pairs who produce a coherent chain (request asks X, confirmation answers X using connectors) have demonstrated functional writing competence in English. Project 2 chains on screen for Q4 analysis — annotate them live: green for correct, yellow for improvable, no red. The last challenge (Q6) is the performance — select a confident student to dictate, but use a quieter student's email as the basis so everyone is the "author."

🎯 This Week at Work — Use These 3 Phrases
"Could you send it to us as soon as possible?"
Replace any urgent request at work with this phrase. "As soon as possible" + object pronoun = professional urgency without rudeness.
"Per our conversation, I'm writing to confirm…"
Send this as a follow-up after your next phone call in English. Three words that show you take notes and follow up — that's the reputation you want.
"First… Then… Finally…"
Write any multi-step instruction at work using these 3 connectors. Delivery procedures, system guides, HR forms — they all need this sequence in English.
