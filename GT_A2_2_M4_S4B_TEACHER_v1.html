Hot Take — The Monday Morning Clash
Two colleagues disagree · React in 60 seconds · Prove you know the difference
⏱ 5 MIN
The Situation
Luis says: "I'm going to present the Q3 results to the board on Thursday at 3 PM — the slides are ready." Andrea replies: "No Luis, you should say 'I'm presenting on Thursday' — it's in the calendar, both parties confirmed!" Luis says: "Going to and present continuous are the same thing — stop correcting me!"
Who is right? Why does it matter? Use what you learned in S4A to explain the difference and give Luis a real example that proves Andrea's point.
🎯 Rules: 60 seconds · Defend Andrea's position using a real-world explanation · Include 1 example with present continuous + 1 with going to that shows the difference · Badge 🔥 if your partner agrees Andrea is right and your grammar was correct
1:00
This Hot Take specifically tests the S4A core contrast. Both sentences are technically understandable — Luis is not "wrong" in the sense that communication breaks down. But Andrea is right about register and professional signaling: "I'm presenting" tells the board you're prepared and committed. "I'm going to present" leaves room for doubt. The goal is for students to articulate WHY — not just state the rule, but explain the real-world implication.
§2
Pitch Round — Calendar vs To-Do List
Side A lives by confirmed arrangements · Side B lives by intentions · Who runs a better week?
⏱ 10 MIN
Debate topic: "The best professionals put EVERYTHING in the calendar and speak in present continuous. The other kind makes plans and intentions that rarely happen."
Side A — Everything confirmed
"I'm doing it = it's in my calendar = it will happen."
  • "I'm reviewing the data at 9 — I blocked the time."
  • "I'm calling the supplier at 2 PM — both sides confirmed."
  • "If it's not in the calendar, it doesn't exist."
  • "Going to is for dreamers. Present continuous is for doers."
Side B — Flexible intentions
"I'm going to do great work — I don't need every minute in a calendar."
  • "I'm going to finish the report — when I have energy."
  • "Over-scheduling kills creativity and spontaneity."
  • "I'm going to call whoever I need to, when I need to."
  • "The calendar is a tool, not a prison."

Solo Challenge — The New Employee Brief
2 minutes · Mix both forms fluently · The S4 mastery test
⏱ 8 MIN
⭐ Solo Challenge
You are Andrea Soto, and today is your first full week in a new role. Your new manager asks: "Give me a picture of your week — what's already locked in and what are you planning to do?" Give a 2-minute briefing using real or invented events.
  • Use present continuous for at least 3 confirmed arrangements (booked with others)
  • Use going to for at least 3 personal intentions (your tasks, your decisions)
  • Include at least 1 uncertain event with may or might
  • Use at least 1 calendar phrase: "I have a conflict" / "My calendar is packed" / "I blocked time for…"
  • Partner scores: 1 pt per correct form · 0.5 pts per colloquial phrase used
  • NO NOTES — produce from memory

3 Phrases You Mastered Today
PHRASE 1
"I'm [verb]-ing at [time] — it's confirmed."
Every time you have a meeting in your calendar, use present continuous. It signals preparation, commitment, and reliability to your colleagues and manager.
PHRASE 2
"I'm going to [verb] [task] this week."
Use for solo tasks, personal plans, and decisions you made alone. More professional than "I will try" — going to signals a real decision, not just a wish.
PHRASE 3
"My calendar is packed — can we find a slot next week?"
The complete professional response to any meeting request when you're busy. Natural, polite, and immediately useful. Use it this week instead of "I'm busy."
