Let the debate run — it IS the warm-up production.

§2 Pitch Round — Email Makeover 12 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — EMAIL MAKEOVER FORMAT

Show each "before" email — it's grammatically incorrect or uses Spanglish closings. Students identify the errors verbally, then the partner reads the corrected version. Round 1 is T-S (teacher shows, student corrects). Round 2 is S-S (partners take turns catching errors). This is peer correction at production level — no grammar tables needed.

T-S · ROUND 1
EMAIL MAKEOVER — Find 5 errors
Dear Mr. Johnson,
I write you to give update about the Apex project.
Last week we are working on the quality audit. We will finish it last Thursday.
Next month we going to present the results.
I attach the report. I will wait for your response.
Regards and hugs,
Valeria
Find all 5 errors. Then read the corrected version aloud.

1. "I write you" → "I am writing to you" (or "I wanted to write") 2. "we are working" → "we worked" (last week = past) 3. "We will finish it last Thursday" → "We finished it last Thursday" (will = future) 4. "we going to present" → "we are going to present" (missing auxiliary "are") 5. "Regards and hugs" → "Best regards" (calco del español — nunca se usa en inglés profesional) CORRECTED: "Dear Mr. Johnson, I am writing to give you an update on the Apex project. Last week we worked on the quality audit and finished it by Thursday. Next month we are going to present the results to the team. Please find the report attached. I look forward to hearing from you. Best regards, Valeria Torres"

S-S · ROUND 2
PAIR CHALLENGE — Write & critique
Partner A writes a 4-sentence email opening (30 seconds, no notes) about their real project — using the 3-paragraph structure. Partner B reads it and identifies: (1) which tense each sentence uses, (2) any vocabulary from S3A that appears, (3) one improvement. Then switch.
Goal: natural email English, not perfect grammar. Does it sound like a real professional email?

Strong email opening criteria: ✓ Greeting + purpose statement (1-2 lines) ✓ Clear past context using Simple Past ✓ At least 1 going to sentence for plans ✓ At least 1 will commitment ✓ Professional closing phrase from S3A Award 2 points if partner identifies all 3 criteria correctly. Award 1 bonus point if they spot a vocabulary word from S3A.

§3 Relay — The Email Assembly Line 12 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — EMAIL ASSEMBLY LINE

This is the unique visual relay for S3B. The class builds one professional email together — each student contributes one section. Student 1 writes the greeting + past paragraph. Student 2 writes the going to paragraph. Student 3 writes the will commitments. Student 4 writes the closing. Teacher assembles it live on screen using the slots below. Award 2 points per section that uses the correct tense AND sounds natural.

§4 Speed Round — Email Vocabulary Flip Cards 8 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — SPEED ROUND FORMAT

Front = a real email situation in Spanish or English context. Back = the correct vocabulary word + a ready-to-use sentence. Students see the front, call out the word, then flip to verify. First correct call = 1 point. Second round: students must use the word in a sentence BEFORE flipping — if their sentence matches the example, 2 points. Keep the pace fast.

§5 Solo Challenge — Write & Read: The Real Email 12 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — SOLO FORMAT

This is the highest-value activity of S3B. Each student writes a real email (4 minutes) then reads it aloud to their partner (1 minute). The partner listens and gives ONE piece of feedback: "The strongest part was [paragraph]" and "I'd change [one thing]." No grammar police — evaluate whether it sounds professional, not whether it's perfect. This simulates real peer review in international companies.

🖊️ SOLO CHALLENGE — Write Your Real Email
Your mission: Write a real professional email about a real project at your company.

Requirements:
✓ Paragraph 1 (Past): What happened — 2-3 sentences, Simple Past
✓ Paragraph 2 (Going To): What's planned — 2 sentences, going to
✓ Paragraph 3 (Will): Your commitments — 2 sentences, will
✓ Closing: Use "Please find attached" OR "I look forward to hearing from you"
✓ At least 3 vocabulary words from S3A used naturally

4 minutes to write. Then read it aloud to your partner. One piece of feedback each.


💰 RETENTION + UPSELL NOTE

After the read-aloud, ask students to keep their email. Say: "This email — with small edits — is something you can actually send at work this week. That's what A2.1 looks like in real life." If a student produced a strong email, ask permission to share it as a class model. Peer recognition is the most powerful retention tool in adult learning.

§6 Speaking Cards — Email Phrases in Context 10 min
⚡ TEACHER NOTE — SPEAKING CARDS FORMAT

Each card gives an email situation. The student must respond using the correct phrase from S3A — spoken aloud, as if reading from a real email. The class votes: does it sound natural? Does it fit the context? Award 2 points per natural response, 1 point for correct but stiff. This tests register awareness — knowing WHEN to use each phrase is the A2.1 → A2.2 skill jump.

🃏 SPEAKING CARDS — What do you say in this email situation?
CLOSING PHRASE
You just sent a long project report. How do you tell the recipient that you included a file?
Use: "Please find [X] attached." — then add one will sentence: what you will do if they have questions.
EMAIL OPENER · PAST
You're following up on a meeting you had last week. How do you open the email?
"I'm writing to follow up on our meeting last [day]." Then add: what happened + what's the plan now.
GOING TO + REGARD
You need to open a formal email about the supplier contract. Use "regarding" + going to in the same email.
"Regarding the [topic] — we are going to [action] on [date]." Make it real.
URGENT + WILL
Something went wrong and you need an immediate response from a client. Write the opening and closing in 2 sentences.
Use "urgent" in the opening + a will commitment in the closing. Keep it professional, not panicked.
FULL EMAIL · ALL 3 TENSES
Speak the full email out loud — as if you're dictating it to an assistant. Past paragraph + going to paragraph + will commitment + professional closing. Use your real project.
Class counts: how many tenses? How many email vocab words? How professional does it sound?
PEER FEEDBACK
Your partner just read their email to you. Give them structured feedback: "The strongest part was [paragraph] because... I'd improve [element] by..."
Focus on: tense accuracy, professional vocabulary, natural closings. One strength, one improvement. Maximum 30 seconds.
