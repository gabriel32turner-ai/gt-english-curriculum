Hot Take — The Last-Minute Change
Andrea's trip just changed · React in 60 seconds · Badge 🔥 at stake
⏱ 5 MIN
Andrea Soto's Problem
Andrea just got an email: her Tuesday flight to Guadalajara is cancelled due to weather. The hotel reservation is still confirmed. The client meeting on Wednesday might be moved to Thursday. Her manager needs an update in 60 seconds.
What is still confirmed? What might change? What won't happen now? Update your manager using may, might, present continuous, and won't.
🎯 Rules: 60 seconds · Use at least: 1 present continuous (confirmed) + 1 might + 1 may + 1 won't · Your partner checks your grammar · Badge 🔥 if you use all four forms correctly
1:00
This Hot Take tests whether students internalized the certainty scale from S3A. The scenario forces all four levels: confirmed (hotel), might (client meeting), may (rebooking), won't (Tuesday flight). Listen for: "might to" errors, "I can travel" instead of "I may travel," and present continuous used for uncertain events. Correct immediately — this is production mode.
§2
Pitch Round — Change or Keep the Plan?
Two sides debate · may/might + present continuous in argumentation
⏱ 10 MIN
Scenario: Your company is deciding whether to send a team to a trade show in Monterrey next month. The budget is tight. Some things are confirmed, some are uncertain. Side A says GO. Side B says CANCEL.
Side A — Go to the trade show
"We should go — some things are uncertain but the opportunity is confirmed."
  • "We're already registered — that's confirmed."
  • "We may get 5 new clients if we attend."
  • "The hotel might be expensive, but the ROI is worth it."
  • "If we don't go, we might lose visibility."
Side B — Cancel the trip
"Too many things are uncertain — we might waste the budget."
  • "The budget might not be approved in time."
  • "We may not have enough people to staff the booth."
  • "The flights aren't booked yet — prices may increase."
  • "We won't recover the cost if we don't get leads."

Solo Challenge — The Full Travel Update
2 minutes · No notes · All certainty levels · The ultimate test
⏱ 8 MIN
⭐ Solo Challenge
You are Andrea Soto. Your manager just called: "Give me the full update on next week — everything. What's confirmed, what might change, and what's not happening."
  • Give a 2-minute spoken travel update — NO NOTES
  • Include at least 3 confirmed plans (present continuous)
  • Include at least 2 uncertain possibilities (may / might)
  • Include at least 1 thing that won't happen
  • Use at least 2 colloquial phrases (TBD / I'll keep you posted / It depends / We'll see)
  • Partner listens and scores: 1 point per correct form used

3 Phrases You Mastered Today
PHRASE 1
"I'm [verb]-ing at [time] on [day] — it's confirmed."
The professional way to report confirmed arrangements. Use in standups, emails, and WhatsApp updates. It signals you're organized and prepared.
PHRASE 2
"That's still TBD — I'll keep you posted."
The honest professional response when something isn't decided yet. Combines two key phrases. Use in meetings, calls, and email replies.
PHRASE 3
"The [event] might change — it depends on [factor]."
Perfect for travel updates and planning emails. Shows awareness of variables and communicates uncertainty professionally. Your manager will appreciate the clarity.
