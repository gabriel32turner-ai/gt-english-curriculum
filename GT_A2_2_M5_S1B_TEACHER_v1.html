Hot Take — Timed Comparison
Remember from last session · Activate comparatives under pressure · 5–8 min
⏱ 5–8 MIN

Remember from S1A: short adjectives → -er than · long adjectives → more … than · good/bad → better/worse. Now put it to work — fast.

🔥 Hot Take — 60 Seconds
"My company is better than our main competitor — and I can prove it."
Use at least 3 comparatives. Mention real aspects: price, quality, speed, reliability, customer service, flexibility. You have 60 seconds. No notes.
HOT TAKE
🔥 Hot Take — Round 2 — 45 Seconds
"For a business trip to Mexico City vs. Monterrey — Monterrey is the better choice for our industry."
Defend your position using comparatives AND a superlative. Preview of today's new tool: the superlative. Try it even if you are not sure yet.
HOT TAKE 2
⚡ Teacher Note — Hot Take
Say: "No notes — just go. This is the S1B opening — you already know the comparatives from last session. Use them." Round 1: T-S first, model with your own company. Then students go. Round 2: plant disagreement — "I think Mexico City is better, who agrees? Who disagrees?" — use the division to fuel the debate. The word "superlative" will come up naturally when a student says "the best" or "the fastest" — capitalize on it: "Good — that is the superlative. That is what we are learning today."
§2
Pitch / Debate — Superlatives: The Final Verdict
New tool: superlatives · 3 rounds (T-S / S-S / Class) · Model answers · 10–12 min
⏱ 10–12 MIN
🌟 New Tool — Superlatives
You already know how to compare two things (comparatives). Now: what about choosing the best one from a group?
Short adjectives: the + adj + -est → the fastest · the cheapest · the biggest
Long adjectives: the + most + adj → the most reliable · the most efficient
Irregular: good → the best · bad → the worst · far → the farthest

T-SRound 1 — Teacher Models
Andrea compared Lumina Express and NovaTrans. Based on the S1A listening, which one was the fastest? Which was the most affordable? Which did Andrea say was the best choice — and why?
NovaTrans was the fastest — their delivery time was only 2 days. Lumina Express was the most affordable — their prices were lower and more flexible. Andrea recommended NovaTrans because she said the delivery speed was more important than the price in her industry. She used the colloquial phrase: "The pros definitely outweigh the cons."
S-SRound 2 — Pair Debate
Student A: You think Supplier X at your company is the best you have ever worked with. Student B: You disagree — there was a better one before. Debate for 90 seconds. Use comparatives AND superlatives. At least 3 of each.
Useful structures for this debate:
"X is the most reliable supplier we have ever had because..."
"Yes, but the previous one was cheaper than X, and the quality was just as good."
"I disagree — X's performance is much better than any supplier we used before."
"There's no comparison — X delivers faster, costs less, and is the most flexible partner we have."
ClassRound 3 — Class Vote
Class vote: What is the most important quality in a supplier? Price / Reliability / Speed / Flexibility / Customer Service. Each student makes a case for their answer using a superlative. "For me, reliability is the most important quality because..."
How to run this: Write the 5 options on the board. Students vote by raising hands. Then: each "side" must defend with at least one superlative sentence and one comparison. Use as a leaderboard warm-up — the best argument (most comparatives + clearest reasoning) earns 3 points for their team. Connect to exit task M5: students will need this exact skill to present their recommendation.
⚡ Teacher Note — Superlative Introduction
Do NOT give the full table before the rounds. Show it AFTER Round 1 as a reference tool — let students try first. The discovery moment (they produce "the most fast" → you correct to "the fastest") is more memorable than reading a table. Key error pattern: "the most best" → always "the best". "the most good" → always "the best." Write these on the board as they appear and do not correct during fluency rounds — collect and review at the end.

🎯 Solo Challenge — Recommendation Pitch
Scenario: Your manager asks you to compare two real options from your work — two suppliers, two software tools, two job candidates, two office locations, or two business travel routes. You have 3 minutes to present your comparison and make a clear recommendation.

Requirements:
▶ At least 4 comparatives (e.g., "Option A is more reliable than B")
▶ At least 2 superlatives (e.g., "This is the most cost-effective choice")
▶ At least 1 colloquial phrase ("it's a no-brainer" / "the pros outweigh the cons")
▶ A clear final recommendation with a reason
▶ No reading from notes — speak freely

GT Originals — Teacher Notes
  • 💼 Real-world bridge: the Recommendation Pitch is exactly what engineers, coordinators and sales reps do in supplier review meetings. Tell students: "After today, you can do this in English at your next team meeting."
  • ⚠️ Watch for: students who compare only 2 adjectives and think that is enough. Prompt with: "What ELSE is better about your option? Give me 2 more reasons." — this builds the habit of thorough reasoning.
  • 🔹 Superlative trap: students may drop "the" before superlatives — "She is most experienced candidate." Correct immediately: "THE most experienced." The definite article is mandatory with superlatives.
  • 🌍 Upsell note: students who struggle with the pitch under time pressure are exactly the profile for our B1.1 program — where they will learn conditionals and reported speech to build even more persuasive arguments. Plant the seed.
🎯 This week, use these 3 phrases at work:
From today's session — production-ready English for your next meeting or email.
"This is the most [adj] option we have found."
Use when presenting a clear winner in a comparison. Sounds professional and decisive in any meeting or report. Replace [adj] with: reliable / affordable / efficient / competitive.
"Option A is better than B because…"
The full unit: comparative + reason. Never stop at the comparison — always add the justification. This is the structure managers expect when you make a recommendation.
"The pros definitely outweigh the cons."
Use when your option has a disadvantage but is still the right choice. Shows balanced, analytical thinking. The word "definitely" adds confidence without sounding aggressive.

Wrap-Up — M5 Session 1 Complete
Key takeaways · Checklist · Session certificate · My Win Today · 5–8 min
⏱ 5–8 MIN
☐ Comparatives — Full System
Short: -er than (faster, cheaper, bigger). Long: more + adj + than. Irregular: better, worse. Equality: as … as.
☐ Superlatives — New Tool
Short: the + -est (the fastest, the cheapest). Long: the most + adj. Irregular: the best, the worst. Always use "the."
☐ The Full Comparison Unit
Comparison + reason = professional English. "Option A is more reliable BECAUSE it has a longer warranty." This is what managers want to hear.

I Can Now...

☐Make comparisons using short and long adjectives confidently
☐Use superlatives (the fastest, the most reliable) to describe the best or worst option
☐Correct common errors: "more better", "the most fast", "as cheaper as"
☐Give a recommendation with a justification: "I recommend X because it is more..."
☐Use colloquial phrases: "it's a no-brainer", "the pros outweigh the cons", "there's no comparison"
