Exit Task — 3 min
💬 Opening Discussion — The Topic That Ties It All
🔥 Hot question: "Is work-life balance actually possible in your industry — or is it just something companies say to sound good?" Give your honest opinion. Use vocabulary from any M5 session. You have 60 seconds.
Target language this session:
• "I try to leave work at work."
• "Balance is different for everyone."
• "I feel guilty when I stop working."
• Reported Speech + Present Perfect together
Teacher note — Recap opener: The work-life balance question is the perfect bridge. It connects all 4 previous sessions: stress (S1), hobbies as escape (S2), exercise routines (S3), mental health (S4) — all of these are components of how people try to balance their life. Students who answer this question well are already doing the Exit Task warm-up. The best answers will naturally use Reported Speech: "My manager said we had to be available after hours, but my doctor told me I needed to disconnect."
S5 session flow: Flash Cards repaso (16+ vocabulary words, all M5) → Grammar consolidation (Reported Speech complete + common errors) → Dice Roller speaking activity → Speaking stations (4 topics, rotate) → Exit Task (3-min solo) → Module 5 Certificate. Grammar Consolidation — Reported Speech: The Complete Picture

15 min
Grammar repaso · Reported Speech: all types, all rules, all errors · In one place
Type 1 — Reported Statements (S1)
Subject + said / told + (that) + past tense clause. Tense shifts back: present → past, past → past perfect, will → would.
"I am stressed." → She said she was stressed.
"I have been struggling." → He said he had been struggling.
"I will ask for help." → She said she would ask for help.
Type 2 — Reported Questions (S2)
asked if / whether (yes/no) · asked + wh- word (open). No inversion in the reported clause — statement word order.
"Do you exercise?" → She asked me if I exercised.
"What did you say?" → He asked me what I had said.
"Will you come?" → She asked whether I would come.
Type 3 — Combined Narrative (S3)
Connect multiple reported types naturally. Use connectors: "She said... She also told me... Then she asked..."
"The coach said consistency was key. She also told us the first weeks were hardest. Then she asked whether we had support at home."
Type 4 — Precision in Sensitive Contexts (S4)
In emotional or professional contexts, exact tense matters. "She said she WAS struggling" (past — a moment in time) vs "she IS struggling" (present — implies it is ongoing now). The wrong form misrepresents people.
✓ "She said she had been feeling anxious." (precise)
✗ "She said she is feeling anxious." (imprecise after past verb)
⚠ The 4 Most Common Reported Speech Errors (B1.1):
1. "She said me" → She told me / She said (no "me")
2. "She asked if she can" → She asked if she could (tense shift)
3. "He said she is struggling" → He said she was struggling (after past verb)
4. "She asked me did I exercise" → She asked me if I exercised (no inversion)
Teacher note — Grammar S5: This is not a teaching session — it is a calibration session. Students should be able to explain these rules, not just use them. Ask each student: "Which of these four types is hardest for you? Why?" This metacognitive question generates high-quality discussion and gives you data for next module. If anyone says "Type 3 — combining," that is your signal to do more narrative Reported Speech in M6.
🗣️ Rapid-Fire Oral Drill — 60 seconds

Teacher says the direct speech. Student says the reported version. Rotate fast. Mark badge b3 when the class completes all 6 without error.

1. "I need a break." → She said she needed a break.
2. "Have you tried therapy?" → She asked me if I had tried therapy.
3. "I will set better limits." → He said he would set better limits.
4. "What do you do to relax?" → She asked me what I did to relax.
5. "I am struggling with anxiety." → She said she was struggling with anxiety.
6. "Can the company help me?" → She asked if the company could help her.

Speaking Stations — Work-Life Balance: 4 Angles

18 min
Work-Life Balance · 4 speaking stations · 3 minutes each · Rotate pairs

4 stations, 3 minutes each. Rotate pairs. Every answer should include at least one Reported Speech sentence and one piece of M5 vocabulary. After the rotation, each pair reports to the class: "My partner said that..."

S↔S Station 1 — Your Reality
Do you actually leave work at work? Or does it follow you home? Report a conversation with someone who has a different approach.
Colloquial: "I try to leave work at work" / "Balance is different for everyone"
S↔S Station 2 — Company Responsibility
What has your company said or done about work-life balance? Report it. Is the stated policy and the lived reality the same thing?
Grammar: "The company said... / HR told us... / My manager asked whether..."
S↔S Station 3 — The Habit You'd Change
If you could change ONE thing about how you manage your time and energy at work, what would it be? Has anyone ever told you something about this that made you think?
Vocabulary: burnout, boundary, priority, consistency, wellbeing
Class Station 4 — The Generational Divide
PROGRAMA note: Gen Z vs. older generations on work-life balance is a highly engaging cross-cultural topic. Do younger colleagues at your company see balance differently? What have they said? What have they asked about?
Colloquial: "I feel guilty when I stop working" — is this generational?
Teacher note — Stations: After the rotation, the debrief is the grammar moment. Each pair must report what their partner said: "My partner said that they had tried to leave work at work, but she told me it was nearly impossible because..." This is Reported Speech produced spontaneously about real content, which is the highest-quality output possible. Count the tense shifts you hear. Celebrate the correct ones.
GT Original — Station 4: The generational divide discussion is documented in the PROGRAMA as one of the most engaging topics at B1.1. In manufacturing and logistics in the Bajío, you often have two or three generations on the same floor. The contrast is real, local, and immediate. Students who have never thought about this in English discover they have very strong opinions. That is the best possible state for a final speaking module.

Exit Task — Module 5 Assessment

20 min
Exit Task M5 · 3-minute solo talk · No notes · CEFR rubric · Reported Speech + Present Perfect
Exit Task M5
Talk about one habit you've tried to build — exercise, sleep, disconnecting, a hobby, anything from M5 — and report what someone close to you said about your work habits.

Your 3-minute talk must include:
• What habit you tried to build — and what happened (use Present Perfect: "I have tried to...", "I have learned that...")
• What someone told you about it — Reported Statement with tense shift
• What someone asked you — Reported Question with no inversion
• What you think now — your honest opinion on work-life balance
• At least 4 vocabulary words from any M5 session

No notes. 3 minutes. This is the final assessment for Module 5.
CEFR B1.1 Assessment Rubric — Click each criterion when achieved
Present Perfect
Uses Present Perfect naturally at least twice: "I have tried..." / "I have learned..." / "I have found..."
Reported Statement
Reports someone's words with correct tense shift: "She said she had been..." / "He told me that..."
Reported Question
Reports a question with no inversion: "She asked me if I had ever..." / "He asked what I did..."
Vocabulary Range
4+ M5 vocabulary words used naturally from any session (burnout, consistency, wellbeing, stigma, etc.)
Fluency & Time
Speaks for 2+ minutes without long pauses or switching to Spanish. Coherent structure.
Opinion Expression
Gives genuine personal opinion on work-life balance: "I believe..." / "In my opinion..." / "I think the reality is..."
Teacher note — Exit Task delivery: Give 3 minutes of silent preparation before starting. Students may write 3 words (topic, grammar type, 1 vocabulary word) as a memory cue — NOT full sentences. After the talk, give feedback in this order: (1) One thing that was excellent — specific. (2) One grammar correction — specific, with the correct form. (3) What level this talk represents. For any student who uses all 6 rubric criteria: "That is a B1.1 Exit Talk. Congratulations."
Assessment note — Grading scale (for GT records):
5–6 criteria: B1.1 Achieved — Ready for M6 / B1.2
3–4 criteria: B1.1 Developing — Continue with M6, revisit RS in M6 sessions
1–2 criteria: Review — Additional practice before M6; consider returning to M5 S3A/S4A
