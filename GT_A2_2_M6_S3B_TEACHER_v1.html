Hot Take — Advice & Recommendations
Remember from S3A · Spontaneous advice production · 8 min
⏱ 8 MIN
⚡ Remember from S3A
S3B produces the Professional Advice Formula from S3A: should/could + comparative + because/so. Four S3A colloquial phrases: "If I were you, I'd..." / "Have you thought about...?" / "It's worth trying." / "My advice would be to..." Key errors to catch: recommend + to (wrong) vs recommend + -ing (correct). "If I were you" always uses "were" — never "was." shouldn't = advice not to do something (not a rule). Listen for whether students produce the full formula (advice + comparative + reason) or stop at the advice alone — that is the S3B gap.
🔥 Hot Take Round 1 — 60 seconds
"Giving advice to a senior colleague in English is one of the most difficult professional situations for an A2.2 speaker — even harder than a job interview."
Agree or disagree in 60 seconds. Use should or could in your argument. Include one S3A colloquial phrase naturally. If you agree, explain why. If you disagree, give evidence from your career.
🔥 Hot Take Round 2 — 60 seconds
"The most useful thing a manager could do for their team is give them honest advice more often — even when it is uncomfortable."
Agree or disagree using the full formula: should/could + comparative + because/so. Bonus: use "If I were you" + "It's worth trying" in the same argument.
⚡ Teacher Note — Hot Take
Round 1 targets the register of advice giving — giving advice to a senior is culturally loaded for Mexican professionals. Listen for: do students use should or hedge excessively? The S3A phrases have specific register values: "If I were you" = empathetic, for equals or seniors; "Have you thought about?" = very soft, sensitive situations; "My advice would be to" = formal presentations. Round 2 forces the full formula under time pressure. Students who produce both "If I were you" + "It's worth trying" in one argument in under 40 seconds are performing above S3B floor. Name them and add bonus to leaderboard.

Pitch & Debate — The Advice Panel
3 rounds · T-S / S-S / Class · Model answers · 20 min
⏱ 20 MIN
T-SRound 1 — The 90-Second Mentor
You are a mentor. A junior colleague has a real work problem. Give a 90-second structured recommendation: (1) what they should prioritize, (2) what they shouldn't do, (3) a soft alternative using could, (4) a comparative to justify, (5) one S3A colloquial phrase to close. Class scores on rubric visibility.
Scenario: junior colleague is overwhelmed and missing deadlines.

"My advice would be to talk to your manager before Friday. You should send a brief email outlining the three main blockers, because a proactive message is more professional than silence. You shouldn't try to solve everything alone. You could also ask a colleague to take one task. It's worth trying — the worst they say is no."

Structures: should + shouldn't + could + comparative + colloquial = full S3A formula.
S-SRound 2 — The Real Problem
Student A: share a REAL professional challenge (30 seconds max). Student B: give structured advice using the full formula (should + comparative + because + could alternative + one colloquial phrase). Then switch. While B gives advice, A counts the formula elements (1–4) silently. After: A says "You used [X] elements."
S3A Professional Advice Formula:
1. Advice: "You should [action] / You could [alternative]"
2. Comparison: "...because it is more [adj] than [other]"
3. Reason: "...so [result] / because [justification]"
4. Colloquial close: "If I were you, I'd..." / "It's worth trying."

Partner counting formula elements (1–4) creates self-assessment without interrupting the speaker.
ClassRound 3 — Best Advice Share-Out
Each student shares the BEST piece of advice their partner gave them in Round 2. Present it: "My partner's advice was that I should [action] because [reason]. They also said I could [alternative]." Class votes: most useful AND most complete formula = 5 bonus leaderboard points.
Scoring for leaderboard:
1pt: should or could used correctly in reported advice
2pts: should + comparative present
3pts: full formula: should + comparative + because/so
4pts: all above + one S3A colloquial phrase
5pts BONUS: class votes most complete AND most useful

Meta-skill note: Reporting advice ("my partner said I should...") is B1.1 reported speech appearing naturally. Students who do this fluently are demonstrating B1 readiness. Name them explicitly.
90-SECOND MENTOR RESPONSE TIMER
⚡ Teacher Note — Pitch & Debate
Round 1 is the S3B summative production task disguised as a warm-up round. Use it to calibrate who is ready for the exit task. Round 2 generates the richest language: real problems + real advice = authentic production. The partner counting formula elements (1–4) creates listening comprehension AND grammar awareness simultaneously without interrupting. Round 3 introduces reported speech naturally — this is B1.1 structure appearing organically. Note which students produce it fluently — they are B1.1 transition candidates. Name them when giving the upsell in §7.

Speed Round — Advice Formula Under Pressure
6 prompts · 15 seconds each · Full formula · 10 min
⏱ 10 MIN

15 seconds per prompt. No notes. 1pt: should/could correct. 2pts: + comparative. 3pts: full formula. 4pts: + colloquial phrase.

Prompt 01
Your colleague is thinking of sending an angry email to a client. Give ONE piece of advice using shouldn't + a reason.
Target: "You shouldn't send it until you calm down, because a professional email is more effective than an emotional one."
Prompt 02
A junior asks how to prepare for their first client presentation. Full formula: should + comparative + so.
Target: "You should prepare a brief script because a structured presentation is more convincing than improvising."
Prompt 03
Use recommend + -ing (NOT "to") to suggest a strategy for your team. Include a comparative.
Target: "I recommend scheduling weekly check-ins because regular meetings are more efficient than emailing back and forth."
Prompt 04
Use "If I were you" to advise about a salary negotiation. Include could + an alternative.
Target: "If I were you, I'd ask for 10% more. You could also negotiate extra vacation days — it's worth trying."
Prompt 05
Use "Have you thought about" + "My advice would be" in ONE connected piece of advice about a career decision.
Target: "Have you thought about asking for a meeting first? My advice would be to present your results before requesting a promotion."
Prompt 06
Final: use the COMPLETE formula with all four S3A phrases in natural sequence about a real work situation. Ceiling task.
"Have you thought about...? If I were you, I'd... because it is more... than... My advice would be to... It's worth trying."
15-SECOND RESPONSE TIMER
⚡ Teacher Note — Speed Round
Prompt 03 (recommend + -ing) is the most common error in professional A2.2 writing. A student who produces "I recommend negotiating" spontaneously in under 8 seconds has the structure fully automatized. Prompt 06 is extreme — any student producing all 4 phrases in one connected piece of advice in 15 seconds is above A2.2. Say explicitly: "What you just did in 12 seconds? In B1.1 you add one conditional: 'If you prepare well, you will make a better impression.' That turns your advice from a recommendation into a logical consequence. That is B1.1 entry English."

💬 Solo Challenge — The Advice Panel
Format: each student presents a real professional dilemma to the class (30 seconds). The class — the "Advice Panel" — gives structured advice in response. Then the presenter evaluates the best advice.

Presenter (30 sec): Situation + why it is difficult + one question for the panel.
Panel (2 min total): Minimum 3 panel members. Each gives ONE piece of advice using the full formula.
Presenter evaluates (30 sec): "The most useful advice was [X] because [reason]. I should [action]."

Target per panel member: should/could + comparative + because/so + S3A phrase = 4 elements = maximum rubric score.

⚡ Teacher Note — Advice Panel
The Advice Panel uses REAL professional content from students. Run 3 rounds minimum. After each panel round, ask: "Which piece of advice used the most complete formula?" Class identifies the best advice, not teacher. rubric rb5 (register: empathetic) is the most subjective — leave to class vote: "Which advice would you actually take?" The bonus item rb6 (second A2.2 structure) is worth 4pts — the highest-value item in S3B because it measures M6 integration across sessions. A student who says "If I were you, I'd negotiate this because a better deal is more important AND it might also improve the long-term relationship" has produced M3 + M5 + M4 in one sentence. That is B1.1 readiness.

    🎯 This week, use these 3 phrases at work:
    Production-ready professional advice — starting tomorrow.
    "You should [advice] because it is more [adj] than [alternative]."
    The complete professional recommendation formula in one sentence. Use it the next time a colleague asks your opinion. The "because it is more" part separates a recommendation from an instruction — it shows you reasoned through the advice, not just told someone what to do.
    "If I were you, I'd [advice] — it's worth trying."
    Empathetic advice + encouragement in one sentence. Use when a colleague is hesitant. "If I were you" shows you understand their position. "It's worth trying" removes the perceived risk. Together they make advice feel safe to follow.
    "My advice would be to [action] — you could also [alternative]."
    Formal recommendation + soft alternative. Use in presentations, reports, or emails. "My advice would be to" sounds authoritative. Adding "you could also" shows flexibility. Works especially well with senior contacts or clients.
