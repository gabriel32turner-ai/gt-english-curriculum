§ 01
Whose is this? — Say it in English!
🔑 Creates the need to express possession · The student feels the gap before the rule · Direct contrast with Spanish
⏱ 5–7 MIN
💼 In your office right now: Look around. Whose laptop is that? Whose coffee is that? Whose chair is that? Try to say it in English: "That is the laptop of Carlos." — Does that sound correct? Is there a more natural way?
👨‍👩‍👧 Your family: Think about your family. What's your mom's name? Do you have brothers or sisters? Are you married? Try to introduce them in English — as best you can!
❓ Key question of the day: If someone points to a laptop and asks "Whose laptop is this?" — how do you answer? "It is the laptop of Carlos" or "It's Carlos's laptop"? Which sounds more like English?
⚡ Note to the teacher: Have the student try "the laptop of Carlos" first — that is exactly the Spanish calque we are correcting today. Write on the whiteboard: of — vs — 's. Leave both sides empty and tell them we fill in the rules in §4. The previous session was adj+noun. This is the second description tool.
⚡
Quick Win — 30 segundos
Point to 3 objects in the room and say whose they are — with what you know!: "That is [name]'s ___." / "This is my ___." / "Whose ___ is this?"
It doesn't matter if it's not perfect yet — we'll adjust in §4.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Put several objects on the table (a pen, a bag, a phone). Point to one: "Whose pen is this?" — the student tries to answer. Note the "of" error.
  • If the student already says "'s" — celebrate: "Exactly! That is today's structure. It's called the possessive 's."
  • Connection to S1A: "Yesterday you described WHAT people are like. Today you learn WHO things belong to. It is the second piece of describing people in English."
  • Family: activate what vocabulary they already know. Most have "mother", "father" from M1 or general knowledge. Write on the whiteboard what they already know.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Do you have American family or colleagues in your contacts? How do you describe your office or your family in an email? With today's content you can already write that message." 📧
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "By the end of this session you will be able to say 'That's my manager's office' and 'My sister's name is Sofia' in completely natural English. No roundabouts, no 'of'."

GT Original — El Error #1 de Hispanohablantes: "of" vs 's 🇲🇽
  • In Spanish we say "el laptop DE Carlos" — in English we use Carlos's laptop. "of" exists in English, but it is very formal and reserved for inanimate objects: "the end OF the meeting" ✅ · "the laptop OF Carlos" ❌
  • 🔴 Practical rule: If there is ONE person or animal → always use 's. "My mother's name" / "The dog's food" — never "the name of my mother" in conversation.
  • ⚠️ Whose vs Who's: "Whose" = whose? (possession) · "Who's" = Who is (contraction). "Whose bag is this?" vs "Who's your manager?" — sounds the same, written differently.
  • 📊 Org Chart Skill: Reading an org chart in English: "The CEO's name is Mark. The manager's team has 5 people. The interns' projects are due on Friday." — real corporate English.
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: The student says the rule BEFORE flipping the card. Then verifies.
  • Quick Test: run as an oral drill. "1 manager" → student: "manager's". "2 managers" → student: "managers'". Speed, no thinking.
  • Whose vs Who's: this is the most frequent writing error. Emphasize the difference with an oral example: "Who's your manager?" (Who is) vs "Whose laptop is this?" (possession).
  • Irregular plural "children's": the main exception in this session. children already has s → we just add the apostrophe before: children's. NOT "childrens'".
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "With two rules — singular 's and plural s' — you can already describe the possession of ANY person or group in English. No roundabouts, no 'of', no literal translations." ✅
Layer 3 · Retention
  • "In S2B the Spinner combines family + possession: 'describe your sister's apartment'. Today you learn each piece. The next step combines them with the S1A adj."
§ 04
Possessive 's — Rules and Errors
📐 Singular 's · Plural s' · Irregular possessives · Contrast "of" vs 's · Whose…? in full questions
⏱ 12–15 MIN
🎯 Real Situation — Before the Rule
You are drafting an introduction email for your company in the U.S. You want to write:
"The name of our CEO is Carlos. The office of the managers is on the third floor. The laptop of David is very new. Are you the sister of Ana?"

That is correct English but sounds very strange — nobody speaks like that. In natural English we use the possessive 's. How do you convert those sentences?
➡ La herramienta: persona + 's + objeto. Dos reglas. Sin excepciones para personas.
↓
✅ The Rules of the Possessive 's
Rule 1 — Singular: Name/singular noun + 's + object → "Carlos's office" · "My sister's name" · "The manager's desk"
Rule 2 — Regular plural: Plural noun + s' (apostrophe AFTER the s) → "The managers' meeting" · "My parents' house"
Rule 3 — Irregular plural: Irregular plural + 's (same as singular) → "The children's school" · "The men's office"
Rule 4 — Whose: Whose + noun + is/are + this/that? → "Whose bag is this? — It's Ana's."

✓ "my manager's name" · "Carlos's office" · "the team's laptop" — natural and immediate

✗ "The laptop of Carlos is new."→✓ "Carlos's laptop is new."

✗ "The managers's office." (double s)→✓ "The managers' office." (apostrophe after)
Regular plural: the s is already in "managers" → just add apostrophe. NO double s.
✗ "Who's bag is this?" (Who is)→✓ "Whose bag is this?" (possession)
"Whose" = possession · "Who's" = Who is. They sound the same, totally different meanings.

Thursday, 10:30 AM

Hi! Since you're new here, let me tell you a little about me and my team — it helps to know who's who! 😊

My manager's name is Carlos Rivera. Carlos's office is on the third floor — it's very organized! He's a very professional manager. The team's meetings are every Monday at nine in the manager's conference room.

🚀 Next Class — S2B · Application & Mastery
In S2B you play "Whose is it?" — describe family and work belongings without notes, combining 's with adj from S1A.
🎯 Competitive Possession Spinner · 🎭 Role play: "Introduce your family to an American colleague" · 🃏 Whose? Bingo · 🔊 Wordwall Family · ⏱ 50–60 min pure production · No new grammar
Layer 1 · Pedagogical
  • ACTION: Flashcard Wall R1 speed. R2 real example from the student (their family, their team).
  • Contrast: read it out loud. Point to specifically "the laptop of Carlos" → "Carlos's laptop". The student must feel the difference in fluency.
  • Exit ticket: "One sentence about something in your family with 's + an S1A adjective." If it comes out correctly → S2A complete. Note what they combined.
  • Card "M3 Combo": this is the most important card. If the student can produce it naturally → M3 is on the right track.
Layer 2 · Engagement
  • "Before you were saying 'the name of my sister is Sofia'. Now you say 'My sister's name is Sofia — she's a very creative designer.' That is the English they speak in Silicon Valley." 🚀
Layer 3 · Retention & Upsell
  • "Tonight: mentally say 3 possessives about your family in English. 30 seconds. 'My mother's name is ___. My sister's ___ is ___. My parents' house is in ___.' That consolidates the memory."
