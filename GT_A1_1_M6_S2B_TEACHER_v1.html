§1 · Hot Take — 8 min
Small Talk Opinions
React fast. Speak first. No perfect sentences needed.
⏱ 8 min
⚡ Teacher Note — Hot Take Routine

Read the main statement aloud. Students shout Agree or Disagree immediately, then one student gives a reason in English. No full paragraphs needed — one sentence is a win. Move through all 4 in under 3 minutes. The mini statements below run simultaneously: show on projector, students vote with hands. Take the most controversial one into a 90-second class debate before moving on.

🔥 Hot Take — Main Statement
"Small talk in an elevator or hallway is NOT important at work."
🔥 Statement 2
"I feel comfortable talking to my colleagues in English every day."
🔥 Statement 3
"Describing my full workday in English is very difficult."
🔥 Statement 4
"My manager understands me when I speak English."
💡 Engagement Layer

After the vote on Statement 2, ask: "Who said NO? Why? Use ONE sentence." This surfaces real anxiety about English at work — the session content directly addresses it. Students who verbalize their difficulty are more receptive to the activities that follow.

§2 · Pitch & Debate — 12 min
A Day in the Life
Role-play · Speaking Cards Mini-Game · Class Discussion
⏱ 12 min
⚡ Teacher Note — Setup

Part 1 (5 min): Role-play in pairs — Student A describes their morning at work, Student B asks questions. Then swap. Part 2 (7 min): Speaking Cards Mini-Game as a class — teacher draws a card, nominates a student, student speaks for 30 seconds. Award badge b2 when the class completes 5 cards. This structure mirrors real elevator and hallway small talk situations.

📌 Student A — The Narrator
Describe your morning at work from the moment you arrive to 12:00 PM. Use: There is / There are to describe your workspace, always/usually/sometimes for your routine, and at least ONE sentence with can or can't.
Example start: "I usually arrive at 7:30. There are two security guards at the entrance..."
💬 Student B — The Colleague
Listen to Student A. Ask 3 questions using: Do you...? / Is there...? / Can you...? Then add ONE sentence about your own morning to connect the conversation naturally.
Example question: "Do you have a meeting on Monday mornings?" / "Is there a cafeteria near your office?"

Project the card on screen. Nominate a student or let volunteers go first. The class listens and gives a thumbs up if they understood. §3 · Relay — 10 min
Small Talk Relay
Elevator situations · Chain build · Timed production
⏱ 10 min
⚡ Teacher Note — Relay Structure

Round 1 (4 min): Give each student one situation card below. They have 45 seconds to prepare one sentence. Then they say it out loud. Class corrects errors collectively. Round 2 (6 min): The Relay Chain — students build a complete elevator conversation. Each student adds ONE sentence. The conversation must be coherent and use only A1.1 structures. Celebrate when it reaches 6+ sentences without breaking.

Situation 1
You are in the elevator with your manager. It is Monday morning. Start a conversation about the week.
Use: "Good morning! How are you?" / "It's a busy week..."
Situation 2
You pass a colleague in the hallway. They look stressed. You want to help but you don't know their problem.
Use: "Are you OK?" / "Can I help you?" / "Do you need anything?"
Situation 3
You meet a new person in the cafeteria. They speak English. Introduce yourself and your job in 3 sentences.
Use: "Hi! My name is... / I work at... / I'm a..."

💡 Retention Layer

The relay chain fails if students say "Yes, I have meetings" and stop. Push them to ADD information: "Yes, I have a meeting at 10. It's with the production team." This replicates real small talk — native speakers never answer with one word and go silent. Teach the rule: answer + add = real conversation.

§4 · Speed Round — 8 min
Workday Description Sprint
Grammar in real situations · 30 seconds each · No notes
⏱ 8 min
⚡ Teacher Note — Speed Round Rules

Teacher reads the situation. Student has 5 seconds to think, then speaks for 30 seconds. The class counts: how many different grammar structures did they use? (to be / can / there is / adverbs of frequency / have). Each structure = 1 point. Track on the board. This gamifies grammar awareness without drilling.

Sprint 1
Tell us about your typical Monday morning at work. What do you do? What is there in your office?
Target: 4+ structures
Sprint 2
Describe your team. How many people are there? What can they do? What do they usually do at work?
Target: 4+ structures
Sprint 3
Your international visitor asks: "What do you do at your company?" Answer in English for 30 seconds.
Target: 4+ structures
Sprint 4
Describe your afternoon routine at work. What do you do after lunch? What time do you finish?
Target: 4+ structures

📚 False Friend Alert

Students often say "I go to work at 7" but mean "I arrive at work at 7." Reinforce: go TO work = the action of leaving home. arrive AT work / get TO work = the moment of arrival. In small talk: "What time do you get to the office?" sounds much more natural than "What time do you go to work?"

🚫 Error Watch — M6 Top Errors
  • ❌ "He work here" → "He works here"
  • ❌ "I am come for a meeting" → "I am here for a meeting"
  • ❌ "There is many people" → "There are many people"
  • ❌ "I go to work at 7" → "I get to work / arrive at 7"
  • ❌ "She can to help" → "She can help"
§7 · Wrap-Up — 8 min
Close Strong
3 Phrases · Certificate · My Win Today
⏱ 8 min
🎯 This week, you can use these 3 phrases at work:
Phrase 1
"How's your week going?"
Use in any elevator, hallway, or break room. The perfect small talk opener. Expect: "Good, busy!" — then you say "Me too!"
Phrase 2
"I usually [verb] in the morning."
Use to describe your routine when an international colleague asks about your day. Replace [verb] with: start meetings / check emails / visit the plant floor.
Phrase 3
"There are [number] people on my team."
Use when introducing yourself or your department. Follow with: "We work on [topic]." Instant professional impression in English.
