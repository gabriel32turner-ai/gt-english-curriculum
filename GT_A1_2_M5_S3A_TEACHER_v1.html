Speaking Topic — Requests & Confirmations
Mónica Herrera · Supplier communication · ConnectPro
8–10 min
T-S
Mónica needs to request an updated price list from her supplier. She wants to be polite but clear. How does she start the email?
Use: "I'm writing to request…" / "Could you send us…?"
"Subject: Request for Updated Price List. Dear [supplier name], I'm writing to request your updated price list for Q3. Could you send it to us before Friday, May 23rd? We need it to prepare our budget. Thank you in advance. Best regards, Mónica."
S-S
Partner A: You are a supplier. You receive this email: "Please confirm delivery for Tuesday." Partner B: You write the confirmation reply — include date, time, and one detail. Use connectors.
Use: "I'm writing to confirm…" / "First… then… finally…"
"Dear Mónica, I'm writing to confirm the delivery for Tuesday, May 20th. First, the truck will arrive at 8 AM. Then, the driver will check in at reception. Finally, please sign the delivery document. Best regards."
Class
At your job, what do you often request by email? And what do you confirm? Give 2 real examples using "I'm writing to request…" and "I'm writing to confirm…"
Connect your answer to your actual work role
"I'm writing to request the production schedule for next week." / "I'm writing to confirm the maintenance visit on Thursday at 9 AM. First, the technician will check the machines. Then, he will send us the report."
TEACHER NOTE — Speaking Rounds

Round 2 is the production core — make sure both partners actually write 3 sentences before reading aloud. Walk the room, check for: (1) connector sequence: first → then → finally (not "and then" or "after"). (2) Object pronoun: "send it to us" not "send to us the price list." (3) Sign-off present. After Round 3, ask 2 students from different industries to share. The variety of real workplace situations reinforces that this grammar is universally useful.

4
Colloquial — Real Written English
4 phrases that make emails sound natural, not robotic
5 min
"Please confirm."
Short, professional, direct. Used at the end of emails to request a response. More natural than "Please send confirmation." Example: "The meeting is on Thursday at 10 AM. Please confirm."
"Let me know if you have any questions."
Warm, open ending. More human than "Don't hesitate to contact us." Perfect for emails to colleagues. Example: "Please find attached the report. Let me know if you have any questions."
"FYI" (For your information)
Used to share information with no action required. Very common in corporate emails. Example: "FYI — the delivery has been moved to Wednesday." Use in internal emails only, not client emails.
"Per our conversation…"
Connects a written email to a previous call or meeting. Professional and creates a paper trail. Example: "Per our conversation on Monday, I'm sending you the updated schedule."
TEACHER NOTE — Colloquial Drill

Ask: "Which phrases can you use this week?" All four hands should go up. Drill "FYI" specifically — many students have seen it but don't know what it stands for. Explain: For Your Information. Then: "Per our conversation" — tell students this phrase makes them sound immediately more professional in international emails. It shows they take notes and follow up. Role play: Teacher simulates a Monday call, student writes the follow-up email on Tuesday starting with "Per our conversation…"

Grammar — Imperatives in Email + Connectors
Problem → Tool · Group Sort mini-game
12–15 min
⚠️ REAL WORK SITUATION

Your manager asks you to write an email to a supplier with 3 steps for the new delivery process. You write: "Arrive at 7 AM. Go to reception. Give document. Wait."

❌ Technically correct, but it reads like a command list — cold and robotic. The tools: polite imperatives (Please + verb) and connectors (First / Then / Also / Finally) transform this into professional written English.

Polite Imperatives in Email

Cold version ❌With "Please" ✅With "Could you…?" ✅✅
Send the report.Please send the report.Could you send it to us?
Confirm the date.Please confirm the date.Could you confirm it by Friday?
Sign the document.Please sign the document.Could you sign it and return it?
Don't forget.Please don't forget to attach it.Could you make sure it's attached?

Connectors — Sequence & Addition

ConnectorFunctionEmail example
First,Step 1First, please arrive at Reception with your ID.
Then,Step 2Then, sign the visitor register and collect your badge.
Also,Additional infoAlso, please bring a copy of the purchase order.
Finally,Last stepFinally, send us the signed delivery note by email.
ButContrast / conditionThe report is ready, but we need your signature first.
SoResult / consequenceThe deadline is Friday, so please reply by Thursday.
TEACHER NOTE — Grammar Delivery

The 3-column table is the visual "ladder" — students see they can upgrade cold → polite → very polite. Tell them: "In international business, 'Please + verb' is the minimum standard. 'Could you…?' is for important requests or clients." For connectors: write the 4 steps from the PROBLEM card on the board and have students rewrite them with connectors + "please". Before Activity A: rapid fire drill — teacher says a connector, student says a sentence.

GT ORIGINAL — The 3-Email Types A1.2 Must Master

By end of M5, every A1.2 student should be able to write these 3 email types without help: (1) Meeting confirmation — covered S1A. (2) Information request — today's focus. (3) Absence / apology notification — S4A. These cover 80% of real corporate email situations at this level. If a student can write all 3 correctly, they're functionally bilingual in business writing at A1.2.

6
Listening — A Request & Confirmation Exchange
Mónica Herrera · Solaris Manufacturing · Celaya
8–10 min
Email Exchange Read Aloud — Solaris Manufacturing, Celaya
A1.2 · M5 · S3A · Voice: Sarah (American English) · Speed: 0.80
ElevenLabs Script — ~158 words · ~60 seconds at 0.80

Hello. I'm Mónica Herrera from ConnectPro International. Today I want to share two short emails — one request and one confirmation. Listen carefully for the connectors and the polite phrases.

Email 1 — Request:
Subject: Request for Delivery Schedule — June. Dear Mr. Torres, I'm writing to request the delivery schedule for June. Could you send it to us as soon as possible? We need it to plan our warehouse space. Also, could you confirm the contact person for deliveries at your Celaya office? Thank you in advance. I look forward to hearing from you. Best regards, Mónica Herrera.

Email 2 — Confirmation:
Subject: Delivery Schedule Confirmed — June. Dear Mónica, Thank you for your email. I'm writing to confirm the delivery schedule for June. First, deliveries will arrive on Tuesdays and Thursdays. Then, please go to Gate 3. Finally, sign the delivery document and send us a copy by email. Don't hesitate to contact me if you have any questions. Best regards, Ing. Torres.

Comprehension — True or False

1. Mónica requests the delivery schedule for July.

She requests the June delivery schedule, not July.

2. Mónica also asks for the contact person for deliveries.

✅ "Could you confirm the contact person for deliveries at your Celaya office?"

3. In the confirmation email, deliveries arrive on Mondays and Wednesdays.

Deliveries are on Tuesdays and Thursdays, not Mondays and Wednesdays.

4. The confirmation email uses three connectors: First, Then, Finally.

✅ "First… Then… Finally…" — the complete connector sequence.

5. Ing. Torres ends his email with "Don't hesitate to contact me."

✅ "Don't hesitate to contact me if you have any questions."
🗣️ MICRO-SPEAKING
From memory: What did Mónica request? What did Ing. Torres confirm? Tell your partner the key information in 2 sentences each.
TEACHER NOTE — Listening Analysis

Play the listening once. Then ask: "How many connectors did you hear?" Play again — students count (answer: 3 in email 2). Third question: "How many polite imperatives with 'Please' or 'Could you'?" (answer: 3 in email 1 + 2 in email 2). This analysis makes the grammar functional, not abstract. Follow up: "Which phrases can you copy directly into your next work email?" Expect students to identify "Thank you in advance" and "Don't hesitate to contact me" as immediately usable.

TEACHER NOTE — Speaking Practice Management

Q2 (dictate 3-step process) is the highest-value activity — it forces real content from students' own jobs into the grammar structure. Collect the written outputs from Q5 (confirmation email) — 2-3 students read theirs aloud. Class checks: (1) Are all 5 email parts present? (2) Which connectors were used? (3) Is there a polite imperative? This peer evaluation is more memorable than teacher correction. The Challenge (Q6) should feel celebratory — this is near-authentic corporate English at A1.2.

🎯 This Week at Work — Use These 3 Phrases
"First… Then… Finally…"
Write ANY multi-step instruction at work using these 3 connectors. Process documents, delivery instructions, system guides — they all need this sequence.
"Thank you in advance."
End your next request email with this. It's warm, professional, and shows you expect a positive response. International partners recognize it immediately.
"Let me know if you have any questions."
More human than "Don't hesitate." Use it with colleagues and internal clients. Replace any Spanish-style closing with this phrase this week.
